Opening a business in Obwalden: trade register and costs

Starting a business in the Canton of Obwalden means moving within a Swiss regulatory framework that is essentially uniform, but with specific cantonal variations regarding taxes, registration fees and administrative burdens. The first concrete decision concerns the legal form, because this choice determines minimum capital, accounting obligations, responsibilities and recurring costs.

The main options for those who intend to operate in Obwalden as an entrepreneur are the sole proprietorship, the general partnership, the limited partnership, the Sagl (limited guarantee company) and the Sa (anonymous company). The first three are not subject to registration in the commercial register, while Sagl and Sa must be registered. The LLC requires a minimum capital of CHF 20,000, fully paid, while the LLC requires at least CHF 100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be paid at the time of incorporation.

The registration procedure

For joint-stock companies, registration in the commercial register represents the formal step that confers legal personality. In Switzerland the register is managed at a federal level but with cantonal offices; in Obwalden it is the cantonal office responsible for receiving the application. The procedure involves the verification of the deed of incorporation, the statute, the identity of the founders and, for Sagl and Sa, the confirmation of the opening of a restricted bank account with a Swiss credit institution for the deposit of the capital.

Registration fees in the commercial register vary according to the canton and the legal form: in general they are made up of federal fees (federal commercial register) and cantonal fees. The precise amounts for Obwalden are not specified in this context and must be requested directly from the cantonal office.

From a tax point of view, those who open a business in Obwalden are subject to the three levels of taxation typical of Switzerland: direct federal tax (IFD), cantonal tax and municipal tax, the latter calculated by applying the municipal multiplier to the basic cantonal tax. The multiplier is set by each municipality and varies significantly even within the same canton.

Operational details

Choosing the legal form is not just a tax issue: it determines the level of personal responsibility, the ability to raise capital and accounting obligations. A sole proprietorship is suitable for a low-risk, low-volume business; Sagl and Sa instead offer limited liability to the contributed capital and a structure more suitable for growth and investments.

Practical implications of payroll contributions

Anyone who starts a business and intends to hire staff must consider mandatory social security and insurance contributions in Switzerland. The following apply to the salary paid: AVS/AI/IPG (5.3% paid by the employee, 10.6% total with the employer's share), AD/AC (1.1% up to the expected annual maximum), LAINF/LAA with rates between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector of activity, and contributions to the second pillar (LPP/BVG) calculated on the coordinated salary according to the age group: 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54), 18% (55 years up to the reference age). The AVS reference age is currently 65 for men and 64 for women, which is currently being harmonised.

For the owner himself, who is often initially also an employee of his own company, the same logic applies: the salary must be consistent with the AVS compensation funds and registered with the mandatory professional pension fund once the threshold for access to the LPP has been exceeded.

Insurance and health obligations

Compulsory health insurance according to the KVG must be taken out within three months of arriving in Switzerland or establishing domicile; the premiums are per capita, private, and do NOT constitute either a tax or a salary contribution. The annual deductibles of your choice for adults are CHF 300, 500, 1,000, 1,500, 2,000 or 2,500. Each canton publishes the regional average premiums, and in Obwalden a premium reduction (cantonal subsidy) is available for people with modest incomes, which must be requested from the competent cantonal authority.

For those who start their own business, the obligation to take out insurance against professional accidents (LAINF/LAA) also applies: if employed workers are subject to compulsory insurance, the independent owner can take out insurance on an optional but strongly recommended basis, especially in risk sectors.

Tax: three levels and municipal multiplier

In Obwalden, as in the rest of Switzerland, three levels of taxation operate: the IFD, regulated by federal law and managed by the Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV); cantonal and municipal taxes, regulated by the cantonal tax law and by municipal multipliers. The responsibilities are distinct: AFC/ESTV for IFD and VAT, cantonal administrations by cantonal and municipality, UFAS/BSV for social security (AVS/AI/LPP). The FSO/BFS produces statistics but does not set rates. This separation is relevant to knowing who to contact for each fulfillment.

For an operational comparison: in terms of overall tax burden, small businesses with sole proprietorships often enjoy more streamlined personal income taxation, while Sagl and Sa are taxed at corporate level with possible dividend distributions then taxed individually. There is no federal minimum wage in Switzerland: the Canton of Obwalden does not provide a cantonal minimum wage; any sectoral minimum wages derive from the collective labor agreements (CCL) declared to be generally binding. The legal maximum working time is 45 hours per week for industry, offices and sales, 50 hours for other sectors; the minimum holidays are 4 weeks (5 for under 20s).

Key points

Step-by-step procedure for opening a business in Obwalden

Step 1 — Choose the legal form. For low-risk businesses with a single owner, a sole proprietorship is often convenient, as it avoids registration in the commercial register. For businesses aiming for growth, investments or limited liability, Sagl (minimum capital CHF 20,000) or Sa (CHF 100,000) are the standard options.

Step 2 — Check the availability of the company name. Before setting up the company it is advisable to check that the desired name is not already registered in the Swiss commercial register (which can be consulted online at a federal level).

Step 3 — Open an escrow bank account. For Sagl and Sa it is necessary to open a capital deposit account with a Swiss bank; the bank issues a payment confirmation which must be attached to the registration application.

Step 4 — Draft the deed of incorporation and the statute. For Sa and Sagl the deed of incorporation is a public deed, which in Switzerland requires authentication before a notary. The statute regulates the corporate purpose, capital, bodies and rights of the members.

Step 5 — Register the company in the commercial register. The registration application is submitted to the cantonal commercial register office in Obwalden, with all the required attachments (deed of incorporation, statute, bank confirmation, identity of the founders and bodies).

Step 6 — Register with the tax authorities. After registration in the register, the new entity is communicated to the cantonal tax administration, which assigns the taxpayer number and indicates the declaration obligations for IFD, cantonal and municipal tax. If an activity with a turnover exceeding the VAT threshold is envisaged, registration with the AFC/ESTV for value added tax must be carried out.

Step 7 — Sign up for social insurance. The employer (even the owner who pays himself a salary) must affiliate with an AHV compensation fund and, if applicable, an BVG pension fund. SUVA manages compulsory accident insurance for employees; for independent owners, membership is optional but recommended.

Step 8 — Take out the KVG. The owner and every person resident in Switzerland must take out compulsory health insurance within three months of arriving or moving domicile. The choice of the deductible (CHF 300–2'500 for adults) directly affects the monthly premium.

Subsequent deadlines and obligations

Once the business has started, the periodic deadlines must be respected: tax declaration (cantonal form and federal form), VAT payments (usually quarterly according to the method agreed with the AFC), monthly or quarterly AVS/AI/IPG/AD contributions, LAINF premium and LPP premium. The four federal voting dates per year may occasionally also concern economic issues relevant to businesses (tax reforms, AVS, LPP); on such occasions the consequences of a yes or no must be assessed on a case-by-case basis.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum capital to open a Sagl or a Sa in Obwalden?
Sagl requires a minimum capital of CHF 20,000 fully paid up, while Sa requires at least CHF100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 paid up at the time of incorporation. The capital must be deposited in a restricted bank account before registration in the commercial register.
Do you need to register in the commercial register for a sole proprietorship in Obwalden?
No, the sole proprietorship is not subject to compulsory registration in the commercial register. Registration becomes mandatory for Sagl, Sa, a limited partnership, as well as for other forms provided for by law.
What social contributions must an employer pay in Switzerland?
On the salary, AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% are applied at the expense of the employee (10.6% total with the employer), AD/AC 1.1% up to the annual ceiling, LAINF/LAA between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector, and LPP/BVG contributions on the coordinated salary with rates ranging from 7% to 18% depending on age.
How long should LAMal health insurance be taken out?
Compulsory health insurance according to LAMal must be taken out within three months of arrival in Switzerland or transfer of domicile. Choice of annual deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500; each Canton publishes regional average premiums and provides for low income premium reductions.
Is there a federal or cantonal minimum wage in Obwalden?
In Switzerland there is no federal minimum wage; the Obwalden Canton has not introduced a cantonal minimum wage. Any minimum wages in the sector derive from collective labour agreements (CLAs) declared to be generally binding.
