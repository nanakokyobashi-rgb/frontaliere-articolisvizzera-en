Geneva approves free contraception with 58,5%

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 September 2026

58,5% of Geneva voters approved free contraception; turnout 44,2%, estimated cost 20 million francs/year, implementation expected in 2028.

The vote on contraception

Geneva is the first Canton to approve the principle of universal free access to contraception. The Socialist initiative «For free contraception» was approved today by 58,5% of voters. Turnout was 44,2%.

According to Keystone-ATS, all methods whose effectiveness is recognized will be included: the pill, intrauterine devices, also called coils, implants, injections, patches, vaginal rings, the morning-after pill and condoms. Costs are estimated at approximately 20 million francs per year.

During the campaign, the left and the Centre supported the text in the name of equal access to sexual and contraceptive healthcare. PLR, MCG and UDC, together with the Council of State, opposed it instead: in their view, the initiative was not sufficiently targeted at people on low incomes.

The next step is institutional. The ball is now in the Council of State's court, which will have to prepare an implementing law. Health official Pierre Maudet intends to bring together gynecologists, pharmacists, associations and initiative organizers to discuss the scope of the text and the measures to be adopted. Implementation of the initiative is expected in 2028.

The other decisions at the ballot box

In today's vote, the people of Geneva also approved a credit of 39,5 million francs to co-finance mobility infrastructure in neighboring France: the yes vote reached 56,8%. The project provides for 1600 additional parking spaces at French P+R facilities, priority lanes for buses and an extension of the tram network, with the aim of reducing by 15'000 the number of vehicles that travel on the Canton's roads every day.

With the exception of MCG and UDC, which allowed members to vote freely, all parties had called for approval of the credit.

On another issue, the people of Geneva instead rejected, with 58,5% of the vote, the proposal to reduce by 2 million francs per year, starting in 2026, the funding provided for by the 2025-2028 performance agreement between the University of Applied Sciences and Arts Western Switzerland and the Canton.

Finally, the legislative amendment aimed at facilitating tenants' purchase of housing was rejected with 63,5%. The Tenants' Association had launched the referendum against the reform because it feared an increase in lease terminations.

Operational details

Practical implications for those who live and work in Geneva

A principle to be turned into rules

The decision on contraception currently produces a political direction, not a complete operational timetable. The approved text enshrines the principle of universal free access; its implementation depends on the law that the Council of State still has to prepare. For those living in the Canton, the distinction is concrete: the ballot outcome clarifies the direction, while the next phase will have to specify the scope and the measures to be adopted. The round table announced by Pierre Maudet with gynecologists, pharmacists, associations and initiative proponents is the indicated step for discussing implementation, but the vote does not yet describe an individual procedure for obtaining free access.

The annual estimate makes the financial dimension of the choice visible. It is not, however, an individual figure: it is associated with the overall implementation of the measure. To read this figure alongside other family expenses, the site provides costo della vita in Svizzera. The contrast that emerged during the campaign also points to something to monitor: on the one hand, universal access; on the other, the request to focus the intervention on people with low incomes.

Mobility, education and housing

The credit for infrastructure in neighboring France has a practical impact linked to travel, but the project brings together different instruments: P+R car parks, preferential lanes for buses and a tram network. The stated objective is to reduce traffic on cantonal roads. Those who travel every day will therefore have to distinguish the vote on funding from the concrete implementation of individual projects: the source does not indicate a timetable for their becoming operational.

The other two no votes concern choices with different effects. The rejection of the proposed reduction in funding for the University of Applied Sciences and Arts Western Switzerland should not be read as approval of a new expenditure: the vote rejected that cut. The legislative amendment on tenants' purchase of their housing, on the other hand, was not approved; the referendum had been promoted by the Tenants' Association out of fear of an increase in terminations. For the other Cantons, the point to retain is the cantonal nature of the decisions: the source describes the Geneva vote and does not automatically announce the same measures elsewhere.

Key points

How to follow implementation

Three steps for the healthcare dossier

1. Separate the outcome from the mechanism. The vote approves the principle of universal free provision; the step to follow is the implementing law of the Council of State. Until that text is defined, do not attribute to the decision a request channel, a payment method or a procedure that the source does not indicate.

2. Check the scope of the law. The discussion announced by Pierre Maudet will bring together gynecologists, pharmacists, associations and initiative supporters. To follow the dossier, verify how the text will translate the list of recognized methods and what measures will be adopted. Implementation is expected in 2028: this is the political deadline indicated by the source.

3. Keep the overall estimate separate from the personal one. The annual estimate concerns the initiative as a whole and does not, on its own, constitute the amount attributed to an individual person. An individual calculation would require implementing rules that the vote does not yet provide.

A framework for the other results

For mobility, follow separately the three elements of the approved project: new spaces in French P+R facilities, priority lanes for buses and extension of the tram network. The objective regarding vehicles is indicated by the source, but is not accompanied by an operational timetable; therefore, it is advisable not to set personal dates based solely on the vote. Anyone who uses mezzi pubblici can use these three items as a reference for reading subsequent updates.

For education, note that the proposal to reduce funding during the specified contractual period was rejected: it should not be described as an approved reduction. For housing, the amendment that would have facilitated purchase by tenants was rejected; the result does not authorize treating it as a new rule.
