Operational details

Recycling fossil carrier infrastructure could have a significant impact on the energy transition. According to the study published in the journal Nature Communications, current fossil energy infrastructures are becoming increasingly obsolete. The Empa research group wanted to find out what raw material potential is hidden in these decommissioned plants and how it can be exploited for the green transition.

The study focuses on the analysis of the fossil energy infrastructures present in Switzerland, with particular attention to the cantons of Zurich, Bern and Vaud, where there are many decommissioned plants. According to Empa estimates, around 30% of the fossil energy infrastructure in Switzerland could be recycled and used for the production of renewable energy.

Recycling fossil energy infrastructure could have a significant impact on reducing greenhouse gas emissions. According to estimates by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), renewable energy production could reduce greenhouse gas emissions by around 70% by 2050.

Key points

Switzerland is one of the leading countries in the energy sector, with a high percentage of renewable energy production and a target to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. However, the current Swiss energy system is still largely based on fossil energy, which represents a limited and depleting resource. In this context, the Empa (Eidgenössische Materialprüfungs- und Forschungsanstalt) presented a report exploring the possibility of recycling fossil energy infrastructure into renewable energy facilities.

"Infrastructure recycling is a winning strategy to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and increase energy efficiency." - Dr. Daniel Götz, head of Empa's energy department

The Empa report highlights that fossil energy infrastructure, such as power plants and transport networks, could be transformed into renewable energy facilities, such as solar power plants and charging stations for electric vehicles. This recycling process could be achieved by replacing existing components with more modern and efficient technologies.
