Open Canton Svitto business: costs and registration

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 23 August 2026

Complete guide to starting a business in the canton of Switzerland: legal forms, registration in the commercial register, minimum capital, tax and social security obligations.

Context

In brief

  • Legal forms: sole proprietorship, general partnership, SA, SARL/GmbH, association
  • Commercial register: mandatory federal registration administered at cantonal level
  • Three levels of taxation: federal direct federal tax, cantonal taxes, municipal taxes
  • Social security: mandatory AVS/AI, optional LPP, mandatory LAMal

Key facts

  • What: Business registration in the Swiss commercial register
  • When: Before starting self-employment
  • Where: Commercial Registry Office of the Canton of Schwyz
  • Who: Entrepreneurs, sole proprietorships, companies limited by shares
  • Tax levels: Federal (direct federal tax + VAT), cantonal, municipal
  • AVS/AI contributions: 9.8% of net income for self-employed individuals
  • Minimum capital: CHF 100'000 for an SA (CHF 20'000 paid in), CHF 20'000 for a SARL

In the Canton of Schwyz, as in every Swiss canton, anyone wishing to carry out an independent economic activity must register in the commercial register. This is a federal regulation implemented at cantonal level: the register is centralized at the competent cantonal office, where the data of all businesses active in the territory is collected.

Registration in the commercial register is not merely an administrative formality: it marks the moment when the business acquires civil rights, makes it possible to open a corporate bank account and — crucially — activates tax and social security obligations. For this reason, it is essential to register BEFORE starting the activity, not afterwards.

Which legal form should you choose?

Swiss law provides for several forms of business activity, each with different tax, asset-related and organizational characteristics.

A sole proprietorship is the simplest form: one person carries out an economic activity independently. Liability is unlimited (personal assets are liable for business debts). Administrative and tax requirements are limited. It is suitable for freelancers, consultants and craftspeople without employees.

A general partnership (SNC) is a partnership between two or more people. The partners are jointly and severally and unlimitedly liable for the partnership's debts. Less common than other forms.

A public limited company (SA) is a company limited by shares with liability limited to the capital paid in. It requires minimum capital of CHF 100'000 (of which CHF 20'000 must be paid in upon incorporation). It is the most rigid form from an administrative perspective (shareholders' meeting, board of directors), but offers maximum asset protection to the owners. Suitable for medium-sized and large businesses.

A limited liability company (SARL/GmbH in German) is the compromise between the simplicity of a sole proprietorship and the protection of an SA. Minimum capital: CHF 20'000. Liability limited to the capital paid in. Less rigid than an SA from an organizational perspective. It is the most widespread form among Swiss SMEs.

An association is a particular form for nonprofit activities or activities serving a common purpose. Unlimited liability, less common for profit-making commercial activities.

Operational details

The three-level tax system

The most important element in managing a Swiss business is understanding the three-level tax system. The Swiss Confederation levies direct federal tax (IFD) on the company’s net income. Each canton adds a cantonal tax on income and profits. Each municipality, in turn, levies a municipal tax with a multiplier applied to the cantonal rate. As a result, the tax burden varies significantly between cantons and municipalities.

In the Canton of Schwyz, as in all cantons, the Cantonal Administration sets the cantonal rate; the municipalities then establish their own multiplier. This means that two identical businesses could pay very different taxes depending on where they are located. It is therefore essential to check the specific rates with the cantonal tax office and the municipality where you intend to locate the registered office.

Value-added tax (VAT) is administered at federal level by the Federal Customs Administration (AFC/ESTV). A business must register for VAT if it exceeds CHF 100'000 in annual turnover. The standard rate is 8.1%. Registering for VAT entitles the business to deduct taxes paid on purchased goods and services (accounting savings), but requires more complex quarterly reports and annual returns.

Pension and insurance: essential obligations

Every self-employed person in Switzerland MUST register with AVS/AI (old-age and disability provision). The contribution rate for a self-employed person is 9.8% of net income (calculated after standard deductions). Registration is handled by the municipality of residence. It is not optional: it is a federal legal obligation.

The LPP (pension fund, occupational old-age insurance) is mandatory for employees. The employer pays a percentage of the coordinated salary into a pension fund according to age brackets: 7% for ages 25–34, 10% for ages 35–44, 15% for ages 45–54, 18% for ages 55 until the reference age. For self-employed persons, the LPP is OPTIONAL, although it is strongly recommended by the majority of Swiss professionals to build an additional pension.

Health insurance (LAMal/KVG) is MANDATORY for all residents in Switzerland, regardless of employment status. It must be taken out within 3 months of arriving in Switzerland. Premiums are charged per person and vary by canton and insurance region. It is not a federal tax, but a private contract with a health insurer. Adult deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2'500.

Occupational accident insurance (LAInf/UVG) is mandatory if you employ staff. If you are self-employed without employees, it is optional but provides protection in the event of an occupational accident. The cost varies by sector (approximately 0.7–1.5% of salary).

Choosing the legal form in relation to expected income

A sole proprietorship is simpler but exposes personal assets to business risks. A SARL offers liability protection and organisational flexibility at reasonable cost. An SA is legally more rigid but suitable for companies with multiple shareholders and substantial capital. The choice depends on the expected size, the number of partners, risk tolerance, and expected income. An accountant or notary can guide you through the assessment.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure: from idea to registration

To start a business in the Canton of Schwyz, you must follow a precise procedure. Here are the essential steps.

Step 1: Choose the legal form. Consult a notary, accountant or cantonal commercial office to understand which form is most suitable. The choice depends on available capital, number of partners, and risk profile. If in doubt, speak to a professional.

Step 2: Register with the Commercial Register. Fill out the registration form and submit it to the Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Schwyz (decentralized offices, if present, accept registrations). Registration BEFORE starting the business is mandatory. Registration creates your federal business identity (IDE number, Enterprise Identification Number).

Step 3: Open a bank account. Once registered with the Commercial Register, open a bank account in the company's name. Banks in Switzerland require an extract from the Commercial Register as proof of the company's existence.

Step 4: Register with AVS. Go to the relevant municipal office (the municipality where you reside if you are self-employed; the municipality of the registered office if you are a company) and register with AVS. You will receive a company AVS number and notifications about the contributions due.

Step 5: Take out health insurance (LAMal). Contact a health insurer within 3 months of the first day of activity. You can choose from different deductible options and insurance models. Don't wait: the obligation is immediate and lack of coverage exposes you to penalties.

Step 6: Register for VAT (if applicable). If expected turnover exceeds CHF 100'000 annually, register with the Federal Customs Administration (AFC/ESTV) for VAT. You can do so online via the federal portal.

Step 7: Check insurance obligations specific to the sector. If you hire employees, accident insurance (LAInf) becomes mandatory. Contact SUVA or authorized private insurers in your sector to obtain a policy.

Verification checklist

  • [ ] Commercial Register registration form completed and signed
  • [ ] Business bank account opened with a Swiss bank
  • [ ] IDE number (Enterprise Identification Number) received
  • [ ] AVS registration completed at the municipality
  • [ ] Active health insurance (confirmation from health insurer)
  • [ ] Federal, cantonal and municipal IFD rate determined
  • [ ] Accounting and taxation plan prepared
  • [ ] Accident insurance taken out (if you have employees)

Administrative processing times and deadlines

The entire basic registration process (Commercial Register + AVS + LAMal) can take 2–4 weeks, depending on the speed of the local bureaucracy. Start in advance of the planned first day of activity. Each canton and municipality has slightly different timelines, so contact the offices of the Canton of Schwyz directly to confirm the exact deadlines.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between a sole proprietorship and a SARL in the Canton of Switzerland?
A single company has unlimited liability: personal assets are liable for company debts. A SARL (limited liability company) has limited liability to paid-in capital. The SARL requires CHF 20,000 of minimum capital and more administrative requirements (annual balance sheet, registers), but offers asset protection. The choice depends on the expected size of the company and the risk profile.
How much does it cost to register with the commercial register in the Canton of Schwyz?
Registration costs vary by canton and by legal form. Please contact the Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Schwyz directly for current rates. Generally, registration involves a one-off fee of a few hundred francs, which varies depending on the type of company.
Do I have to register for VAT immediately after registering?
VAT registration is mandatory only if the annual turnover exceeds CHF 100,000. You can also voluntarily register if you stay below this threshold (to deduct VAT on costs), but it is not mandatory. If you plan to exceed CHF100,000, register with the Federal Administration of Customs (AFC/ESTV) within the first month of operation.
Is social security (AVS, LPP, insurance) mandatory for an independent?
AVS (old age and disability) is compulsory for all, with contributions at 9.8% of net income. Health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory by federal law. The LPP (pension funds) is optional for the self-employed, although recommended. Accident insurance (LAInf) is mandatory only if you hire employees; otherwise optional.
In which office should I register my company?
Register the company at the Commercial Register Office of the canton where your company has its registered office. In the case of the Canton of Svitto, at the competent office of the canton itself (at central level or in decentralised offices). The registered office does not necessarily have to coincide with the personal domicile, but must be located in the canton chosen for registration.
