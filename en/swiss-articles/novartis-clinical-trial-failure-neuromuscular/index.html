Novartis: setback in the neuromuscular sector

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 September 2026

Novartis fails the Harbor study for the drug del-désiran in myotonic dystrophy type 1. It is the second negative trial in a few days, but the company maintains a 5-6% annual growth target for 2025-2030.

Context

In short

  • Novartis fails Harbor trial for del-désiran drug on type 1 myotonic dystrophy
  • Does not achieve primary objective despite "signs of clinical efficacy"
  • It is the second clinical failure in a few days for the Basel giant
  • Growth target of 5-6% per year 2025-2030 remains confirmed

Key facts

  • What: Failure of the Harbor clinical trial
  • Drug tested: delpacibart etédésiran (del-désiran)
  • Therapeutic indication: Myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1)
  • Outcome: Primary objective not achieved
  • Sequence: Second trial failure in a few days
  • Corporate headquarters: Basel, Switzerland
  • Economic prospects: Annual growth 5-6% confirmed 2025-2030

The multinational pharmaceutical company Novartis, based in Basel, has announced the failure of the clinical trial called "Harbor", dedicated to the drug delpacibart etédésiran (del-désiran) for the treatment of myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1). According to the company's official statement, the study did not meet its primary objective, although it demonstrated detectable "signals of clinical efficacy" during development.

This setback represents the second major late-stage clinical trial failure announced by Novartis in a matter of days. Myotonic dystrophy type 1 is a degenerative genetic disease of the neuromuscular system, and the tested drug represented a possible therapeutic path for patients suffering from this rare disease.

The meaning of clinical failure

Not reaching the primary objective of a clinical trial is a disappointing outcome from a scientific and strategic perspective, but it is an intrinsic part of the pharmaceutical development process. Novartis has invested years and significant resources researching del-désiran for the neuromuscular indication, and this announcement marks a major milestone in the development program.

However, it is crucial to understand that “signals of clinical efficacy” observed during the study are not sufficient to proceed towards regulatory approval. International regulatory bodies, including the Swiss agency Swissmedic, require that the drug clearly achieves the primary endpoints before the formal review phase.

Confidence confirmed despite obstacles

Despite recent clinical failures in the neuromuscular sector, Novartis has maintained full confidence in its overall economic prospects. The company confirmed its annual growth target of between 5% and 6% for the period 2025-2030. This signal is relevant for Swiss stakeholders: the multinational recognizes the obstacles in research, but considers its commercial strategy solid.

Confirmation of the growth target remains an indicator of Novartis' portfolio diversification. With dozens of therapeutic programs in development in different areas, failure to achieve the objective of the Harbor study does not compromise the company's expected consolidated performance in the coming years.

Operational details

Implications for the Swiss research economy

Novartis represents one of the pillars of the Swiss pharmaceutical industry and the overall national economy. Headquartered in Basel, it employs tens of thousands of people in Switzerland and maintains a comprehensive network of research, development and manufacturing facilities. Clinical trial failures, although they do not cause immediate dramatic economic effects, influence the company's market valuation, institutional investor confidence and sentiment in the Swiss life sciences sector.

Novartis' neuromuscular research strategy was a recognized priority. The drug del-désiran had been identified as a promising drug candidate for a rare and severe disease. Failure to achieve the primary endpoint in the Harbor study means that the regulatory path to approval for this therapeutic indication has been interrupted, at least temporarily, requiring a strategic reevaluation of the program.

However, it is important to highlight that Novartis operates on a very large and diverse portfolio of research projects. A single clinical failure, while significant from a scientific and commercial perspective, does not compromise the overall strength of the company's business model or Novartis' position in the global pharmaceutical market.

Switzerland's role as a clinical research hub

Switzerland is a recognized international hub for the pharmaceutical and biotechnology industry. Novartis, together with other large players such as Roche and GSK, represents the strategic core of this industry. Massive investments in research and development, including complex clinical trials such as Harbor, keep the Swiss economy competitive and attractive in the global life sciences and sciences sector.

Announcements such as the failure of the Harbor study have implications that ripple across Swiss industry: university and hospital researchers collaborating with multinational pharmaceutical companies, diagnostic and clinical service providers, specialized research institutes, and patient organizations. Excellent research in this area requires constant investment, acceptance of scientific risk and a deep-rooted culture of innovation.

Growth prospects and diversification strategy

Despite the failure of the Harbor study, Novartis maintains its annual growth target of 5-6% for 2025-2030. This goal reflects a deliberate diversification strategy: the company's overall success does not depend on a single therapeutic program, but on the sum of numerous research initiatives in different fields, expected regulatory approvals, penetration into emerging markets and operational efficiency.

The confirmation of the growth target is a message that international market analysts will monitor carefully. If Novartis can maintain this growth trajectory despite recent clinical failures in the neuromuscular space, it will demonstrate the resilience of the business model and the validity of the diversified portfolio strategy domestically and globally.

For those who live and work in Switzerland and have a personal, professional or investment interest in the healthcare and biotechnology sector, these documents provide concrete and verifiable information.

Access to experimental drugs and the Swiss healthcare system

The failure of the Harbor study also illustrates the importance of excellent clinical research conducted in Switzerland. Swiss hospital centers and accredited research institutes regularly participate in international clinical trials of multinational pharmaceutical companies, contributing with expertise and patient recruitment sites to studies such as Harbor.

For patients with rare diseases such as myotonic dystrophy type 1, participation in controlled clinical trials can represent an opportunity to access experimental therapies when approved therapeutic options are unavailable or insufficient. The Swiss healthcare system, regulated by KVG law (mandatory health insurance), includes specific mechanisms for access to experimental drugs through compassionate use programs and extended access in particular clinical situations, always subject to rigorous ethical evaluation.

What is the Harbor study and why is it important for patients with myotonic dystrophy?
The Harbor study was a clinical trial conducted by Novartis to evaluate the drug delpacibart etédésiran (del-désiran) as a treatment for myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1), a rare genetic degenerative disorder of the neuromuscular system. Although the drug showed "signs of clinical efficacy" during the trial, the study did not reach its primary goal, blocking the path to regulatory approval at least in the short term. For rare diseases such as DM1, well-designed trials represent potential pathways to new treatment options when approved treatments are limited.
How does the failure of the Harbor study affect the Swiss economy and Novartis?
Novartis is one of Switzerland's leading economic engines based in Basel.
