Novartis: setback in the neuromuscular sector (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 September 2026

Novartis fails the Harbor study for the drug del-désiran in myotonic dystrophy type 1. It is the second negative trial in a few days, but the company maintains a 5-6% annual growth target for 2025-2030.

Context

In short

  • Novartis fails Harbor trial for del-désiran drug on type 1 myotonic dystrophy
  • Does not achieve primary objective despite "signs of clinical efficacy"
  • It is the second clinical failure in a few days for the Basel giant
  • Growth target of 5-6% per year 2025-2030 remains confirmed

Key facts

  • What: Failure of the Harbor clinical trial
  • Drug tested: delpacibart etédésiran (del-désiran)
  • Therapeutic indication: Myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1)
  • Outcome: Primary objective not achieved
  • Sequence: Second trial failure in a few days
  • Corporate headquarters: Basel, Switzerland
  • Economic prospects: Annual growth 5-6% confirmed 2025-2030

The multinational pharmaceutical company Novartis, based in Basel, has announced the failure of the clinical trial called "Harbor", dedicated to the drug delpacibart etédésiran (del-désiran) for the treatment of myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1). According to the company's official statement, the study did not meet its primary objective, although it demonstrated detectable "signals of clinical efficacy" during development.

This setback represents the second major late-stage clinical trial failure announced by Novartis in a matter of days. Myotonic dystrophy type 1 is a degenerative genetic disease of the neuromuscular system, and the tested drug represented a possible therapeutic path for patients suffering from this rare disease.

The meaning of clinical failure

Not reaching the primary objective of a clinical trial is a disappointing outcome from a scientific and strategic perspective, but it is an intrinsic part of the pharmaceutical development process. Novartis has invested years and significant resources researching del-désiran for the neuromuscular indication, and this announcement marks a major milestone in the development program.

Operational details

Implications for the Swiss research economy

Novartis represents one of the pillars of the Swiss pharmaceutical industry and the overall national economy. Headquartered in Basel, it employs tens of thousands of people in Switzerland and maintains a comprehensive network of research, development and manufacturing facilities. Clinical trial failures, although they do not cause immediate dramatic economic effects, influence the company's market valuation, institutional investor confidence and sentiment in the Swiss life sciences sector.

Novartis' neuromuscular research strategy was a recognized priority. The drug del-désiran had been identified as a promising drug candidate for a rare and severe disease. Failure to achieve the primary endpoint in the Harbor study means that the regulatory path to approval for this therapeutic indication has been interrupted, at least temporarily, requiring a strategic reevaluation of the program.

However, it is important to highlight that Novartis operates on a very large and diverse portfolio of research projects. A single clinical failure, while significant from a scientific and commercial perspective, does not compromise the overall strength of the company's business model or Novartis' position in the global pharmaceutical market.

Switzerland's role as a clinical research hub

Switzerland is a recognized international hub for the pharmaceutical and biotechnology industry. Novartis, together with other large players such as Roche and GSK, represents the strategic core of this industry. Massive investments in research and development, including complex clinical trials such as Harbor, keep the Swiss economy competitive and attractive in the global life sciences and sciences sector.
