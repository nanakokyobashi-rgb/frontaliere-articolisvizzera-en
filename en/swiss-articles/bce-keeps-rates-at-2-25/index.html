Operational details

The European Central Bank (ECB) has decided to keep interest rates at 2.25% despite the uncertainty remaining high. This decision was taken by the Governing Council of the ECB, which closely follows the intensity and duration of the energy shock and its indirect effects.

ECB President Christine Lagarde said: "The ECB will closely follow economic data and trends to make the most appropriate decisions." The ECB warned that the inflationary impact of the energy shock has yet to fully manifest and that the future path will remain data-driven.

The ECB's decision to keep rates at 2.25% was influenced by economic trends in Switzerland. Swiss GDP grew by 2.5% in 2022, while inflation was 3.4%. However, the ECB is concerned about the impact of the energy shock on economic growth and inflation.

Switzerland is one of the most stable countries in Europe, but the impact of the energy shock cannot be underestimated. The city of Geneva, for example, saw a 15% increase in rental prices in 2022, while the city of Zurich saw a 12% increase in house prices.

The ECB also warned that the impact of the energy shock could be strongest in the most energy-dependent cantons. The Canton of Graubünden, for example, saw a 20% increase in energy prices in 2022.
