A Succession That’s Already Political

BERN — Guy Parmelin, President of the Confederation and Federal Councilor, has announced his resignation, scheduled for the end of the year. The decision opens a debate on the profile of his successor and on how the Federal Council should address Switzerland’s current and future challenges.

The Center Party responded by acknowledging the SVP’s right to retain its two seats on the Federal Council. In the same statement, the party linked the succession to a specific political demand: the new member of the government must contribute to constructive cooperation, thereby enabling the executive branch to address the country’s challenges.

The Center acknowledges the SVP’s claim to two seats on the Federal Council.

The SVP paid tribute to Parmelin for his long-standing commitment and expressed regret over his resignation. In its statement, the party noted that, as defense minister, Parmelin initially championed the strengthening of the armed forces and national defense. In the Department of Economic Affairs, the SVP continued, he strengthened Switzerland’s economic, educational, and research sectors.

The statement also highlights his commitment to open markets and favorable framework conditions. According to the SVP, Switzerland has improved, diversified, and expanded its trade relations. The party believes this policy facilitates companies’ access to growing markets, protects jobs, and strengthens the country’s prosperity.

The PLR, in turn, thanked the Federal Councilor for his many years of service and expressed an expectation to the UDC: the list of potential successors should also include figures from French-speaking Switzerland. This request is motivated by the country’s quadrilingual character and national cohesion.

Business associations emphasized the Department of Economic Affairs’ budget. The USAM praised the free trade agreements with India, Vietnam, and other countries, as well as the start of negotiations to renew the agreement with China. It also cited the Business Relief Act, the revision of the Vocational Training Act, and the Professional Bachelor’s and Professional Master’s degrees, which will take effect on October 1, 2026. Economiesuisse, through Monika Rühl, expressed regret over the decision and acknowledged the work done on the free trade agreements. The association believes that the successor at the helm of the WBF must commit to ensuring a competitive business environment.

Operational details

The criteria included in the comparison

The source does not announce any immediate measures for families or businesses. However, it outlines four criteria that could guide the discussion: political representation, linguistic cohesion, economic orientation, and cooperation within the government. For those who live or work in Switzerland, the practical implication today is to follow these criteria without confusing them with decisions that have already been made.

Four Criteria for Evaluating Candidates

The first criterion is representation. The Center acknowledges the two SVP seats; the PLR requests that the selection process also consider candidates from French-speaking Switzerland. The second is collaboration: the Center’s request calls for a Federal Council capable of working constructively on current and future challenges.

The third concerns foreign economic policy. The SVP, USAM, and Economiesuisse emphasize open markets and free trade agreements. India and Vietnam are cited among the agreements, while the renewal of the agreement with China is presented by USAM as an achievement. The fourth point addresses the domestic economy: relief for businesses, higher vocational education, and social partnership.

Key points

How to Follow the Succession Process

The text does not mention any candidates’ names or a date for the selection. For the public, therefore, the best approach is a systematic review: distinguishing between what has been announced and what political parties and associations expect from the next Federal Councilor.

Four Practical Steps

1. Establish the facts. Parmelin’s departure is scheduled for the end of the year. Any subsequent statements should be viewed as part of the succession process, not as a change that has already taken effect.

2. Review the SVP’s list using the criteria outlined by the FDP. The request calls for candidates from French-speaking Switzerland, given the country’s quadrilingual nature and the need for national cohesion. The source does not state that a candidate meeting these criteria has already been nominated.

3. Compare the policy dossiers cited by the associations. Regarding economic policy, the references include open markets, free trade agreements with India and Vietnam, other agreements, and the renewal of the agreement with China. Regarding domestic policy, one can examine the positions on the Business Relief Act, higher vocational education, and social partnership.

4. Distinguish between assessments and actions. The recognition of the two seats reflects the Center’s position; praise for the economic performance comes from the SVP, USAM, and Economiesuisse. These are elements of the succession debate, not a mandate already assigned to the successor.

For those following the issue from the labor perspective, the annunci di lavoro documents can be consulted as a separate resource. However, they do not allow one to attribute an automatic impact on hiring to Parmelin or the future councilor: the source refers to job protection as the SVP’s assessment of trade relations.

The same criterion applies to personal accounts. Before linking the succession to a concrete change, we must wait for an actual decision and distinguish between the parties’ statements and the positions of economic associations.
