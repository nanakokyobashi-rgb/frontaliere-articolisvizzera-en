The Swiss Army, a step forward in the fight against sexism and discrimination

The Swiss Army has announced that it has made significant progress in combating sexism and discrimination within its ranks. According to official data, half of the measures announced about two years ago have already been implemented, marking an important step in the direction of a culture of inclusion and respect.

The action plan is based on a 2024 study on discrimination and sexualized violence, which highlighted the need for cultural change within the Army. According to the study, sexualized violence is still a widespread problem within the armed forces, with only 30% of victims reporting abuse.

To address this problem, the Swiss Army has implemented several measures, including the creation of an office for the protection of women's rights and the establishment of an anonymous reporting system for victims of abuse. In addition, the Army has also introduced a training course for officers and soldiers on the importance of diversity and inclusion.

The Swiss Army is committed to gender equality

The Swiss Army has announced a series of measures to combat sexism and discrimination within its ranks. About two years ago, the Army began implementing these measures, with the goal of creating a more inclusive and equal working environment.

One of the first measures announced was the establishment of a special office for gender equality. This office, located in Bern, is responsible for monitoring and preventing any cases of discrimination within the Army.

In addition, the Army has also introduced a training program for its military, with the aim of raising their awareness of the importance of gender equality and preventing discrimination. This programme has been implemented in collaboration with the University of Bern.

According to statistics, half of the announced measures have been successfully implemented. For example, the number of women who joined the Army increased by 20% compared to the previous two years. In addition, the number of recruits who reported cases of discrimination decreased by 30%.
