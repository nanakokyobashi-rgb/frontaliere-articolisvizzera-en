Population alert: 165,4 mln for modernization

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 28 October 2026

The Federal Council is requesting 165,4 million francs.

165,4 million francs. This is the credit the Federal Council is requesting from Parliament to modernize the population warning system. The message, adopted at today's meeting in Bern, also proposes amending federal law and transferring responsibility for sirens, including the related funding, to the cantons.

According to the Executive's announcement, the reform aims to provide the population with information, warnings and alerts in the event of an incident, using modern channels. The approach is that of a multichannel strategy, designed to provide comprehensive information in crisis situations and take advantage of the widespread use of smartphones. The Federal Office for Civil Protection, referred to by the acronym UFPP, manages the necessary systems.

Alertswiss and the new siren system

The Alertswiss app and website, operational since 2018, are to be maintained and enhanced, with priority given to accessibility and the availability of important information on smartphones even when there is no network connection. Communications should also be offered in standardized formats so that they can be integrated by third-party providers.

As regards sirens, the transfer concerns both fixed and mobile installations. Responsibility and financing would be transferred entirely to the cantons. According to the proposal, the measure is intended to clarify the division of responsibilities. The Confederation would instead retain responsibility for ensuring the uniformity and security of the remote activation system and would be expected to provide a new one by 2035.

Operational details

The comparison between the credit and spending through 2035

The message distinguishes the credit requested from Parliament from the cost framework indicated by the Confederation. The difference does not concern two versions of the same figure: the first amount is presented as a commitment credit for investments, whereas the second comprises investment and operating costs intended to maintain and develop the information, alert and warning systems.

The difference of 55,5 million results from subtracting 165,4 from 220,9. It is a calculation based on the reported values, not a new expenditure item announced by the Federal Council. The text does not provide any further breakdown among the individual components, nor does it assign a specific amount to the cantons.

The scope of the 220,9 million includes the central system, the siren activation system and the development of Alertswiss. The 165,4 million credit, on the other hand, is the one Parliament is being asked to grant to finance investments. For this reason, the figures should be read together but not conflated: one describes the financing requested for investments, while the other describes the overall investment and operating cost through 2035.

Also in terms of channels, the proposal does not describe a straightforward replacement. Alertswiss, with its app and website operational since 2018, is maintained and enhanced. Cell broadcast is introduced to complement and strengthen the existing system, sending short messages to all mobile phones present in the affected area. Fixed and mobile sirens remain part of the system, but responsibilities and funding are transferred to the cantons. The multichannel strategy thus connects tools already in operation, a new mobile-phone channel and the siren infrastructure, while the Confederation retains the task specified for remote activation.

Key points

The timeline and limits indicated in the message

The text from the Federal Council provides some fixed points, but not a complete implementation timeline. The most precise date concerns the remote activation system for the sirens: the Confederation will continue to guarantee its uniformity and security and will provide a new one by 2035. The same year delimits the period under consideration: through 2035, investment and operating expenses are estimated at 220,9 million francs.

Consensus concerns cell broadcast

For the mobile phone alert system, the source reports that cell broadcast received unanimous support during the consultation. It adds that its launch was brought forward thanks to a separate commitment credit. The text, however, does not indicate the amount of this credit, nor does it describe a service activation date. The consensus cited therefore concerns this specific element; it is not presented as a unanimous outcome for the entire package.

Regarding the transfer of sirens, the proposal indicates where responsibility would lie: the Cantons would take over full responsibility for the management and financing of fixed and mobile sirens. However, it does not indicate the precise timing of the transfer, the distribution of costs among the individual Cantons, or the number of systems involved. The source also does not specify which third-party providers will integrate the standardized communication formats, or in what sequence the Alertswiss app and website will be enhanced.

Parliament remains the addressee of the request to amend the Federal Act and grant the credit of 165,4 million francs. The source does not report a date for parliamentary consideration, final approval, or entry into force. The objective, the multichannel architecture, the new cantonal responsibility for sirens, and the financial framework through 2035 are therefore documented; the operational details for each territory or each channel are not yet. For the population, the message defines how the dissemination of alerts could be strengthened, without indicating individual procedures or instructions linked to a specific event.

How much does the modernization of the alarm system cost?
