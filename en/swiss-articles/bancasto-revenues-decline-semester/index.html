The Cantonal State Bank of Ticino recorded a 6% contraction in revenue on a year-on-year basis in the first half of 2026, falling to CHF 153 million. The operating result decline was more pronounced, contracting by 22% to CHF 97 million. Despite these declines, net profit maintained substantial stability thanks to extraordinary income, registering a slight increase of 1% to CHF 48 million.

What held back results

The institution attributes the revenue decline to current reference rate levels and a challenging macroeconomic environment. In this scenario, trading margins compressed. However, the bank's management emphasizes that revenues remain, from a historical perspective, 'at an excellent level'. The chief executive officer, Fabrizio Cieslakiewicz, describes the results as 'certainly positive' and worthy of 'genuine satisfaction', considering the competitive environment of minimal trading margins.

Operational details

The Impact of Rates on Savings Choices

In the context of low benchmark rates, returns on traditional deposits are significantly reduced. For Ticino and Swiss savers, this means that income from interest-bearing current accounts or term deposits is compressed. At the same time, the institution must adapt while maintaining the competitiveness of the service portfolio. The growth in assets under management (+4.1% to 25.1 billion) suggests that clients continue to seek investment and asset management solutions to compensate for the low return on liquid savings. This behaviour reflects a national trend: when rates fall, households diversify towards complementary investment instruments.

## Mortgage loans that are resilient in the real estate market

The growth in mortgage loans of 1.9% to 13.1 billion reflects stable residual demand in the Ticino real estate market, despite the context of theoretically less favourable rates. This is consistent with the national trend, where mortgages remain a central tool of family financial management and housing planning. The bank, as a historical regional institution, therefore maintains a consolidated position in the mortgage credit segment, which continues to represent a significant component of the balance sheet and a stable source of revenue.

A story of reliability since its founding in 1915

The Banca dello Stato del Cantone Ticino (Bank of the State of the Canton of Ticino) has deep and significant roots in Swiss economic history. It was established in 1915 precisely to respond to the great turmoil in the Ticino banking market, when several institutions were in difficulty and forced to close their doors. In those dramatic years, Ticino savers lost approximately 40 million francs, almost half of the savings deposited in the canton's credit institutions. The public institution was created as an anchor of solidarity. In 1961, BancaStato opened to unsecured credit, expanding its offering beyond the mortgage sector alone. The transformation into a true universal bank was completed only in 2003, consolidating a range of modern services. This historical path underscores how BancaStato has always been part of financial stability and the protection of territorial savings.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why did BancaStati Ticino's revenues drop by 6%?
According to the institute, the decline is due to the current level of benchmark rates and the challenging macroeconomic environment. In a low rate environment, trade margins compress, reducing credit intermediation revenue. Despite this, the bank stresses that revenues remain 'at an excellent level' from a historical point of view.
Has the bank's net profit decreased or remained stable?
Net profit remained virtually stable, registering a slight increase of 1% to CHF 48 million in the first half of 2026. This was thanks to extraordinary income that offset the decline in ordinary revenues deriving from credit intermediation operations.
Are the assets managed by BancaStat still growing?
Yes, assets under management increased by 4.1% to CHF 25.1 billion in the first half of 2026. Net inflows reached 554 million, confirming customers' confidence in the institution and the ability to attract new deposits and resources to invest.
What does BancaStat expect for the second half of 2026?
The bank expects that the second half of 2026 should not deviate significantly from what was recorded in the first half of the year, unless unexpected developments occur. The management therefore maintains a certain caution, while referring to the positive results demonstrated by the business model and the diversification of revenues.
