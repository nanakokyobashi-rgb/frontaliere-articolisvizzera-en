The Swiss Confederation has already launched several measures to address the drought. The law also provides for the creation of an emergency plan for the management of water resources in the event of drought.

However, Ufam points out that the drought could have negative consequences for agriculture and industry, especially if the situation does not improve in the coming months.

According to Ufam, drought is most severe in mountain regions, where water scarcity can have serious consequences for agriculture and forestry. For example, in the canton of Graubünden, the drought has already had negative consequences for fruit and vegetable harvesting, with a loss of about 10 million francs.

In addition, Ufam reported that drought could have negative consequences for water quality. For example, in Lake Geneva, drought has already decreased the amount of water, with negative consequences for water quality and fisheries.
