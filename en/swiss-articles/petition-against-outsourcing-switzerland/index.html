Transfair launches a petition to stop the relocation of jobs and know-how abroad by Posta and Swisscom.

The petition against the outsourcing of jobs abroad by Post and Swisscom was launched by the public sector union Transfair. The organization urges the Federal Council and Parliament to intervene to stop what it considers an evolution contrary to the interests of the country. Post and Swisscom, both majority-owned by the Confederation and therefore by the Swiss population, transfer jobs and know-how abroad solely to save costs, according to Greta Gysin, president of Transfair. 'In this way, Switzerland loses control over sensitive activities: and the Confederation allows both groups to act freely, instead of imposing clear rules,' adds the Ticino Green National Councillor.

Since 2022, for example, Post has transferred 130 IT jobs to Lisbon. Swisscom has outsourced over 600 positions to Latvia and the Netherlands and has opened additional offices in Kosovo, Poland, and Bulgaria. The telecom company has announced plans to open a new unit in Lisbon, while Post has had to temporarily pause its plans in the Portuguese capital until summer 2027.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

The petition launched by Transfair raises crucial questions regarding the relocation of overseas jobs by Posta and Swisscom. These two companies, mostly owned by the Confederation, are transferring jobs and know-how abroad to save costs, a practice that the union considers contrary to the interests of the country.

Impact on the Labour Market

The relocation of IT jobs and other specialist positions abroad has a significant impact on the Swiss labour market. Since 2022, Swiss Post has relocated 130 IT jobs to Lisbon, while Swisscom has outsourced over 600 positions in Latvia, the Netherlands and opened additional offices in Kosovo, Poland and Bulgaria. This trend risks reducing job opportunities for young Swiss and compromising the quality of performance offered by the two companies.

Safety and Quality of Services Issues

Strategic and sensitive activities must remain in Switzerland to ensure the security of supply and the country. The relocation of these activities could compromise the quality of the services offered by the Post Office and Swisscom, which are fundamental for the population. In addition, the loss of specialist know-how could have negative repercussions on the safety and efficiency of services.
