Context

In breve

  • National strike of 24 hours in the aviation sector in Italy.
  • Approximately 160 flights canceled between Malpensa and Linate.
  • Protest for the non-renewal of the collective agreement.
  • Request for salary adjustment to inflation.

Operational details

Operational Implications for Passengers The management of passengers during the day of unrest was entrusted to the preventive planning of SEA, the company that manages the Milan connections. Users were pre-informed about the situation, allowing in many cases to reschedule the connections outside the guaranteed time slots set by Italian law. Despite the high number of cancellations, the airports did not register significant delays for the remaining operational flights. The situation is continuously monitored to ensure that the flight operations respect the safety measures for the crew and the final users.

Key points

Procedure of Protection and Management of Discomforts In case of air transport strike, the first action recommended for each passenger is to check the status of their flight through the airline's website or the departure airport portal. Airlines are required to promptly communicate any changes, and in case of cancellation, to offer alternatives for subsequent flights or, alternatively, a full refund of the travel ticket. If the flight falls within a protected range by law, the airline must provide the service; however, in case of a general strike, the protected ranges may experience variations or limitations. Passengers are invited not to go to the airport without first confirming the operability of their flight.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main causes of the strike in the aviation sector?
The strike was called by the CUB union to protest against the non-renewal of the national employment contract, which has expired for over six months. The demands include adjusting wages to inflation, more equitable management of holidays and Sunday shifts, and greater job security protections.
Which airports were affected by the mobilisation?
The mobilisation affected the Milanese airports of Malpensa and Linate. Altogether about 160 flights were cancelled, of which 140 in Malpensa and about 20 in Linate.
What should passengers with cancelled flights do?
Passengers are invited to check the status of their flight through the airline's official channels. The companies are required to inform users and offer protection solutions or a refund of the ticket, depending on the conditions provided for by current legislation in the event of cancellation.
