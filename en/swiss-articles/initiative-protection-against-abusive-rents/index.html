Abusive pensions initiative: the situation in Switzerland (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 August 2026

The popular initiative 'Yes to protection against illegal rents' collected 108,437 valid signatures. Here's what's changing for Swiss tenants.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Reached 108,437 valid signatures for the popular initiative.
  • The initiative aims to combat rents defined as abusive.
  • Calculated an average surcharge of CHF 360 per month per tenant.
  • The proposal now goes to the Federal Council and Parliament.

Key facts

  • What: 'Yes to protection against abusive rents' initiative
  • When: Confirmation of success today
  • Where: Switzerland
  • Who: Swiss Tenants Association and Yannick Demaria
  • Amount: 360 francs average monthly surplus
  • Volume: 10.6 billion francs total surplus

The Federal Chancellery has officially certified the success of the popular initiative called 'Yes to protection against illegal rentals'. With 108,437 signatures declared valid, the text continues its institutional process, reaching the tables of the Federal Council and Parliament. The resulting political debate touches closely on the majority of residents in Switzerland, in a context where housing is an essential need. The initiative does not merely raise a question of principle, but is based on detailed economic calculations prepared by the BASS institute under the mandate of the Swiss Tenants' Association. The central figure that emerged concerns an average surplus of 360 francs per month for each rental dwelling, a figure that on an annual basis exceeds 4,300francs. Altogether, the volume of these surpluses is estimated at CHF 10.6 billion.

Operational details

The analysis of the current mechanism highlights how the protection system is based on the initiative of the individual. The structure of the market, where the landlord often holds an advantageous position, makes it difficult for the tenant to challenge the rent without risking complex procedures. The initiative proposes a redefinition of 'abusive rent', strictly linking it to the actual management costs and the purchase price of the property. This transformation is not aimed at denying the owners a return, but at limiting the speculation that occurs in changes of tenant. In recent years, pressure from the real estate lobby has manifested itself through proposals aimed at facilitating cancellations, perceived by tenants as attempts to weaken existing guarantees. Each time a tenant leaves the apartment, the landlord has the opportunity to increase the rent, fueling a spiral of cost growth. The proposed regulatory amendment intends to intervene precisely on these dynamics, relieving individual citizens of the burden of having to independently demonstrate the iniquity of the fee. ### The impact on the cost of living. The issue is not only about the balance at the end of the month, but directly affects the possibility of maintaining one's social network and living close to one's workplace or children's schools. When rents increase uncontrollably, entire sections of the population are forced to move away from the centres

Key points

For those living or working in Switzerland, the evolution of this initiative represents a moment of reflection on how the right to housing is protected by institutions. Currently, those who reside in rental properties must refer to the provisions of the Code of Obligations to contest a rent deemed excessive. It is essential to understand that, in the absence of new laws, the procedure remains the responsibility of the tenant. To delve into one's financial situation, it is possible to consult tools such as the salary calculator, which allows for monitoring the impact of fixed expenses on the family budget. The current procedure requires verifying whether the rent is higher than what is justified by management costs and the permitted return, an operation often complex without the support of specialists. The initiative aims to simplify this framework by introducing periodic checks that would eliminate the need for citizens to pursue legal action in isolation. ### Guidance tools. Beyond the issue of rents, managing a family budget in Switzerland involves various expense items, such as health insurance premiums or social contributions. For those who wish to have an overview of their expenses, it is useful to use the cost of living as a reference point to compare their own spending with the national average. Housing remains, however, a central pillar: affordable housing allows for better long-term savings planning, for example through the third pillar, which is fundamental for supplementing the AVS/LPP pension income. The initiative does not claim privileges, but asks for the respect of a principle of contractual fairness. Anyone in a situation of uncertainty regarding their rent can monitor the developments of this initiative through the official channels of the Confederation. Pending the examination by the Federal Council and the eventual parliamentary debate, it remains essential to stay informed about current provisions. The construction of moderately priced housing and protection against unjustified terminations remain the cornerstones for ensuring the housing stability requested by citizens.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does the initiative concretely propose?
The initiative wants the rents to be considered abusive if they exceed the actual costs of the property, including an adequate return and the purchase price. It proposes automatic periodic checks instead of leaving the burden of the dispute to the individual tenant.
What are the estimates of the economic impact?
According to calculations by the BASS institute, commissioned by the Swiss Tenants' Association, in 2023 the average surplus paid for each rental dwelling was CHF 360 per month, for an annual total of about CHF 10.6 billion.
What will happen to the initiative now?
After the validity of 108,437 signatures has been confirmed by the Federal Chancellery, the proposal is forwarded to the Federal Council and Parliament for discussion and legislative evaluation.
