The federal voting round on September 27 mobilizes considerable financial resources. The committees involved in the two items on the ballot declared total budgets of almost 8 million francs, according to data published by the Federal Financial Control (EFK). About half of the sum goes to the campaign of the supporters of the neutrality initiative, which is confirmed as the most expensive dossier of this electoral round.

Neutrality: the yes front more than twice exceeds the opponents

For the neutrality initiative, the favorable front declared 3.84 million francs, against 1.67 million made available by the opponents.

The rules on electoral transparency in Switzerland impose precise reporting obligations. Whoever conducts a voting campaign spending more than 50,000 francs must declare its funding. Donors who pay contributions starting from CHF 15,000 must also be indicated. This is a mechanism designed to give voters visibility into the money flows that influence referendum and popular campaigns.

A comparison with June's record expenses

The amounts declared for the September vote — almost 8 million francs — are significantly lower than those used for the "No to a 10 million Switzerland" initiative (also known as the "Sustainability Initiative"), which was put to the vote in June. For that dossier, much discussed and then rejected by the voters, a total of 16.27 million francs had been declared.

For those living and working in Switzerland, the rules on campaign financing transparency offer the possibility to inform yourself before voting. The Swiss Federal Audit Office (EFK) publishes data on financing declarations, accessible to anyone who wants to verify who economically supports the various positions at stake.

How the declaration works

Committees that spend more than 50,000 francs on a voting campaign must submit a declaration to the Swiss Federal Audit Office. In this declaration, all contributions received must be indicated, with separate evidence of donors who contribute sums equal to or greater than 15,000 francs. The data is then made public by the EFK, typically in the weeks preceding the vote.

This system allows voters to evaluate not only the arguments of campaigns, but also the economic and political interests that lie behind each position. A side that raises funds primarily from the food industry, for example, may have economic reasons to oppose an initiative that could affect prices or industry regulations.

What to do concretely

Before each federal vote, you can consult the EFK website to verify declared funding. The procedure is simple: access the EFK portal, search for the September 27 vote, and download the declaration documents for each committee. This way, you can get a precise idea of who funds the campaigns and, consequently, which economic interests might influence the vote's outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions
What items are on the ballot on September 27?
Two federal initiatives: the neutrality initiative and the nutrition initiative. The former absorbs most of the resources with CHF 5.51 million declared, while the food initiative mobilises CHF 2.35 million.
Who funds the yes front for the neutrality initiative?
The favorable front declared 3.84 million francs. The main contributions come from Christoph Blocher with over 2 million, Pro Schweiz with 1.3 million and the SVP with 50,000 francs.
How does electoral transparency work in Switzerland?
Anyone who spends more than CHF 50,000 on a campaign must declare the funding to the Federal Financial Controller. Donors paying CHF 15,000 or more must be publicly identified.
How much did the record June 2025 campaigns spend?
The “No to a 10 million Switzerland” initiative registered CHF 16.27 million, the record since the introduction of the reporting obligation. The September sums are lower but still considerable.
Where can I check campaign funding?
The data is published by the Federal Finance Control (EFK) on its online portal, where you can consult the funding statements for each committee.
