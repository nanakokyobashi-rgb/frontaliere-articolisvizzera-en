How compulsory health insurance premiums work in Glarus, what deductibles are available and how to claim cantonal reduction benefits.

Compulsory health insurance (KVG) is a pillar of the Swiss healthcare system with completely different characteristics from AVS/AI (federal salary insurance) and LPP (company pension). In the canton of Glarus, as in the whole of Switzerland, residents must take out a KVG within 3 months of arriving in the territory. Unlike the AVS/AI contributions (5.3% paid by the employee, 10.6% total with the employer), the KVG is a private insurance with premiums, taken out directly with the insurer of your choice. It is not a tax or a salary contribution deducted from your paycheck.

KVG premiums are private per capita amounts paid directly to the insurer (monthly or quarterly). Each resident must take out their own policy. Premiums vary based on: canton of residence (Glaruna has its own premiums compared to other cantons), geographical region within the canton, age of the insured, gender, deductible chosen and coverage model selected.

Deductibles and KVG models in the canton of Glarus

In the canton of Glarus, as in the other Swiss cantons, the insured can choose between six available deductibles: CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 or 2500 per year. Your deductible is the amount you pay entirely out of pocket for medical expenses before insurance begins to cover it. A higher deductible (CHF 2000-2500) corresponds to a significantly lower monthly premium; a low deductible (CHF 300-500) leads to a higher monthly premium. Your choice depends on your anticipated medical needs.

In addition to the chosen deductible, the KVG provides a copayment (co-payment) of 10-20% of further medical expenses, up to an annual maximum (usually CHF 500-1000 for adults in the canton of Glarus, with variations per model). Once the copayment limit is reached, the insurance covers 100% of subsequent expenses in the calendar year.

The canton of Glarus also offers alternative coverage models to the ordinary model (with freedom of choice of doctor): managed care or HMO models with networks of chosen doctors, where the premiums are generally lower in exchange for less freedom in choosing the doctor.

For those with a low income, the canton of Glarus offers a reduction in premiums (cantonal subsidy). This is not a federal allowance, but an aid managed directly by the cantonal administration (canton Glarus health insurance office). The subsidy reduces the premium burden for families and individuals with income below a threshold established by the canton. Anyone wishing to access this reduction must submit an application to the competent cantonal office, providing documents verifying their income and family composition.

Operational details

Which franchise to choose in the canton of Glarus

The choice of deductible is a trade-off between monthly premium and medical risk. Those who expect few medical expenses during the year (young and healthy person, without chronic diseases) could opt for a high deductible (CHF 1500-2500) to significantly reduce the monthly premium. On the contrary, those with frequent medical needs (diabetes, hypertension, recurrent specialist visits, continuous therapies) may prefer a low deductible (CHF 300-500) and pay a higher monthly premium, knowing that they will recover the value through lower copayments and more extensive coverage.

Other profiles (elderly people, unemployed, single-parent families) benefit from the combination of medium deductible (CHF 500-1000) premium reduction subsidy: the cantonal subsidy reduces the premium until it is sustainable, while an average deductible maintains reasonable access to care.

Premiums also vary based on the coverage model chosen. Multiple insurers operate in the canton of Glarus, each with their own prices for different combinations of deductible and model. How copayment works

After paying the chosen annual deductible, the insured remains responsible for 10-20% of additional medical expenses, up to an annual copayment maximum. The copayment limit in the canton of Glarus is generally CHF 500-1000 for adults, but varies slightly by coverage model and insurer. Once the copayment limit is reached, the insurance covers 100% of subsequent expenses until December 31st.

Hypothetical scenario: an adult in the canton of Glarus chooses a CHF 500 deductible and a standard model. During the year he has total medical expenses of CHF 2000 (medical visits, tests, medicines). You will pay the deductible of CHF 500, then the 10% copayment on the remaining CHF 1500 (CHF 150), for a total copayment of CHF 650. The insurance covers the remaining CHF 1350. This is very different from a tax or wage contribution: you actually only pay if you have medical expenses, and the amount you pay depends on the deductible and copayment you choose.

When premium reduction makes the difference

For unemployed people, pensioners, self-employed workers with low incomes, single parents and large families with modest incomes, the premium reduction subsidy represents essential support. In the canton of Glarus, the subsidy can cover a significant fraction of the monthly premium (the exact amount varies according to cantonal criteria and is not yet specified here). This means that those who are entitled to the subsidy, instead of paying CHF 300-500 per month in full premium, could only pay a reduced fraction (depending on the subsidy awarded). The annual difference is considerable and makes health insurance sustainable for groups with limited resources. Those who benefit from the subsidy are not obliged to choose the maximum deductible (CHF 2500): they can opt for a medium deductible (CHF 500-1000) and an ordinary model, maintaining adequate access to care without sacrificing protection.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Requesting Premium Reduction

Step 1: Verify the Right to Subsidy The right to premium reduction depends on income and family composition criteria defined by the Glarus cantonal administration. An initial estimate is obtained by calculating gross income, possible tax deductions (health insurance is deductible up to limited amounts from federal thresholds), the number of dependents, and marital status. If the annual gross income is below a certain threshold (to be verified directly with the cantonal office, not specified here), there is a likelihood of entitlement to the subsidy.

Step 2: Collect Documentation Generally required: previous year's tax declaration (or income certificate from employer), certificate of domicile issued by the municipality of residence in the canton of Glarus, documentation of family composition (marriage certificate, birth certificates for children, cohabitation declaration), employment contract or unemployment certificate if relevant for income, and AVS/LPP pension amount notification if retired.

Step 3: Submit Application to Cantonal Office The application is submitted to the health insurance office (or social security office) of the canton of Glarus. It is possible to submit the application in person, by registered mail, or (in some cantons, to be verified) electronically through the cantonal portal. Contact the official website of the canton of Glarus (www.gl.ch) to identify the exact office and available form.

Step 4: Verification and Notification of Outcome The cantonal office verifies the documents within 2-4 weeks and notifies the outcome in writing. If approved, the subsidy generally takes effect from the following month, or (in some cantons) retroactively from the beginning of the civil year if the application is submitted before a certain deadline (e.g., June 30). The amount of the reduction is notified in writing.

Step 5: Notify the Health Insurer LAMal The subsidy is not paid directly to the insured person but is notified to the health insurer. Once approved, the insured person must notify their LAMal insurer that they are entitled to the subsidy, attaching a copy of the written notification from the cantonal office. From then on, the insurer deducts the amount of the subsidy from the monthly premium: the insured person pays only the difference (full premium minus subsidy).

Deadlines and Validity of Subsidies

The application for premium reduction can be submitted at any time during the year. However, check the canton of Glarus's website to see if there are deadlines for retroactivity: some cantons grant a reduction from the beginning of the civil year (January 1) if the application is submitted by April or June; if submitted later, the subsidy takes effect from the following month. The validity of the subsidy is one civil year (January 1 - December 31). It must be renewed every year through a new application within the deadline set by the canton.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is LAMal and who should sign it in the canton of Glarus?
LAMal (compulsory health insurance) is a private premium insurance, compulsory for all residents in Switzerland, including those in the canton of Glarus. It must be stipulated within 3 months of arrival in the cantonal territory. Unlike the AVS/AI (federal salary contributions, 5.3% employee and 10.6% total with employer), the LAMal is not a tax or a salary contribution, but a private insurance policy per capita. Each insured person personally chooses the insurer, the deductible and the coverage model.
Which franchises are available in the canton of Glarus and which one to choose?
In the canton of Glarus, as in the whole of Switzerland, adults can choose from 6 deductibles: CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 or 2500 per year. Higher deductibles result in lower monthly premiums, but require more direct payment for medical expenses. The deductible is personal: each family member chooses their own according to their expected medical needs.
How does premium reduction work in the canton of Glarus?
The premium reduction (cantonal subsidy) is an aid for those with low income. In the canton of Glarus, the cantonal administration defines criteria for income, family composition and the amount of the subsidy. To apply for the subsidy, you must apply to the health insurance office of the canton of Glarus, attaching a tax return and documentation of the family unit. If approved, the subsidy reduces the monthly premium paid to the insurer.
What is the difference between deductible and LAMal copayment?
The deductible is the annual fixed amount (CHF 300-2500) that you pay in full before the insurance starts to cover. Copayment is the percentage (10-20%) of medical expenses that remains at your expense even after the deductible, up to an annual ceiling (usually CHF 500-1000). Example: With CHF 500 deductible, you pay the first CHF 500 of expenses. Then you pay 10-20% of the subsequent expenses, up to the annual copayment ceiling. Beyond that point, the insurance covers 100%.
