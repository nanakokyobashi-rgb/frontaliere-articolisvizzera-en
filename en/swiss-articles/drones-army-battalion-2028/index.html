The presentation to Thun

Defence Minister Martin Pfister, army chief Benedikt Roos and Armasuisse director Urs Loher unveiled a new centre of expertise dedicated to drones, devices that are gaining increasing importance as tools of hybrid warfare, on the Thun parade ground on Thursday. The presentation took place at a time when even in Switzerland there is a phenomenon that makes us think: an increase in drone overflights over military infrastructure and sensitive installations.

Operational details

What changes for Switzerland

Before and after the turning point

Until now, the use of drones in the army has been entrusted to skills distributed across various units, without a dedicated centralized structure. With the new competence center and the future battalion, the training, equipment, and operational deployment of devices are brought together in a single national supply chain. For those living or working in Switzerland, this means a structural strengthening of a capability that directly concerns the defense of the country's airspace and critical infrastructure.

Implications for national security

The source emphasizes that drones are becoming increasingly important as instruments of hybrid warfare and that Switzerland is recording a rise in overflights above military infrastructure and sensitive installations. The creation of a specialized battalion responds precisely to this evolution: having trained personnel and systems tested at troop level allows for a more timely response to potential incursions or unauthorized reconnaissance activities. This is a significant step for the operational sovereignty of the Swiss army, which will be able to rely on a dedicated structure rather than on fragmented skills.

Economic and industrial spillovers

The competence center works alongside the activities of armasuisse, the federal office responsible for defense procurement and technological development. The development, testing, and integration of new technologies require investments in research, infrastructure, and qualified personnel, with effects that extend beyond military structures, involving suppliers and technology partners active in the Swiss market.

Key timelines

  • 2028: Operational launch of the drone battalion for reconnaissance and defence
  • To be defined: Schedule of selections for the dedicated recruit school, not yet specified in the source

Who manages the project

The project involves three institutional levels: the Federal Department of Defence represented by minister Martin Pfister, the army command led by chief Benedikt Roos, and armasuisse, the federal defence procurement office headed by Urs Loher. Governance is therefore shared between political direction, operational command and technical-procurement responsibility.

How the competence centre will work

The centre will be tasked with developing, testing and integrating new technologies at troop level. In practice, this means that every new system or platform will go through a path that goes from research to operational use in the field, with the direct involvement of the units that will use the drones.

Frequently asked questions about the new Swiss drone battalion

For those who want to closely follow the evolution of the project, it is worth monitoring the communications of the Federal Department of Defence and armasuisse, which will publish updates on location, staffing and recruitment procedures.

Frequently Asked Questions
When will the new drone battalion of the Swiss army be operational?
According to what was presented on the Thun parade ground by Defense Minister Martin Pfister, Army Chief Benedikt Roos, and Armasuisse Director Urs Loher, the drone battalion intended for reconnaissance and defense will be operational starting in 2028. The source does not yet specify organic, final location in addition to Thun nor calendar of selections.
What tasks will the drone competence center have?
The new competence center presented at Thun will have to develop, experiment and integrate new technologies at the troop level, bringing innovations directly to the army's operational units. It is therefore not just a matter of research, but of a complete supply chain up to the use in the territory.
Is there a specific recruit school for drones?
Yes, the source indicates that in parallel to the battalion, education will be enhanced with a specific recruit school dedicated to systematically developing the necessary skills and ensuring the contribution of personnel to the battalion. Enrolment methods and criteria are not yet specified.
Why is Switzerland now investing in a drone battalion?
The presentation took place at a time when, as the source points out, there is also an increase in drone overflights over military infrastructure and sensitive installations in Switzerland. Drones are referred to as increasingly relevant hybrid warfare instruments, and the new facility aims to react with trained personnel and tested systems.
Who manages the drone competence center project?
The project involves three levels: the Federal Department of Defense, represented by Minister Martin Pfister, the Army Command led by Chief Benedikt Roos, and armasuisse, the Federal Office for Defense Procurement headed by Urs Loher, responsible for technological development.
