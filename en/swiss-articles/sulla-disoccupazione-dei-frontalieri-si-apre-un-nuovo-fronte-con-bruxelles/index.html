Context

In brief, the European Union and Switzerland have reached a new social security agreement through Regulation 883, which updates the earlier rules from 2004 and 2009. The reform introduces rules on unemployment benefits for cross-border workers, now in force in more than 10 European countries, including Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Iceland. Switzerland is not a member of the European Union or the European Economic Area, but it signed the latest Frontier Agreement on 23 December 2020, which entered into force on 1 January 2024.

This could lead to more applications for unemployment benefits from cross-border workers, especially if their work activity cannot be recovered in Switzerland. For example, the catering sector could be affected by a lockdown caused by a health emergency.

Switzerland has planned a practical recommendation to help cross-border workers manage applications for unemployment benefits. The document includes an operational checklist for workers seeking to resume their work activity after a period of unemployment. For example, cross-border workers must submit a detailed work plan including information about the activity they intend to carry out and the skills required. They must also show that they have made every possible effort to support their activity, for example by buying the necessary tools or working with a team.

Regulations involving amounts have been updated for the new European context, including the adjustment of payments in cases of mandatory redundancy in Switzerland. The new Regulation 883 provides that payments are now calculated from the average income of cross-border workers during the six months before unemployment began, rather than from the average income during the previous eighteen months.

Key points

Unemployment among cross-border workers: a new front opens with Brussels

Switzerland, with more than 1 million cross-border workers, is strongly affected by the new EU/EEA Regulation 883. The regulation already applies beyond the borders of the European Union and will enter into force on 1 January 2024. The New Frontier Agreement, signed on 23 December 2020, introduced reforms to improve working conditions and social protection for cross-border workers, including a new rule on unemployment benefits.

This reform updates the earlier regulations from 2004 and 2009 and will introduce a more flexible and transparent system for unemployment benefits, providing a minimum payment of 60% of the basic income for individuals and 80% for families. The new agreement also includes measures to facilitate access to social protection and the labour market, such as a single shared register of cross-border workers between Switzerland and the EU/EEA.

Contributions on the employment front

One of the main innovations of the New Frontier Agreement concerns the contribution system for cross-border workers. At present, cross-border workers contribute to the Swiss contribution system and, in the event of unemployment, may receive unemployment benefits like other workers in Switzerland. The new agreement provides that cross-border workers who work regularly in Switzerland for 12 months or more and have paid contributions for at least 12 months may receive unemployment benefits below 100% of their basic income, according to the criteria activated by the new Regulation 883.

The impact on Swiss cities and cantons

The introduction of the New Frontier Agreement will have a significant impact on Swiss cities and cantons because the contribution system and unemployment benefits are regulated at cantonal level. For example, the Canton of Ticino, which has a significant cross-border workforce, could experience increased demand for social and support services for cross-border workers who become unemployed. However, the new agreement also provides for stronger cooperation between cantonal authorities and the EU/EEA to address common challenges and ensure uniform and effective access to social services and the labour market.

Future forecasts and challenges

The outlook is positive but not without challenges. Switzerland will have to ensure greater uniformity and transparency in the contribution system and unemployment benefits for cross-border workers and local workers. Swiss authorities will also have to make sure that cross-border workers have easy and affordable access to social services and the labour market, even when they do not reside permanently in Switzerland.

In summary, the New Frontier Agreement is a significant step towards improving working conditions and social protection for cross-border workers, promoting a more flexible and transparent approach to contributions and unemployment benefits. Switzerland will nevertheless have to address the challenges created by the agreement and work closely with the EU/EEA to ensure fairness and social justice at work for all cross-border workers.
