Foreign driver's license in Vaud: conversion and exams (cross-border guide)

Cantonal procedure for converting a foreign driver's license into Vaud: theoretical and practical exams, compulsory courses, required documents and contacts with the Traffic Office.

Key facts

  • What: Conversion of foreign driving license in the Canton of Vaud
  • Where: Road Traffic Office of the Canton of Vaud (Lausanne and decentralized offices)

  • Competence: Federal law (Road Traffic Act) plus cantonal regulations
  • Procedure: Theoretical-practical exams and update courses if required

Anyone holding a driving license issued by a foreign state and residing or working in the Canton of Vaud is required to convert it according to cantonal regulations within a certain period from taking up residence in Switzerland. The conversion of the license is a matter of cantonal competence, regulated by the Canton of Vaud according to the Federal Road Traffic Act (RTA).

Each Swiss canton manages the issuance and recognition of foreign driving licenses autonomously. In Vaud, this function is handled by the Road Traffic Office, which assesses the validity of the foreign license and decides whether the holder needs to take competency verification exams.

The principle of conversion

The conversion is not automatic. The Canton of Vaud examines the foreign license to verify if it was issued by a recognized authority and if it meets international road safety standards. If the license comes from a European Union or EFTA (European Free Trade Association) state, the process is often simplified. For licenses from non-EU countries, the cantonal office may require additional exams to verify the driver's competence.

Operational details

Converting your driving license in Vaud is not a mere administrative formality. The process requires the candidate to demonstrate a thorough knowledge of Swiss road traffic regulations, even if they already have driving experience in their home country. The theoretical exam assesses knowledge of Swiss traffic rules, road signs, safe driving behaviors, and penalties outlined in the LCS. In Vaud, this exam is administered in French (or other languages, as per cantonal regulations), and the candidate must show sufficient competence in recognizing dangerous situations and respecting right of way. The exam consists of multiple-choice questions and practical scenarios, often using a projector to simulate real traffic situations. The candidate must achieve a minimum percentage of correct answers to pass the test. If the theoretical exam is not passed, it can be retaken on subsequent dates, but the cost of retaking remains the candidate's responsibility. The practical exam evaluates actual driving skills. A cantonal examiner accompanies the candidate on public roads in Vaud, assessing vehicle control, hazard anticipation, knowledge of road signs, and adherence to speed limits. The examiner also observes the driver's behavior in heavy traffic, parking, and adverse conditions. To pass the practical exam, the candidate must demonstrate safe driving and understanding of Swiss regulations. If the exam is not passed, it can be retaken, but at least 5 calendar days must elapse between attempts. Those moving to Vaud should also inform themselves about the general cost of living in the canton and housing implications, as a new residence involves multiple administrative tasks beyond license conversion. In some cases, the canton of Vaud requires candidates to take refresher courses on specific aspects of Swiss driving, such as mountain driving, winter driving, or eco-friendly driving. These courses are mandatory if the examiner identifies significant gaps in areas relevant to road safety. Their duration and content vary based on individual needs identified during the theoretical and practical exams.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I need to convert my licence if I have an EU licence?
Yes, European Union licenses must also be converted to be legally recognized in the Canton of Vaud after the first 12 months of residence. The Vaud assesses the EU licence and, if it complies with Swiss standards, can recognise it with a simplified procedure. However, the practical examination may still be required to verify the knowledge of the specific Swiss rules and the particularities of the mountain guide.
How much does it cost to convert my driver's license to Vaud?
The exact cost of conversion is not specified in this guide and varies according to cantonal legislation. It is advisable to contact the Vaud Canton Traffic Office directly to obtain the precise amount of conversion fees, exams and any mandatory courses, as the amounts may vary and be subject to change.
What is the deadline to start converting your license?
Most Swiss cantons, including Vaud, allow you to drive with a foreign license for 12 months after taking up residence. It is essential to start the conversion procedure before the expiry of this deadline, otherwise the document loses validity and driving without a Swiss licence carries administrative and criminal penalties.
Can I drive in Vaud with my foreign driver's license during the conversion process?
Yes, during the grace period (usually 12 months from residency), you can legally drive with your foreign driver's license. However, it is advisable to start the conversion procedure as soon as possible, as once the grace period has expired, driving without a Swiss licence is illegal.
If the theoretical or practical exam is not passed, can I repeat it?
Yes, both the theoretical and practical exams can be repeated at later dates. At least 5 calendar days must pass between the failed practical exam and the next attempt. Each repetition entails an additional fee to be borne by the candidate.
