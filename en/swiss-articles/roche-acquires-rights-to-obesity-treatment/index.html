Roche Acquires Rights to Experimental Obesity Treatment (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 24 August 2026

The deal with South Korean company Hanmi could be worth up to $2.3 billion.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Roche acquires the rights to an experimental anti-obesity treatment
  • Deal with South Korea's Hanmi may be worth up to $2.3 billion
  • The treatment, called HM17321, is currently the subject of a phase I clinical trial

Key facts

  • What: experimental anti-obesity treatment
  • When: not yet specified
  • Where: not yet specified
  • Who: Roche and Hanmi Pharmaceuticals
  • Amount: up to 2.3 billion dollars

The Californian subsidiary Genentech of the Rhenish pharmaceutical giant Roche has secured worldwide rights to develop, manufacture and market an experimental treatment against obesity from the South Korean company Hanmi Pharmaceuticals. The agreement excludes South Korea, Hanmi said today. The South Korean company will be able to collect a total of 2.3 billion dollars, equal to about 1.8 billion francs. Much of the amount is contingent on achieving clinical, regulatory, and commercial goals, while the initial payment amounts to $190 million.

The treatment, named HM17321, is currently undergoing a phase I clinical study, which will be carried out by Hanmi. If successful, Genentech will take over from Phase II and subsequent stages of development.

Operational details

Roche's acquisition of the rights could have significant implications for obesity treatment developers. If the investigational treatment HM17321 is approved, it could become an important option for individuals seeking an alternative treatment for obesity. However, it is important to note that the experimental treatment has not yet been approved by the Swiss health authorities.

According to the available information, the experimental treatment HM17321 was developed by a team of researchers from the University of Zurich, in collaboration with Roche. The treatment uses a combination of innovative drugs and technologies to reduce appetite and increase sensitivity to exercise. Clinical studies conducted so far have shown promising results, with an average reduction of 15% in body weight in treated individuals.

The approval of the experimental treatment could have a significant impact on the Swiss population, especially in regions such as Ticino and Graubünden, where obesity is a major public health problem. According to data from the Federal Office of Public Health (BAG), in 2020, 25% of the adult Swiss population had a body mass index (BMI) greater than 30, which is considered obese.
