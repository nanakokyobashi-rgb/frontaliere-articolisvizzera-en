Heating oil price skyrocketing, fault of USA and Iran (cross-border guide)

Homeowners in Switzerland must prepare for significant prices for heating oil due to the Iran-US conflict.

Key facts

  • What: Increase in heating oil prices
  • When: September 2024
  • Where: Switzerland
  • Who: Homeowners
  • Amount: 1400 dollars per ton

Homeowners in Switzerland must prepare for significant increases in heating oil prices. Prices have skyrocketed due to the new escalation in the Middle East. According to the comparison platform Heizoel24.ch, the prices of diesel contracts, which determine heating costs, have reached the highest level in the last five months. The contract for middle distillates, which determines the price of heating oil, is currently trading at around 1400 dollars per ton (1120 francs), more than double the level at the end of February, when it was around 700 dollars.

Causes of the surge

The cause of the surge can be found in the new flare-ups of the Iranian conflict, which keep the risk for oil transport high. But the increase in diesel is even more pronounced than that of crude oil. According to experts, the reason is that the continuous attacks on Russian refineries are reducing supply, while US stocks have fallen to the lowest level in the last thirty years.

Concrete examples

In Zurich, a homeowner using heating oil for a 150 square meter house could see an annual expense increase of about 500 francs, bringing the total to 2500 francs. In Geneva, where winter temperatures are generally milder, the increase could be less drastic but still significant, with an increase of about 300 francs.

Regulations and dates

Switzerland introduced new regulations in 2023 requiring homeowners to declare the type of heating used and annual expenses. This regulation aims to promote energy efficiency and reduce the use of fossil fuels. However, with the current price surge, many homeowners may find it difficult to comply with these regulations without a significant increase in expenses.

Operational checklist

Energy efficiency: Consider installing more efficient heating systems or thermal insulation of the home. 4. Financial planning: Prepare a budget to cope with the increase in heating expenses.

Comparisons between practical scenarios

  • Current scenarios: With current prices, a homeowner in Bern could spend about 2000 francs a year on heating, compared to 1500 francs last year.
  • Future scenarios: If prices continue to rise, expenses could increase further, making regulatory intervention necessary to stabilize costs.

Conclusion

The surge in heating oil prices in Switzerland is a real problem that requires the attention of homeowners and authorities. With adequate planning and the adoption of energy efficiency measures, it is possible to mitigate the impact of these increases on household budgets.

Andreas Flütsch, CEO of Migrol, the largest Swiss supplier of heating oil, predicts a "very high" demand for the coming months. Sales have so far been more than 20% lower than the previous year, because many homeowners probably hoped for a price drop, he explains in statements to the Awp agency.

Practical implications

As a result, many tanks in homes may be empty or poorly supplied. Flütsch warns that if winter arrives earlier than expected or lasts longer than usual, the increasing demand could further drive up prices. The professional's advice to customers is therefore to check the tank level promptly and not to postpone orders. "Those who want to reduce the price risk can purchase their needs in installments, rather than buying the entire annual quantity at once," he recommends.

As is known, the effects of the Middle Eastern conflict are also felt on fuel prices. According to the latest data from the Touring Club Svizzero (TCS) – released yesterday – the average cost of a liter of unleaded gasoline 95 is around 2.10 francs, 98 reaches 2.12 francs and diesel rises to 2.26 francs. The values are just one cent below the peaks reached last August 20. Since the start of the war in Iran, which broke out at the end of February, fuels have risen between 20% and 26%.

Concrete examples

In the Canton of Ticino, for example, domestic heating costs have increased by 25% compared to last year. In Zurich, heating oil prices have risen by 22%, while in Geneva the increase has been 20%. These data highlight how the situation is critical in different regions of the country.

Regulations and rules

The Swiss Confederation has introduced measures to mitigate the impact of high fuel prices. From October 1, 2023, a temporary reduction in fuel taxes has been implemented, which should reduce the average cost of a liter of heating oil by about 0.05 francs. However, this measure may not be sufficient to compensate for the price increase caused by the Middle Eastern conflict.

Scenario 2: Installment purchase The same homeowner could opt for monthly purchases of 83 liters, spending about 188 francs per month. This approach could help mitigate the impact of any price increases, as the risk is spread over multiple periods.

The current situation of the fuel market in Switzerland is complex and requires careful management by consumers. With the increase in demand and the shortage of supply, it is essential to adopt targeted purchasing strategies to avoid unwanted surprises. The measures introduced by the Confederation can offer some relief, but they are not sufficient to completely solve the problem. Consumers must be proactive and informed to best face this challenge.
