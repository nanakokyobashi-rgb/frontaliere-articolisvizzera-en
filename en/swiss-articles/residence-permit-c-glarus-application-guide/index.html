Glarus C Permit: Requirements and How to Apply (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 September 2026

Complete guide to residence permit C in the canton of Glarus: years of residence required, integration, application procedures and transition to naturalization.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Permit C requires 10 years residence (5 years for EU/EFTA)
  • It does not expire, unlike permit B
  • Opens access to naturalization after an additional 10 years
  • Obliges federal, cantonal and municipal taxation

Key facts

  • What: Residence permit C (long-term permanent residence)
  • Duration required: 10 years for non-EU; 5 years for EU/EFTA
  • Validity: No expiry (non-renewable)
  • Canton: Glarus (north-eastern Switzerland)
  • Requirements: Continuous residence, integration, economic stability
  • Next step: Access to naturalization after 10 years of possession

Residence permit C is the permanent residence status in Switzerland. Those who intend to move permanently to the canton of Glarus must meet the requirements of the federal law SEM (Secretariat of State for Migration) and the specific cantonal regulations.

The application for a C permit in the canton of Glarus normally requires 10 years of uninterrupted residence on Swiss territory for citizens not belonging to the European Union or the European Free Trade Association (EFTA). For citizens of EU and EFTA countries, the period is reduced to 5 years. Unlike the B permit, the C permit has no expiration date and does not require periodic renewals: once obtained, it remains valid indefinitely as long as you maintain your residence in the canton.

Operational details

Practical implications: naturalization, tax and rights

The C permit is the bridge to ordinary naturalization in Switzerland. Once obtained, you can start counting the years towards the application for Swiss citizenship. Federal law prescribes that ordinary naturalization requires the C permit already in possession, plus a further 10 years of uninterrupted residence (during which periods between the 8th and 18th year of residence count as double in the calculation), in addition to the specific requirements of the canton of Glarus and your municipality of residence, such as adequate language skills, a clean criminal record and social integration.

From a tax perspective, the C permit has direct and important consequences. As soon as you obtain the status, you become subject to full taxation in Switzerland at three levels: federal (federal direct tax of the Federal Tax Administration, FTA/ESTV), cantonal (each canton has its own brackets and rates) and municipal (each municipality applies a multiplier on the cantonal tax base). The canton of Glarus, like all other cantons, publishes its tax rates annually; your municipality of residence within the canton adds a specific local multiplier. This means that your annual tax return must include all earnings, investments and properties within Swiss territory.

From a work perspective, the C permit allows you to exercise any dependent or self-employed profession in the canton of Glarus and the rest of Switzerland without restrictions. Unlike the B permit (residence permit), which in some cantons has sectoral limitations, the C permit offers total freedom of professional choice. You can access public jobs, practice free professions and start economic activities independently.

Key points

How to apply in the canton of Glarus: step-by-step procedure

The application for a C permit in the canton of Glarus must be submitted to the Immigration Office (cantonal designation: Amt für Migration or similar; the exact name can be found on the official website of the canton). The procedure follows these fundamental steps:

Step 1: Check personal prerequisites

Before submitting your application, ensure that you have completed the required residence period (10 years for non-EU/EFTA; 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens). Also, verify that your economic situation is stable: the canton of Glarus requires that you have sufficient income to support yourself without relying on social benefits. Prepare a clean criminal record (requestable from your municipality of residence).

Step 2: Gather the required documentation

The canton of Glarus generally requires: valid identity document (passport, national ID card); current employment contract (if employed) or self-employment declaration, residence certificate issued by your municipality, criminal record, civil status certificate (if married, with marriage certificate), integration declaration (the canton evaluates knowledge of the German language and integration into the local community). For citizens with family members, additional documents may be required.

Step 3: Complete the form and submit the application

The Immigration Office provides the official application form (available on the canton of Glarus website or directly at the office). Fill out the form in all required sections and sign it. Submit the application at the office, along with the complete documentation. Some cantons allow submission by certified mail; check the specific procedures with the cantonal office.

Frequently Asked Questions
How many years of residence are required to apply for the C permit in the canton of Glarus?
Non-European Union and EFTA (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway) citizens are normally required to have 10 years of continuous residence in Switzerland. Citizens of EU and EFTA States can apply for the C permit after 5 years of continuous residence. The canton of Glarus checks each application individually according to the criteria of integration, language skills (German) and economic stability.
What is the main difference between permit B and permit C?
Permit B (residence permit) is renewable every 5 years and remains subject to a stable economic situation and acceptability criteria. The C permit (residence permit) does not expire and does not require periodic renewals: once obtained, it remains valid indefinitely. The C permit represents the long-term stable and definitive status in Switzerland and is the only federal requirement for access to ordinary naturalization.
Can I work with a C permit in the canton of Glarus?
Yes, the C permit allows you to work without any limitations. It is possible to practise any profession, whether employed or self-employed, in the canton of Glarus and in the rest of Switzerland. There are no industry, professional or geographic restrictions. The C permit offers maximum freedom of employment, unlike the B permit which may have limitations in certain cantons.
When can I apply for naturalization after the C permit?
After obtaining the C permit, the federal law requires an additional 10 years of uninterrupted residence in the Swiss territory (counted from the day you received the C permit). During these 10 years, you must maintain stable residence. In addition, you must meet the specific requirements of the canton of Glarus and your municipality of residence, such as adequate language knowledge (generally level B1-B2 of German) and social integration. Does permit C entail special tax and social security obligations?
With the C permit you are subject to full taxation in Switzerland: you must declare your income to the three authorities (federal, cantonal and municipal). The canton of Glarus applies a cantonal tax with its own rates, and individual municipalities add a local multiplier. You are also obliged to contribute to the AVS/AI (5.3% as an employee), the LPP (pension fund, with contributions varying by age group) and other mandatory pension systems in Switzerland.
