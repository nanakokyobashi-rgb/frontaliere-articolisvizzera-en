Permit L Freiburg: validity and extension (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 September 2026

The short-term L permit allows foreigners to work in Switzerland for up to 1 year. Learn about extension rules, employer constraints, and how to switch to B and C permits.

Context

In brief

  • The L permit allows you to work in Switzerland for a maximum of 1 year
  • Extension is possible but depends on federal criteria and job availability
  • The permit is specifically tied to the employer and professional sector

Key facts

  • What: Short-term permit for foreign workers
  • Maximum duration: 1 year
  • Scope: Canton of Fribourg (federal SEM system)
  • Managed by: State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) at the federal level
  • Main restriction: Tied to the specific employer indicated in the permit

The short-term L permit is the regulatory tool through which Switzerland authorizes foreign workers to engage in dependent professional activities within the national territory. Managed by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) at the federal level, the L permit applies identically across all Swiss cantons, including the Canton of Fribourg, without additional authorizations from the cantonal administration beyond those stipulated by federal regulations.

Maximum duration of the L permit

According to federal regulations, the L permit has a maximum validity of 1 year. This means that the foreign worker can reside and work in Switzerland for a period not exceeding 12 months from the date of issuance. Unlike residence (B) or settlement (C) permits, the L permit does not offer long-term stay and requires an explicit extension if the employment relationship continues beyond the initial term.

Operational details

Conditions of extension and constraints with the employer

The extension of the L permit is not automatic. When the permit is about to expire within 12 months of initial validity, the employer must submit a new application for renewal to the competent cantonal office. The extension is granted on the condition that the economic and working conditions justifying the initial authorisation still exist. In particular, the cantonal office verifies that the employment relationship continues to be active and that there are no significant changes in the contractual conditions.

If the employer does not submit the request for an extension before the expiry date, the permit automatically ceases and the worker cannot continue to work as a wage earner in Switzerland. There are no transition periods or tacit extensions: the deadline is peremptory and binding.

An important aspect concerns the constraint on the specific employer. While the permit is valid, the worker cannot change employer autonomously. If the worker wishes to move to a different job, the new employer must submit a new application for an L permit to the cantonal office. This means that the change of employment is not immediate: the permit must be formally modified by the administration. In the meantime, the worker cannot start the new work activity.

Key points

Transition to residence permit (B) or residence permit (C)

During the period of validity of the L permit, the foreign worker may consider switching to a residence permit (B) or domicile permit (C), which offer greater stability and broader rights. The residence permit (B) is renewable and is generally intended for those who have completed several years of continuous work in Switzerland. The residence permit (C) is the most stable and is usually accessible after an extended period of residence (typically 10 years, but with reductions for EU/EFTA citizens).

The transition from permit L to permit B is not automatic: the worker must submit a formal application to the cantonal office in Freiburg, demonstrating that the legal conditions for granting permit B exist. These assumptions vary under federal law and may include continuity of employment, economic stability, and failure to exploit public social services. Once the B permit is obtained, the worker acquires a series of additional rights, including the freedom of choice of the employer (albeit with constraints) and greater protection in the event of unemployment.

Frequently Asked Questions
How long does a short-term L permit last at most?
The L permit is valid for a maximum of 1 year from the date of issue. Upon expiry, an explicit request for extension submitted by the employer to the competent cantonal office is required. Without renewal, the permit automatically ceases.
Can I extend the L leave if the employment relationship continues?
Yes, but not automatically. The employer must submit a new application for an extension before the permit expires. The extension is granted if the initial economic and working conditions still exist, in the opinion of the competent cantonal office.
What happens if the L permit expires while I am still working?
If the permit expires and the employer has not submitted an application for an extension, the permit ceases and the worker loses the right to stay and work in Switzerland. There are no transition periods: the deadline is binding.
Can I change employer with a valid L permit?
No, not independently. The L permit is linked to the specific employer indicated in the permit. To change employment, the new employer must submit a new application for an L permit to the cantonal office. In the meantime, the worker cannot start the new relationship.
How can I switch from L permit to B or C permit?
The transition to permit B (residence) or C (domicile) requires a formal application at the cantonal office in Freiburg. The prerequisites include the continuity of the employment relationship, economic stability and the period of residence in Switzerland according to federal legislation.
