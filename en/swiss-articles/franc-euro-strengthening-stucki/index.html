Thomas Stucki, head of investments at St. Gallen Kantonalbank, expects the franc to strengthen against the euro by the end of the year. The warning, reported by ATS, comes after a new phase of tension on the European bond market, which has rekindled doubts about debts, budget discipline and structural divergences in the Eurozone.

The euro, explains the expert in the interview published by the Cash portal, had been able to present itself as an alternative to the dollar for large investors intending to reduce exposure to the American currency. Now the same operators are being reminded that the fundamental problems of the Eurozone have not been solved.

The tension was reflected in the gearbox. In a short time, the Swiss currency appreciated and the euro rate dropped from 95 to 93 cents, before stopping temporarily. For Stucki, the first reaction has passed; the breadth of the movement also depends on the surprise of many bulls on the euro. A return to summer levels of strength appears unlikely, as the French issue will remain under observation.

France and Swiss rates

French finance is the most delicate point of the analysis. The budget deficit is around 5% of gross domestic product, while the nominal growth of the economy reaches just 1.9%. The debt/GDP ratio is therefore destined to rise and, according to Stucki, there is no political will to adopt austerity measures. Ahead of next year's presidential elections, populist parties at opposite ends are instead promising to hand out more money. The youth protests and the government's impotence, he adds, do not communicate the image of a stable and reliable debtor.

By the end of the year, the forecast is for the euro to be weaker than current levels but still above 0.90 francs.

«Our forecast remains unchanged: we had already considered the euro's rise to almost 95 cents unsustainable».

The strengthening of the franc reduces the price of imported goods and helps to contain inflation, but the speed of the movement still poses a danger to the SNB. With inflation at 1% in September, prices remain in line with the objectives of the institute directed by Martin Schlegel, so there is currently no reason to change rates. According to Stucki, the SNB should sooner or later leave rates at zero: the next step should be a hike, while the first tightening is only expected next year and will depend on the trend in rates abroad.

Operational details

Practical impact for Switzerland

The move from 95 to 93 cents shows how quickly the euro-franc exchange rate can change when investors seek a safe haven. For people living in Switzerland, the consequence identified by the source is seen in imported goods: a stronger franc lowers their cost and can help curb inflation. It provides support for the cost of living, not a promise that every price will fall immediately or by the same amount.

The other side concerns the SNB. Inflation at 1% in September remains compatible with its objectives, but a rapid appreciation of the franc is described as a greater and more likely risk than a weakening. The bank must therefore weigh two signals together: lower import costs help keep prices down, while sudden demand for the franc shows that currency conditions can change quickly. For a Swiss budget, the benefit of lower import costs coexists with exchange-rate uncertainty.
