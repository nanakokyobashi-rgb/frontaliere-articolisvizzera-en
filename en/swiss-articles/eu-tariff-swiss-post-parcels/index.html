EU tariff of 3 euros: Swiss Post parcels drop

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 October 2026

EU customs duty of 3 euros per item: Swiss Post parcels to the Union plunge to double digits. China’s share rises from 6% to 11% in ten years.

Context

In Brief

  • EU tariff: €3 per item from third countries
  • Parcels to the EU drop by double digits
  • Chinese parcel share from 6% to 11% in ten years

Key Facts

  • Tariff: €3 per item
  • Scope: Parcels from third countries
  • Decline: in the order of a double-digit percentage
  • CEO: Pascal Grieder
  • Additional volume: 100 million parcels per year compared to ten years ago

The flat-rate tariff of €3 per item introduced by the European Union on parcels from third countries has had repercussions for Swiss Post. The number of parcels shipped to the EU has dropped "in the order of a double-digit percentage," Keystone-SDA reports, based on an interview with CEO Pascal Grieder in Schweiz am Wochenende.

The 49-year-old manager, however, does not consider the reduction to be definitive. According to Grieder, the situation should normalize at least partially. His assessment is based on similar experiences with the United States, where the effects were stronger initially. The reference therefore separates the initial impact from the possible subsequent evolution of shipments to the European Union.

Doubts about the effectiveness of the tariff

Grieder expressed skepticism about the €3 tariff imposed by Brussels. In his opinion, the measure might not achieve its goal of protecting the market from Chinese imports. The reason given in the interview concerns the logistics of large companies like Temu, a Chinese online discounter.

Temu, the Swiss Post CEO notes, is building large warehouses in Europe and can ship parcels from those facilities. In this way, according to his interpretation, the company is circumventing the tariff. This reasoning shifts the focus from the individual package to the route chosen to deliver it to the customer: the tariff affects shipments from a third country, while shipments from a European warehouse follow a different configuration.

The size of the market is also reflected in Swiss Post's overall volumes. Today, the company transports 100 million more parcels annually than it did ten years ago. Grieder divides this increase into three groups: 60 million parcels from large European suppliers, 20 million from small shops, and another 20 million from Chinese parcels.

The share of Chinese parcels in total volume has risen from 6% to 11% in ten years. These are the figures used in the interview to describe the growth of that segment, alongside the double-digit decline in shipments to the EU. The picture reported by ATS therefore combines long-term growth in overall volumes with a decline in shipments to the EU following the introduction of the tariff.

Operational details

For those living in Switzerland, the first check concerns the scope of the news. The measure cited is a flat-rate fee on parcels coming from third countries, while the traffic figure reported by Grieder concerns parcels shipped to the EU. The double-digit decline must therefore be associated with that route; the Post's overall volume is described separately, with an increase of 100 million parcels per year compared with ten years ago.

How to read the numbers without confusing them

The first comparison is between different units. The 3 euros are stated per item; the 100 million are additional parcels transported each year compared with ten years ago. The first figure describes the fee, the second the growth in total volumes. Putting them on the same level would lead to an imprecise reading. The role of European warehouses

The Temu case adds a second element of analysis. If the goods leave from a warehouse in Europe, the tariff on parcels coming from third countries can be circumvented, according to Grieder. For the reader, the point to check is therefore the route indicated: the source links the Chinese platform with warehouses in Europe and the dispatch of parcels from there.

The most cautious reading is limited to the available data: the initial effect on the route to the EU was strong, but the CEO expects a possible partial recovery and points out that European warehouses can change how the tariff affects flows.

Key points

An operational reading can start from five checks. No assumptions on total traffic are needed: just follow the categories used in the interview.

Five steps to find your way

1. Identify the destination. The news concerns parcels sent to the European Union. This is the first filter to understand whether the case falls within the double-digit drop data. 2. Verify the origin of the parcel. The flat-rate fee mentioned by Brussels concerns parcels coming from third countries. This distinction must be kept separate from the nationality of the platform selling the product. 3. Read the calculation unit. The indicated amount is 3 euro per item. It must not be confused with the 100 million more parcels carried annually by Swiss Post compared to ten years ago: these are different measures. 4. Consider the logistic route. Temu's example shows that warehouses in Europe can allow bypassing the fee. For this reason, the place from which the parcel departs is decisive information in reading the case. 5. Separate the initial effect from subsequent expectations. Grieder predicts a partial normalization and recalls the United States, where the impact was stronger at the beginning. This is the scenario indicated by the interview, not a new volume figure.
