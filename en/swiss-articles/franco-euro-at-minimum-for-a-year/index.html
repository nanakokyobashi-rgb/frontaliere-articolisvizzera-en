Swiss franc at a minimum against the euro: what it means for investors (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 2 September 2026

The Swiss franc continues to weaken against the euro, hitting a one-year low. Learn about the implications for investors.

Context

In brief

  • The Swiss franc falls against the euro.
  • It reaches a one-year low.
  • Interest rate differential between Switzerland and the Eurozone.

Key facts

  • What: The Swiss franc falls against the euro.
  • When: Today.
  • Where: Switzerland.
  • Who: Swiss National Bank (SNB), European Central Bank (ECB).
  • Amount: 0.9435 francs per euro.

The Swiss franc continues to weaken against the euro, a trend that has been noticeable for days. Today, the European currency reached a high of 0.9435 francs, a level not seen since August 2025.

Experts attribute this phase of weakness in the Swiss currency primarily to the interest rate differential between Switzerland and the Eurozone. The currency trend may seem paradoxical in an international context that is still tense, but it is mainly explained by the sudden return of the interest rate theme to the forefront, explains John Plassard, the investment strategy manager at the bank Cité Gestion. "It is not Switzerland (and its safe-haven currency) that is suddenly inspiring less confidence, but the euro that is regaining an advantage in terms of yield that it had largely lost in recent years."

The gap between the Swiss National Bank's (SNB) key rate, currently at 0.00%, and the European Central Bank's (ECB) rate, at 2.25%, makes investments in euros more profitable than those in francs. With inflation in the Eurozone reaching 3.3% in August, a more restrictive monetary policy from the ECB is also on the horizon. "The market already considers a move to a deposit rate of 2.50% as a given, and, above all, is beginning to wonder how long Frankfurt will have to maintain this stricter line," comments Plassard.

Operational details

Practical analysis

The weakening of the Swiss franc against the euro has significant implications for investors and the Swiss economy. The rate differential between Switzerland and the Eurozone makes investments in euros more attractive, as they offer higher returns than those in francs. This phenomenon could lead to an increase in investments in euros, with potential consequences for the Swiss financial market.

Impact on inflation

Inflation in the Eurozone reached 3.3% in August, which could lead to tighter monetary policy by the ECB. An increase in the deposit rate to 2.50% is already considered discounted by the market. This scenario could influence the decisions of the SNB, which could keep its lead rate at 0.00% to avoid further pressure on the Swiss economy.

Future scenarios

The upcoming decisions of the ECB and the Federal Reserve will be crucial in determining the trend of interest rates and, consequently, the value of the Swiss franc. Analysts expect the SNB to maintain the status quo, but the market will continue to closely monitor central banks' moves to assess the potential repercussions on the Swiss economy.
