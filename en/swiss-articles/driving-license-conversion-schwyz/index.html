The management of the driver's license and the conversion of driving documents issued abroad are fundamental bureaucratic steps for those who settle in Swiss territory. In the Canton of Schwyz, the competent authority for all matters relating to road traffic, the issuance of documents and the verification of driving licences is the cantonal traffic office. Federal and cantonal regulations establish precise procedures that vary depending on the country of origin of the foreign license and the date of issuance of the original document.

The role of the competent office

The traffic office of the Canton of Schwyz examines applications submitted by drivers who wish to convert their foreign licence into a Swiss licence. The procedure requires the presentation of specific forms, a visual or medical check in the cases provided for and the verification of the validity of the foreign driving licence. Operational details

# Conversion methods and controls

The procedure for converting a foreign driving licence in the Canton of Schwyz involves different levels of verification depending on whether the licence has been issued by a Member State of the European Union or the European Free Trade Association, or by third countries. For EU and EAEU licenses, the conversion usually takes place administratively without the need to undergo comprehensive examinations, without prejudice to any mandatory medical examinations for certain professional categories or for drivers who exceed specific age groups established by federal legislation.

On the contrary, for licenses obtained in States that do not have reciprocity agreements with Switzerland, the traffic office requires the passing of a practical control exam, known as a control run, and in some cases a reduced theoretical exam. This test serves to demonstrate knowledge of the particular traffic regulations in force in Switzerland, including road signs and specific precedence on cantonal and motorway roads. Applicants must prepare properly and submit a vehicle that complies with the safety regulations for the practical test.

The step-by-step procedure for residents

To start the driver's license conversion process in the Canton of Schwyz, you need to follow a series of well-defined administrative steps. First of all, it is necessary to go to the counters of the cantonal motor vehicle office or download the official forms directly from the institutional web portal. The conversion application must be accompanied by a recent passport-sized photograph, the certificate of domicile issued by the municipality of residence in the canton, the original foreign driver's license, and any medical certificate issued by a recognized doctor, which is mandatory for higher category licenses or older drivers.

Once the documentation has been submitted and the required cantonal administrative fees have been paid, the office evaluates the dossier. If a check ride is required, the applicant receives a summons with the date and place to take the test with a driving expert. Once this phase is passed, the old foreign license is confiscated or invalidated, and the new Swiss license is issued.

Frequently Asked Questions
What documents do I need to convert my driving licence to Svitto?
To convert a foreign driver's license in the Canton of Schwyz, you must present the official form of the traffic office, the original driver's license, a recent photo card, the residence certificate issued by the Swiss municipality and, if required by age or category, a medical certificate.
Do I need to pass a practical exam for conversion?
It depends on the country of issuance of the original license. For licences issued by Member States of the European Union or EFTA, the conversion usually takes place by administrative means. For non-EU licences, on the other hand, it is mandatory to pass a practical test and, where required, a theoretical examination.
Which entity manages driving licenses in the Canton of Schwyz?
The competent authority for issuing, converting and revoking driving licences in the Canton of Schwyz is the cantonal traffic office, which applies the provisions of federal and cantonal road traffic legislation.
