Opening a business in Solothurn: costs and procedures (cross-border guide)

From legal forms to the costs of registration in the commercial register, insurance obligations: how to start a business in the Canton of Soletta.

Opening a business in the Canton of Solothurn means navigating a federal and cantonal administrative system with uniform national regulations. Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage — some cantons have their own — but requires registration with the federal commercial register, choice of legal form, and compliance with mandatory insurance and tax obligations.

The four legal forms

The choice of legal structure is the first critical step. Each form has different implications for liability, taxation, and administrative complexity.

The sole proprietorship is the simplest form: the entrepreneur is a natural person, with unlimited liability for business debts. It does not require minimum capital, but personal assets remain exposed. Ideal for freelancers and professionals who want reduced initial costs.

Operational details

Minimum capital and registration fees

The minimum capital thresholds are set at the federal level for legal uniformity, but the registration fees for the commercial register are managed by the cantons, so they vary.

For a limited liability company, the minimum capital is CHF 20,000. At least half (CHF 10,000) must be deposited in a Swiss bank before registration. The remainder can be paid within 5 years without penalty, provided it is paid according to the company's regulations.

For a joint-stock company, the minimum capital is identical: CHF 20,000. Half must be deposited before registration.

A sole proprietorship has no mandatory minimum capital, making it a low initial cost option.

The registration fees for the commercial register in the Canton of Solothurn vary according to the legal form and the declared capital. It is advisable to contact the Solothurn Canton Commercial Register Office directly for exact amounts and updated procedures, as the rates can be reviewed annually.

The three-tier tax system

Anyone opening a business in Switzerland faces a tax burden determined by three levels of government:

Federal level: Federal direct tax (FDT) on individuals or business profits. Managed by the Federal Customs Administration. The rate is progressive and varies with income.

Canton level: Cantonal income and capital tax. In the Canton of Solothurn, it is managed by the cantonal administration. Rates vary significantly among Swiss cantons.

Municipal level: Multiplier applied to the cantonal tax. Each municipality applies its own multiplier, making the final rate variable even within the same canton of Solothurn. A common multiplier of 1.5 means that the cantonal tax is multiplied by 1.5 at the municipal level.

Key points

Federal and cantonal insurance obligations

Anyone starting a business and hiring employees must comply with mandatory federal insurance obligations.

Health insurance (LAMal): Mandatory for all residents in Switzerland within 3 months of arrival. Premiums are private, per capita, and not deductible as salary contributions. Adult deductibles range from CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000, 2500. Each canton offers premium reductions (subsidies) based on income bracket. In the Canton of Solothurn, subsidies are managed by the cantonal administration.

AVS/AI/IPG (mandatory pension): For each employee, the employer contributes 5.3% (matching the employee's 5.3%). The total amount is 10.6% of gross salary. Included: AVS 2.2% (employee) + AI 0.7% (employee) + IPG 0.5% (employee) + Unemployment reserve 1.1% (employee) + identical percentages for the employer.

Accident insurance (LAINF/LAA): Mandatory if employees are hired for more than 8 hours per week. The rate varies by economic sector: 0.7–1.5% of gross salary. For high-risk sectors, the rate is higher. Managed by SUVA or authorized private insurers.

Unemployment insurance (AD/AC): Mandatory for employees with a salary above a minimum threshold. Total contribution 1.1% up to an annual maximum. Employee and employer pay equal shares.

Professional pension (LPP/BVG): Mandatory for employees with a coordinated salary ≥CHF 22,050 annually (figure indexed each year). Contribution rates vary by age bracket: 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44 years), 15% (45–54 years), 18% (55+ years up to the reference age). Managed by authorized pension funds.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I need to register my sole proprietorship with the Trade Register?
Yes, it is mandatory. Anyone who exercises professional economic activity autonomously and independently must register with the Federal Trade Register before starting the activity. The registration has national validity. No minimum capital is required for a sole proprietorship, but registration remains mandatory. Contact the Commercial Registry Office of the Canton of Solothurn for forms and procedure.
What minimum capital is needed to open a SARL?
The minimum capital for a Limited Liability Company is CHF 20,000. At least half (CHF10,000) must be paid into a Swiss bank before registration and proven with a bank certificate. Residual capital can be paid up within 5 years. This threshold is set at the federal level for legal uniformity throughout Switzerland.
How much are the registration fees in the Canton of Solothurn?
The registration fees vary according to the legal form and the declared capital. There is no fixed national figure; each canton sets its own tariffs. In the Canton of Solothurn, I advise you to contact the Cantonal Trade Register Office directly (or consult the cantonal website) for the exact amounts and any benefits for new businesses. Rates may vary annually.
What are the main insurance obligations for those who hire employees?
The main federal obligations are: (1) AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% employee and 10.6% total with employer contribution; (2) LAMal: compulsory insurance within 3 months for residents (per capita premiums, NOT deductible as salary contributions); (3) LAINF/LAA: accident insurance 0.7-1.5% of the salary according to the sector; (4) AD/AC: unemployment insurance 1.1% up to the annual maximum. Does the Canton of Solothurn have a cantonal minimum wage?
Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage. Some Swiss cantons have adopted it (e.g. Geneva, Neuchâtel, Basel-City), others have not. For the Canton of Solothurn, I recommend checking directly with the cantonal administration or the relevant labour department if there is a cantonal minimum wage, to which sector it applies, and what the current amounts are.
