Swiss franc at a minimum against the euro: what it means for the economy (cross-border guide)

Key facts

  • What: The Swiss franc falls against the euro.
  • When: Not specified.
  • Where: Switzerland.
  • Who: Swiss National Bank (SNB), European Central Bank (ECB).
  • Amount: 0,9435 francs per euro.

The Swiss franc continues to weaken against the euro, a trend that has been noticeable for days. Today, the European currency reached a high of 0,9435 francs, a level not seen since August 2025. Experts attribute this phase of weakness in the Swiss currency primarily to the interest rate differential between Switzerland and the Eurozone.

The currency trend might seem paradoxical in an internationally tense context, but it is mainly explained by the sudden return of the interest rate theme to the forefront, explains John Plassard, the investment strategy manager at the bank Cité Gestion. 'It is not Switzerland (and its safe-haven currency) that suddenly inspires less confidence, but the euro that is regaining an advantage in terms of yield that it had largely lost in recent years.'

The gap between the Swiss National Bank's (SNB) key rate, currently at 0.00%, and the European Central Bank's (ECB) rate, at 2.25%, makes investments in euros more profitable than those in francs. With inflation in the Eurozone reaching 3.3% in August, a more restrictive monetary policy from the ECB is also on the horizon. 'The market already considers a deposit rate of 2.50% as a given, and, above all, it is beginning to wonder how long Frankfurt will have to maintain this stricter line,' comments Plassard.

Operational details

Practical analysis

The weakening of the franc against the euro has concrete implications for Swiss citizens and businesses. For consumers, a weaker franc means that imported products, such as electronics and consumer goods, could become more expensive. On the other hand, holidays in Europe may be cheaper for Swiss tourists.

Impact on businesses

For Swiss companies, a weaker franc can represent an opportunity to increase exports. However, companies that depend on imports may face higher costs. Firms will need to closely monitor currency fluctuations and adopt strategies to mitigate the associated risks.

Monetary Policy

The SNB kept the lead rate at 0.00%, while the ECB has a rate of 2.25%. This differential makes investments in euros more attractive than those in francs. With inflation in the Eurozone at 3.3%, the ECB is likely to adopt a tighter monetary policy, further increasing the rate differential.

Future decisions

In the next two weeks, both the ECB and the Federal Reserve will decide on monetary policy. While an increase in rates is a given in the Eurozone, the situation in the United States is less clear. The SNB will present its assessment on September 24, and analysts expect the institution to opt for the status quo.

Action

For Swiss citizens and businesses, it is important to monitor currency fluctuations and adopt strategies to mitigate the associated risks. Here are some concrete actions that can be taken:

Monitoring currency fluctuations

Citizens and businesses should closely monitor fluctuations in the euro-franc exchange rate. This can be done using currency monitoring tools available online. It is important to be aware of currency trends in order to make informed decisions.

# Risk mitigation strategies

Companies that depend on imports or exports should adopt strategies to mitigate the risks associated with currency fluctuations. This may include the use of financial instruments such as forward contracts or currency options to hedge against exchange rate changes.

Financial Planning

Swiss citizens should consider diversifying their investments to reduce exposure to currency risk. This may include investing in assets denominated in euro or other currencies to balance the portfolio.
