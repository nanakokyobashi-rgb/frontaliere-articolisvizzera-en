More Banks looking for more staff, announcements +14% in July

The Swiss banking sector continues to grow, with a significant increase in available jobs. According to data provided by various industry sources, the number of job listings increased by 14% in July 2026, bringing the total to 586 listings.

This increase is particularly evident in large Swiss banks. Raiffeisen, for example, saw a 24% increase in available jobs, bringing the number to 206 ads. UBS followed with a 27% increase to 61 listings, while Pictet saw a 53% increase to 26 listings. Banca Migros had an even more significant increase, of 263% to 40 listings.

This increase in jobs is due to several factors, including the growth of the Swiss economy and the demand for advanced banking services. In addition, Swiss banks are increasingly investing in technology and innovation, creating new job opportunities.

Key points

More banks looking for more staff: job postings in July 2026 +14% year-on-year

The job market in Swiss banks shows signs of recovery in July 2026, with a 14% increase in job postings compared to the same month in 2025. According to Indeed's survey for AWP, the data confirms a positive trend after the lows recorded in May 2026, when hiring had fallen by 7% on a monthly basis. Among the main financial centres, Zurich (+18%) and Geneva (+15%) drove growth, while centres such as Lugano (+10%) and Basel (+12%) showed a more moderate trend.

"The demand for specialized personnel is increasing, especially for roles in compliance, fintech and wealth management," said a spokesman for Swiss Banking.

Among banks, Raiffeisen recorded the largest increase with +24% (206 listings), followed by UBS (+27%, 61 listings) and Pictet (+53%, 26 listings). The most striking figure came from Banca Migros, which saw a jump of 263% (40 ads), partly explained by the expansion of digital services in the Canton of Ticino. Geographically, Zurich saw a 22% increase in job postings, with a focus on IT profiles and risk management, while in Geneva (+19%) demand was more oriented towards financial advisors and regulatory experts (SILTY).
