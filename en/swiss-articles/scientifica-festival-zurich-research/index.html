The Federal Polytechnic and the University of Zurich inaugurated the Scientifica festival, an event dedicated to presenting the latest technological innovations and making university research accessible to the general public. Kicking off Saturday in Zurich and several surrounding locations, the festival invites visitors of all ages to discover how the most innovative projects developed by Swiss students work. The organizers' stated objective is clear: to allow research to leave university laboratories and meet citizens directly.

Operational details

Swiss university research is a national strategic asset of great importance. The Federal Polytechnic and the University of Zurich are among the institutions driving innovation in the country, but often this work remains confined to laboratories. The Scientifica festival responds to a specific need: to democratize access to research and show citizens which concrete projects are being developed at Swiss universities.

This type of initiative has direct practical implications for the Swiss education system and labour market. When young people, especially those at the stage of choosing vocational training, come into contact with research such as Alexander Hefele's on automotive engineering or on the applications of autonomous drones, they acquire a concrete vision of what it means to work in the innovation sector. These are not just abstract images of technological careers, but tangible demonstrations of real projects that have already been completed and recognized worldwide.

Key points

For those living in Switzerland who wish to stay updated on developments in research and innovation, there are various channels and resources available. The Scientifica festival is a tangible opportunity to experience these projects firsthand, but it is only part of the landscape. Federal research institutions regularly publish reports on their progress and offer internships, traineeships, and job positions for recent graduates and experienced professionals.

If you are a student seeking guidance or a professional interested in transitioning to the technology innovation sector, the first step is to understand which specialization area attracts you the most. The projects presented at Scientifica cover a broad spectrum: from automotive engineering to robotics, from autonomous drone research to still-emerging sectors like sustainable energy and advanced materials.

How to access engineering studies in Switzerland

The federal institutes of technology (ETH Zurich and EPF Lausanne) offer bachelor's and specialized degree programs in engineering. Access to these programs requires a Swiss maturity diploma or a recognized equivalent. Courses are offered in German, French, and, in some cases, English. The federal institutes of technology maintain some of the highest quality standards worldwide, and their industry recognition is significant.

What is the Scientifica festival and who organises it?
Scientifica is a festival organized by the Federal Polytechnic and the University of Zurich, which began on Saturday in Zurich and in several surrounding locations. The event aims to make university research accessible to the public, presenting the projects developed by the students and researchers of the two institutions. What is the world record for the race car presented at the festival?
The race car developed by the team of Alexander Hefele, a mechanical engineering student, accelerates from 0 to 100 kilometers per hour in just nine tenths of a second, setting a world record. The project was completed three years ago and during the festival Hefele explains how it was made using simple explanations, images and videos. Visitors can sit in the vehicle and experience the thrill of being in the place of a professional driver.
How can I pursue a career in engineering in Switzerland?
In Switzerland, you can study engineering at federal polytechnics (ETH Zurich and EPF Lausanne) or at cantonal universities such as the University of Zurich. Access requires the recognised Swiss baccalaureate diploma or equivalent. Courses are offered in German, French and in some cases in English. After graduation, the Swiss job market for engineers is robust: companies in the technology, automation and robotics sectors regularly hire recent graduates. What other research projects are presented at Scientifica?
In addition to the record-breaking car, the festival showcases autonomous drones that autonomously recognize and avoid obstacles, an area crucial for robotics and automation. Visitors can discover state-of-the-art robots and understand developments in the most innovative research sectors. The great interest expressed by younger visitors shows how these projects can inspire the choice of careers in the technology sector.
Why is it important for university research to be accessible to the public?
According to Elisabeth Stark, Vice-Rector for Research at the University of Zurich, allowing citizens access to research helps students understand the importance of their work and the social value of research. In addition, events such as Scientifica inspire new generations to consider careers in the technology sector. University research also creates skilled employment in Switzerland and contributes to the country's economic competitiveness globally.
