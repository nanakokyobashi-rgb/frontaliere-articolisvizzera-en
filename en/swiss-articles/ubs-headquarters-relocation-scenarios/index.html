UBS is reportedly evaluating various scenarios to move its headquarters out of Switzerland. The news was reported on Wednesday by the Wall Street Journal, which cited sources familiar with the matter and linked the hypotheses to the tightening of rules on capital requirements.

There are three options indicated. The first involves the acquisition of a US financial institution and the subsequent transfer of its headquarters to the United States. The second is a merger with a European competitor. The third consists of the acquisition of UBS by an American bank.

The comparison on capital reserves

After the collapse of Credit Suisse in 2023, the provisions at the center of the story were formulated. The Council of States supported the obligation for the Swiss parent company to fully guarantee, through quality primary capital, i.e. CET1, shareholdings in foreign branches. The minimum requirement indicated today is 60%.

The measure aims to allow the bank to deal with any losses without resorting to state intervention. For UBS, however, full coverage could translate into additional capital needs of around $20 billion and weaken its international competitiveness.

Artisan Partners also described the new provisions as punitive and requested a transfer out of Switzerland. According to estimates by the US investor, the additional capital needed would be around 16 billion dollars.

Speaking on CNBC, Sergio Ermotti reaffirmed that keeping the headquarters in Switzerland would be advantageous for the national economy and for shareholders. However, Colm Kelleher warned that UBS could reconsider its presence in the country if the rules were too strict. The declared objective remains the achievement of a Swiss compromise.

Finally, the source specifies that UBS has not commented on the rumors about concrete plans to leave Switzerland. The scenarios reported by the Wall Street Journal therefore remain hypotheses linked to the comparison of capital requirements, not a transfer announcement already communicated.

Operational details

The issue for the Swiss financial center

The debate is not only about the registered office. The difference between CET1 and AT1 changes how the bank's safety is described. CET1 is capital immediately available to absorb losses. AT1 bonds, by contrast, can be written down or converted into equity only when certain conditions are met.

Three readings of the same risk

During the rescue of Credit Suisse in 2023, AT1 bonds worth approximately 16 billion francs were completely written off. The transaction gave rise to legal disputes concerning the conditions and timing of the write-down. The debate thus pits those who consider CET1 a more reliable form of protection against those who still regard AT1s as an important additional instrument.

For a reader in Switzerland, the point to keep separate is therefore this: the political proposal concerns coverage of the parent company's foreign holdings, while the debate over AT1s concerns the functioning of a different safety instrument.

Financial estimates require the same caution. The approximately 20 billion dollars are the additional capital requirement attributed to UBS as a result of stricter coverage. The approximately 16 billion, by contrast, are Artisan Partners' estimate. The source presents them as different estimates of the capital needed, not as a single figure.

Why an exit appears unlikely

Experts Simon Amrein and Peter V. Kunz consider an actual departure from Switzerland unlikely. Amrein points to political and economic stability, the strength of the currency, and the availability of qualified staff. A relocation could also hurt its reputation and client base. Kunz emphasizes the appeal of the Swiss Banking brand for wealthy international clientele, especially Asian and South American, and views the possibility primarily as a lever for political pressure.

The national impact identified by the source is therefore linked to UBS's position in the Swiss economy and the bank's international competitiveness. For the reader, the practical point is to follow the balance between capital soundness, the attractiveness of the financial center, and the headquarters remaining there: these are the effects and reasons highlighted by the source.
