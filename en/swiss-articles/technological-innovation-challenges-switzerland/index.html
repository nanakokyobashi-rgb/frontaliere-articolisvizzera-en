The Swiss scientific landscape is evolving rapidly, ranging from space research to materials science. Astrophysicists at the University of Bern, for example, have developed innovative tools to study the ice present on comets, aiming to better understand the formation of the Solar System. At the same time, the Federal Polytechnic of Zurich has introduced a master's degree in space sciences, a unique training path in Europe, while student projects across the country are giving impetus to the development of reusable rockets. This turmoil not only affects the sky, but also the national soil, where Swiss companies, such as the one based in Laufenburg, are making the most powerful redox flow battery in the world. Semiconductor research also sees an 'open source' movement that aims to free universities and small design nuclei from the commercial ties of large international companies, guaranteeing greater technological independence. The environmental impact of digitalization The growth of the data center sector in Switzerland is raising concrete questions regarding the consumption of natural resources. The push towards digital sovereignty and the widespread adoption of artificial intelligence require increasingly powerful computing infrastructures, which in turn put pressure on the country's water resources. To address this challenge, the Swiss Association for Data Center Efficiency has developed a specific, internationally awarded calculator capable of measuring the environmental and climate impact of these facilities. This tool represents a crucial step forward for companies operating in the sector, allowing a transparent assessment of energy and water consumption. The need to balance technological innovation and sustainability has become a priority, pushing towards more efficient and less impactful solutions for the environment. In addition to resource management, the debate extends to the dependence on US tech giants, the so-called Big Tech. Experiments conducted by journalists, who have tried to operate without the aid of such platforms to test digital independence, highlight the practical difficulties and daily challenges linked to this choice. Frequently Asked Questions
What is the impact of data centers on Swiss water resources?
The data center sector is booming and requires increasing amounts of water for cooling, a need dictated by the intensive use of artificial intelligence and the search for digital sovereignty. The Swiss Association for Data Center Efficiency has introduced a calculator to monitor the environmental impact of such facilities.
What is Switzerland doing for chip independence?
The country supports an 'open source' movement that aims to free universities and small design centres from the commercial constraints of large multinationals, promoting open technology to prevent a few players from dominating the global semiconductor market.
Is there a debate on social media for children in Switzerland?
Yes, the debate was sparked by the example of Australia, which banned social media for children under the age of 16. Swiss experts stress, however, that bans alone may not be enough to protect young people, requiring a more articulated approach.
