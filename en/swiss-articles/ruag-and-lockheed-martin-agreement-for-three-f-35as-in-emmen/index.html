RUAG MRO and Lockheed Martin have signed an agreement to continue the cooperation in the framework of the RIGI project. The program involves the assembly of three F-35A fighter aircraft at the RUAG headquarters in Emmen (LU), destined to become one of only four locations in the world for the final assembly and testing of the fighters.

The industrial calendar

The agreement opens a new phase of the project and will allow Switzerland to acquire skills for future aircraft maintenance. The arrival of the first components in Switzerland is expected in the first half of 2027; production is expected to begin in the autumn of the same year. RUAG, a company owned by the Confederation, indicated these steps in a note.

The initial plan included four planes. In detailed planning, the number was reduced to three to adapt the program to the requirements and available resources. Planning therefore reduces the expected size of the assembly; in the same initiative, RUAG indicates the acquisition of know-how as one of the objectives of the project.

According to RUAG, the project will allow Switzerland to acquire know-how on the F-35A, a new generation fighter, and increase national autonomy in maintenance activities. The company also aims to equip itself with the skills to offer the Swiss Army comprehensive support for the entire operational life of the fighters in the future.

The project also aims to support regional employment and consolidate RUAG as a European center of competence in military aviation. In this framework, the initiative combines the acquisition of technical capabilities, support for maintenance and strengthening of the industrial role. In the press release, Jürg Rötheli, chairman of the board of directors of RUAG MRO Holding SA, underlined its strategic value for the future of the company. He also highlighted that participation in the F-35A program aims to contribute to Switzerland's security and defense capability.

Operational details

What it means for Switzerland

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the significance of the project lies not only in assembling three aircraft in Emmen. The new development concerns the type of expertise RUAG intends to build around the F-35A: final assembly, testing, knowledge of the aircraft and future maintenance. The Emmen site will become one of only four sites worldwide for the two main operational activities, a position that RUAG links to consolidating its role as a European center of expertise in military aviation.

Comparison with the initial plan

The first practical point is the change from four to three aircraft. The initial plan called for four aircraft; detailed planning reduced the number to align the program with the requirements and available resources. The updated figure is therefore three. The number four describes the initial setup, while three is the reference figure for the project in its detailed planning.
