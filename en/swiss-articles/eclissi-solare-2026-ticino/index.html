The solar eclipse has concluded its path over Switzerland. The show reached its maximum occultation around 8.20 pm and ended with sunset. In Ticino and the Graubünden the Moon covered the sun for 91%.

In several locations, the population gathered to observe the event in company, perhaps even taking advantage of it for a picnic. In Lugano, for example, more than 500 people gathered in Piazza della Riforma, while in Bellinzona the crowd gathered in Piazza Collegiata.

Switzerland is a particularly suitable country for observing solar eclipses because of its geographical location. According to data from the Federal Office of Meteorology (MeteoSwiss), the last solar eclipse visible in Switzerland was on June 10, 2021, when the Moon covered the sun 95% in Lugano.

To prepare for this event, the Federal Office of Meteorology has published a guide for solar eclipse observers. According to the guide, it is important to wear sunglasses with polarized lenses to protect your eyes from the reflection of sunlight on the surface of the Moon.

The last partial eclipse of similar magnitude observable from Switzerland dates back to August 1999 (when in some regions of the Confederation the solar disk was 99.5% covered). Other partial eclipses were visible at our latitudes in 1912 (90%), 1961 (98%) and 2015 (75%).

The solar eclipse was a unique and rare event, but Switzerland is ready to handle it. Swiss legislation provides that astronomical events are managed by the Swiss Astronomical Society (SAS), which is responsible for promoting and managing astronomical events in Switzerland. SAS worked closely with local and regional authorities to ensure that the solar eclipse was handled safely and efficiently.
