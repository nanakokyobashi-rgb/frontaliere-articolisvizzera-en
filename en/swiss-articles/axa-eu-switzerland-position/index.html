EU bilateral agreements, AXA CEO: «important, not decisive»

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 October 2026

Thomas Buberl, CEO of AXA, considers the bilateral agreements with the EU important but not decisive for Switzerland's position in Europe.

Thomas Buberl, 53 years old, head of AXA, considers the bilateral agreements between Switzerland and the European Union an important factor, but not the decisive factor in maintaining the Confederation's position on the continent. The executive leads the French insurance giant, which is also highly active in Switzerland, and holds Swiss, German and French passports.

His starting point is not a diagnosis of decline. Buberl considers Switzerland one of the best-positioned countries in Europe. In the manager's view, the Confederation needs agreements with the EU because the Union is its main trading partner.

The distinction between importance and decisiveness

“They play an important role, but they are not the decisive factor”

The sentence contains a clear distinction. Recognizing the importance of the bilateral agreements means, in the executive's reading, acknowledging the value of the relationship with the EU; calling them non-decisive means rejecting the idea that Switzerland's position depends on a single factor.

In support of this view, Buberl points to countries that do not have agreements of the same kind and have nevertheless managed to find their own way. His argument does not negate the need for agreements with the main trading partner; rather, it places that relationship alongside other characteristics of Switzerland.

Among these, he points to the ability to respond flexibly and pragmatically. He then adds two elements he considers key to future success: proximity to citizens and decentralization. In his account, these are internal factors that complement the relationship with the outside world.

The manager's profile supports this assessment. Buberl studied in Germany and the United Kingdom and earned a doctorate in economics at the University of St. Gallen. Before moving to AXA, he was responsible for Switzerland at the insurance giant Zurich from 2008 to 2012.

The message combines openness toward the main trading partner with confidence in the country's ability to maintain a strong position through a flexible, pragmatic and decentralized model. It is this combination, rather than a stark opposition between agreements and autonomy, that defines the position expressed by Axa's head.
