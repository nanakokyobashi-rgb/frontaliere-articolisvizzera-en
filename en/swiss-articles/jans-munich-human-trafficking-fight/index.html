Switzerland's position at the summit

Federal Councillor Beat Jans attended an immigration summit in Munich, convened at the invitation of German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt. Ministers from 18 European states and representatives of the European Commission were present. The United Kingdom was present; Spain was absent and France did not join the meeting.

Jans indicated the need for swift and efficient European procedures, effective transfers under the Dublin Regulation and consistent enforcement of removal orders. Switzerland intends to intensify the cross-border fight against human traffickers, while respecting international law and human rights, the FDJP said.

The talks concerned the European Pact on Migration and Asylum, which entered into force on 12 June, return policy and cooperation within the Schengen area. The FDJP says that Switzerland is fulfilling its obligations and expects the same from its partners; initial experiences with the new procedures are overall positive.

According to the department, a credible migration policy strengthens Schengen as an area of freedom, security and justice. Switzerland, described in the statement as a highly interconnected country, wants to preserve and develop this area by deepening cooperation with neighboring countries and at European level against transnational crime, traffickers and trafficking networks.

Frontex and return centers

The summit also addressed surveillance of the EU's external borders and the strengthening of Frontex. The Commission is preparing changes: today the agency supports member states with expulsions, but responsibility remains with national authorities. The agency should be given a stronger mandate to cooperate with non-EU countries and a greater role in returns.

The CEAS allows centers in third countries for people who have no right to remain in the EU and are subject to return. The «Return Hubs» are promoted by Denmark, Greece, the Netherlands, Austria and Germany. Destinations mentioned include Rwanda, Uganda and Uzbekistan; the agreements are intended to be finalized by the end of the year. The source notes concerns expressed by international organizations about respect for human rights in Rwanda.

Operational details

From the summit to discernible implications

In the text provided, the announcement concerns a direction for cooperation, not an individual procedure. It does not indicate documents to submit, amounts or deadlines for Swiss residents. The national link is institutional: the DFGP links migration management to the defence and development of Schengen, while Switzerland declares that it wants to work with neighbouring countries and European partners.

Three operational levels

The first level is Swiss: the country fulfils its obligations and wants to intensify the cross-border fight against traffickers. The second is European: the Pact on Migration and Asylum, returns, external borders and Schengen. The third concerns implementation: national authorities remain in principle responsible for expulsions, while Frontex provides support and could receive a broader mandate.

Key points

How to follow the developments without confusing the announcements

To read future updates in an orderly manner, it is advisable to separate what Switzerland has communicated from what Europe is still preparing. The following sequence uses only the steps indicated in the source.

1. Identify the level. If the update concerns the cross-border fight against traffickers, the reference is the Swiss position communicated by the FDFA. If it concerns Frontex, ASYLUM, or the «Return Hubs», the reference is the European framework and cooperation with third countries.

2. Verify the status. The European Pact on Migration and Asylum entered into force on June 12. According to the FDFA, the new procedures have produced overall positive initial experiences. Changes to Frontex, on the other hand, are currently being prepared.

3. Check the responsibility. In deportations, Frontex supports Member States, but responsibility generally lies with the national authorities. This is the criterion for distinguishing agency support from a decision by national authorities.

4. Note the political deadline. Agreements with third countries for the «Return Hubs» aim for closure by the end of the year. The five promoting states are Denmark, Greece, the Netherlands, Austria, and Germany; among the mentioned countries are Rwanda, Uganda, and Uzbekistan.

5. Verify the legal constraints. Every development must be read alongside the FDFA's reference to international law and human rights. The source also notes the doubts of international organizations regarding the respect for human rights in Rwanda.

A checklist

For every new press release, note four items: who is speaking, which geographical area is involved, which measure is being described, and at what stage it is. The institutions named in the text are the FDFA, the European Commission, Frontex, and national authorities. The areas are Switzerland, neighboring countries, the European Union, and third countries. The measures concern procedures, repatriations, external borders, or cooperation against traffickers.

This checklist prevents a project from being mistaken for a rule already in application. Frequently Asked Questions
What are Switzerland's goals at the Munich summit?
Federal Councillor Beat Jans stressed the need for swift and efficient European procedures, functioning referrals under the Dublin Regulation and consistent enforcement of removal orders. Switzerland also intends to step up the cross-border fight against human traffickers and trafficking networks, operating in compliance with international law and human rights, as communicated by the DFGP.
What is the European Pact on Migration and Asylum?
This agreement entered into force on 12 June. During the summit in Munich, talks covered this Pact, return policy and cooperation in the Schengen area. According to the DFGP, the first Swiss experiences with the new procedures introduced by this regulatory framework are overall positive, and the country declares to fulfill its obligations.
What are Return Hubs and which countries promote them?
"Return Hubs" are centres located in third countries for people who do not have the right to stay in the EU and are subject to repatriation, as permitted by the CEAS. This initiative is promoted by Denmark, Greece, the Netherlands, Austria and Germany. Among the destinations mentioned are Rwanda, Uganda and Uzbekistan, with the aim of closing the agreements by the end of the year, despite the doubts of international organizations about respect for human rights in Rwanda.
