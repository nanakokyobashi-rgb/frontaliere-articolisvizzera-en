The study's figures

The 2026 heatwave had a profound impact on the Swiss economy, generating an estimated loss in labor productivity of over two billion francs. This figure emerges from calculations by the Swiss Cities Association, which are based on a model developed by the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology in Zurich (ETH). The publication was previewed by the NZZ am Sonntag newspaper and subsequently confirmed by Keystone-ATS. The analysis considered the months from June to August, a period during which anomalous temperatures reduced worker performance across the country.

The mechanism is clear: intense heat alters sleep quality and increases the sensation of fatigue, slowing down work rhythms. These heat stress effects are considered established parameters in occupational medicine. To quantify the damage, researchers used temperature data provided by MeteoSwiss. The reference value for an average summer, according to the 2022 ETH study, is 665 million francs. However, the 2026 summer showed values approximately 3.2 times higher than this baseline, justifying the record figure of two billion francs.

Cities under pressure

Urban areas proved to be the most vulnerable. This is due to two reasons: the high concentration of economic strength and the intensity of temperatures. The 172 centers statistically defined as cities suffered a total productivity loss of 1.3 billion francs. Although the 15 largest cities recorded the highest absolute losses, the phenomenon disproportionately affected smaller municipalities located in agglomerations. The latter suffered the highest loss rates in percentage terms relative to their economic base. The most emblematic case is that of Birsfelden, in the canton of Basel-Country, which tops the rankings with a 2.47% loss in productivity.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the amount of productivity losses caused by summer 2026 in Switzerland?
Summer 2026 generated productivity losses estimated at over two billion francs, according to calculations by the Union of Swiss Cities based on a model from ETH Zurich and data from MeteoSwitzerland. This is about 3.2 times higher than the average of CHF 665 million for a typical summer.
Which municipality had the highest loss rate and what was the value?
The municipality of Birsfelden, in the canton of Basel-Country, recorded the peak loss of productivity with 2.47% compared to its economic base, the highest value among all the Swiss municipalities analyzed in the study.
How did urban areas respond to small municipalities in agglomerations?
The 172 cities defined statistically suffered a total loss of productivity of CHF 1.3 billion in absolute value. However, the small municipalities located in the agglomerations showed the highest percentage rates of loss, suffering a more acute decline in efficiency despite smaller economic size.
