Summer 2026: over 2 billion francs of lost productivity

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 21 September 2026

Summer 2026 caused productivity losses of over CHF 2 billion, with Birsfelden at 2.47% and the affected cities at 1.3 billion.

The numbers of the study

The hot summer of 2026 had a profound impact on the Swiss economy, generating a loss of work productivity estimated at over two billion francs. The data emerges from calculations by the Union of Swiss Cities, which is based on a model developed by the Federal Institute of Technology in Zurich (ETH). The publication was anticipated by the newspaper NZZ am Sonntag and subsequently confirmed by Keystone-ATS. The analysis took into consideration the months from June to August, a period in which abnormal temperatures reduced the performance of workers across the country.

The mechanism is clear: intense heat alters the quality of sleep and increases the feeling of tiredness, slowing down the pace of work. These effects of thermal stress are considered consolidated parameters in occupational medicine. To quantify the damage, the researchers used temperature data provided by MeteoSwiss. The reference value for an average summer, according to the ETH study from 2022, is 665 million francs. However, the summer of 2026 showed values ​​approximately 3.2 times higher than this base, justifying the record figure of two billion francs.

Cities under pressure

Urban areas were the most vulnerable. This is for two reasons: the high concentration of economic strength and the intensity of temperatures. The 172 centers statistically defined as cities suffered a total loss of productivity amounting to 1.3 billion francs. Although the 15 largest cities recorded the highest absolute losses, the phenomenon disproportionately affected smaller municipalities located in agglomerations. The latter suffered the highest loss rates as a percentage of their economic base. The most emblematic case is that of Birsfelden, in the canton of Basel-Landschaft, which ranks at the top of the ranking with a 2.47% loss in productivity.

Operational details

Loss rate analysis

The distribution of losses is not uniform across Switzerland. Calculations indicate that the municipalities in the cantons of Basel-Landschaft, Solothurn and Aargau were particularly affected. The ranking of loss rates highlights an interesting dynamic: while metropolitan centers absorb larger volumes of loss in absolute terms, small municipalities in agglomerations suffer a more acute drop in efficiency. Birsfelden (BL) leads the ranking with a decrease of 2.47%, a figure that highlights how the local economic structure can amplify the effects of the heat. This differentiation suggests that economic resilience depends not only on the size of the city, but also on its sectoral composition and infrastructural adaptive capacity.

The Union of Swiss Cities has highlighted a critical gap: the economic dimension of climate change has so far not been studied systematically for Switzerland. This omission makes it difficult to plan adequate mitigation strategies. Without precise data, cantons and municipal governments struggle to allocate resources for climate adaptation. The lack of a detailed statistical framework prevents us from fully understanding which industrial or tertiary sector is most exposed to thermal risk, limiting the effectiveness of public policies to support businesses.

Projections for the future

The medium-term prospects are cause for concern. A risk analysis predicts that Switzerland will suffer a 5.31% loss in economic performance due to extreme climate events between 2030 and 2035. These include heat waves, floods, heavy rain, wind and drought. The figure becomes even more alarming if we consider the worst-case scenario: without the current adaptive capacity, this percentage would reach 7.32%. This difference of two percentage points represents a huge cost to the national GDP. The cost of living in Switzerland, already high, could experience further upward pressure if productivity losses are not offset by targeted investments. Key points

Implications for the labor market

The reduction in labor productivity has direct repercussions for companies and, indirectly, for employees. Although the source does not specify individual compensation mechanisms, aggregate losses raise questions about the sustainability of current work models. Employers are called upon to review company policies to mitigate the effects of heat, such as adjusting working hours or investing in cooling systems. For workers, accumulated fatigue can affect quality of life and long-term health. It is advisable to monitor your well-being and, if necessary, consult your doctor to manage heat stress. Lost productivity cannot be recovered, but the economic consequences can be limited through careful planning.
