On October 14, 2026, from Bern, the Confederation announced Switzerland’s participation in the annual meeting of the IMF and World Bank, scheduled to take place in Bangkok from October 15 to 17, 2026. The meeting will be attended by President of the Confederation Guy Parmelin, Federal Councillor Karin Keller-Sutter, and Martin Schlegel, President of the Swiss National Bank.

During the same summit, the G20 finance ministers will meet with central bank governors. The Swiss delegation will also use the opportunity to hold bilateral talks.

The Confederation’s position

The meetings will address current global challenges facing the economy and development policy. The communiqué emphasizes that persistent geopolitical tensions and trade conflicts are weighing on the global economy. Switzerland is committed to reliable international economic relations, open markets and resilient supply chains.

The position also lists three conditions for resilience and growth: sound public finances, financial stability and integrity, as well as an economic policy conducive to innovation. With regard to the IMF, Switzerland calls for qualified and independent advice focused on fundamental risks and core competencies. It also supports comprehensive and objective assessments of member countries’ positions on external economic policy, prudent lending and sound Fund finances.

The World Bank is viewed positively for its greater focus on jobs and private investment. The stated priority remains development impact and the fight against poverty, with favorable economic framework conditions, effective and inclusive labor markets, and high environmental and social standards.

Finally, Switzerland will sign a new partnership agreement with the IMF; the Federal Council approved it on August 26, 2026. Implemented by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs, the partnership allocates 40 million francs over the 2026–2030 period to technical assistance for building capacity and state institutions in member countries. At the G20, under the presidency of the United States, Switzerland will support economic openness, reliable and growth-friendly framework conditions, as well as greater debt transparency and more effective conversions in emerging and developing countries.

Operational details

The statement links Switzerland’s ongoing commitment to strengthening its position within the IMF and the World Bank and points to two possible benefits: more favorable conditions for trade and investment and lower exposure to economic risks.

The partnership amount should be read together with its intended use. The 40 million francs earmarked for the 2026–2030 period will fund IMF technical assistance to build capacity and state institutions in member countries. For a Swiss operator, the practical takeaway is therefore to observe whether this funding line helps make international markets more reliable, rather than treating the amount as directly available for individual activities.
