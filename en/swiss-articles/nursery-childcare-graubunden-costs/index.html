Finding reliable childcare during working hours is a concrete priority for Swiss families. In the Canton of Grisons, as in other cantons, the available options are diverse: daycare for the youngest, family daycare with private educators, and after-school care for school-age children. A central element is that the fees do not follow a fixed rate but are calculated based on the family's gross income, with public subsidy systems designed to make access to services more equitable.

Understanding how these services work, identifying real costs, and accessing cantonal contributions can make a significant difference in the household budget. A family with two incomes does not bear the same economic burden as a single-income family, and in many cases, public subsidies cover a significant portion of the monthly fee.

Operational details

Cantonal subsidies and family support

In addition to income-based fees, the Canton of Graubünden, like most Swiss cantons, provides direct subsidies for families whose income falls below certain levels or who have documented special needs.

The subsidy covers a variable percentage at the cantonal level: in some cases from 30 to 50% of the fee, in others up to 100% for the most vulnerable families. Access requires the submission of the most recent tax return and any other documents attesting to income (pay slips, statements from pension funds, self-employed declarations). The canton evaluates the request and, if approved, communicates the monthly contribution to the parent, who applies it directly to the facility's invoice.

This system allows many families to access childcare services that would otherwise be prohibitively expensive. Where a couple of teachers might benefit from a modest subsidy, a single-income family with an average income receives a more substantial contribution.

Practical scenarios: how real costs vary

The difference between a single-income family and a dual-income family is significant.

Case 1 — Single-income family: One working parent (gross income CHF 50,000/year) and a two-year-old child. The childcare facility offers a base fee of CHF 1,800 per month. Key points

How to search for a custodial facility in the Canton of Graubün

The first recommended step is to contact the relevant cantonal office — usually the Social Service Office, the Family Office or the equivalent welfare office. This body keeps an up-to-date register of all public and private services authorised in the canton, with information on availability, timetables, teaching languages (Graubünden is characterised by mainly German-speaking areas, but also includes Romance and Italian areas), and available staff.

The cantonal office also provides detailed guidance on how to access benefits and what documentation to submit. Although it is not mandatory to contact this entity, it is highly recommended to do so before undertaking independent research, as it avoids unproductive research and clarifies your eligibility for public contributions immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the main difference between a daycare center and a daycare family?
The kindergarten is a collective structure with specialized educators, dedicated spaces, scheduled activities and usually at least 15-20 children. A daytime family is run by an educator at his or her private home, generally accommodates 5-8 children, offers a more home-like environment, and often more flexible hours. Both must be authorized by the canton and meet training and safety standards.
How exactly is the monthly fee calculated based on income?
The canton applies a tariff scale that divides the daily/monthly fee into a percentage borne by the family and one borne by the public subsidy. The percentage depends on declared gross income, family composition (number of children, number of working parents), and sometimes assets. The cantonal facility or office provides an online simulation by entering your income.
What documents are required to apply for a cantonal grant?
Generally: most recent tax return, payroll of the last three months, income certificates if self-employed, valid identity document. Some cantons also require an additional application form. Please contact the cantonal office for the exact list, as it may vary for particular situations (unemployment, disability, exclusive custody).
At what age can children attend a daycare or daycare family?
Most accept children from the end of mandatory parental leave (14 weeks postpartum for mothers). Some kindergartens have separate sections for infants (0-12 months) and older children (12-36 months). Day families often do not accept infants under 6 months. The after-school school welcomes children from primary school (usually from 5-6 years old).
Can I stop or change the case during the year?
Yes, but you generally need to give notice (usually 30 days). Check the facility contract for specific details. If you change the custody, also inform yourself if the cantonal allowance is recalculated automatically or if you need to submit a new application.
