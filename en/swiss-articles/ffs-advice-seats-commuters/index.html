SBB has drawn irritated reactions from some users with its advice on finding seats on trains. The railway company proposed five tips—including delaying your departure time or reserving a seat for a fee—which some commuters have called “absurd.”

According to SBB data, more than 70% of seats remain empty throughout the day, but most of the demand is concentrated during the morning and evening rush hours. This leads many travelers to feel forced to stand during their commute.

SBB explained that adding cars is not straightforward and outlined the measures taken to increase seating capacity. However, the proposed advice has drawn criticism, with some users calling SBB’s post “passive-aggressive” and the suggested solutions “absurd.”

One reader wrote: “Do you have to get to the office at 11 a.m.?” Another criticized the proposal to reserve a seat for a fee, calling the way SBB is trying to downplay the problem “in poor taste.”

SBB spokesperson Reto Schärli clarified that the blog post did not cause much of a stir, but the issue of seating during peak hours is a recurring theme in customer feedback. Schärli emphasized that not all travelers can adjust their travel times and that the recommendations are intended as additional guidance for those with greater flexibility.

Practical Analysis

The SBB’s proposals for finding seats on trains have drawn criticism from commuters, who consider them “absurd.” This demonstrates just how deeply felt the problem of overcrowding on trains is, especially during rush hour.

SBB has explained that throughout the day, more than 70% of seats remain empty, but demand is concentrated during peak hours. This leads many passengers to feel forced to stand, despite the overall availability of seats.

The measures adopted by SBB to increase seating capacity include adding train cars and using apps and platform displays to show occupancy rates per car. However, these solutions do not appear to be sufficient to resolve the problem of overcrowding during peak hours.

Criticism from commuters highlights the need for more effective solutions to manage crowding on trains. SBB should consider additional measures, such as increasing train frequency or introducing extra trains during peak hours, to improve the situation.

Furthermore, SBB should work to improve communication with commuters by better explaining the reasons behind its decisions and offering more practical and acceptable solutions. This could help reduce criticism and improve customer satisfaction.

SBB should also consider introducing a seat reservation system, which could help better manage demand and ensure that all passengers find a seat. However, it is important that this system be accessible and affordable for all commuters, so as not to create further inequalities.

In conclusion, commuters’ criticism of SBB’s proposals for securing seats on trains highlights the need for more effective solutions to manage overcrowding. SBB should consider additional measures and improve communication with commuters to resolve this issue.

What to Do

If you’re a commuter and have trouble finding a seat on trains, there are a few steps you can take to improve your travel experience.

First, check the SBB’s online schedule and crowd forecasts. The app and the new platform displays show the occupancy rate per car, which can help you choose the least crowded train.

If your travel schedule is flexible, consider delaying your trip by a few minutes. Later trains are often less crowded. Also, try traveling at the front or back of the train, where there are often empty seats.

If you’re interested in reserving a seat, consider booking a seat in the dining car. The reservation fee will be deducted from the cost of food and drinks, making this option more affordable.

If you feel that the solutions proposed by SBB are insufficient, you can share your concerns directly with SBB. The railway company welcomes feedback and suggestions from commuters and may consider additional measures to improve the situation.
