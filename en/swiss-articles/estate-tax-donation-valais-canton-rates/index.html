Inheritance and gift tax in Valais: rates

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 6 September 2026

Guide to inheritance and gift taxes in the Valais Canton: cantonal structure, exemptions for spouse and children, official declaration and terms.

Context

In brief

  • In Valais, inheritance tax is cantonal, not federal
  • Spouses and children usually benefit from reduced rates
  • Mandatory declaration within the deadlines of Valais law

Key facts

  • What: Tax on the transmission of property for inheritance and donation
  • Who: Cantonal tax administration of Valais
  • Where: Canton of Valais, Switzerland
  • Structure: 3 levels (federal, cantonal, municipal)
  • Base: Degree of kinship determines the applicable rate
  • Benefits: Surviving spouse and direct children often exempt or favored

Inheritance and donation tax in Valais is a matter of national relevance that concerns anyone who inherits or receives a donation within the canton. Unlike many other taxes, this tax is not regulated at the federal level: the competence belongs entirely to the cantons. Valais, like all 26 Swiss cantons, has its own legislation on inheritance tax and its own rate structure.

In Switzerland, the tax system rests on three levels: federal, cantonal, and municipal. For inheritance tax, the federal level does not intervene. It is the cantons that establish the normative discipline, the rates, the exemptions, and the declaration deadlines. This cantonal autonomy means that the rules in Valais can differ significantly from those of other cantons.

The structure of the tax in Valais

Valais, like most Swiss cantons, applies a system of differentiated rates based on the degree of kinship between the deceased (or donor) and the heir (or donee). Generally, cantonal laws classify heirs into "classes of inheritance": spouses and direct children in class I (lowest rates), parents and siblings in class II, other relatives in class III, and unrelated persons in class IV (highest rates).

The taxable base is the net value of the inheritance (deceased's estate net of debts and expenses). The total tax includes two components: the cantonal share (set by Valais) and the municipal share (added by the municipality of residence of the deceased at the time of death).

Both inheritances by death and donations are subject to tax in Valais. In the case of donations, the mechanism is similar: the taxable base is the value of the transferred property, and the rate depends on the degree of kinship. The donation declaration must be submitted to the cantonal tax administration of Valais according to the deadlines set by the law.

Operational details

How the rate system works

The rate applied mainly depends on the degree of kinship and, often, on the amount of the inheritance. In a hypothetical scenario, a spouse who inherits in Valais will be subject to a significantly lower rate than a brother or a stranger. This reflects the widespread practice in many cantons of favoring close relatives.

Some cantons, including Valais, also provide a system of 'exemptions': if the inheritance falls below a minimum amount set by the cantonal law, no tax is due. This threshold varies from canton to canton and sometimes also depending on the class of inheritance.

Exemptions and concessions

A common feature in many Swiss cantons is the total or partial exemption of the surviving spouse. In some cases, the spouse pays no tax; in others, they benefit from a significant reduction. Direct descendants (children) also enjoy reduced rates or exemptions that allow them to inherit part of the estate without paying tax.

In Valais, the cantonal legislation specifies the categories of heirs who benefit from concessions and the conditions. For example, there may be exemptions for modest amounts, concessions for the spouse, or discounts for minor children. However, the exact details must be verified in the currently applicable Valais cantonal law, as regulations can change or be updated.

Practical implications of the cantonal structure

Since each canton has legislative autonomy, the practical implications for those inheriting in Valais are very specific: the rates and exemptions that apply are those determined solely by Valais, not by other cantons. An inheritance in Valais will follow Valais rules, regardless of where the heir lives.

Furthermore, the communal level adds an additional layer of complexity: the commune where the deceased resided at the time of death may apply an additional inheritance tax. This means that the total tax calculation requires considering both the Valais cantonal share and the specific communal share.

Key points

Inheritance declaration procedure in Valais

Anyone called to inherit in Valais must submit an inheritance declaration to the Valais cantonal tax office. The deadlines for submission are set by cantonal law and generally range from a few months to one year from the date of the deceased's death. It is essential to respect the deadline: delay in declaration can result in penalties.

The declaration must contain complete information: personal data of the deceased, detailed list of all inherited assets (real estate, bank accounts, securities, vehicles), value assigned to each asset at the date of death, data of all heirs and their degree of kinship with the deceased, declarations of any debts or obligations on the inheritance.

Required documentation

To correctly complete the declaration, several documents are required: death certificate of the deceased, will (if any) and certified copy, bank account statements at the date of death, property certificates for real estate, valuations of artworks or particularly valuable assets, previous tax declarations of the deceased and identification documents of all heirs.

The Valais cantonal tax administration provides standard forms for the declaration and guidelines on asset valuation. Contact the office directly to receive the complete list of required documentation based on your specific situation.

Donations: parallel procedure

Donations follow a slightly different procedure. In Valais, the donor or the donee (depending on the cantonal discipline) must declare the donation within a short term from its completion (usually from the notarial deed). The rates applied to donations may differ from those of inheritance: generally, cantons provide more favorable rates for donations, as an incentive to estate planning.

How to contact the cantonal administration

For precise information, declaration forms, and support in calculation, contact the Valais cantonal tax administration directly. In German, it is called Kantonale Steuerverwaltung Wallis; in French, Administration cantonale des impôts du Valais. The cantonal tax office provides standardized forms, guidelines on asset valuation and tax base, updated information on current benefits and exemptions, and assistance in calculating the tax due.

For complex legal issues (disputes over wills, international inheritances, optimal estate planning), it is advisable to consult a lawyer or accountant specialized in Swiss inheritance law.

Frequently Asked Questions
How is inheritance tax calculated in Valais?
The tax is calculated based on the net value of the inheritance and the degree of kinship of the heir. Valais applies differentiated rates: spouses and direct children benefit from reduced rates, while more distant relatives suffer higher rates. The total calculation includes the cantonal and municipal share. The exact details must be verified with the cantonal tax administration of Valais.
What are the exemptions in Valais?
The Valais legislation provides benefits for the surviving spouse (often exempt from tax) and for direct children (reduced rates or deductibles). Some cantons provide exemptions for modest amounts: if the inheritance falls below a minimum threshold, the tax is not due. The exact details are specified by Valais cantonal law and may change over time.
What documents are needed to declare a succession in Valais?
Death certificate, will (if any), bank statements, property certificates for real estate, valuations of valuables, previous tax returns of the deceased and identification documents of all heirs are required. The declaration must be submitted to the cantonal tax administration of Valais within the time limits established by law (generally a few months to a year after death).
Are donations taxable in Valais?
Yes, donations are taxed in Valais. The mechanism is similar to that of succession: the rate depends on the degree of kinship between donor and donor. The declaration of donation must be submitted to the cantonal tax administration within a short period of completion. Donation rates are generally more favorable than inheritance rates.
Who to contact for specific information in Valais?
Contact the cantonal tax administration of Valais directly (Kantonale Steuerverwaltung Wallis in German, Administration cantonale des impôts du Valais in French). The office provides standardized forms, asset valuation guidelines, facilitation information, and calculation support. For complex legal matters, consult a lawyer or accountant specialising in Swiss inheritance law.
