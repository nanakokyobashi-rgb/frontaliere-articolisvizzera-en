Complementary services Unscrewed: AVS and vital minimum (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 23 August 2026

Guide to the calculation of the AVS annuity, contribution gaps and supplementary benefits in the Canton of Schwyz. Role of the cantonal clearing house and practical procedures to ensure minimum viability.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Supplementary benefits (PC) supplement the AVS when the annuity does not cover the minimum living
  • The cantonal compensation fund manages registration of contributions, gaps and PC coordination
  • In the canton of Schwyz, the CPs are financed by the Confederation, canton and municipalities
  • Contribution gaps and low incomes are the main causes of access to PCs

Key facts

  • What: Supplementary benefits integrated into the AVS annuity to ensure minimum living
  • When: Requests during or after retirement (reference age: 64 women, 65 men)
  • Where: Canton of Svitto via cantonal compensation fund
  • Who: Crooked residents with insufficient AVS income
  • Amount: Calculated as the difference between living minimum and rent plus lump sums for rentals and LAMal
  • Management: Federal Social Insurance Office coordinates; cantonal administration examines applications

In the canton of Svitto, the cantonal compensation fund is the central body of the first pillar of social security (AVS). It manages the collection of contributions, the recording of insurance periods, the calculation of annuities and coordinates access to supplementary benefits (PC) for residents.

When the monthly AVS annuity does not cover the actual costs of living, supplementary benefits intervene to ensure the minimum living. This system ensures that no pensioner in the canton falls below the poverty line, as long as they apply and meet the requirements

Operational details

How to Calculate AVS Rent and the Role of Contribution Gaps

The monthly AVS income depends on two main factors: the amount of the average salary recorded during the contribution years and the number of years of contributions paid. A full insurance period for men is 45 years; for women 44 years.

When an insured has worked fewer years than necessary, or has had periods without payments, the compensation fund records "contribution gaps". These gaps directly reduce the amount of the final monthly annuity. A common scenario concerns those who have interrupted their career for family reasons, illness or unemployment without contributory coverage.

Not all gaps count equally. The case distinguishes between:

  • Justified gaps : periods covered by contributions due to sickness, invalidity, unemployment, maternity when accredited by the system
  • Unjustified gaps: periods of total absence from the system, sometimes recoverable retroactively through voluntary payments

Voluntary Deposit Strategies

Those who discover that they have gaps before retirement can pay additional voluntary contributions to the compensation fund, thus increasing their monthly income. After retirement, recovery is generally more difficult and subject to tighter administrative terms.

Key points

How to Apply for Complementary Benefits in the Canton of Schwyz

The process for accessing complementary benefits in the Canton of Schwyz usually begins several months before the expected retirement date.

Phase 1: Request a Payout Estimate — Contact the cantonal compensation fund and request an extract of your insurance account (Versichertenkonto). This document shows all contributory periods, gaps, and the estimated payout at the time of retirement. You can also obtain this document online through the cantonal compensation fund's portal.

Phase 2: Verify Gaps — Carefully examine the extract. If you notice missing periods (unregistered work years), report this immediately to the fund with supporting documents (pay slips, contracts). You have a deadline to submit appeals or provide clarifications.

Phase 3: Decision on Additional Contributions — If your payout will be below the minimum vital amount, consider with the fund whether it makes sense to pay voluntary contributions in the months leading up to retirement. The fund will calculate how much to pay to achieve a more adequate payout.

Phase 4: Application for Complementary Benefit — About 3-4 months before retirement, submit a written application to the Canton of Schwyz's Complementary Benefits Office. Attach:

  • AVS (AVS payout certificate) received from the fund after the final review
  • Declaration of income and assets (cantonally issued form)
  • Certificate of mandatory health insurance (LAMal)
  • Recent bank statements if required

Phase 5: Decision and Payment — The cantonal office examines the application within 2-4 weeks. If approved, the PCs are paid automatically along with the AVS payout from the first month of retirement.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the requirements for access to supplementary services in the Canton of Swiss?
To access Swiss CPs you must be a resident of the canton, be entitled to an AVS annuity (after reaching the reference age: 64 for women, 65 for men), and prove that your AVS annuity plus supplementary income does not cover the minimum living allowance set by the canton. There are no strict equity limits, but very high equity values can limit access to benefits.
How do I know if I have tax gaps before I retire?
You can request a statement of your insurance account (Versichertenkonto) from the Swiss Compensation Fund. This document reports all recorded contribution periods and any gaps. If you notice missing periods, contact the cashier with documentary evidence (coupons, contracts). It is advisable to check at least 2-3 years before your expected retirement to have time to plan voluntary payments.
Should I pay voluntary contributions if I have contribution gaps?
It depends on your specific situation. The Swiss Compensation Fund can calculate the cost and benefit of a voluntary payment: how much CHF to pay today and how much the monthly rent would increase. If the payment is less than the pension increase received in the first years of retirement, it is generally advisable to proceed. Ask for a free consultation at the cashier to evaluate your case.
What is the minimum life expectancy in the Canton of Schwyz and how does it affect supplementary services?
The Living Minimum in the Canton of Schwyz is updated annually by the Federal Office and the canton. It is not a national fixed figure, but varies slightly between Swiss cantons and municipalities based on the local cost of living. PCs bridge the gap between your AVS annuity and the living minimum, ensuring you can cover rent, food, utilities, and mandatory health insurance (LAMal). For precise amounts, consult the cantonal office.
