Context

In a nutshell

  • Salaries in the Canton of Basel-City vary significantly depending on the sector and the level of vocational training
  • Switzerland has no federal minimum wage; wage protections are based on cantonal and sectoral collective agreements
  • The Swiss labour market requires mandatory affiliation to AVS/AI, LPP and LAMal for residents and employees

Key facts

  • System: The Swiss labour market operates on three pillars (federal, cantonal, municipal)
  • Wage protection: No federal minimum wage; protection through cantonal/sectoral collective agreements (CCL)
  • Contributions: Employees pay AVS/AI 5.3%, AD/AC 1.1%, LAINF 0.7–1.5% according to sector
  • Hourly flexibility: Max 45 hours/week (industry, offices, sales) or 50 in other sectors
  • Minimum holidays: At least 4 weeks per year (5 under 20); cancellation deadlines: 1/2/3 months

The labour market in the Canton of Basel-City reflects the general structure of the Swiss system, where wages and conditions of employment are not regulated by a single federal law, but by a complex of collective agreements at sectoral and cantonal level. Unlike other European countries, Switzerland does not have a minimum wage set by federal law. This means that wage protection depends entirely on negotiated agreements between employers and trade unions.

Operational details

How wage contributions work in Switzerland

For those working in the Canton of Basel-City, the gross salary indicated in a contract is subject to compulsory reductions due to social contributions. These are not taxes in the traditional sense, but funding for social protection systems run by federal agencies.

The AVS/AI system (Old Age and Survivors Insurance/Disability Insurance) is the first pillar of Swiss social security. Employees and employers each pay 5.3% of gross salary to these schemes. Unemployment insurance (AD) and occupational accident insurance (AC) add an additional 1.1% up to an annual cap. Non-occupational accident insurance (LAINF) varies from 0.7 to 1.5% depending on the sector of activity.

The second pillar, occupational pension (LPP — Law on occupational pension for old age, survivors and disability), is mandatory for those who earn above a minimum coordination threshold. LPP contributions start at 7% of the coordinated salary (for workers aged 25–34) and increase significantly with age: 10% for those aged 35–44, 15% for those aged 45–54, and 18% for those aged 55 up to the reference retirement age. Key points

What to do concretely: step-by-step for job seekers in the Basel City Canton Step 1: Verify your qualification and applicable collective contract Before negotiating a job offer, identify the collective contract (CCL) applicable to the sector and position of interest. If the employer claims to operate under a specific CCL (e.g., the Basel City chemical-pharmaceutical CCL), ensure the proposed salary respects the minimum wages and benefits outlined in the contract. Many employers provide this document upon explicit request. If they don’t indicate a CCL, it doesn’t mean one doesn’t exist: verify with the trade association or local unions. Step 2: Calculate net salary from gross Upon receiving a job offer with a monthly gross salary, deduct mandatory social contributions (AVS/AI, AD/AC, LAINF) to estimate the net. Then, approximate the federal, cantonal, and communal direct taxes: Basel City has its own tax rates, differing from other cantons. Use an online salary calculator for a realistic net estimate, noting the result is indicative and depends on personal deductions (rent, health insurance, LPP contributions). Step 3: Understand LPP (Pension Plan) Benefits Request a copy of the company’s LPP regulations from the employer and verify the employee’s vs. employer’s contribution percentage. The federal rule (7–18% of coordinated salary) is a legal minimum; many qualified companies in Basel City offer better conditions. Clarify the “coordination deduction” amount (minimum salary threshold included in LPP calculations), which the employer must communicate transparently. Step 4: Subscribe to LAMal within legal deadlines If not already insured, purchase health insurance within 3 months of starting employment in Basel City. Choose a deductible suitable for your risk profile. Check if Basel City offers subsidies to reduce premiums based on the previous year’s declared income. This is a right, not a favor: formally request it from cantonal authorities if your income is low. Step 5: Register with AVS The employer typically registers the employee with the local Old Age and Survivors’ Insurance (AVS) Office. However, it’s advisable to verify within the first weeks that the AVS number has been assigned and contributions have started. This prevents gaps in your contribution record that could reduce your future pension. Step 6: Understand notice and termination rights Before accepting a contract, carefully read the notice terms. While federal law provides for 1/2/3 months’ notice based on seniority, individual contracts might include longer or shorter terms (usually longer). If terminated during the first month of probation, it’s generally possible to interrupt with short notice: check the contract. Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a legal minimum wage in the Canton of Basel-City?
No, there is no federal minimum wage in Switzerland. Wage protection takes place through collective agreements (CCLs) negotiated between employers and trade unions at sectoral and cantonal level. In the Canton of Basel-City, the main sectors (chemical-pharmaceutical, finance, trade) have active CCLs that set minimum wages by category. Always check which CCL applies to your industry.
What are the main salary contributions that an employee must pay in the Canton of Basel-City?
Employees pay: AVS/AI 5.3%, AD/AC 1.1% (up to the maximum), LAINF 0.7–1.5% according to sector. To these are added LPP contributions (7–18% of the coordinated salary, based on age) if the salary exceeds the minimum coordination threshold. Finally, there is the direct federal, cantonal and municipal tax, calculated on total income with deductions. Use a salary calculator for an accurate estimate.
What happens if my employer does not respect the collective agreement in the Canton of Basel-City?
If an employer applies wages or conditions lower than those provided for by the CCL in force, the employee may appeal to the competent trade union association or to the cantonal labour authorities. Switzerland offers strong legal protections: Violation of a CCL is subject to both civil (compensation) and criminal penalties. Always document written offers and contracts, and report non-compliance to local authorities.
What are the maximum working hours in the Canton of Basel-City?
Federal law sets 45 hours per week for industry, offices and commerce, and 50 hours for other sectors. Collective agreements in the Canton of Basel-City often provide for shorter hours, especially in qualified sectors. Check your individual contract and the relevant CCL for actual hours and rules on overtime and rest days.
How much is the security deposit (deposit) for an accommodation in the Canton of Basel-City?
Federal law (CO art. 257) limits the security deposit to a maximum of 3 months of rent for residential properties. The deposit must be paid into a escrow account in the name of the tenant and managed by a neutral third party (often a bank or cantonal office). At the end of the lease, the deposit must be returned in full if there is no documented damage or delinquency.
