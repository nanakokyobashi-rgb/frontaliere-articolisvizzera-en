The health tax for old cross-border commuters is a health contribution introduced by the Italian Parliament in the 2024 budget law. This measure has been criticized by Swiss experts, who consider this tax a tax and, if applied by the Regions, would violate international agreements.

The health tax for old cross-border commuters affects Swiss citizens who have lived in Italy for more than 10 years and are over 65 years old. According to the law, they will have to pay a monthly health contribution, which will be used to cover the costs of their health care. However, Swiss experts argue that this tax is a tax and that it does not comply with international agreements.

In the Canton of Ticino, for example, there are about 30,000 Swiss citizens who have been living in Italy for more than 10 years. If the health tax for old cross-border commuters were to be applied, this could lead to an increase in living costs for these citizens, who could be forced to reduce their expenses in order to pay the health contribution.

In addition, the health tax for old cross-border commuters could also have negative repercussions for Swiss municipalities, which could suffer an increase in money flows to Italy. According to experts, this could lead to increased operating costs for Swiss municipalities and could also negatively affect their ability to offer public services.
