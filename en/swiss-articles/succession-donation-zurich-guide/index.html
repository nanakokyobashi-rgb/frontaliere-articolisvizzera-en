Zurich inheritance tax: rates and exemptions 2026 (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 25 August 2026

Inheritance and gift taxes in the Canton of Zurich: rates for degree of kinship, total exemptions for spouse and children, mandatory declaration and deadlines to be met.

Context

In a nutshell

  • In the Canton of Zurich, inheritance taxes are the exclusive responsibility of the canton, not the federal government.
  • Total exemptions for the surviving spouse and significant reductions for children; distant relatives pay progressive rates.
  • Compulsory declaration within 30-90 days of death at the Zurich Tax Office.

Key facts

  • What: Inheritance and gift taxes managed at cantonal level
  • Where: Canton Zurich (each Swiss canton has its own rules)
  • Who: Zurich Cantonal Tax Administration
  • Levels: Federal + cantonal + municipal system
  • Structure: Progressive rates by degree of kinship (spouse, children, siblings, others)
  • Competence: Cantonal, not federal laws

Switzerland does not have a federal inheritance tax. Unlike many foreign jurisdictions, inheritance and gift taxation is the exclusive competence of the 26 Swiss cantons, each with its own tax law and specific multipliers. This means that those who receive an inheritance in the Canton of Zurich are subject to Zurich's tax rules, which differ from those in Bern, Geneva, Valais or other cantons. Municipalities, within certain limits, also modify cantonal rates through local multipliers, creating further territorial variations.

Operational details

Rates and degrees of kinship

The degree of kinship determines the applicable rate. The Swiss system generally distinguishes four tax classes:

  • Class I: Spouse, children, parents — minimum or no rates
  • Class II: Siblings, grandchildren — intermediate rates
  • Class III: Cousins and more distant relatives — high rates
  • Class IV: Non-relatives — maximum rates

In the Canton of Zurich, as in every canton, the specific rates are established by cantonal legislation and vary depending on the hereditary income bracket. A close heir (child or spouse) may pay a minimal or no rate on small amounts, while higher rates apply to larger inheritances. A cousin faces significantly higher rates, even on small amounts.

# Spouse Exemptions

The surviving spouse generally enjoys preferential treatment in almost all Swiss cantons. In the Canton of Zurich, the spouse benefits from specific rules that guarantee patrimonial protection after the death of the partner. This preference reflects the desire to protect the surviving partner and ensure the couple's economic continuity. To verify the exact amount of the exemption and the specific conditions (e.g. legal separation or property regime), it is advisable to contact the Tax Office of the Canton of Zurich directly.

Key points

How to Declare an Inheritance in the Canton of Zurich

The declaration of succession is mandatory in the Canton of Zurich and must comply with specific deadlines to avoid penalties and surcharges. The procedure is articulated in several precise stages:

1. Notification to the canton

Within a term set by the canton (usually 30-90 days from death), one must inform the Canton Zurich Fiscal Office of the opening of the succession. This notification is usually made through a notary or directly by the heir or the executor of the will.

2. Filling in the declaration form

The heir must fill in the official cantonal form (available at the Canton Zurich Fiscal Office) indicating:

  • The deceased's name and dates of birth and death
  • A complete list of inherited assets (immovable property, bank accounts, securities, businesses, rights)
  • The identity of all heirs with degrees of kinship
  • The amounts of the inheritance shares for each heir
  • Inherited debts and liabilities (mortgages, unpaid debts, funeral costs)

3. Payment of the tax

Calculated according to the Zurich tax rates, the payment must be made within the term set by the canton. Failure to pay or late payment result in interest on arrears and possible administrative penalties.

4. Conservation of documentation

It is advisable to keep a copy of the declaration, detailed calculations, and payment receipts for at least 7 years, the term of prescription for fiscal disputes in many Swiss cantons.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between inheritance tax and gift tax in the Canton of Zurich?
Both are taxed according to Zurich cantonal rates based on degree of kinship and amount. In the Canton of Zurich, as in most Swiss cantons, donations during the donor's lifetime are subject to the same succession rates after death. Some cantons have minor differences in terms of exemption or periodicity. Please consult the Tax Office of the Canton of Zurich to confirm specific rules.
Does the spouse pay inheritance tax in the Canton of Zurich?
No, in the vast majority of Swiss cantons, the surviving spouse is completely exempt from inheritance tax. The Canton of Zurich follows this general rule. However, it is always advisable to check with the Zurich tax authorities for specific details, especially if there are special restrictions or complex estates.
What are the terms for declaring a legacy in the Canton of Zurich?
The declaration of succession must be filed within a time limit set by the Canton of Zurich, generally within 30-90 days of death. Delay results in default interest and possible administrative penalties. We recommend that you contact the Tax Office of the Canton of Zurich or a notary to confirm the exact deadline and filing methods.
Does a cousin pay inheritance tax if inherited from the deceased in the Canton of Zurich?
Yes, cousins belong to class III (non-direct collateral relatives) and are subject to higher rates than children. In the Canton of Zurich, as in many cantons, cousins pay a significant tax, with rates increasing as the amount inherited increases.
Can I donate money to my children without paying tax in the Canton of Zurich?
Donations to children are subject to the same rates as inheritance in the Canton of Zurich. However, many cantons offer exemptions for small amounts or periodic donations. Check with the Zurich Tax Office the non-taxability thresholds, the methods of exemption for repeated donations over time, and the validity times of these benefits.
