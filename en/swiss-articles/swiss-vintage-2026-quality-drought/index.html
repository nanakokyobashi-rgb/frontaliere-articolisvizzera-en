Operational details

Unexpected positive effects of drought

Paradoxically, drought has brought with it beneficial aspects for production. The heat and low humidity have limited the development of unwanted fungi, significantly reducing the need for phytosanitary treatments. This has positive consequences both for the environmental sustainability of Swiss viticulture and for the production costs of winemakers, who will be able to invest fewer resources in chemical interventions.

The small berries, due to the scarcity of water, represent a further distinctive element of this vintage. Although they result in lower yields per hectare, fewer high-quality berries can lead to more concentrated and characteristic wines, potentially increasing their value in the cost of living in Switzerland — that is, the final consumer price.

Economic implications for the domestic market

The 2026 harvest will have repercussions for the Swiss wine market in the coming months. With reduced quantities, producers will be able to count on a relative scarcity that will support the prices of the vintage wines. At the same time, the high quality represents a positive element for the reputation of Swiss wines in national and international markets.

Key points

What to do concretely: the end of August calendar

For those working in the Swiss wine sector, the calendar of late August and early September 2026 represents a critical period. Winemakers will have to monitor the bunches daily, checking both the acidity and the sugar content through laboratory analysis or, for small producers, through direct measurement instruments such as the refractometer.

Optimal harvest windows will likely shrink to a few days, requiring flexibility in labor mobilization. Wineries that operate with mechanical harvesting will have to schedule the interventions precisely; those who practice manual harvesting will be able to count on a slightly wider but still defined window. The lack of moisture represents an opportunity to reduce the risk of rotting during collection and transport, an element that reduces post-harvest losses.

Opportunity for conscious consumers

Swiss wine enthusiasts can take advantage of this vintage to look for bottles that combine high quality and unique characteristics. The limited presence of unwanted fungi and the absence of intensive treatments will make the 2026 vintage a potential candidate for organic and biodynamic viticulture, where already adopted by producers.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why will the 2026 harvest be of high quality despite the drought?
According to the Valais Office of Vine and Wine, early ripening and especially the lack of moisture have limited the development of unwanted fungi. This reduces the need for intensive phytosanitary treatments. The smaller berries, due to the scarcity of water, concentrate the aromas and sugars more, improving the final taste profile.
When does harvesting start in Valais?
The Valais Office of Vine and Wine foresees the start of the harvest in the week of 24 August 2026 in the best exposed vineyards, generalizing from 31 August. Maturation checks indicate an advance of 6-12 days compared to 2022, a year also characterised by high temperatures.
In the canton of Vaud, has the drought significantly damaged the vineyards?
Francois Montet, president of the Federation of Vodese Wine Growers, says that some plots have suffered from drought, but the phenomenon remains "almost anecdotal" with respect to the entire vineyard area. Clay soils hold water better, while older vines can tap into deeper water reserves.
What are the main challenges of early harvesting?
Early harvesting requires achieving a balance between physiological maturity (acidity and sugars) and phenolic maturity (tannins and colour). A too early harvest accentuates the plant aromas, while a late one creates imbalances between alcohol and acidity. Winemakers must monitor the evolution of each vineyard on a daily basis.
How will this harvest impact the prices of Swiss consumer wine?
With reduced quantities and high quality, and prices are likely to remain stable in the short term, as scarcity is offset by superior quality. 2026 wines will gradually enter the market from autumn 2026 and winter 2027, without drastic changes in the overall cost of living.
