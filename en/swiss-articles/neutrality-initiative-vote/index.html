Neutrality, the press highlights the UDC's defeat

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 29 September 2026

Over 70% of voters and all cantons rejected the neutrality initiative. The campaign was supported by Christoph Blocher.

More than 70% of voters and all cantons rejected the neutrality initiative in Sunday’s vote. The following day, Swiss newspapers interpreted the result as an expected defeat, a heavy blow for Christoph Blocher and a result capable of strengthening the Government’s position.

The verdict according to the newspapers

Il Corriere del Ticino interprets the 70% of votes against as a final verdict against those who were aiming for a rigid neutrality pursued for its own sake. The clear result, the editorial adds, endorses the policies of Parliament and the Executive and leaves no serious room for relaunching the initiative.

laRegione offers a different reading of the same outcome: the rejection prevents neutrality from becoming a dogmatic principle. For the newspaper, it is an important tool of Swiss foreign policy, a means rather than an end, which must be able to remain flexible in relation to circumstances in the world.

NZZ calls the result decisive and recalls that Blocher’s initiative had been supported with almost 4 million francs. According to the Zurich-based newspaper, it would have had no chance from the outset. The German-speaking Swiss newspapers belonging to the Tamedia group speak of a personal defeat for the former UDC Federal Councillor, who apparently failed to convince even his own party.

Blick sees the outcome as a gradual distancing from the UDC’s tribune. The next dossier identified by the daily is that of the EU, considered even more important: according to this reading, it could be entrusted to his daughter Magdalena Martullo-Blocher.

La Liberté calls the result a masterful thrashing inflicted by the people and a clear signal to Blocher. The newspapers of the ESH group — Le Nouvelliste, ArcInfo and La Côte — highlight the cost of the campaign: posters and advertisements can be bought, but a popular majority is harder to buy.

Le Temps completes the picture: for the newspaper, this is the third defeat of the year for UDC, particularly painful because it concerns one of its flagship causes. The same outcome is nevertheless read as a strong endorsement of the way the Federal Council applies neutrality, with a popular mandate for this approach that had never been obtained before.

Operational details

The practical reading for those living in Switzerland

The vote does not merely deliver a numerical result. Its significance, for the press cited, lies in the relationship between the national level of the decision and the line with which Parliament, the Executive and the Federal Council apply neutrality. The fact that the rejection came from all Cantons prevents it from being read as a choice limited to a single area; the figure above 70% becomes the starting point for every subsequent interpretation.

The text examined contains no operational deadlines or new procedure resulting from the vote. The focus remains on the political significance attributed by the newspapers to the ballot outcome. For the reader, the distinction is concrete: an endorsement of the Government's line does not, by itself, amount to an administrative instruction. The source describes an orientation, not a form to fill out or a deadline to meet.

The second concerns institutions: Corriere and Le Temps see the vote as support for the line already pursued by Parliament, the Executive and the Federal Council. The third concerns the party: the interpretations in Blick, Tamedia and Le Temps shift attention from the individual initiative to Blocher's and the UDC's position.

These are not three different results, but three ways of using the same outcome in the national debate. Key points

How to follow the dossier

The source also offers a method for reading the next news reports without confusing facts and commentary. There is no need to attribute effects to the vote that the text does not describe; instead, each time, it is necessary to verify which level of the matter is being referred to.

1. Establish the electoral result. Before reading the assessments, note the subject of the vote, its rejection by all the Cantons and the share of more than 70% of votes against. These are the common points from which the comments of Swiss media outlets begin.

2. Separate institutions and parties. When an article talks about Parliament, the Executive or the Federal Council, the theme is the endorsement of the policy and the application of neutrality. When it talks about Blocher or the UDC, attention shifts to the personal defeat, the third setback of the year or the distancing indicated by Blick.

3. Treat prospects as prospects. The EU dossier and the possible role of Magdalena Martullo-Blocher belong to Blick's reading of the next step. They must therefore be retained as the newspaper's forecast, not transformed into a decision already made. The same caution applies to NZZ's thesis that the initiative would not have had any chance from the outset.

4. Compare the outlets. Corriere del Ticino and laRegione help follow the meaning attributed to neutrality; NZZ and Tamedia focus attention on Blocher; La Liberté and the ESH outlets insist on the campaign; Le Temps links the result to the Federal Council's line and to the UDC's current situation. Arranging these readings in sequence makes it possible to distinguish the shared fact from the explanations.

The check before sharing

Before repeating the news, verify three elements: that the percentage remains above 70%, that the vote is attributed to all the Cantons and that each assessment is traced back to the correct outlet. The available text is a snapshot on the day after the vote; therefore the reader must keep separate the result already reported, the editorial comments and the developments only hypothesized.

Frequently Asked Questions
How did the vote on the neutrality initiative go?
The initiative on neutrality was rejected by more than 70% of voters and all Swiss cantons in Sunday's vote. The result is interpreted by the Swiss press as a clear rejection and endorsement of the foreign and neutrality policy already applied by Parliament, the Executive and the Federal Council.
What was Christoph Blocher's role in the campaign?
The campaign in favour of the initiative was financed by Christoph Blocher with almost 4 million francs. Newspapers describe the outcome of the vote as a heavy personal defeat for the former federal councilor of the UDC and as the third setback of the year for the party.
How do leading Swiss newspapers react to the outcome of the vote?
The newspapers read the vote in various ways: the Corriere del Ticino speaks of a sentence without appeal, the Region stresses that it avoids turning neutrality into a rigid dogma, while NZZ and Tamedia highlight the defeat for Blocher. The Blick assumes that the EU dossier can be entrusted to Magdalena Martullo-Blocher and Le Temps reads the result as a big yes to the application of neutrality by the Federal Council.
