Safety must be addressed, demonstrated by setting a good example and put into practice throughout the internship. Suva has launched the "Internship in safety" campaign to integrate safety at work into the training process from the outset. Every year in Switzerland, around 23,000 apprentices are injured at work.

The issue of occupational safety is particularly sensitive in work environments with apprentices. According to 2022 data, the cantons of Bern and Zurich are the ones with the highest number of injuries recorded during the internships. Nationally, the total number of serious injuries is around 8,500, with an estimated cost of over CHF 150 million.

The legislation in force in Switzerland is to protect young workers from specific risks, such as the lack of adequate instructions. The Federal Law of 24 March 2000 on Occupational Safety (LSI) states that apprentices must be trained and supervised during the traineeship. In addition, the 1927 International Convention for the Protection of Adolescent Workers (CPLA) was ratified by Switzerland in 1935 and takes into account the specific safety needs of young workers.
