According to the source, the fifth round table on containing costs in the healthcare sector adopted six new measures in Bern on Monday. In addition to Federal Councillor Elisabeth Baume-Schneider, the meeting was attended by the Price Supervisor, the scientific community, patients, the pharmaceutical industry, health insurers, hospitals, doctors and the cantons.

Areas covered by the six measures

The package concerns outpatient and home care, the goal of reducing overcrowding in emergency departments, and care programmes for chronically ill patients. The proposals also include greater transparency regarding the costs of medical devices and coordinated medicine supplies in nursing homes.

The six measures add to the 12 decided in 2025 and, according to reports, will be implemented from next year. However, the text does not provide an economic estimate for the new package. Compared with the first, it describes it as more systemic. The content of the six decisions is described by area rather than in a separate fact sheet for each measure.

The available reference remains the first package: if the new measures work, the savings should exceed the 300 million estimated annually at that time. The wording maintains a precise condition: the amount is not presented as an outcome already achieved, nor as a certain forecast for each measure.

From care to coordination

The most concrete connection concerns how the stakeholders should collaborate. The medical societies will draw up a list of therapies to be carried out primarily at home. The insurers can then inform patients about them. Invoices must also include more detailed information about the medical devices used.

This sequence links a professional decision, information for patients and invoice transparency, without quantifying savings for each step. The package also includes coordinated procurement of medicines for nursing homes, but the source does not indicate separate volumes, prices or timelines for this measure.
