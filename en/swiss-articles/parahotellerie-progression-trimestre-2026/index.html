Paralympic sector, slight progression in the second trimester (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 28 August 2026

3.9 million overnight stays between April and June (+0.4%). Decreasing in the semester (-3.1%). UST DATA

The Swiss hospitality sector recorded 3.9 million overnight stays in the second quarter of thisyear, a slight increase of 0.4% compared to the same period in 2025. This is reported today by the Federal Statistical Office (FSO), in a framework that confirms domestic demand as the main driver of statistics.

The three sectors in detail

Holiday homes contributed 1.0 million overnight stays, down 8.9% compared to the second quarter of 2025. Collective accommodation — a category that includes youth hostels and group accommodation — totaled 1.3 million nights, an increase of 3.2%. Campsites stood at 1.6 million, recording growth

Operational details

Analysis: what the progression means for the Swiss economy

The figure of +0.4% on an annual basis should be read in perspective. The second quarter represents only part of the picture: the first half of the year as a whole closed at -3.1%, with 8.2 million overnight stays compared to about 8.46 million in the same period of 2025. The six-monthly decline is driven by the holiday home sector, which alone loses 360,000 nights compared to the previous year (-8.9%).

Countertrend Funds

Campsites and collective accommodation represent the most dynamic segments. Campsites registered +4.7% in the quarter and +4.4% in the semester, a sign of demand oriented towards low-cost outdoor solutions — consistent with the pressure on the cost of living that continues to characterise 2026. Collective accommodation (hostels, groups) grew by 3.2% quarterly and 1.0% semi-annually, confirming the recovery of school tourism and organized groups after the difficulties of previous years.

68% indigenous demand

The 68% share of Swiss guests is the most structurally relevant figure: it means that more than two out of three nights depend on domestic demand. For those operating in the sector — apartment owners, campground managers, hostels — this implies a direct correlation with the propensity to consume of resident families, influenced by the increase in sickness insurance premiums (LAMal), residual inflation and wage trends

Key points

What to do: how to read the FSO data and take action

The para-hotel statistics are part of the FSO's thematic surveys, published on a quarterly basis alongside data on employment, prices and tourism. Here's how to find your way around practically.

Step 1 — Consult the primary source

The figure released today (Keystone-ATS) refers back to the FSO's para-hôtellerie tables. To download the historical series and subsequent cantonal comparisons, you need to wait for the HESTA publication, which breaks down arrivals and overnight stays by canton and country of origin.

Step 2 — Place the data in your economic context

The +0.4% quarterly figure and the -3.1% half-year figure are not in themselves a sign of crisis, but should be read alongside:

IndicatorWhere to find it
Tourism employmentSECO, Labour Market Statistics
Consumer pricesFSO, monthly CPI
Health insurance premiumsFOPH, annual cantonal comparison
Nominal/real wagesFSO, wage index

Step 3 — For those working in the sector

Operators of campsites, hostels and holiday apartments can cross-reference the data with their own accounts to assess their position relative to the national average. The growth in campsites (+4.7%) and collective accommodation (+3.2%) suggests expanding demand for these formulas, while the decline in holiday homes (-8.9%) requires a rethink of pricing, distribution channels and target audience.

Step 4 — Plan the season

For those planning the next season, the three figures to monitor are: domestic demand trends (currently 68% of the total), available accommodation capacity (provided by the FSO in the HESTA statistics) and the cantonal school calendar, which directly influences flows towards hostels and campsites.

Frequently Asked Questions
How much did the Paralympic sector grow in Q2 2026?
According to data released today by the UST, the Swiss hospitality sector recorded 3.9 million overnight stays in the second quarter of 2026, a slight progression of 0.4% compared to the same period in 2025. The overall figure hides very different trends between the three sectors: campsites +4.7%, collective accommodation +3.2%, holiday homes -8.9%.
Why is the first half of 2026 down despite the positive quarter?
The first quarter of 2026 weighed on the half-yearly balance sheet: the sector closed at 8.2 million overnight stays, down 3.1% compared to the same period in 2025. The decline is mainly determined by the holiday home sector, which in the semester marked -8.9% to 3.7 million nights, offset only in part by campsites (+4.4%) and collective housing (+1.0%).
How much of the paralhotel demand is indigenous?
According to the UST, 68% of the hotel room nights in the second quarter of 2026 were generated by guests residing in Switzerland. This is the most structurally relevant figure in the survey, because it highlights the sector's dependence on domestic demand and, consequently, on Swiss households' propensity to consume.
Are regional data available on the Paralympic sector?
No, the FSO reports that regionally disaggregated data is not available in the quarterly survey. To obtain the breakdown by canton and country of origin of guests, it is necessary to wait for the publication of the HESTA statistics, which the Federal Statistical Office releases annually.
Which Paralympic sector grows the most in 2026?
Campsites are the sector with the most marked growth: +4.7% in the second quarter of 2026 and +4.4% in the first half, standing at 1.6 and 1.7 million overnight stays respectively. Collective housing follows (+3.2% quarterly, +1.0% half-yearly), while holiday homes are the only sector in decline (-8.9% in both periods).
