Context

The Communist Party and the Independent Union of Students and Apprentices (SISA) have set up a unitary national committee to support the neutrality initiative. The initiative aims to curb Switzerland's rapprochement with NATO and end direct and indirect participation in wars.

Switzerland, as we know, has been a neutral country for over 150 years, and its neutrality is guaranteed by the United Nations Charter and the Swiss Neutrality Convention, signed in 1940. However, in recent years, Switzerland has begun to participate in foreign military operations, such as the UN mission in Lebanon and participation in the stabilization mission in Afghanistan.

The initiative on neutrality, which will be put to a referendum on 12 September, aims to put an end to this policy of participation in wars and to guarantee Switzerland's neutrality. The bill, presented by the Communist Party and SISA, provides for the amendment of the United Nations Charter and the Swiss Neutrality Convention, to ensure that Switzerland does not participate in foreign military operations and that it does not provide logistical support to foreign armed forces.

The neutrality initiative has already received the support of many Swiss cantons and cities, such as Canton Ticino, which decided to vote in favour of the initiative, and the city of Lausanne, which expressed its support

Key points

The neutrality initiative is an important step for Switzerland, which could curb its rapprochement with NATO and end direct and indirect participation in wars. The Communist Party and the Independent Union of Students and Apprentices (SISA) are among the main supporters of this initiative.

Article 8 of the Swiss Federal Constitution states that Switzerland is neutral and does not engage in external conflicts. However, in recent years, Switzerland has begun to participate in international peacekeeping missions and provide military support to European Union member countries. These actions could be seen as a violation of neutrality and lead to a loss of Switzerland's credibility internationally.

The neutrality initiative proposes to add a new article to the Swiss Federal Constitution, which explicitly affirms Switzerland's neutrality and prevents direct and indirect participation in wars. In addition, the initiative proposes to abolish the law on compulsory military service and replace it with compulsory civilian service.

The Communist Party and SISA support this initiative because they believe that neutrality is essential for international peace and stability. Moreover, they believe that compulsory military service is incompatible with the principles of democracy and freedom.
