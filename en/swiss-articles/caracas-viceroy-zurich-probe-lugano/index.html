The connection with the Swiss banking system

The news raises a question that concerns the entire Swiss banking system, and in particular the Ticino one: how could a subject with a controversial international profile — described by the source as 'the viceroy of Caracas' and 'Trump's man for Venezuelan oil' — enter the shareholding structure of a bank based in Switzerland? The Betancourt case, according to the source, is emblematic of a period in which the Canton of Ticino hosted capital and figures from complex geopolitical contexts.

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the case raises a question of trust in the national financial system. Switzerland has a multi-level oversight architecture: at the federal level, the FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) operates, competent for the authorization and supervision of banks, securities dealers, and other financial institutions. At the cantonal level, each institution is subject to the federal banking law (LBCR) and cantonal implementing regulations. The Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) also imposes obligations to identify the chain of shareholders up to the beneficial owner.

Key points

What to know if you are a client of a Ticino bank

Anyone who is a current account holder or saver with a bank based in Ticino can check the health status of the institution through some public tools. FINMA publishes on its website the list of banks authorized in Switzerland, along with enforcement decisions and penalties imposed. For each bank, a card is available with the name, headquarters, authorization, and any extraordinary procedure status.

For residents who wish to open an account or transfer their savings, the standard procedure involves personal identification at the branch, presentation of a valid identity document, and, for foreign citizens, a residence permit (B, C, G, or L). Swiss banks are required to identify the beneficial owner of the deposited funds, as provided by the Money Laundering Act.

Useful checks for those operating with Swiss financial institutions

Here is an operational checklist for those who want to verify the reliability of their bank in Switzerland:

  • Consult the FINMA register of authorized banks on the official website
  • Check if the bank has been subject to published enforcement proceedings
  • Ensure the bank is subject to LBCR and LRD
  • Request from the bank documentation on the chain of shareholding control
  • Verify the existence of deposit insurance coverage (Swiss Banks' Deposit Insurance)
