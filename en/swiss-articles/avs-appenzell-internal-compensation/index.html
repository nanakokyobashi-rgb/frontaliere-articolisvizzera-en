AVS and complementary services in Appenzello Interno (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 26 August 2026

First pillar in Inner Appenzell: guide to the compensation fund, calculation of the AVS annuity and complementary benefits for the minimum living.

Context

In brief

  • The first pillar AVS is managed by the cantonal compensation fund of Appenzell Innerrhoden
  • Contribution gaps reduce the pension and are offset by complementary benefits
  • Complementary benefits protect the minimum living standard for the insured

Key facts

  • What: first pillar (AVS/AHV) and minimum social protection
  • Where: Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden, Switzerland
  • Who: Federal UFAS/BSV, cantonal compensation fund, AVS insured persons
  • Function: pension calculation, coverage of contribution gaps, minimum living standard protection
  • Authority: federal management (UFAS/BSV) + cantonal administration

Appenzell Innerrhoden, like all 26 Swiss cantons, has a compensation fund that administers the first pillar, namely old-age, survivors' and disability insurance (AVS/AHV). The federal body that oversees the entire system is the Federal Office of Social Insurance (UFAS), also known as the Bureau of Social Insurance (BSV in German). The cantonal compensation fund of Appenzell Innerrhoden concretely manages the registration of new insured persons, the collection of mandatory contributions, the calculation of future pensions and, if necessary, the provision of complementary benefits. This federal system represents one of the pillars of Swiss social security and constitutes the basic social protection network for those who work and reside in Switzerland.

Each canton has its own compensation fund, but all operate according to the same federal rules laid down by the Federal Law on Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance (LAVS/AVG). This means that the level of protection is identical throughout the national territory, even though the administrative bodies are local and decentralized.

Operational details

Understanding how the AHV pension is calculated means knowing the system of credits in insurable years. It is not a simple salary average: the procedure is more sophisticated. Each contribution year is revalued according to the average development of national wages, so earnings from the 1990s are adjusted to current salary levels before being entered into the formula. The Federal Office of Social Insurance publishes updated pension tables each year to reflect Switzerland's economic and demographic dynamics. Anyone wishing to know their pension amount in advance can request an individual account statement – called an AHV account statement – from the competent compensation fund, in this case the one in Appenzell Innerrhoden. The service is free.

Contribution Gaps and Their Concrete Impact

A contribution gap is a year (or more years) in which an insured person did not pay contributions. This can happen for various reasons: periods of unemployment not covered by unemployment insurance (AD/AC), years of study before age 20 not automatically recognized, career interruptions due to family or health reasons, or temporary emigration without contribution buyback. Each gap reduces the number of insurable years and therefore decreases the future pension. A person with 43 years of contributions will have a lower pension than someone with 44, even if their salaries were identical.

Key points

If you reside in Appenzell Innerrhoden and are approaching retirement, the first concrete step is to verify your AVS account with the cantonal compensation office. You can request an account statement free of charge, a document that shows the number of insured years recognized, salaries recorded year by year, any gaps or discrepancies, and an estimate of the future pension based on current data.

Procedure for access and verification of eligibility

The compensation office of Appenzell Innerrhoden accepts requests in writing or electronically. You will need to provide a copy of your identity document (passport, identity card), the account statement request form (available online on the UFAS/BSV portal), and a possible statement from your current employer if still employed. Processing time is usually 10–15 working days. Once you receive the account, you can verify the accuracy of the data, report any suspected errors or gaps, and calculate a projection of your pension with the help of a consultant at the office.

If you discover critical gaps, you can buy back missing contributions by paying a flat-rate amount calculated according to UFAS/BSV tables. This buyback increases your future pension and reduces reliance on supplementary benefits. Buyback is an important tool for those who have interrupted their career for documented reasons.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Appenzello Interna Compensation Fund?
It is the cantonal administrative body in charge of managing the first AVS/AHV pillar in the canton. It collects contributions from employers and employees, maintains individual insurance accounts, calculates AVS annuities at retirement age, and provides supplementary benefits. It acts according to the federal directives of the UFAS/BSV, ensuring uniformity throughout the Swiss territory.
How do I calculate my AVS annuity?
The AVS annuity is based on the number of insurance years with contributions paid and a federal formula that revalues past wages according to the national average wage index. It's not a simple average: each year of income is recalculated to current levels before entering the calculation. The clearinghouse provides a free estimate upon request via the AVS account statement.
What are contribution gaps and how do they affect my income?
Contribution gaps are years without paid contributions (unemployment, career breaks, unrecognised studies). Each gap reduces the number of insurance years and lowers the future annuity. They can be filled by redeeming the missing contributions, paying a lump sum calculated from UFAS/BSV tables. The redemption increases the annuity and reduces the use of supplementary benefits.
What are the complementary benefits and when are they due?
Complementary benefits are checks paid by the state (confederation + canton) when the AVS + second pillar LPP income does not reach the living minimum. They protect pensioners from situations of poverty, automatically covering the difference up to the subsistence threshold. They are publicly funded and guarantee everyone a minimum pension income base.
How do I request a statement of the AVS account at the cashier of Appenzello Interno?
Contact the Appenzello Interno clearing house by filling in the application form available on the UFAS/BSV website or by sending a letter. Attach a copy of your ID. The service is free of charge and takes approximately 10–15 business days to process. The statement shows insurance years, recorded wages, gaps and estimated annuity.
