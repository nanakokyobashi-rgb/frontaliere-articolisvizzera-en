Introduction

The second pillar LPP (Secondary Pension Pillar) is a supplementary pension system introduced in Switzerland in 2015. Its objective is to complete the social security coverage of the Swiss, providing an additional source of income in old age. The guide below describes in detail the mechanisms of the second LPP pillar, with a particular focus on the canton of Bern and the national comparison.

Contributions

Contributions to the second LPP pillar are mandatory for all Swiss who have a working income. The contribution share is set at 6% of labour income, which is split in half between worker and employer. For example, if a worker earns CHF 80,000 per year, their contribution to the second LPP pillar will be CHF 4,800 per year (6% of CHF 80,000).

How the second LPP pillar works

The second LPP pillar is a system of contributions and levies that is added to the first pillar, that is, the compulsory pension system (SPP). The second pillar is voluntary and allows Swiss citizens to contribute independently to their pensions. Contributions are paid by workers and their employers, and are intended to fund future pensions.
