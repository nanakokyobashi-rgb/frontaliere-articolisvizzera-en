EU Commission approves Switzerland-EU agreements: a step forward (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 11 September 2026

The Foreign Affairs Committee of the European Parliament approved the package of bilateral agreements with Switzerland by a large majority. Vote in plenary not yet set.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The Foreign Affairs Committee of the European Parliament approves the Switzerland-EU agreements
  • Green light by a large majority, without discussion
  • An accompanying resolution has also been approved
  • Plenary vote not yet scheduled

Key facts

  • What: Approval of the Switzerland-EU package of agreements
  • When: Today, according to the source
  • Where: Committee on Foreign Affairs of the European Parliament
  • Who: Committee on Foreign Affairs of the Eurochamber
  • Rapporteur: Christophe Grudler, French MEP
  • Outcome: By a large majority, in a few minutes, without discussion
  • Next step: Vote in plenary, date not known

The competent committee of the European Parliament today approved the package of agreements between Switzerland and the European Union by a large majority. The item on the agenda was accepted in a few minutes and without discussion, as reported by Keystone-ATS.

In addition to the treaties, the Committee on Foreign Affairs of the European Parliament also approved an accompanying resolution. The purpose of the document is to justify the approval. The text states that the package has been "negotiated and concluded as a balanced whole".

The role of the speaker

Christophe Grudler, MEP and rapporteur on the package of agreements, told the media that the debate had previously taken place in several committees. The approval represents an important step for bilateral relations.

Operational details

What it means for Switzerland

The vote of the foreign committee of the Eurochamber is a procedural step, but with real political weight. The fact that the package was approved in a few minutes and without discussion signals that the negotiating work conducted by the Federal Council and the European Commission in 2024 produced a text considered mature and balanced.

The ratification process

The procedure is twofold. In Brussels, the committee's approval represents a formal recommendation to the European Parliament. The plenary session will then have to decide, but the date has not yet been set. In Bern, the package is under parliamentary ratification, with the Federal Chambers called upon to express their views.

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the agreements touch on concrete issues: economic relations, market access, cooperation in regulated sectors. The source does not go into detail of the individual chapters, but the reference to the stabilization and expansion of existing relationships indicates a medium-long term horizon.

The principle of balance

The accompanying resolution insists on one point: the package is "negotiated and concluded as a balanced whole". This is not a rhetorical detail. In bilateral negotiations between Switzerland and the European Union, the balance between market access, financial contributions and regulatory autonomy has always been the central node.

Key points

What to follow in the coming weeks

The ratification process is not concluded. For those following the dossier closely, the next steps can be identified with precision, although the dates are not yet known.

Key steps

The first hurdle is the plenary vote in the European Parliament. The committee has issued a recommendation, but final approval rests with the assembly. It has not been communicated when the item will be scheduled.

The second hurdle is parliamentary ratification in Switzerland. The Federal Council negotiated the package in 2024. Now the federal chambers must examine it and vote. Again, the source does not indicate certain timing.

Why it is worth monitoring the dossier

The bilateral agreements touch sectors that directly affect daily life in Switzerland: labour market, wage conditions, cost of living, taxation and social security. Any change in relations with the European Union could have effects on salaries, AVS/AHV contributions, LAMal/KVG premiums and direct federal tax.

The source does not provide operational details on these aspects. But the principle of balance invoked by Grudler suggests that any renegotiations or unilateral changes should be assessed carefully.

What exactly did the European Parliament committee approve?

When will the plenary vote take place in the European Parliament?
