The Swiss healthcare system is based on compulsory health insurance, known as LAMal, which every person residing in Switzerland must take out. For 2026, anyone who lives or works in the canton of Geneva must contend with premiums that vary according to the deductible, provider and insurance model. Choosing the deductible is one of the main tools for containing the monthly cost: for adults, the minimum deductible is CHF 300, while the maximum is CHF 2500. The higher the deductible, the lower the monthly premium, but in return the first medical expenses of the year are paid out of pocket.

For cross-border commuters with a G permit, the situation has a specific feature: the right of option. In practice, they can choose whether to take out insurance in Switzerland or in their country of residence, subject to certain conditions. This choice should be considered carefully, because premiums and coverage vary significantly between the two systems. From a tax perspective, it should be remembered that withholding tax on employment income is deducted in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation by granting a tax credit, which must be reported in section CE of Form 730.

The legal framework for cross-border commuters

For those who work in Switzerland but reside in Italy, the reference is the New Cross-Border Commuter Agreement, signed on December 23, 2020 and applied from January 1, 2024, ratified by Italy through Law 83 of June 13, 2023. The Convention for the avoidance of double taxation between Italy and Switzerland dates back to March 9, 1976. One aspect to bear in mind is that Switzerland is not a member of the EU or the European Economic Area (EEA), which entails specific rules for workers and insured persons.

Legacy cross-border commuters remain taxed only in Switzerland (Article 9 of the 2020 Agreement). The transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. Practical analysis: how to choose the right deductible

The choice of the deductible is the main lever for reducing the monthly premium. Those who are young and healthy can opt for the maximum deductible of CHF 2,500, agreeing to pay the first medical expenses of the year out of pocket. In return, the monthly premium drops significantly. Those who need frequent treatment, for example for chronic pathologies, might prefer the minimum deductible of CHF 300, so as to have immediate coverage without having to pay large sums in advance.

A concrete, purely hypothetical example: a 35-year-old adult, resident in Geneva, without health problems, could choose a deductible of CHF 2,500 and an insurance model with a family doctor or telemedicine. In this way, the monthly premium will be lower than a deductible of CHF 300. On the contrary, a 60-year-old person with continuous therapies will probably find the minimum deductible more convenient, because medical expenses will still exceed the deductible during the year.

Comparison of insurance models

In addition to the deductible, the insurance model affects the premium. Alternative models (family doctor, HMO, telemedicine) offer reduced premiums compared to standard insurance with free choice of doctor. This choice must be made based on your habits and trust in your doctor. For cross-border commuters, the right to choose between Swiss and Italian insurance adds a further level of complexity: if on the one hand the Swiss system offers high standards, on the other the premiums can be higher than the Italian ones. The decision must also be made by considering taxation: the withholding tax withheld in Switzerland is not applied twice, thanks to the tax credit recognized by Italy.

To orient yourself, it may be useful to compare premiums between different cantons. Geneva, like many urban cantons, tends to have higher premiums than other regions. However, those with a modest income can benefit from cantonal subsidies (individual premium reductions), which in some cases cover a significant part of the cost. To verify the amount of the subsidy, it is necessary to submit an application to the competent cantonal office, attaching the tax return.

Another aspect to consider is the change in health insurance. In principle, it is possible to change insurer every year, subject to the cancellation deadlines. The procedure involves sending a cancellation to your fund within the established deadline, and signing a new contract with another fund. It is important to compare not only the premium, but also the coverage, services and quality of care.

For those who work in Switzerland, the choice of health insurance must also be made based on your canton of residence or work. Cross-border workers with a G permit must check whether their canton of work offers special conditions. In any case, the KVG covers basic benefits, but does not include, for example, accident insurance (LAINF) which is paid by the employer, nor occupational pension provision (LPP), which is mandatory for employees.

In summary, the best strategy depends on your personal profile: age, health, income and risk propensity. Those who are willing to pay higher medical expenses in case of need can save on premiums; those who prefer peace of mind choose a low deductible. The important thing is not to let the choice of health insurance be dictated only by the premium, but also to evaluate the quality of the services and the ease of access to care.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the LAMal deductibles for adults in 2026?
For adults, the minimum deductible is CHF 300, the maximum is CHF 2500. Choosing a higher deductible reduces your monthly premium, but increases your medical expenses. The choice depends on the state of health and the ability to bear unforeseen expenses.
Do frontier workers with a G permit have the right of option for sick pay?
Yes, border workers with a G permit have the right of option, that is, they can choose whether to insure in Switzerland or in their country of residence. This choice must be carefully evaluated, considering premiums, coverage and tax implications, such as the tax credit recognized by Italy.
How does the border tax at source work?
The income tax at source is only withheld in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation by recognizing a tax credit, to be indicated in the EC framework of model 730. This procedure applies to all frontier workers, old and new.
What are the rates of Swiss social contributions for employees?
Employee contributions are: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3%, unemployment insurance (AD/AC) 1.1% up to a salary of CHF 148,200, LAINF 0.7-1.5% and LPP 7–18% based on age (from 25 years). These values are set at the federal level and can affect net income.
When does the deadline expire to change sick pay for 2026?
Generally, the cancellation must be sent by 30 November of the previous year. For 2026, therefore, the deadline is 30 November 2025. It is advisable to compare the offers and send the cancellation in advance, so as to have time to subscribe to a new policy without interruptions in coverage.
