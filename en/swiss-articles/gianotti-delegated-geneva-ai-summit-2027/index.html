The Federal Council has defined the strategic guidelines of the Geneva Summit on Artificial Intelligence. The appointed delegate is Fabiola Gianotti, former Director General of CERN. The high-level international summit will bring together governments, international organisations, academia, business and civil society. The strategic priority is the use of artificial intelligence as an engine of innovation, well-being and social progress.

The Geneva summit will take place next Wednesday and will see the participation of over 200 delegates from all over the world. The Swiss delegation will be led by Fabiola Gianotti, who has already demonstrated her experience and expertise in the field of artificial intelligence during her tenure at CERN.

The main objectives of the summit are:

  • Promote the use of artificial intelligence for well-being and social progress. - Develop strategies to address the risks and challenges associated with artificial intelligence. - Promote international cooperation for development and use

Operational details

Summit on artificial intelligence, Gianotti delegate

The objective is to develop concrete collaborations, initiatives and common approaches that have an impact even beyond the summit. The delegate Fabiola Gianotti will implement the objectives established by the Federal Council.

Switzerland, notorious for its innovation and focus on technology, has stepped up with major initiatives to support artificial intelligence (AI). In 2022, the Federal Council launched the national plan for artificial intelligence, which envisages an investment of CHF 1.5 billion over the next five years. This plan aims to strengthen Switzerland's position as a leader in AI technology.

Delegate Fabiola Gianotti, a noted theoretical physicist, was chosen to lead this plan. He said: “Artificial intelligence has the potential to revolutionise many areas of our lives, from healthcare to finance, and we cannot afford not to make the most of this potential.”

Switzerland has already made great strides in this field. For example, the city of Lausanne has become a hub for the development of AI technologies, thanks to the presence of companies such as Google and Microsoft. In addition, the canton of Geneva has launched a plan to develop AI in the healthcare sector, with the aim of improving patient care and reducing costs.

The Geneva Summit on Artificial Intelligence will be an important opportunity to develop concrete collaborations and initiatives that have a positive impact on society. Delegate Fabiola Gianotti will be an important factor in achieving the objectives set by the Federal Council.

The summit, to be held in the City of Geneva, is an opportunity to discuss the benefits and risks of artificial intelligence (AI) and to identify areas where Switzerland can play a leadership role. AI is a rapidly evolving industry and Switzerland is already a major player in the global market. According to a 2022 report, Switzerland is the fourth country in the world for the amount of funds invested in AI, with over 1.3 billion Swiss francs (CHF) allocated in this sector.

The Federal Council set some clear goals for the summit: to develop collaborations between universities, companies and public institutions to create research and development projects; to identify areas where Switzerland can play a leadership role; and to create an enabling environment for AI innovation and development.
