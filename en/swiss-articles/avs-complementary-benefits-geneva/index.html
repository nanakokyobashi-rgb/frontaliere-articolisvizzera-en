AVS and complementary services Canton Geneva (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 9 September 2026

How the AVS works in the Canton of Geneva: compensation fund, pension calculation, contribution gaps and supplementary benefits for the minimum living wage.

Context

In brief

  • AVS is the first pillar of Swiss social security, managed federally by UFAS
  • The cantonal compensation fund collects contributions and manages individual accounts
  • Supplementary benefits complement insufficient pensions to the subsistence level
  • Total contributions amount to 10.6% (5.3% employee + 5.3% employer)

Key Facts

  • What: Three-pillar social security system (AVS first pillar, LPP second, pillar 3 private)
  • Who manages: Federal UFAS, cantonal compensation fund at local level
  • Contributions: 5.3% from employee for AVS/AI/IPG, another 5.3% from employer (total 10.6%)
  • Requirements: 44 years of contributions for women, 43 for men (full pension)
  • Supplement: Supplementary benefits cover the subsistence level if AVS pension is insufficient

The Canton of Geneva, like all Swiss cantons, applies the federal old-age and survivors' insurance system (AVS) managed by the Federal Office of Social Insurance (UFAS). AVS represents the first pillar of social security, fundamental to guarantee a basic livelihood for retirees, the disabled, and their families. The structure is articulated on two levels: the federal level, which defines general rules and contribution rates, and the cantonal level, where the cantonal compensation fund operates.

The cantonal compensation fund: role and functions

The cantonal compensation fund of the Canton of Geneva is responsible for the operational management of contribution payments from workers and employers. It collects monthly AVS/AI/IPG contributions (5.3% from the employee, 5.3% from the employer), maintaining individual accounts for each insured person. When a worker changes employment or canton, the fund coordinates the transfer of contribution rights to ensure continuity in the system.

Operational details

How to Calculate AVS Rent

The calculation of the AVS pension in the Canton of Geneva follows a federal mechanism based on two essential parameters: the number of contribution years and the average amount of income declared during the working career. To obtain a full annuity (100% of the calculated annuity), contributions must be paid for 44 years if female, or 43 years if male. If an insured has contribution gaps — periods without payments — his pension is reduced proportionally, according to a uniform federal formula.

The average amount of income considered for the calculation is not the simple arithmetic average of annual wages: the legislation provides for a revaluation mechanism. Earnings from previous years are revalued according to federal wage evolution indices, in order to faithfully reflect purchasing power over time and prevent those who worked decades ago from being penalized by historical inflation.

The cantonal compensation fund applies this complex calculation, verifying each component: actual years of payment, eligible notional receivables, revalued income, and deductions for gaps not covered. Once the amount of the annuity has been determined, the cashier communicates the result to the insured. If the sum is less than the minimum living limit established by the canton, the supplementary benefits will automatically be triggered.

Concrete Procedure for Accessing AVS Pensions

To obtain an AVS pension in the Canton of Geneva, you must follow a structured procedure, starting years before retirement.

Phase 1: Registration and account verification. If not yet registered in the system, visit or contact the compensation fund of the Canton of Geneva for formal registration. It is advisable to request an updated statement of your personal account at least 5-10 years before retirement age to verify the accuracy of recorded contributions and identify any gaps. The fund provides this statement free of charge upon request.

Phase 2: Identification of gaps and filling options. If the statement reveals gaps not covered by credited contributions, contact the fund for personalized advice. The fund will analyze whether those periods can be covered by retroactive credited contributions (for example, from forgotten periods of unemployment or illness), or if a voluntary redemption is possible. It is important to understand the impact of each gap on your future pension.

Phase 3: Formal pension application. Approximately three months before the actual retirement date, submit a pension application to the cantonal compensation fund. The application can be completed online or on paper using a form available at the fund's offices. It must be accompanied by identity documents, an updated residence certificate, and income certification for recent years (tax extract or income declaration).

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the exact role of the cantonal clearing house of the Canton of Geneva?
The cantonal compensation fund collects monthly AVS/AI/IPG payments from employers, manages the individual accounts of policyholders by recording each payment and figurative credit, processes pension applications on retirement, and calculates the amount of the pension on the basis of the overall contribution history. It is also responsible for interacting with the federal level, managing workers who have worked in multiple cantons, and coordinating with supplementary benefit entities.
How many years of contributions are required for a full AVS annuity in the Canton of Geneva?
For a full AVS annuity (100% of the calculated annuity), 44 contributory years are required for women and 43 for men. If the contributory history is shorter, the amount of the annuity is reduced proportionately. However, many periods such as unemployment, illness, or family assistance may be covered by figurative contributions that count as tax years.
How is the amount of the AVS annuity calculated in the Canton of Geneva?
The amount of the annuity is calculated on the basis of two components: the number of contribution years (44 or 43 for a full annuity) and the average amount of income during the career, revalued according to the federal wage evolution indices. If there are unmet contribution gaps, the annuity is reduced in proportion to the duration of the gap. The cantonal clearing house applies this uniform federal calculation.
What exactly are complementary benefits and who is entitled to them?
Supplementary benefits (PC) are a supplement to the AVS annuity when the latter is not sufficient to cover the cantonally defined minimum wage. They are granted only after an assessment of the economic situation of the insured and any family members (income, real estate, movable assets). Once recognized, they ensure that the AVS + PC sum covers at least the vital minimum. How can I fill a contribution gap in my AVS story?
Involuntary gaps resulting from unemployment, illness, injury, family assistance, or military service are often automatically covered by notional payments from unemployment insurance, disability insurance, or public benefits. For voluntary gaps, retroactive voluntary payments may be made for years prior to the end of the contributory obligation, subject to federal and cantonal limitations. The cantonal clearing house provides personalised advice on the options available.
