Residence permit B: how to apply and renew it (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 20 August 2026

Residence permit B in Switzerland: essential requirements, documentation, competent authorities (SEM), renewal times and procedures. Guide for non-EU foreigners.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Permit B = renewable residence permit for non-EU/EFTA foreigners in Switzerland
  • Management: SEM at federal level; cantons for procedural instructions
  • Essential documentation: employment contract, non-impedance certificate, active LAMal
  • Duration: renewable every 1–5 years, variable by canton and personal situation

Key facts

  • What: Residence permit (category B) for non-EU foreigners
  • Federal authority: SEM (State Secretariat for Migration)
  • Territorial management: Cantonal migration offices
  • Type: Renewable, distinct from permit L (short, ≤12 months) and C (domicile, almost permanent)
  • Prerequisites: Stable employment, financial autonomy, LAMal health insurance
  • Main steps: Application with documentation at the cantonal office, SEM verification, release

The B residence permit is the main residence permit for non-citizens of the European Union and the European Economic Area (EFTA) who intend to live and work in Switzerland for a medium-long period. Unlike the L permit, which is limited to 12 months and is non-renewable, the B permit allows for an extended and renewable legal stay in the Swiss territory.

Operational details

Essential Requirements for Permit Authorization B

Access to the residence permit B is subject to the fulfilment of a series of prerequisites verified by the cantonal authorities and controlled through the federal databases of the SEM. None of these requirements is optional; failure to meet any of them will result in rejection of the application.

Stable employment situation and adequate remuneration: the applicant must have an employment contract signed with a Swiss employer, or a confirmed job proposal (signed letter offer) attached to the application. Employment must be consistent with the level of qualification and remuneration must guarantee financial autonomy: the exact threshold varies by canton and family situation, but in general it is considered unacceptable to resort to public social aid during the first year of residence.

Certificate of non-impedance : official document of the country of origin or citizenship attesting to the absence of significant criminal convictions. The designation varies by country (some countries call it “certificate of good conduct”, others “extract from the central criminal register”). It is a fundamental prerequisite for the assessment of public safety. Obtaining it may take weeks or months, depending on the speed of the issuing country's administrative proceedings.

Step-by-step procedure to apply for residence permit B

Step 1: Preparation of documentation (before departure to Switzerland)

Still in the country of origin, the prospective applicant must collect the essential documentation:

  • Make sure you have an employment contract signed by a registered Swiss employer. The job offer must indicate the duration of the position, the job and the salary.
  • Initiate the application for the certificate of non-impedance with the competent authorities of the country of origin. This process can take anywhere from 2 to 12 weeks, depending on the country.
  • Collect valid identity documents: passport with a future expiry date of at least 6 months, birth certificate (if required by cantonal legislation), marriage or civil union certificate (if applicable).
  • Contact a Swiss bank to open an account in advance or for a pre-registration. Many institutions accept online bookings. Check out the [nav:banks] guide for available options.

Phase 2: Arrival in Switzerland and registration

Upon arrival in the canton of residence:

  • Register in the municipal registry within 2 weeks. The municipality provides a temporary registration certificate.
  • Take out compulsory health insurance (LAMal) within 3 months of arrival. If not fulfilled within the deadline, the cantonal authorities may assign an office cashier and charge the premiums

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the main difference between residence permit B and residence permit C?
The B permit is renewable every 1–5 years and is the initial title for non-EU foreigners. Upon renewal, the authorities reassess the employment situation and integration; renewal could theoretically be denied in the event of significant changes. The C permit is almost permanent (revocable only for serious reasons such as criminal offences) and requires at least 10 years of legal residence with a regular permit (5 years for EU/EFTA citizens) plus cantonal integration requirements. Do I need to already have health insurance (LAMal) before applying for B leave?
It is not strictly necessary before filing the application, but compulsory health insurance (LAMal) is a prerequisite for the final authorization of permit B. It must be taken out within 3 months of arrival in Switzerland. It is not a tax or wage contribution, but a private contract per capita with variable premiums by canton, age and coverage. How long does it take to obtain permit B from the submission of the application?
Investigation times vary significantly depending on the canton and the complexity of the personal situation, but generally range between 2 and 6 months from the submission of the complete and correct application. Standard and linear cases resolve in 2–3 months. During the investigation period, the applicant may work with a temporary residence permit (often the L permit). For more precise histories specific to your canton, it is advisable to contact the relevant cantonal migration office directly
Can I renew the B permit if I changed jobs during the validity period?
Yes, renewal is possible even after a change of employer, as long as the new employment situation is documented with a signed employment contract and the remuneration is adapted to the cantonal criteria. It is important to communicate the change to the cantonal migration office during the renewal process by providing the new contract. A change of employer does not automatically result in rejection of the renewal, as long as occupational continuity is guaranteed.
What happens if I do not renew my B permit before it expires?
Failure to apply for renewal by the expiry date indicated on the permit renders the stay irregular and can lead to significant legal consequences: administrative obligation to leave Switzerland, cantonal pecuniary sanctions, suspension of the right to work, and potential future denial of any residence permit in the country.
