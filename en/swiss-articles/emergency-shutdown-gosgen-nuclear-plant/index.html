Around 6:40 on Tuesday morning, a drop in electrical voltage in the system powering the control rods caused the automatic shutdown of the Gösgen nuclear power plant in the canton of Solothurn. These components serve to regulate activity inside the reactor.

The source presents the case as a distinct sequence: first the automatic shutdown, then verification of the behavior of the safety systems, and finally the suspension of operations pending authorization. KKG, the company that owns the plant, indicated that all safety systems operated correctly and in accordance with protocols.

The situation at present

According to the report, the plant remains safe and no radioactive material leak occurred. The Federal Nuclear Safety Inspectorate, referred to in the source by the acronym IFSN, emphasizes the same absence of leaks.

At present, the plant remains disconnected. Operations will resume as soon as IFSN gives the go-ahead: the text thus separates the current technical status from the subsequent restart decision.

The technical point to distinguish

The report brings together three aspects that should not be confused: the electrical anomaly indicated as the cause, the plant’s automatic response and the safety check. The drop in voltage concerns the system that supplies power to the control rods; the shutdown is the response recorded by the plant; the proper operation of the safety systems, on the other hand, concerns compliance with protocols. The source adds a fourth, radiological element: no release of radioactive material.

This distinction makes the information easier to interpret without turning it into a judgment different from the one reported. The automatic shutdown is a change in the plant’s operating status. It is not described as a radioactive release, and the source explicitly says that the plant is currently safe. At the same time, the plant is not operational: it is disconnected and awaiting IFSN approval. These are therefore two compatible pieces of information that should be kept separate: declared safety and operations still suspended.
