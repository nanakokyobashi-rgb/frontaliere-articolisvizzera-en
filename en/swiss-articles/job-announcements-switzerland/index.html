Operational details

The Swiss tax system is complex and differs from canton to canton. The federal direct tax (IFD) is a tax that applies to all Swiss citizens, while cantonal and municipal taxes vary depending on where you live. Municipalities apply a multiplier on cantonal taxes, which means that the total taxes paid by residents are higher than those paid by non-residents of that municipality.

According to the Federal Tax Act (LIFD), direct federal tax is 4.5% of annual income, while cantonal and municipal taxes can range from 4.4% to 13.2% and 0% to 22.3% respectively. For example, in the canton of Zurich, the cantonal tax is 10.8% and the municipal multiplier is 1.4, which means that a resident of Zurich will pay a total of 15.1% on their income.

The municipal multiplier can vary greatly from one municipality to another. According to 2022 data, the municipality of Geneva has a multiplier of 1.3, while the municipality of Bern has a multiplier of 1.1. This means that a resident of Geneva will pay a total of 14.4% on their income, while a resident of Bern will pay a total of 12.4%.

It is important to note that Swiss taxes are very low compared to other European countries. According to 2022 data, Switzerland has one of the lowest taxes in the European Union, with a total incidence of 9.3% on income.
