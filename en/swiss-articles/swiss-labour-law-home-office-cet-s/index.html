BERN - The competent committee of the Council of States has aligned itself with the National Council in supporting more flexible rules for working from home. Its support does not, however, extend to the entire bill: it wants to amend the provisions concerning disconnection and Sunday work.

The bill under discussion

A year ago, at the end of a heated debate, the People's Chamber had increased from 14 to 17 hours the maximum window within which the working day may be carried out. The minimum rest period, however, should be reduced from 11 to 9 hours. The National Council's text also provided for an explicit right to disconnect for all employees, not limited to those who work from home or outside their company.

CET-S, that is, the Economic Affairs and Taxation Committee of the Council of States, intends to remove the right to disconnect from the bill. According to the prevailing view in the committee, this protection would be guaranteed even without an explicit statutory provision.

Sunday work also divides the two chambers. The National Council intends to allow, without authorization, a maximum of five hours of work on no more than nine Sundays per year. The committee of the Council of States, on the other hand, proposes deleting the provision entirely, as a compromise, leaving the current restrictions in force.

The bill received 7 votes in favor, 2 against and 3 abstentions from CET-S in the overall vote. The Council of States will examine it during the winter session. In the People's Chamber, the groups of the Swiss People's Party, The Centre, the Radical Liberals and the Green Liberals had voted in favor.

For the majority, the bill updates labor legislation to current conditions while maintaining health protections. The Social Democratic Party and the Greens, on the other hand, fear a deterioration in working conditions, weaker health protection, an unnecessary increase in bureaucracy and insufficient consideration for solutions agreed between the social partners and for those specific to individual sectors.

The comparison shows that CET-S does not reject the idea of making working from home less strictly regulated. Instead, it intervenes on two specific elements of the text approved by the National Council: the explicit wording of the right to disconnect and the opening to Sunday work without authorization.

For an employee, the first difference concerns how the safeguard is presented. In the National Council's text, the right to disconnect would be set out in writing and would apply to everyone, not only to those who work from home or outside their own company. Frequently Asked Questions
What changes to the work-from-home schedule does the proposal put forward?
The proposal, supported by the National Council and CET-S, provides for extending the maximum time span within which a working day may be carried out, increasing it from 14 to 17 hours. At the same time, the minimum rest period between two working days should be reduced from eleven to nine hours.
What will happen to the right to disconnect?
While the National Council had provided for an explicit right to disconnect for all employees (not only those who work from home or outside the company), the Economic Affairs and Taxation Committee of the Council of States (CET-S) intends to delete this provision. The committee majority believes that this right is already guaranteed even without an explicit statutory provision.
What is CET-S's position on Sunday work?
The National Council proposed allowing work without authorization for a maximum of five hours, on up to nine Sundays a year. The CET-S instead proposes eliminating this provision entirely to maintain the current restrictions on Sunday work, thereby seeking a compromise.
