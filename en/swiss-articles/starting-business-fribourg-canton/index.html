Operational details

The practical analysis of starting a business in the Canton of Freiburg highlights the importance of balancing initial operating costs with business growth prospects. The choice between a sole proprietorship and a limited company involves substantial differences in the taxation and protection of personal assets. While in the sole proprietorship the owner responds unlimitedly with his own assets, in joint-stock companies liability is limited to the share capital contributed. From the point of view of social security, a self-employed entrepreneur must personally make the AVS/AI/IPG payments, whose overall rate is a fixed component of labour costs, and assess membership in the LPP to supplement occupational social security. It is advisable to constantly monitor the cost of living and the incidence of LAMal premiums, which, despite being a private insurance for residents, represents a constant financial burden to be included in the budget plan. VAT management, if applicable, requires careful bookkeeping, as failure to meet deadlines to the AFC may result in default interest. Key points

The procedure for starting a business in Fribourg follows a standardized path that requires precision and adherence to bureaucratic timelines. After defining the business plan, the first operational step is verifying the availability of the company name with the Commercial Registry. Subsequently, it is necessary to draft the articles of association, in the case of a company, and proceed with the deposit of capital at a bank, obtaining the documentation required for registration. Once registered, the company receives a certificate attesting to its legal existence. In parallel, it is essential to register with the competent OASI compensation fund to regularize the social security status of the owners and, if applicable, the employees. Regarding personnel management, if the business involves hiring staff, the employer must proceed with affiliation to an LPP pension fund, calculating contributions based on the age brackets provided for by federal regulations. Please note that there is no federal minimum wage, but it is necessary to comply with collective labor agreements (CLA) or standard employment contracts (SEC) if they exist in the relevant sector. As for accident insurance (UVG), it is mandatory for all employees and must be taken out with an authorized institution. Daily business management must also include taking out appropriate professional insurance policies, which, while not always legally mandatory, protect the company from significant financial risks. Finally, it is recommended to periodically consult official guides to stay updated on legislative changes that could affect company management. For proper management of business and personal finances, you can consult the salary calculator to simulate the tax impact of business income and plan social security outflows. Following these steps methodically allows you to focus on developing your business, minimizing the risks associated with bureaucratic errors or administrative oversights that could compromise the stability of the business over time.

Frequently Asked Questions
When is registration in the Trade Register mandatory for a sole proprietorship?
For sole proprietorships in the Canton of Freiburg, registration in the Commercial Register becomes mandatory if a federally defined annual turnover threshold of CHF 100,000 is exceeded. For joint-stock companies, such as SA or Sagl, registration is always necessary to confer legal personality on the company and requires the payment of the minimum capital to a restricted bank account before formalisation.
What are the social security obligations for a self-employed entrepreneur?
A self-employed entrepreneur must personally provide the payments for the AVS, AI and IPG, which constitute a significant share of the salary contributions. It is also essential to evaluate adherence to the LPP to supplement occupational pensions. For employees, membership in an LPP pension fund and taking out accident insurance (LAINF) with an authorised body are mandatory obligations for the employer.
How does the choice of the operational location affect tax costs?
Taxation in Switzerland and the Canton of Freiburg is divided into three levels: federal, cantonal and municipal. Since each municipality applies its own multiplier on the cantonal tax, the choice of the operational headquarters is a determining factor in the management of the company's fixed costs. Rigorous tax planning, supported by up-to-date accounting, makes it possible to optimise the tax burden and ensure the long-term sustainability of the business.
