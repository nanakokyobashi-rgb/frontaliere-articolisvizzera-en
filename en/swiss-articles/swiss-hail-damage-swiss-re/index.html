On August 28, 2026, a violent thunderstorm hit Switzerland with strong gusts of wind and hail. In some locations, the hailstones reached a diameter of up to five centimeters. The cantons identified as the hardest hit are Bern, Solothurn, Aargau, Zurich and Thurgau.

According to Swiss Re, insured losses related to the hailstorm at the end of August amount to approximately 1,7 billion francs. Leopold Camara, Swiss Re’s head of reinsurance for Northern, Central and Eastern Europe, presented the estimate during a press conference held in Baden-Baden as part of the reinsurers’ industry meeting.

The geographical scale of the damage

The relatively high population density of the affected area contributed to the scale of the estimated losses, Camara explained. The Swiss Re executive also outlined a worst-case scenario, noting that the same event in Zurich would probably have caused an even greater loss.

The figure distinguishes total insured damage from the motor vehicle component. The first figure describes the event as a whole; the second shows the weight of automobiles in the estimate. The prevention measures indicated by the group include warnings issued as promptly as possible and access to covered parking.

Swiss Re estimates approximately 800 million francs in hail damage to motor vehicles. The group’s executives note that a single extreme weather event can affect thousands of vehicles.

The reinsurer does not consider the Swiss case isolated. According to the source, hailstorms that hit northern Italy in July and August 2026 caused extensive damage to vehicles. Swiss Re also observes that extreme weather is becoming a driving factor behind catastrophic events in Europe as well.
