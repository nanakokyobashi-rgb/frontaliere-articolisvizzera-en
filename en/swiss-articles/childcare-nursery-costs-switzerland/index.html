Child care in Switzerland is a central issue for families where both parents work. The system is divided into three levels: nursery schools, daytime families (tagesmütter) and after-school facilities, each with its own characteristics of cost and availability.

The responsibility for financing is shared between public bodies and families. The cantons and municipalities, through public budgets, cover a portion of the management costs of the facilities. Families pay fees proportional to their income in many cantons, while others apply fixed rates. The cantons also provide direct subsidies to families who meet certain income thresholds.

The custody structure in Switzerland

Nurseries are public or private facilities authorized by the cantonal authorities. Day families offer care in home environments, often with greater time flexibility. The after-school club, active after school hours, serves compulsory school children. Each canton regulates access through public registrations and guarantees, where possible, a quota of places in municipal facilities.

The quality of the standards is ensured by federal safety and hygiene regulations, applied uniformly throughout the territory. Nursery schools managed by municipalities or public bodies benefit from direct funding from cantonal and municipal budgets. Private ones sometimes receive partial contributions, but are self-financed mainly through family fees.

A decisive aspect is the method of calculating the tuition fee. Many cantons adopt a formula based on the family's gross income, with the tuition fee increasing proportionally to earnings. This balance aims to guarantee accessibility for low-income families, maintaining a fair contribution from those with greater economic capacity. Some cantons maintain fixed rates, relying entirely on subsidies to support struggling families.

Cantonal subsidies are assigned based on an assessment of income and family composition. The income thresholds vary from canton to canton, as does the maximum amount of the subsidy. This reflects the fiscal and social priorities of each territory, as well as budget availability. Custody therefore represents one of the relevant factors when families with children choose where to live and work in Switzerland.

Operational details

The choice of childcare services has significant implications on the family budget and the ability to participate in the labor market. For a family where both parents are employed, the cost of childcare directly affects disposable income and, consequently, effective taxation at federal, cantonal and municipal levels.

How costs affect disposable income

Let's assume a family with a gross monthly income of CHF 10,000. If the caretaker fee absorbs CHF 2,000 per month, the disposable income is reduced to CHF 8,000 even before taxation. After the application of direct federal tax, cantonal and municipal taxes, the net income is further reduced. Cantonal subsidies and tax deductions operate as compensation mechanisms, reducing the cost perceived by the family.

The calculation of the fee based on income allows the contribution to be calibrated according to economic capacity. A family with a gross income of CHF 5,000 per month will pay a lower fee than one with CHF 15,000, consistently with the principle of progressivity. It is crucial to understand that the fee is not a federal, cantonal or municipal tax, but rather a payment to the facility manager.

Impact on Social Security

An often overlooked aspect is the effect on social security contributions. When a parent is absent from work for parental leave, the period of contributions to the AVS (old age and survivors insurance) and the LPP (pension fund) is covered by the Confederation and the employer according to federal laws. However, the taxable income is reduced, which may influence the future amount of the occupational pension (LPP) and the basis for calculating AVS benefits (if subsidiary).

Storage costs are deductible in the federal, cantonal and municipal tax returns. The deduction reduces your tax base, lowering the total amount of taxes you owe. The effect is proportional to the marginal rate applicable to the household: families with higher incomes benefit from a greater percentage reduction in terms of fiscal savings.

Key points

To access custody services in your canton, you must follow a standard procedure that varies slightly from territory to territory, but follows common principles at a national level.

Step-by-step to find and request custody

Step 1: Identify available facilities. Each canton maintains a public register of authorized nurseries and day care homes. The website of the cantonal administration (usually the Social Division or the Family Office) provides a list with contact details, opening hours, availability of places and indicative prices. It is advisable to contact several facilities at the same time, as waiting lists are often long.

Step 2: Check the admission requirements. Many cantons require residence in the territory and a current employment contract. Some assign places by order of registration, others by priority (single-parent families, income, fragile situations).

Step 3: Prepare the documentation. The cantonal authorities will require the tax declaration (Confederation Form 1, cantonal declaration) for the previous two years, sometimes also bank details to verify the ability to pay. You also need the insured number (AVS number) of all family members to correctly identify the family unit and calculate the benefits.

Step 4: Fill out the grant application. If your income is lower than the cantonal thresholds, you can submit an application at the same time as registration. The request must be forwarded to the responsible municipal office (often the Social or Family Benefits Office), providing the income and composition documentation of the household.

Step 5: Calculate the final cost. Once the assignment of the nursery school and the payment of the subsidy have been received, the monthly fee is calculated according to the cantonal formula. It is advisable to verify the calculation by receiving a confirmation letter with amount, validity period and payment method.

Tax deductions and important deadlines

Custody expenses must be documented: monthly fee receipts, invoices, manager's details. When completing the tax return (usually by March 31st of the following year), they must be indicated in the "various deductions" or "childcare expenses" section, according to the cantonal instructions. The tax administration automatically applies the tax benefit during processing.

Some cantons offer increased deductions if both parents work, or if the income is low. It is advisable to consult the cantonal tax administration website to clarify the applicable details. Frequently Asked Questions
What are the different types of cases available in Switzerland?
In Switzerland, childcare is divided into three main options: kindergartens (public or private collective facilities), day families or tagesmütter (care at the home of a licensed caregiver), and after-school (after-school services for children aged 5-6 years and older). Each type has different hours, costs and availability. Nurseries offer more pedagogical structuring; day families allow more flexibility; after-school supports families where both parents work full-time.
How is the custody fee calculated in the cantons?
Many cantons calculate the fee based on the household's gross income. The higher the income, the higher the tuition; low-income households pay proportionately lower amounts. Some cantons have flat rates. The calculations take into account the composition of the nucleus (number of children, marital status). The fee is not a tax, but a direct payment to the property manager (public or private).
How to apply for a cantonal custody allowance?
A request is submitted to the municipal office in charge (Family Office or Social Benefits). The tax return for the last two years, the AVS number and a specific cantonal form are required. Subsidies are subject to income thresholds that vary from canton to canton. The answer may come within 2-4 weeks. It is advisable to submit the application at the same time as registering for kindergarten.
Can I deduct custody fees from taxes?
Yes, many cantons and the Confederation allow documented childcare expenses to be deducted from the tax base in federal, cantonal and municipal tax. The expenses must be indicated on the tax return by the deadline (usually 31 March of the following year). The tax economy depends on the marginal rate applicable to your income.
How soon do I have to enroll my child in a kindergarten?
Waiting lists can be long, often 6-12 months. It is advisable to register as soon as you decide to use external custody, even if the child will not start immediately. Many cantons operate common online waiting lists. Contact the municipal office (Family Office) to find out about the procedure and specific times in your area.
