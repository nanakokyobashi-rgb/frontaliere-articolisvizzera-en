Child Custody in Switzerland: Facilities, Fees and Subsidies (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 30 August 2026

How kindergartens work, childcare fees and subsidies. Financing models and tax deductions.

Context

In a nutshell

  • In Switzerland custody is co-financed by parents, cantons and municipalities
  • Many cantons offer subsidies based on parental income
  • Rates vary significantly between cantons and property types
  • Some costs are deductible from the tax return

Key facts

  • What: Public-private funding of childcare in Switzerland
  • Where: Cantons and municipalities (different systems per canton)
  • Who: Parents, cantons, municipalities, Confederation
  • Facilities: Kindergartens, day families, after-school
  • Rates: Calculated on income or flat rates, vary by canton

Childcare in Switzerland is a multi-stakeholder co-funded service. Parents pay direct rates generally calculated on the basis of family income. The cantons and municipalities, together with the Confederation, provide contributions and subsidies to reduce costs, especially for low- and middle-income families. This model allows families, especially mothers, to participate in the mercato del lavoro svizzero while their children receive professional care.

Custody facilities vary: kindergartens for children aged a few months to three years, day families that welcome small groups of children into a home environment, and after-school services for school-age children. Each canton organises its own system, establishing different criteria for subsidies, maximum tariffs and access criteria.

Operational details

Income-based subsidies and fees

In many Swiss cantons, the subsidy system is organised according to the principle of progressivity: lower-income families benefit from significant reductions, while high-income families pay full fees. This mechanism is similar to that of health insurance (LAMal/KVG), where cantons provide premium subsidies to reduce the burden on vulnerable families.

The cantons manage subsidies according to their own rules. Some set a maximum percentage of income earmarked for custody (e.g. 10-15%), others offer lump sums that vary by income bracket. To access the subsidies, parents must generally submit tax documentation to the competent cantonal administration, similar to what happens with deductions in federal, cantonal and municipal taxes, which represent the three levels of Swiss taxation.

Single-parent families often have access to higher subsidies, recognizing the greater economic and organizational difficulty. Parents with very low incomes or who are unemployed can access additional support programmes, coordinated between cantons, municipalities and social services.

Key points

How to find facilities and apply for subsidies

Parents looking for childcare in a given canton must contact the municipal administration, which maintains registers of authorised facilities, both public and private. Many cantons and municipalities offer online portals where available facilities, schedules, and contacts can be searched.

To apply for subsidies, parents fill in an application at the relevant office (municipality or canton, depending on the local organisation), attaching income declarations, employment certificates, and sometimes documentation on the chosen facility. Processing times vary: generally between a few weeks and a couple of months.

Required documents and deadlines

There is no single federal deadline: each canton sets its own terms, usually indicated in the notice or in the application form available online. It is advisable to start the search and the paperwork at least 2-3 months before the child's entry into a facility, to allow the subsidy application to be processed and the place to be confirmed.

Parents living in Switzerland and working abroad must check with the local administration whether their foreign income is taken into account in the subsidy calculation. Similarly, it is important to check whether health insurance premiums (LAMal/KVG) affect the calculation, as some cantonal systems consider net income after standard deductions.

Frequently Asked Questions
How is the custody allowance calculated in Switzerland?
Calculation varies by canton. It is generally based on the family's annual income bracket. Parents file a tax return with the local government, which applies cantonal formulas to determine the percentage or amount of the subsidy. Some cantons use a progressive system: the lower the income, the higher the subsidy. Check with your canton for specific criteria.
What is the difference between kindergarten, daytime family and after-school?
Nursery welcomes children from 3 months to 4 years with qualified staff in dedicated facilities; daytime family is the service at a private home with 3-5 children and a qualified person; after-school is for school children (5-6 years old and up), before and after school. Costs, hours, and availability vary significantly between the three options.
Are custody costs tax deductible?
In many cantons, yes, but the rules differ. Some allow full deduction, others partial or none at all. Check the cantonal declaration instructions. Federal family allowances can also supplement the family budget for children. Check with your local tax authority.
What to do if income changes during the year?
If your income changes significantly (job change, unemployment, salary increase), you can request a review of your benefit application. The cantonal administration generally considers declared income, but some realities allow adjustments during the year. Please contact the relevant office for details on the procedure.
