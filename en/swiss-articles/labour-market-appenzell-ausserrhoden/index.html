In Switzerland, there is no federal minimum wage. However, the situation varies significantly from canton to canton: some have introduced their own local regulations, reflecting the specific cost of living in each region. The salary structure depends on multiple factors: the worker's level of education, the sector of activity, professional experience, and especially the canton of work.

Regarding education, salary levels generally follow a progression: those with only lower secondary education earn less than those who have completed an apprenticeship (AFC), who in turn earn less than those with tertiary education (university, polytechnic, higher specialized school). This progression is not rigid but represents a general trend in the Swiss job market.

Operational details

How are gross and net salaries calculated? A gross salary is the total amount agreed upon between the employer and the employee, from which social security contributions and taxes are deducted. The employee contributes 5.3% of the gross salary to AVS/AI/IPG (old-age, disability, and loss of earnings insurance), plus 1.1% up to an annual maximum for unemployment insurance (AD/AC), and 0.7–1.5% for accident insurance (LAINF/LAA), the amount of which varies by sector. The employer also pays their own 50% of the AVS/AI/IPG contributions.

Key points

How to find out about salaries in your sector and canton? The first step is to consult official sources. The Federal Statistical Office (UST/BFS) regularly publishes data on median salaries by sector, level of education, and region. The State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) provides information on federal labor regulations, including details on working hours, vacations, and protections. The competent cantonal administration (labor office or human resources department) has data on local average salaries and any cantonal minimum wages.

Is there a federal minimum wage in Switzerland?
No, Switzerland does not have a minimum wage at the federal level. However, several cantons have adopted their own local minimum wage regulations to protect employees. In addition, many sectors operate under collective agreements (CCS) negotiated between trade unions and employers' associations, which establish binding sectoral minimum wages. How do salaries vary by level of education in Switzerland?
Salaries vary significantly based on education level. Those who have only lower secondary school generally earn less than those who have completed an apprenticeship (AFC). A graduate in tertiary education (university, polytechnic, specialized higher school) receives higher salaries. The differences also reflect professional experience: a new hire receives less than someone with years of experience in the same role. What are the maximum working hours and minimum holidays in Switzerland?
The Federal Labor Law sets a maximum time of 45 hours per week for industry, offices and sales. Other sectors may require up to 50 hours. Minimum vacation is four weeks a year for employees, five for those under 20. Maternity covers fourteen weeks at 80% through loss of earnings allowance (IPG), while paternity provides for two weeks. Collective agreements may provide for even more favourable conditions than these legal minimums.
How do I check if my salary is fair in my canton?
Consult the data published by the Federal Statistical Office (FSO/BFS), which publishes median wages by sector, training and region. Compare your gross salary with the average of your canton and professional sector. If you work in an industry covered by a collective bargaining agreement (CCS), your salary must not be less than the minimum under the contract.
