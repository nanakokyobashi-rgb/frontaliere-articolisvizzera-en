2027 electricity bill drop: -4% in Switzerland (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 September 2026

In 2027 electricity rates fall by 4% according to Elcom. A typical household saves CHF 53 per year, but regional variations remain significant.

Context

In brief

  • Rates down 4% in 2027, average CHF 53 annual savings per household
  • A household consuming 4,500 kWh will pay CHF 26.45/kWh in 2027
  • Elcom announced the new rates on Tuesday; they vary between municipalities and network operators
  • Check online your network operator's rate by end of August

Key facts

  • What: General decline in electricity rates in Switzerland
  • When: Starting January 1, 2027
  • Where: Entire Switzerland (variations by canton and network operator)
  • Who: Elcom (Swiss Federal Electricity Commission)
  • Percentage: -4% national average
  • Annual savings: CHF 53 for a typical household
  • Reference consumption: 4,500 kWh/year
  • Average 2027 price: CHF 26.45/kWh

The Swiss Federal Electricity Commission (Elcom), Switzerland's regulatory authority, announced on Tuesday the electricity price verdict for 2027: an average 4 percent decrease compared to 2026. In concrete terms, a household consuming 4,500 kilowatt-hours per year will pay 26.45 cents per kilowatt-hour, which is 1.2 cents less than the current year, for an annual total of 1,194 francs. This decline translates to savings of approximately 53 francs on year-end electricity bills.

This decrease comes after a period of pronounced volatility. In 2026, compared to 2025, the rate had already fallen 4 percent. In 2025, the decline was even steeper, at 10 percent. These two years of reduction have partially offset the significant increases recorded in 2023 and 2024, when shocks in European energy markets had substantially raised electricity costs for Swiss consumers. Rates have now stabilized at an elevated level, but the trend is clearly downward.

Operational details

What Changes in the Bill Structure

The electricity bill consists of three elements: the energy tariff (the price of gross kWh), network costs (transport and distribution), and the metering tariff (meter management). In 2027, the energy component will decrease thanks to more favorable prices on global markets, but network costs will continue to rise. This phenomenon is not accidental: network operators are investing in stabilization infrastructure (transition to distributed photovoltaic, storage, smart grid) and these costs are gradually passed on to customers.

For families and small businesses, the crucial point is that the net balance 2026→2027 is positive: they will pay less than before, even if the margins of decline are narrowing. Prospects for 2028 and beyond will depend on geopolitical developments and European energy prices.

The Trend from 2023 to 2027

Historical analysis clearly shows the path of Swiss consumers in recent years. In 2023 and 2024, increases were significant due to the European energy crisis and market volatility. In 2025, the first major decline of 10 percent marked the beginning of stabilization. In 2026, the decline solidified at 4 percent, and the same will continue in 2027. Elcom emphasizes that tariffs have now stabilized at a high level compared to the pre-2022 period, which means that although consumers pay less this year, they remain in a more expensive range compared to the recent past.

Key points

How to check your provider's rates

If you live in Switzerland and want to know exactly what rate you'll pay in 2027, the process is simple and straightforward:

1. Access the official website — Visit the Elcom electricity price website (or the equivalent cantonal platform) 2. Enter your postal code and municipality — The system identifies the network operator responsible for your area 3. Select your provider — If multiple operators work in your municipality, choose the correct one from the menu 4. Check the 2027 rate — The price in cents per kWh will already be displayed (by end of August 2026) 5. Compare with the average — Compare your data with the 26.45 cents/kWh national average to understand if you're in a high-cost area

This verification is also useful for those living in cantons with partial market liberalization (Zurich, Basel, St. Gallen, Geneva, Thurgau). In some regions it's possible to change energy supplier while staying with the same network operator; in other cantons the network operator remains a monopoly.

When the new rates come into effect

The 2027 rates come into effect on January 1, 2027. Operators will communicate the details in the end-of-2026 bill or early 2027, with comparison tables for 2026 vs 2027. If you notice an abnormal increase compared to the national average, verify if your operator works in a region with significantly higher network costs.

Frequently Asked Questions
How much will the bill fall in 2027 compared to 2026?
On average 4 percent. For a household consuming 4500 kWh per year, the savings are around CHF 53, paying 26.45 cents per kWh in 2027 instead of 27.65 cents in 2026. Caution: Actual savings depend on your municipality and network operator, as rates vary considerably from region to region.
Are electricity rates the same throughout Switzerland?
No. Elcom provides a national average, but the actual price varies from operator to operator and from municipality to municipality. The differences depend on the energy supply strategy: some municipalities rely mainly on local hydropower (more stable), others buy on the European wholesale market (more volatile). Check the Elcom website to see the specific rate for your municipality.
When will the new 2027 rates go into effect?
January 1, 2027. The approximately 580 Swiss network operators were obliged to communicate their 2027 tariffs to Elcom and customers by the end of August 2026. Full details will be included in the bill at the end of 2026 or beginning of 2027.
Why do prices fall if network costs increase?
The bill consists of three elements: energy tariff, network costs and metering tariff. In 2027 the energy tariff will fall significantly (thanks to lower prices on global markets), while grid and metering costs will rise. The net balance remains positive (down 4 percent), but the trend shows that grid increases will continue in the coming years to finance the energy transition.
How do I check my network operator's rate?
Go to Elcom's official electricity pricing website, enter your zip code and municipality of residence, select your network operator from the menu (if thereis more than one in your area) and see the 2027 rate in cents per kWh. This compares to the national average of 26.45 cents/kWh.
