The Swiss tax framework consists of three levels: federal direct tax, cantonal tax, and municipal tax. This framework serves as the starting point for the 2026 comparison among the cantons and for analyzing the case of the Canton of St. Gallen without confusing different components.

The Structure to Be Compared

Each canton has its own law and its own multiplier. The municipality applies its own multiplier to the cantonal tax. The cantonal figure, therefore, must be accompanied by the municipal component: looking only at a cantonal percentage does not reflect the full local level considered in the comparison.

In the case of St. Gallen, the practical question is not merely what the cantonal tax rate is, but how to contextualize that figure across the three levels. The comparison with other cantons must maintain the same distinction: federal direct income tax (IFD) on one hand, and cantonal and municipal taxes on the other. Only in this way can the reader avoid mistaking a national figure for a measure defined by the canton, or a municipal multiplier for an independent tax rate.

Jurisdictions help verify the documents. The FTA/ESTV administers the federal income tax and VAT. Cantonal administrations handle cantonal and municipal taxes. UFAS/BSV oversees social security, including AVS/AI/LPP, but does not set taxes. UST/BFS produces statistics and does not set tax rates.

This institutional map is also useful when opening the dichiarazione delle imposte file. Before comparing St. Gallen with another canton, one must understand which tax level is reported and which portion depends on the municipality. The key to the 2026 comparison is the breakdown, not the search for a single number.

A clear comparison table should list the federal, cantonal, and municipal levels separately. For St. Gallen, as for the other cantons, the name of the jurisdiction alone is not sufficient to describe the tax burden if the municipal multiplier is missing. This same rule also makes the data comparable at the national level.

Operational details

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the comparison becomes useful when you avoid confusing the tax bill with other mandatory expenditure. The cantonal tax is a component of the budget; AHV/AHV, BVG and KVG and KVG follow different logics. The cost of living and any cantonal minimum wage are also not synonymous with the tax rate. There is no minimum wage at the federal level; some cantons have their own.

The KVG follows a different logic: for residents it is mandatory, must be taken out within 3 months of arrival and has premiums that vary by canton and region. The premium reduction is a cantonal subsidy. The deductibles for adults are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500. The premium is not a tax and is not a salary contribution.

For the canton of St. Gallen, comparing with the other cantons therefore means constructing at least two readings. The first concerns taxation, separated into three levels; the second sorts the items that affect the budget but are of a social, insurance or salary nature. Key points

The safest way to use the 2026 comparison is to turn it into a verification worksheet. Start with the relevant authority and update each indexed figure annually, rather than treating all amounts as if they were fixed.

Four Practical Steps

1. Identify the tax jurisdiction. For an audit in the canton of St. Gallen, note the canton and the relevant municipality. Keep the cantonal law and the municipal multiplier separate, because the latter applies to the cantonal tax.

2. Separate the three taxes. Record the federal income tax (IFD), cantonal tax, and municipal tax in separate categories. For IFD and VAT, refer to the AFC/ESTV; for the cantonal and municipal components, consult the cantonal administrations.

3. Exclude other deductions from the tax comparison. AVS/AHV, AD/AC, LAINF/LAA, and LPP/BVG pertain to contributions or social security. LAMal/KVG is mandatory for residents, has a per-capita premium, and is neither a tax nor a payroll contribution. Anyone arriving in Switzerland must take out LAMal coverage within 3 months of arrival.

4. Check the year of the figures. The third-pillar (3a) contribution limit, the LPP coordination threshold or deduction, the AD/AC limit, average LAMal premiums, and the median wage are indexed annually. If the value for 2026 has not been verified, do not replace it with a specific amount: consult the updated official source.

This procedure avoids two common errors: attributing to the FSO (UST/BFS) the task of setting tax rates, or using social security data to describe cantonal taxes. It also helps in reading a pay stub without confusing taxes, contributions, and insurance premiums; a guide for busta paga svizzera is available for this step.

The final worksheet should retain the name of the canton, the municipality, the three tax levels, and the verification date. This way, the comparison between St. Gallen and the other cantons remains updatable without mixing values that belong to different institutions or systems. Frequently Asked Questions
What are the three tax levels to compare in Switzerland?
In Switzerland, the comparison starts from three levels: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. Each canton has its own law and multiplier; the municipality applies the multiplier on the cantonal tax. For the canton of St. Gallen, as for the other cantons, the municipal data must therefore be read together with the cantonal data and separate from the IFD.
What is the role of the FTA/ESTV, cantonal administrations, FSIO/BSV and FSO/BFS?
FTA/ESTV deals with IFD and VAT. The cantonal administrations are responsible for cantonal and municipal tax. FSIO/BSV is responsible for social security, including AHV/IV/BVG, but does not set taxes. FSO/BFS produces statistics and does not set rates. Distinguishing between institutions avoids using statistics or pension data as if it were a tax percentage.
KVG/KVG is covered by the cantonal tax?
No. For residents, the KVG is compulsory and must be concluded within 3 months of arrival. It is financed by per capita premiums that vary by canton and region, with premium reductions as a cantonal subsidy. It is not a tax and it is not a salary contribution. The adult deductibles are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500.
Which 2026 figures need to be checked every year?
The third pillar 3a ceiling, the BVG coordination threshold or deduction, the AD/AC ceiling, the average KVG premiums and the median salary must be checked from an official source. These figures are indexed annually; if the 2026 amount is not verified, it should not be replaced with a precise figure.
