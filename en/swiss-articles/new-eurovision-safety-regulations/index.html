Eurovision: new rules on safety and age of artists (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 August 2026

The SRU updates the rules of the contest: stop the host nation in case of war and raise the minimum age to 18 years.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Exclusion from the headquarters for countries at war or with geopolitical tensions
  • Minimum age of participants from 16 to 18 years old
  • Updated regulations in view of the 2027 edition

Key facts

  • What: Updating the Eurovision Song Contest rules
  • When: Officially announced on Wednesday
  • Where: Headquarters of the European Broadcasting Union (EBU)
  • Who: European Broadcasting Union (EBU)
  • Minimum age: 18 years

The European Broadcasting Union (EBU) has officially announced the update of the regulation governing the organisation and conduct of the Eurovision Song Contest. The new provisions, announced on Wednesday, introduce stringent constraints regarding the selection of the host nation and the personal requirements of the competing singers. These changes were introduced in view of the 2027 edition of the music festival. The body clarified that the winning country will no longer have the automatic certainty of being able to host the event the following year if conditions of instability were to exist in its territory.

# Host Nation Exclusion Criteria

The official note of the EBU states that the winning broadcaster will be automatically excluded from the possibility of hosting the competition if an armed conflict, a sensitive geopolitical situation or any other situation significantly affects the security, safety or stability of the State or the region

Operational details

Protection of young artists

In addition to the rules on territorial security, the EBU has introduced a substantial change regarding the minimum age of competing artists. Starting from the next events, the singers must be at least 18 years old to be able to step on the Eurovision stage. Previously, the minimum age limit was set at 16 years. This regulatory change has the declared objective of strengthening protection measures for younger artists, protecting them from the strong psychological and professional pressures that characterize participation in an event of such global media resonance. The protection of minors and young adults has become, according to the organization, a focal point for the organization, which seeks to balance the needs of the show with ethical responsibility towards its participants.

The impact of this decision must be analysed in the context of a sector, that of entertainment, which in Switzerland and Europe is governed by strict rules on labour law and child protection. Although Eurovision is not an employment contract under Swiss public law, raising the age threshold aligns the competition to higher maturity standards. Key points

Procedures and future participation

For those who follow the evolution of the European cultural and media landscape, it is essential to understand how these EBU decisions affect member countries' participation. The adoption of a more restrictive regulation in terms of security and demographic requirements shifts the planning axis towards greater caution. Broadcasters wishing to apply to host Eurovision must now integrate risk assessments that go beyond purely logistical or infrastructural aspects into their feasibility analyses, including geopolitical stability parameters. This transformation in the way the major European television event is conceived indicates a trend towards a standardisation of security protocols that, although not directly related to tax calculations or minimum wages, defines the perimeter within which public broadcasters operate at a transnational level.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the new age limit for participating in Eurovision?
The regulation has been updated and now requires all participating artists to be at least 18 years old. Previously, the minimum age limit was set at 16 years. The change aims to protect young artists from pressures related to participation in the competition.
What happens if the winning country is in a state of war?
Under the new EBU regulation, the winning broadcaster will automatically be excluded from hosting the competition if an armed conflict, sensitive geopolitical situation or other situation compromises the security, safety or stability of the state or region.
Who decides if a country is eligible to host the event?
The decision rests with the European Broadcasting Union (EBU). The organisation has the right to commission an independent assessment of the situation to determine if the necessary security and stability conditions exist to proceed with the organisation of the event in the winning country.
