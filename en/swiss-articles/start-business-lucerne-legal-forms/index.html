Register with the trade register

Every activity started in Switzerland must be registered in the trade register of its canton. In the Canton of Lucerne, as in every canton, this registration is mandatory for sole proprietorships, partnerships, joint-stock companies, cooperatives and associations operating for economic purposes. Those who intend to start a business must first choose the most suitable legal form, then proceed with registration in the register (paying cantonal taxes), and finally comply with federal and cantonal insurance obligations: AVS/AI contributions, health insurance (LAMal), accident damage insurance (LAINF if applicable), and occupational pension (LPP if employees). Operational details

Insurance obligations for entrepreneurs and employers

If you open a business as an independent contractor (sole proprietorship, no employees), you must comply with specific federal obligations. You are automatically enrolled in the AVS/AI (old age, disability and death insurance): you contribute about 10.6% of your net income (50-50 split between employee and employer share, but you pay the full amount). Health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory for all residents in Switzerland: you must choose a sickness fund within 3 months of starting business and pay monthly premiums at your expense (premiums vary by canton, region, age and choice of deductible). If your business income exceeds CHF 9'100 per annum, you are also obliged to take out accident damage insurance (LAINF); the rate varies depending on the business sector (0.7%–1.5%). Occupational pension (LPP) is optional if self-employed without employees; however, it is recommended to supplement the AVS pension.

If you hire employees, your obligations increase significantly. For each employee, you must pay: AVS/AI contributions (5.3% employee + 5.3% employer, total 10.6% on the salary basis), unemployment insurance (AD/AC: about 1.1% up to the annual ceiling, divided between employee and employer), accident damage insurance (LAINF: 0.7%–1.5% depending on the sector). Key points

Step-by-step: procedure to start a business in Lucerne

Step 1: Choose the legal form

Evaluate which legal form best suits your project. A sole proprietorship is appropriate if you work alone, without partners and with limited capital. A company (GmbH/LLC, AG/Ltd) is suitable if you have partners or investors. A cooperative is ideal if you share a mutual purpose with others. Consult an accountant or a lawyer to analyze tax implications (direct federal tax, VAT, cantonal and municipal taxes) and insurance requirements.

Step 2: Registration in the Lucerne Commercial Register

Contact the commercial register office of the Canton of Lucerne (check the official website of the Canton of Lucerne for address, email, and opening hours). Fill out the official registration form (provided by the office itself). Attach documents according to the legal form: for a sole proprietorship, a copy of the identity document is required; for a company, a copy of the partnership agreement and notarized deed (if required); for a cooperative, the statutes; for an association, the statutes and the list of founders.

Step 3: Payment of fees and receipt of the certificate

Pay the registration fees according to the Lucerne cantonal tariff (inquire directly with the office). Once paid and approved, you will receive a registration certificate — a fundamental document for the next steps.

Step 4: Opening a dedicated bank account

With the registration certificate, visit a Swiss bank and open an account dedicated to the business. The bank will require this certificate as proof of the company's legal existence.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I open a business as a sole proprietorship with no minimum capital?
Yes. The sole proprietorship according to the Swiss Bond Code has no minimum capital requirement. However, you must register with the commercial register of your canton and comply with federal insurance obligations: AVS/AI (contributions about 10.6% of net income), LAMal (compulsory health insurance, within 3 months), LAINF if income > CHF 9'100 per annum.
What are the costs of registering with the Trade Register in Lucerne?
The costs of registering with the trade register vary by canton and municipality. In the Canton of Lucerne, please contact the commercial register office of the Cantonal Administration directly to verify the initial registration fee, any annual maintenance fees, and costs of future changes. Exact costs are available on the official website of the Canton of Lucerne or at the office.
Do I need to take out health insurance if I open a business?
Yes. LAMal (compulsory health insurance) is payable by all residents in Switzerland, regardless of employment status (employee, self-employed, retired, unemployed). You must choose a sick fund within 3 months of starting the business. Premiums vary by canton, region, age and selected deductible. In the Canton of Lucerne, [consulta il comparatore casse](nav:health) to find the best conditions.
If I have employees, what are the social contribution obligations?
You must pay for each employee: AVS/AI (5.3% employee + 5.3% employer, total 10.6%), AD/AC unemployment insurance (about 1.1% up to the annual ceiling, divided between employee and employer), LAINF accident insurance (0.7%–1.5% depending on the sector), and LPP occupational pension (if salary exceeds about CHF 21'330 per year: contribution 7%–18% according to age). I recommend consulting an accountant for exact details.
Can I use a personal bank account for the business?
It is not recommended and often mandatory by law (depending on the legal form and banks) to open an account dedicated to the business. A separate account maintains tax transparency, facilitates tax reporting, allows the bank to verify the legitimacy of the company through the registration certificate, and legally protects the owner.
