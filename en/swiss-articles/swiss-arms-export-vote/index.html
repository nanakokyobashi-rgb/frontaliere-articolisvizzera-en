Arms, easier exports: vote on November 29

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 29 September 2026

The Federal Council supports the revision of the War Material Act: the vote is scheduled for 29 novembre to strengthen industry and jobs.

Context

In brief

  • The vote on the law is scheduled for 29 November.
  • The Federal Council wants to relax export rules.
  • The reform focuses on defence and jobs.
  • The proposal also concerns re-exports.

Key facts

  • Vote: 29 November
  • Subject: revision of the War Material Act
  • Proponent: Federal Council
  • Objective: strengthen the defence industry and safeguard jobs
  • Current rule: ban on exports to countries involved in internal or international conflicts
  • New: greater leeway on exports and re-exports
  • Control: right of veto and retention in the purchasing country if there are reservations
  • Exceptions: exceptional circumstances, with international obligations respected

On 29 November, a vote will be held on the revision of the War Material Act backed by the Federal Council. The Government's call to approve it effectively opens the campaign for the referendum, which the source places exactly two months after the Executive's position. The stated objective is twofold: strengthen the defence industry and safeguard jobs.

The starting point is the regime currently in force. Switzerland bans the export of war material to countries involved in internal or international conflicts. According to Bern, since the start of the war between Russia and Ukraine, this rigidity has made the Swiss arms industry unattractive and subjected it to strong pressure.

The leeway sought by Parliament

The bourgeois majority in Parliament wants to grant the Government greater leeway over exports and re-exports. The operational objective is to facilitate the transfer, by Western countries, of war material purchased in Switzerland to third countries. The proposal therefore concerns exports and re-exports.

The Federal Council should also be given a right of veto. The Government could also require the weapons to remain in the purchasing country when there are reservations about security or neutrality policy. In exceptional circumstances, to protect foreign-policy or security interests, it would be possible to depart from the current authorisation criteria, but only in compliance with international obligations.

The Federal Department of Economic Affairs, Education and Research presents the reform as a way to ensure greater flexibility in foreign policy during exceptional crises and to strengthen the Swiss arms industry as a foundation of national defence. Guy Parmelin, President of the Confederation and economics minister, invoked the guarantee of neutrality before Parliament.

“The new regime will always be applied in strict compliance with Swiss neutrality and international law,” Guy Parmelin stressed before Parliament.

Operational details

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the proposal would primarily change how the Government could assess a sale or re-export. The source describes the new framework as a revision to be put to a vote, not as a rule already applicable. The comparison should therefore be read as a possible before and after, not as a change that has already entered into force.

Before and after

Before, the stated rule is clear: Swiss war materiel cannot be exported to countries involved in internal or international conflicts. If approved, the Federal Council would have greater leeway, but within precise conditions.

| Element | Proposed measure | Control element | | Exports | Greater room for maneuver | Exceptions to the current criteria only in exceptional circumstances | | Re-exports | Facilitate Western transfers to third countries | Federal Council veto right | | Purchased weapons | Possible requirement to remain in the purchasing country | Reservations concerning security or neutrality | | Exceptions | Safeguarding foreign policy or security interests | Compliance with international obligations |

The table does not describe an automatic authorization. The proposal retains a direct role for the Federal Council and links decisions to security, neutrality policy, foreign policy interests and international obligations. The exception is also presented as a tool for exceptional circumstances, not as an ordinary rule.

The stated economic impact concerns the arms sector and jobs. However, the source does not quantify employees, wages, orders, companies involved or jobs that would be safeguarded. It therefore does not allow the political objective to be turned into a forecast of new hires or regional effects. To track the possible employment impact, annunci di lavoro is a useful reference point, but it does not replace the data that are absent from the source.

The key issue for the domestic audience is the combination of industrial appeal and state control. Bern wants to respond to the pressures reported after the start of the war between Russia and Ukraine; at the same time, the new regime should remain tied to Swiss neutrality and international law.

Key points

To follow the dossier without confusing a proposal with a law currently in force, it is advisable to use a simple sequence. The source does not indicate forms, individual applications or administrative procedures for workers and companies: the concrete step indicated is the vote on 29 novembre. The rest of the analysis must remain anchored to the criteria explicitly described.

Five checks before the vote

1. Mark the deadline. 29 novembre is the date of the vote on the revision of the War Material Act. Do not add a year that the source does not indicate.

2. Establish the starting rule. Today, Swiss war material cannot be exported to countries involved in internal or international conflicts. This is the reference point for understanding the scope of the proposal.

3. Isolate the requested expansion. The bourgeois majority in Parliament wants more leeway for exports and re-exports, so as to facilitate transfers by Western countries to third countries.

4. Check the safeguards. The Federal Council should have a right of veto and could require that the weapons remain in the purchasing country if reservations arise regarding security or the policy of neutrality.

5. Verify the exceptions. Every derogation from the current criteria is linked to exceptional circumstances, the protection of foreign policy or security interests and compliance with international obligations.

This checklist also helps to read the economic statements with caution. The objective of strengthening the defense industry and safeguarding jobs is explicit; the source, however, provides no figures on companies, employees, wages or expected results. Therefore, no guarantee of employment can be inferred from a revision that is still subject to a vote. The link to aziende che assumono may be used to observe the labor market, without attributing every opportunity to the reform.

For a complete reading, it is advisable to consider together the two political documents mentioned by the source: the press release of the Federal Department of Economic Affairs, Education and Research and the position expressed by the Government before Parliament. The criterion to be verified remains the compatibility between greater flexibility, Swiss neutrality and international law.

Frequently Asked Questions
When is the vote on the revision of the law on war material scheduled?
The vote is scheduled for next 29 November. The Federal Council's position effectively opens the campaign toward the referendum, which the source indicates is set for exactly two months after the Government's communication. The revision concerns the War Materiel Act and the related rules on exports.
Why does the Federal Council want to change the rules on exports?
The Government argues that, since the beginning of the war between Russia and Ukraine, the strict export rules have made the Swiss arms industry less attractive and subjected it to strong pressure. The reform is therefore presented as a tool to strengthen the defence industry and safeguard jobs.
What would change for re-exports and third countries?
The bourgeois majority in Parliament wants to give the Government greater leeway over exports and re-exports. The stated objective is to facilitate the transfer, by Western countries, of war materiel purchased in Switzerland to third countries. The proposal also provides for control mechanisms, including a veto right for the Federal Council.
What limits would remain under the proposal?
The Federal Council should have a right of veto and be able to require that the weapons remain in the purchasing country when there are concerns about security or neutrality policy. Only in exceptional circumstances could the current authorization criteria be waived, in order to protect foreign policy or security interests and in compliance with international obligations.
