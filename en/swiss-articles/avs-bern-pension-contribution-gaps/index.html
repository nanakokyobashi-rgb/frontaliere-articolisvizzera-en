AVS Canton Bern: annuity, gaps and benefits (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 22 August 2026

First AVS pillar in the Canton of Bern: cantonal compensation fund, pension calculation, contribution gaps and benefits complementary to the minimum living allowance.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The cantonal clearing house manages the first AVS pillar in the Canton of Bern
  • Contribution gaps reduce the final income, but can be partially recovered
  • Supplementary benefits guarantee the minimum life for beneficiaries below the threshold
  • AVS/AI/IPG contributions: 5.3% paid by the employee, 10.6% total with employer

Key facts

  • What: Cantonal Compensation Fund that manages the first AVS pillar
  • Who: UFAS/BSV (federal) coordinates, Cassa Cantonale Bern administers
  • When: From insurance registration until the reference age (65 years)
  • Where: Canton of Bern, Switzerland
  • Amount: 5.3% of contribution paid by the employee on each salary

The first pillar of old-age and survivors' insurance (AVS) is the foundation of Swiss social security. In the Canton of Bern, as in all Swiss cantons, a cantonal compensation fund collects contributions from employers and employees, records them in the individual insurance account, and calculates pensions upon reaching the reference age.

The UFAS (Federal Office of Social Insurance, designated as BSV in the official Germanic nomenclature) coordinates the system at the national level according to uniform regulations, while the cantonal fund of Bern manages the local administration and the payment of benefits. Operational details

The calculation of the annuity and the impact of contribution gaps

When an insured reaches the reference age and submits a pension application to the cantonal bank of Bern, it makes a precise calculation based on two key elements: the average income of the insured during the entire working life and the total number of years of full contribution.

Contribution gaps do not clear spontaneously. If a specific year is missing a contribution, that gap remains permanently in the insurance account. The compensation fund counts the gaps and subtracts them from the total of the considerable years of insurance. A person who has paid contributions for 40 years without interruption will receive a higher annuity than someone who has the same average income but only has 35 full years of contributions: the 5-year difference directly translates into a reduction in the monthly amount.

There are, however, compensation mechanisms provided for by federal law. For example, periods of training after the age of 20 can be counted as years of contribution even without actual payment. Unemployment insurance benefit periods are fully recognised. Periods of illness or injury, under certain conditions, do not generate gaps. Key points

Complementary benefits: when the pension is not enough to cover the minimum vital needs

The AVS provides the first pillar of protection, but the pension may not always be enough to cover the minimum vital needs determined by the canton. This is where complementary benefits (CB) come in, also known as income supplements. If the AVS pension of a person falls below the threshold of need set by the Canton of Bern, the latter, in coordination with the compensation fund, provides complementary benefits. It is not social assistance in the traditional sense: it is a legal right for those who do not reach the minimum vital needs with the sole AVS pension, derived from federal legislation.

Complementary benefits cover three distinct areas: ordinary living expenses (housing, food, clothing, essential items), uncovered medical expenses (for example, uncompensated health insurance deductibles not covered by the pension), and other specific needs related to disabilities or complex health situations.

Step-by-step procedure to access the benefits

There is no automatic entitlement: those who are entitled must submit an explicit application. The operational steps are:

1. Contact the compensation fund of the Canton of Bern as soon as you receive the final decision on your AVS pension. If the amount is particularly low, report it explicitly to the staff at the counter.

2. Collect the required documentation: extract of the AVS insurance account (provided free of charge by the fund), official decision on the pension, copy of the residence permit or identity card if you are a foreign citizen, documentation of other income or assets if present.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the cantonal clearing house in Bern and what role does it play?
It is the administrative body that collects AVS/AI/IPG contributions from employers and employees in the Canton of Bern, records them in the individual insurance account of each member, and calculates AVS pensions when the insured reaches the reference age. It applies uniform federal rules throughout Switzerland, but actually handles local administration and payments.
How are contribution gaps calculated and what impact do they have on AVS income?
Each year without contributions reduces the total number of years of insurance counted by the compensation fund. An annuity is calculated on the average income during working life multiplied by the number of full years of contribution: fewer years = lower annuity. Periods of recognized sickness, subsidized unemployment, and post-20 training may make up for some gaps under federal law.
Who is entitled to supplementary benefits in the Canton of Bern?
Those who receive an AVS annuity which, added to any other income or assets, does not reach the living minimum set by the canton of Bern. There is no automatism: you must submit an explicit application to the Cantonal Office of Supplementary Services. The benefits cover ordinary living expenses, medical expenses, and special situations.
At what age can you apply for the AVS annuity in the Canton of Bern?
The reference age is 65 years for women and men (the Swiss system periodically harmonizes this threshold according to the federal regulations in force). An ordinary AVS pension is not possible before thatage, except for exceptions for recognised disability, which falls under the AI pillar (disability insurance).
Can I make up years of contribution gap by making voluntary contributions?
In part, yes. The cantonal fund of Bern offers the possibility of paying voluntary contributions for past years in certain cases (example: periods of uninsured self-employment, years of residence abroad). However, not every year can be recovered: within specific limits set by federal law. Consult the Bern Compensation Fund directly to assess your personal situation.
