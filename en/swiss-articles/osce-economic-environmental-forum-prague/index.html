Key facts

  • Organizer: Office of the OSCE Coordinator of Economic and Environmental Activities

The Office of the OSCE Coordinator of Economic and Environmental Activities has officially launched the proceedings of the 32nd Economic and Environmental Forum in the city of Prague. The event, structured over two days of institutional meetings and debates, falls directly under the leadership of the Swiss OSCE Chairmanship for the year 2026. At the heart of the meeting is an in-depth analysis of how anticipating technological developments and strengthening science-based diplomacy can concretely contribute to security and stability throughout the international organization's area of competence.

Objectives and priorities of the presidency

The main objective set for this edition of the forum is to provide targeted support to participating States. The aim is thus to enable the various countries to prepare more effectively and efficiently for the current and emerging challenges that characterize the international geopolitical and economic landscape. Economic cooperation and environmental protection have always represented the fundamental pillars underlying the OSCE's action in this specific area of intervention, coordinated at the operational level by the dedicated structures present across European territory.

Operational details

The role of Switzerland and scientific diplomacy

The conduct of the work by the Swiss Chairmanship of the OSCE in 2026 highlights the constant Swiss commitment to promoting multilateral dialogue and regional stability. Integrating scientific expertise with traditional diplomacy is an innovative tool to address systemic vulnerabilities. Participating States have the opportunity to discuss how new technologies can be monitored and anticipated to prevent economic and environmental crises.

Analysis of technological and environmental challenges

During the working sessions held in Prague, delegates examine the connections between technological innovation and ecological safety. The rapid evolution of markets and industrial systems requires constant international coordination. The Office of the Coordinator of Economic and Environmental Activities acts as a catalyst for the exchange of good practices between the various Member States, focusing on preventive strategies capable of mitigating transboundary risks related to climate change and the digital transition in the OSCE area.
