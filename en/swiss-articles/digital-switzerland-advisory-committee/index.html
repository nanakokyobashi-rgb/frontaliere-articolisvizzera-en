On September 3, 2026, the meeting of the Swiss Digital Advisory Committee was held in Bern, chaired by Federal Councillor Beat Jans with the participation of the Federal Chancellor Viktor Rossi. The event brought together leading figures from the political, scientific, economic and civil society landscape to discuss central issues related to the nation's technological evolution. At the heart of the debate, the digital sovereignty of Switzerland and the delicate dossier on electronic identity, a key tool for future access to online services. The ultimate goal, as reiterated during the work, is to allow the entire population to benefit from a digital transformation that is ecologically, economically and socially sustainable. A path that must allow citizens to move in the virtual space with maximum autonomy and self-determination.

Why has the introduction of the e-ID been postponed?
The introduction of electronic identity (e-ID) has been postponed by the Federal Department of Justice and Police (FDJP) to give top priority to system stability and user trust. The Confederation has chosen not to accelerate the times, preferring to conduct rigorous tests and proactive analysis of system failures. The goal is to ensure that the technology is secure and resilient before launch, avoiding vulnerabilities related to threats such as deepfakes or malware attacks.
What measures are taken for cybersecurity?
To ensure security, the Swiss strategy integrates preventive measures such as the use of open source software, penetration testing and bug bounty programs to identify weaknesses in the source code. The e-ID program is under constant stress testing, treating security not as a static goal but as a dynamic process.
