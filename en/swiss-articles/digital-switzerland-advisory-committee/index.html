Digital Switzerland Committee: security and e-ID priorities (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 September 2026

On 3 September 2026, the Advisory Committee discussed the national digital strategy, postponing the e-ID to ensure maximum security and trust.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The Digital Swiss Advisory Committee met on 3 September 2026.
  • Focus on digital sovereignty, security and electronic identity (e-ID).
  • The introduction of the e-ID has been postponed to ensure trust.
  • The strategy aims at a responsible and secure digital transformation.

Key facts

  • What: Meeting of the Swiss Digital Advisory Board
  • When: 3 September 2026
  • Where: Bern
  • Who: Beat Jans, Viktor Rossi, representatives of politics, cantons and economy
  • Objective: Security and trust in digital transformation

On September 3, 2026, the meeting of the Swiss Digital Advisory Committee was held in Bern, chaired by Federal Councillor Beat Jans with the participation of the Federal Chancellor Viktor Rossi. The event brought together leading figures from the political, scientific, economic and civil society landscape to discuss central issues related to the nation's technological evolution. At the heart of the debate, the digital sovereignty of Switzerland and the delicate dossier on electronic identity, a key tool for future access to online services. The ultimate goal, as reiterated during the work, is to allow the entire population to benefit from a digital transformation that is ecologically, economically and socially sustainable. A path that must allow citizens to move in the virtual space with maximum autonomy and self-determination.

Operational details

Error management as a development method

The Swiss strategy for digitalization adopts a critical approach to technological innovation, based on the proactive search for system failures. As highlighted by Federal Councillor Beat Jans, the e-ID program integrates preventive measures aimed at identifying weak points in the source code and optimizing the stability of the infrastructure. The use of practices such as open source, penetration testing, and bug bounty programs demonstrates a necessary willingness for transparency to build a network of trust around digital services. In a rapidly evolving technological landscape, especially due to developments in the field of artificial intelligence, security is not considered a static goal but a dynamic process that requires constant reviews. The enhancement of online issuance procedures is oriented to counteract concrete threats, such as the introduction of malware into end devices or the recognition of deepfakes, through the implementation of additional technical defense measures. This approach is also reflected in the management of time and resources: the courage to review previously communicated deadlines is an integral part of a responsible policy. While aware of the potential media impact, the Minister of Justice has reiterated that the priority must remain the integrity of the system. For citizens and businesses in Switzerland, this means being able to rely on digital infrastructures subjected to rigorous stress tests. Transparency, understood as the sharing and critical analysis of processes, thus becomes the main tool of defense against cyber vulnerabilities. This methodology, applied transversally to digitalization projects, aims to minimize operational risks, ensuring that technological development proceeds in parallel with the resilience of the system. The ability to learn from one's own mistakes, explicitly cited by the minister, transforms digital security management into a reliability driver for the entire national system, ensuring that each future update is based on foundations tested and validated by the scientific and technical community involved in the work.

Key points

Participation and transparency in national strategies

The Swiss Digital Advisory Committee serves as the hub for dialogue between institutions and the various components of Swiss society. Organized by the Federal Chancellery in close collaboration with the various departments, the committee meets several times a year to discuss the central themes defined by the Federal Council within the framework of the Swiss Digital Strategy. The variable composition of the participants, which includes experts from the scientific, academic, and economic worlds, as well as representatives from the Cantons, ensures that every decision is the result of multidisciplinary discussion. For those living in Switzerland, the importance of these meetings lies in the nature of the decisions made, which directly affect the way citizens interact with the administration. The transparency of the decision-making process, despite the technical challenges it entails, aims to maintain a high level of trust in the federal institutions. In this context, the consultation procedures and reviews of digital projects, such as the e-ID program, follow a process that prioritizes the quality of the outcome over the speed of implementation. Citizens can monitor the evolution of the strategy through the official communications of the Federal Chancellery, staying updated on the changes that will impact their digital daily life. The management of security, although a technical issue, has direct implications for the lives of those who use online services daily, making it necessary to have a clear understanding of the paths taken by the Federal Council. Those who wish to deepen their understanding of the interaction with digital services and the protections provided can consult the official resources of the Confederation to understand how new regulations will affect their access to online services. For optimal management of one's financial and pension situation in view of future digital integrations, it is useful to maintain a clear view of one's data and contributions, taking advantage of the information services on pensions to best plan one's long-term needs, regularly verifying one's contribution status and current pension coverage.

Why has the introduction of the e-ID been postponed?
The introduction of electronic identity (e-ID) has been postponed by the Federal Department of Justice and Police (FDJP) to give top priority to system stability and user trust. The Confederation has chosen not to accelerate the times, preferring to conduct rigorous tests and proactive analysis of system failures. The goal is to ensure that the technology is secure and resilient before launch, avoiding vulnerabilities related to threats such as deepfakes or malware attacks.
What measures are taken for cybersecurity?
To ensure security, the Swiss strategy integrates preventive measures such as the use of open source software, penetration testing and bug bounty programs to identify weaknesses in the source code. The e-ID program is under constant stress testing, treating security not as a static goal but as a dynamic process.
