Operational details

Buying a home is not a purely real estate decision, but also a complex financial decision that requires a careful assessment of debt sustainability. Before taking out a mortgage, the buyer must ensure that they have the necessary lending capacity and are able to pay the monthly installments without compromising their lifestyle and financial situation. In Switzerland, banks apply standardized criteria to verify this sustainability.

# Mortgage Sustainability and Lending Capacity Calculation

Lending capacity is the starting point. Banks calculate the maximum amount of mortgage they grant based on a formula that considers the annual gross income of the buyer (or spouses in case of joint purchase). Compulsory social contributions are subtracted from gross income: AVS (Old Age Insurance), AI (Disability Insurance) and the unemployment component (IPG) — overall 5.3% for the employee, while the employer pays an additional 5.3%, for a total of 10.6%. Contributions to the second pillar (LPP) vary according to the age of the taxpayer and the coordinated salary (salary net of the coordination deduction), but represent a significant share of income, typically between 7% and 18% based on age. Frequently Asked Questions
How is the lending capacity calculated for a mortgage loan?
The bank calculates the lending capacity on the buyer's annual gross income, from which it subtracts the mandatory social contributions (AVS, AI, IPG at 5.3%), the payments to the second pillar (LPP, 7–18% depending on age), and the LAMal premium (health insurance). The net amount available is multiplied by a percentage (typically 40–50%) to determine the maximum loan that can be granted. Banks apply this criterion to ensure debt sustainability.
What are the main taxes paid by homeowners in the Outer Appenzell canton?
Owners pay three levels of tax: federal direct tax (IFD) at the federal level, cantonal and municipal taxes in the canton of Outer Appenzell (with specific multipliers for each municipality), and a substance tax (real estate). At the time of purchase, the transfer tax is added, which is the responsibility of the cantonal and municipal authorities. These taxes vary greatly depending on the location in the canton.
What is the role of the notary in the purchase of a house?
The notary draws up the deed of purchase according to federal and cantonal laws, verifies the legitimacy of the property in the Land Registry, collects the signatures of buyer and seller, calculates the amount of the transfer tax, and submits the documents to the cadastral administration for registration. Notary fees vary based on the value of the transaction and cantonal fees.
What is pass-through tax and how does it affect the total cost of the purchase?
The transfer tax is a cantonal and municipal tax applied to the transfer of real estate property. It is calculated on the value of the property acquired and the rates vary considerably between the Swiss cantons. In the canton of Appenzello Esterno, the amount depends on the specific cantonal laws. This tax is in addition to the purchase price and represents a significant expense that the buyer must consider in the total budget.
What do I need to know about health insurance (LAMal) if I buy a house in the canton of Appenzello Outer as a new resident?
Health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory for every resident in Switzerland and must be taken out within three months of arrival in the canton. The premium is individual and varies according to age, canton and choice of deductible (from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year). Banks require a copy of the policy or confirmation of enrollment before disbursing the loan. The cost of LAMal is included in the calculation of the lending capacity.
