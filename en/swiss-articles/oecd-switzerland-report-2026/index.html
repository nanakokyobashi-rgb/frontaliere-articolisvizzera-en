OECD Report on Switzerland: Stability and Economic Reforms (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 16 September 2026

The OECD released the report on Switzerland appreciating economic resilience but calling for reforms in health, welfare and administration by the end of 2027.

Key facts

  • Publication date: September 15, 2026
  • Issuing body: OECD
  • Subject of analysis: Switzerland's economic policy
  • Reform horizon: Long-term
  • Growth report deadline: End of 2027

On September 15, 2026, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development published its report on Switzerland, expressing appreciation for the country's economic policy approach. In light of multiple challenges, however, the OECD believes that action is necessary to ensure Switzerland's long-term competitiveness and innovative capacity. Proposed measures include further reforms in the healthcare sector and old-age pension system, a reduction of administrative burdens on businesses, better exploitation of the labor force potential, and the strengthening of competition. The international organization highlights the solid performance of the Swiss economy. In its view, stable institutions and a prudent, moderate economic and financial policy, focused on favorable framework conditions and sound public finances, have helped guarantee Switzerland's resilience. At the same time, the document highlights the complexity of the international context. The main challenges include, in particular, the further development of relations with the European Union and uncertainties in trade policy with major export markets, especially the United States. The OECD recommends further strengthening economic relations with key trading partners and aiming to forge additional trade partnerships.

Operational details

To ensure the long-term sustainability of public finances, according to the OECD, further reforms are needed in the health sector and in old-age pensions. In the health sector, the organisation recommends increasing efficiency, for example through better coordination in hospital and care planning. With regard to old-age pensions, the importance of sustainable solutions is underlined in the light of demographic trends. To strengthen and broaden the foundations of productivity growth, the OECD recommends further reducing administrative burdens on businesses, making even better use of the potential of the workforce, and improving the competitive environment, for example in the energy sector. The report also analyses climate change adaptation and housing supply in Switzerland. With regard to adaptation to climate change, the OECD recommends stronger coordination between state levels and better incentives for prevention measures. To expand the housing offer, he mentions, among other things, greater densification and more efficient and faster authorisation procedures. Key points

The guidelines provided by the international organization offer a clear compass to guide future legislative and executive choices in Switzerland. These are not immediate interventions, but rather a structured path that will accompany the federal political agenda in the coming years. The Swiss executive will use these analytical foundations to define the next steps of the growth policy report, the publication of which is scheduled for the end of 2027. For those who live, work, or do business in Switzerland, following the evolution of these reforms is essential to anticipate regulatory changes regarding social security, healthcare costs, and framework conditions for labor. Regularly check your pension status and plan your future financial wellbeing using the analysis tools available online.

Next steps for national economic policy

The path leading to the Federal Council's growth policy report at the end of 2027 will see the involvement of various federal departments and consultation with the cantons. The OECD recommendations do not have immediate legal force, but they constitute an authoritative benchmark that stimulates political and institutional debate in Bern. Cantonal and federal authorities will have to evaluate how to incorporate the guidelines on healthcare, social security, construction, and energy competition within their respective legislative programs.
