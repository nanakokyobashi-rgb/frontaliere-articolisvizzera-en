Overview of the labor market in the canton of Uri

The canton of Uri is an alpine region in central Switzerland with an economic fabric traditionally linked to agriculture, tourism, and services. As in the rest of Switzerland, the labor market is regulated at the federal level for certain aspects—mandatory social contributions, maximum working hours—and managed autonomously by the cantons for others, including the minimum wage where provided.

Switzerland does not set a federal minimum wage. However, some Swiss cantons have introduced their own minimum wages. In the canton of Uri, as in other cantons, salary dynamics are determined by market forces, collective labor agreements (CCL), and individual employment contracts. Each canton maintains autonomy on this crucial matter.

Operational details

Economic sectors and salary levels by education

The Swiss labor market is segmented by sector and salary varies significantly based on the level of education achieved. The three levels recognized by the Swiss education system are: apprenticeship (2-4 years, CFC - Certificate of Vocational Education and Training), high school diploma (maturità or equivalent), and tertiary education (university or professional).

In the canton of Uri, historically important is tourism, linked to the interest in the alpine landscape and transportation (the Gotthard road passes through Uri). The hotel and catering sector represents a significant source of employment, although it typically offers lower salaries compared to sectors such as technology, metalworking industry, or financial services. A worker with an apprenticeship in the hotel sector earns on average less than an engineering graduate in a manufacturing plant. This salary differential reflects the national dynamics of the Swiss labor market.

Construction and manual labor represent another important employment component in alpine cantons like Uri, linked to both the construction of infrastructure and the maintenance of mountain real estate. The service sectors (trade, health, public administration) employ an increasing share of workers, in line with national Swiss trends towards a more service-oriented economy.

Collective labor agreements and wage protection

Many economic sectors in Switzerland are covered by collective labor agreements (CLA). A CLA is an agreement between unions and employers' associations that sets minimum wages, hours, bonuses, and working conditions for a sector or company. If a worker is covered by a CLA, the salary must be at least equal to the minimum set by the contract; the employer cannot offer less.

Key points

Professional pension fund (LPP/BVG) and pension contributions

All employees with an annual salary above a certain threshold are required to participate in a professional pension fund (LPP - Federal Law on Occupational Old Age, Survivors' and Disability Pension). The employer contributes a minimum amount linked to age: 7% of the 'coordinated salary' for employees aged 25-34, 10% for 35-44, 15% for 45-54, and 18% for 55 until the reference age. This contribution is not visible in the gross monthly salary reported in the contract but constitutes savings for the pension. In the canton of Uri, the operation of the LPP follows the same federal rules; however, the conversion rate (the percentage of the accumulated sum that becomes a monthly pension at retirement) may vary slightly between professional funds.

Job search procedure and registration

Anyone looking for a job in the canton of Uri must contact the cantonal employment and unemployment offices to register as unemployed and access job placement services. In Switzerland, unemployment insurance (UI) covers up to 70-80% of the previous salary in case of involuntary job loss, but registration is required within 7 days of unemployment.

Before accepting a job offer:

1. Check if the sector is covered by a CCT by consulting the SECO website; if so, your salary must comply with the contractual minimum.

2. Consult the cantonal statistical office for data on average salaries by sector and level of education in Uri.

3. Use an online salary calculator to estimate federal, cantonal, and communal taxes in Uri based on the proposed gross salary. In the canton of Uri, cantonal taxation is generally moderate but varies by municipality.

Frequently Asked Questions
In which canton are AVS/AI/IPG social contributions mandatory?
AVS/AI/IPG contributions at 5.3% on the part of the employee are mandatory throughout Switzerland, including in canton Uri. The employer pays an equivalent amount (total 10.6%). These contributions are deductible from gross salary. Unemployment insurance (AD) is also compulsory at 1.1% up to the annual ceiling. They do not depend on the canton: they are identical federal regulations in URIs as in every other Swiss canton. Only income taxes vary between cantons.
Does Uri Canton have a legal minimum wage?
Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage. However, some Swiss cantons have introduced their own wage minimums on their own. In canton Uri, the main wage protection mechanism is sectoral collective labour agreements (CLAs). If your industry is covered by a CCL, the employer cannot offer less than the minimum set out in the contract. Check with SECO if your sector has a registered CCL.
What is the taxation in canton Uri compared to other cantons?
Switzerland has a tax system at three levels: federal (identical everywhere), cantonal and municipal. The direct federal tax is equal in all cantons, but the cantonal rate and especially the municipal multiplication vary significantly. Uri generally has a moderate cantonal rate compared to highly urbanized cantons (Zurich, Geneva, Basel). The municipality of residence applies a multiplier on a cantonal basis. Where do I check if my industry has a collective agreement (CCL)?
You can consult the website of the SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs) which publishes an official register of all CCLs registered in Switzerland. You can search by canton (uri) and economic sector (e.g. 'construction', 'trade', 'food industry'). Alternatively, ask the employer directly if they apply a CCL at the time of the job offer. If yes, your salary must comply with the minimum established by the CCL.
What are the probationary and cancellation periods in Switzerland?
During the first month of employment, there is a probationary period in which both (employee and employer) can withdraw with very short notice (usually 7 days). After the probationary period, the cancellation periods are fixed by federal law: 1 month during the first year of employment, 2 months from the second to the ninth year, 3 months from the tenth year onwards. These terms apply identically in the Uri canton as in every Swiss canton. The cancellation must be communicated in writing.
