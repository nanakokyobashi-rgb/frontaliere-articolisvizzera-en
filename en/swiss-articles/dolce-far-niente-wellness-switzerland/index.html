Meeting new people, visiting postcard places and launching into original activities to photograph and share with friends on their social channels: even during our free time on holiday we seem to have an irrepressible need for programs. It will be our productivity-oriented society, it will be our inability to give ourselves a break in a world that always needs us connected, but stopping, for real, seems to have become a luxury for a few. Yet, indulging in moments of authentic inactivity to spend with oneself, without smartphones, music or other forms of entertainment, does not mean wasting time, on the contrary. When we do absolutely nothing, we are taking care of our body and mind. Modern society imposes tight rhythms that often leave no room for reflection or the simple recovery of psychophysical energies. Operational details

The analysis of the relationship between the urban environment and psychophysical recovery offers insights of great interest to those who live in Swiss urban centres, where traffic noise and daily hustle and bustle represent a constant source of acoustic and visual stress. Research from the WSL has shown that trees, water and birdsong can compensate for traffic noise, ensuring a good recovery from stress. Within this framework of studies, the 3% and 2% and 80% percentages emerge as reference parameters related to environmental analyses and the measurement of acoustic and natural impacts on the population residing in metropolitan and peripheral areas. Swiss cities, while offering a high quality of life and state-of-the-art infrastructure, require careful planning of green spaces to guarantee citizens or people active on the territory immediate access to regenerating natural contexts. The presence of urban parks, accessible waterways and peri-urban wooded areas is not only an aesthetic or ornamental element, but constitutes a real preventive health infrastructure capable of lowering cortisol levels and improving public health at the cantonal and national levels. For those who work in Switzerland, taking advantage of lunch breaks to immerse yourself in a green area or enjoy brief moments of silence outdoors allows you to break the tension accumulated in front of the screen or in office meetings.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why is the absence of stimuli important for the body?
The absence of stimuli reduces stress, slows the heartbeat, regulates breathing and promotes the transition from the sympathetic mode of activation to the parasympathetic mode of recovery, also reducing cortisol levels.
What does the WSL's research say about green spaces?
Research by the WSL has shown that the presence of trees, water and birdsong can effectively compensate for urban traffic noise, ensuring a good recovery from stress and promoting psychophysical well-being.
How does rest affect the cells of our body?
Stopping and allowing yourself moments of true inactivity allows the body to activate cellular maintenance processes, offering tissues the opportunity to repair themselves and restore the correct biological balance.
