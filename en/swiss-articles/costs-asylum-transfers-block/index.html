Asylum, Quadri asks the Federal Council about the costs (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 18 August 2026

Lorenzo Quadri asks for clarity on the costs incurred by the Confederation for the blockade of Dublin transfers to Italy since December 2022.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Lorenzo Quadri asks for the costs of the asylum block.
  • The blockade of transfers to Italy dates back to December 2022.
  • Reimbursement request to Italy for the costs incurred.
  • New European Migration Pact changes the rules.

Key facts

  • What: Parliamentary question on asylum costs
  • When: From December 2022 to date
  • Where: Swiss Confederation
  • Who: National Councillor Lorenzo Quadri
  • Subject: Suspension of Dublin readmissions from Italy

Lorenzo Quadri, member of the Swiss National Council, formally presented a motion to the Federal Council to obtain a detailed report on the expenses that the Confederation has had to face due to the suspension of the readmissions of asylum seekers to Italy. The blockade, which began in December 2022, prevented Switzerland from transferring people to the peninsula in accordance with the Dublin system, generating a stalemate that lasted for several months. The issue becomes particularly relevant following the approval of the new European Pact on Migration, which should theoretically unblock the situation by allowing postponements again.

Operational details

Analysis of administrative impacts

The impact of the transfer freeze has created significant pressure on the Swiss reception system. When a Dublin procedure is converted into a national procedure, the costs for the Confederation increase considerably. These include, in the first place, accommodation costs for an extended period, as the stay of asylum seekers on Swiss territory extends beyond the originally envisaged deadlines. Added to this are the costs for assistance, which cover basic daily needs, and the use of human resources within the Secretariat of State for Migration (SEM) to manage files that, under normal conditions, would have been transferred to the competent State.

Comparison of procedures

The transition from a Dublin procedure to a national procedure represents a challenge for the federal budget. While the Dublin system is designed to optimise accountability between European Member States, the suspension of readmissions has upset this balance. The Federal Council is now called upon to assess not only the past cost, but also the future cost, considering the transport and accompanying costs necessary to restart transfers now that the European regulatory framework is evolving. Frequently Asked Questions
Since when is the blockade on transfers to Italy in force?
The blocking of transfers of asylum seekers to Italy, based on the Dublin system, began in December 2022. Since that date, Switzerland has experienced difficulties in deferring people who had reached the Confederation via the peninsula.
What costs does Lorenzo Quadri dispute with the Federal Council?
Quadri asks for clarity on the additional costs incurred by the Confederation for housing, assistance, management of asylum procedures and the administrative staff needed to handle cases that, due to the suspension, have been converted from Dublin procedures to national asylum procedures.
Is there a refund from Italy?
It is not yet specified whether a refund will take place. Lorenzo Quadri's motion explicitly asks the Federal Council about the will to ask Italy for compensation, in whole or in part, for the extra expenses incurred by Switzerland during the period of inactivity of the transfers.
