Police intervention in Goldach: no gunfire (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 September 2026

After the police intervention in Goldach at a high school: 300 people evacuated due to pyrotechnic noises mistaken for gunshots.

Context

In brief

  • Police intervention ended in Goldach after a school party
  • About 300 people evacuated from the building as a precaution
  • No gunshots fired during the event
  • The noises heard were presumably of pyrotechnic origin

Key facts

  • What: Police intervention for reports of alleged gunshots
  • When: Night between 4 and 5 September
  • Where: High school center in Goldach, Canton of St. Gallen
  • Who: Canton of St. Gallen police
  • People involved: About 300 students and staff

The police intervention in Goldach concluded in the night, following a report received by the operations center during a school party. Authorities were alerted by several calls reporting alleged gunshots heard inside the high school center. As a precautionary measure, the police led the students and teaching staff inside the building, simultaneously initiating standard safety procedures for such alerts.

Facts

The Canton of St. Gallen police confirmed that, following investigations on the scene, there was no gunfire. The spokesperson for the institution clarified that the explosion noises heard by those present during the school party likely came from pyrotechnic objects used on that occasion. The operation, which saw the deployment of several patrols, special units, and support from police forces from neighboring cantons, officially ended around 2:00 AM. The evacuation of the approximately 300 people present in the area was completed by 1:00 AM, allowing all participants to return to their homes safely. No injuries were reported during the event or in the subsequent phases of the alert. Currently, the Canton of St. Gallen police have initiated a formal investigation to clarify the details of the incident.

Operational details

Public Safety Implications

The Goldach incident raises relevant issues regarding the management of security during public events organized in school or community settings. In Switzerland, the management of similar emergency situations follows strict protocols that prioritize the safety of citizens. The collaboration between different cantonal police forces, as in this specific case, is a fundamental element to ensure a timely and effective response. For school administrations, the event serves as a reminder of the importance of carefully monitoring the use of pyrotechnic materials, even in celebratory contexts, as such tools can easily generate unjustified alarms that mobilize significant public resources and create panic among participants.

Key points

Emergency Procedures

In Switzerland, reporting crimes or dangerous situations must be done promptly by calling the emergency number 117. In the case of public events, it is the responsibility of the organizers to inform local authorities about planned activities that may cause alarm, such as the use of pyrotechnic effects, to avoid unnecessary deployment of law enforcement. If you find yourself in a situation where you suspect danger, always follow the instructions given by the agents on site or by the security personnel. Collaboration with law enforcement is essential to allow them to quickly distinguish between a festive event and a real threat.

Frequently Asked Questions
What caused the police intervention in Goldach?
The intervention was triggered by several calls to the operations center that reported alleged gunfire heard during a school party at the Goldach high school center. Law enforcement has mobilised patrols, special units and support from neighbouring cantons. The investigations confirmed that there was no explosion of weapons; the noises perceived by those present were, in all probability, of pyrotechnic origin, related to the current event.
How many people were evacuated during the operation?
As a precautionary measure, the cantonal police of St. Gallen proceeded to evacuate and secure about 300 people, including students and teaching staff present inside the school building. The evacuation operations ended by one o'clock at night, while the entire police intervention officially ended around 2:00 am. No injuries were recorded either during the course of the party or in the phases following the alarm.
What are the emergency procedures in Switzerland?
In the presence of such a danger or suspicion, it is necessary to promptly call the emergency number 117. Organizers of public events are responsible for informing local authorities about potentially alarming activities, such as the use of pyrotechnics. In situations of uncertainty, authorities follow standard safety protocols, such as evacuating or securing buildings, to ensure the safety of participants. Collaboration with law enforcement is critical to effective emergency management.
