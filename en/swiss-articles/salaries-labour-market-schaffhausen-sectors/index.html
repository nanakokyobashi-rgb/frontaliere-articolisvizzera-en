Wages and labour market in Schaffhausen (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 September 2026

Swiss wage structure for training levels, collective agreements, social security system (AVS/LPP) and cantonal minimum wage. A practical guide to verifying your salary.

Context

In brief

  • There is no federal minimum wage in Switzerland; some cantons, including Schaffhausen, have defined it
  • Salary levels vary with the level of education (VET, high school, university)
  • Employee contributes 5.3% AVS/AI/IPG and LPP contributions proportional to age
  • Collective agreements set specific conditions for sector and company

Key facts

  • What: Salary structure and Swiss labor market for education levels
  • Where: Canton of Schaffhausen and national Switzerland
  • Who: Employees, employers, SECO and cantonal statistical offices
  • Employee contributions: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% + AD/AC 1.1% (annual maximum)
  • Maximum hours: 45 hours per week (industry/offices/sales) or 50 hours (other sectors)
  • Minimum holidays: 4 weeks per year (5 for those under 20 years)
  • Education: VET, high school, university are the main drivers of wage differences

The Swiss labor market is characterized by strong wage differentiation linked to the level of education. There is no federal minimum wage, but the Canton of Schaffhausen and other cantons have defined their own minimum levels where applicable. Working conditions, including wages, are regulated by specific collective agreements for sector and company.

The Swiss education structure (VET, high school, university) is the main driver of wage differences. A worker with completed vocational training earns on average less than someone with a tertiary qualification. Additionally, the integrated pension system (AVS, LPP, third pillar) represents a significant part of the total remuneration, in addition to the gross salary.

Operational details

Training, pension and salary differentials

Training is the most determining factor in salary positioning. An apprenticeship completed with a federal certificate (AFC) opens access to modest initial salaries, which grow with seniority. A high school diploma or a post-compulsory specialization course represents a significant salary jump. A university degree or a polytechnic diploma places the worker at the highest salary levels, although the actual earnings depend on the employment sector.

The Canton of Schaffhausen, like all Swiss cantons, applies a three-level tax system: federal, cantonal and municipal. Each canton has its own multipliers, established by cantonal and municipal tax laws. A worker in Schaffhausen will have to calculate their tax burden considering all three rates. In addition to direct taxation, the employee bears non-negotiable mandatory pension costs. The pension fund LPP is managed by the employer and represents a substantial part of the total remuneration, as the company contributes an amount equal to the employee's contribution.

Furthermore, the employee must take out health insurance (LAMal) within 3 months of starting work: it is mandatory for all residents in Switzerland and the premiums vary by canton and region, with deductibles from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 annually, and often a premium reduction is available based on income.

Key points

How to check your salary and find data on the labor market

Before accepting a job offer or negotiating a contract renewal, it is essential to verify if your salary is aligned with market parameters. At the federal level, the SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs) regularly publishes reports on the labor market situation, including data on unemployment, average salary levels by sector, and labor mobility. At the cantonal level, the statistical offices of Schaffhausen provide local data on salary structures. Collective agreements are a key element: consult the SECO website for a search of the collective agreements in force in your sector and canton.

If your employer applies a collective agreement, the salary is bound by those parameters; otherwise, it remains the result of individual negotiation. In the absence of a collective agreement, the employer is still required to comply with federal labor law and cantonal ordinances.

Step-by-step: checking your salary

1. Request the complete payslip: it must indicate gross salary, deductions for AHV/AI/IPG (5.3%), AD/AC (1.1%), LPP, tax withholdings, and insurance. Compare the entries with the federal labor law.

2. Calculate the net take-home: use our salary calculator to simulate the effect of federal, cantonal, and municipal taxation. Enter your canton of residence for an accurate estimate of the tax burden.

3. Check the collective agreement: if applicable, verify if your level of education corresponds to the salary bracket provided for your role.

4. Compare with average data: SECO and cantonal statistics provide median salaries by sector and level of education, useful for future negotiations.

5. Consult an expert: if you have doubts about deductions, LPP, or taxation, contact the cantonal labor office.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a federal minimum wage in Switzerland?
No. Switzerland does not have a minimum wage at the federal level. However, several cantons, including Schaffhausen, have set a cantonal minimum wage for certain sectors or for all workers. Collective agreements, specific to sector and company, often provide for minimum wages above the cantonal minimum. Check the ordinances of your canton for any minimums in force.
How does the salary vary by level of training?
The degree of training is the main wage driver in Switzerland. A completed apprenticeship (AFC) opens access to modest starting wages; a high school or tertiary education represents a pay leap; a university degree or polytechnic diploma places the worker at the highest levels. In addition, seniority, sector of employment and the application of collective agreements further influence wage positioning.
What social security contributions do they deduct from your salary?
The employee pays 5.3% of salary for AVS/AI/IPG (old age, disability, sick pay), plus 1.1% for AD/AC (unemployment), up to an annual ceiling. In addition, a percentage of the coordinated salary ends up in the pension fund (LPP), with rates that vary by age group: 7% at 25-34 years, 10% at 35-44, 15% at 45-54, 18% at 55+. Health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory and premiums are set by the resident.
How does the wage tax system work in Switzerland?
The salary is subject to three levels of taxation: federal (IFD), cantonal and municipal. Each canton has its own tax laws and multipliers, established by the cantonal and municipal authorities. The total tax burden varies significantly between cantons. Use a tax calculator to estimate your specific tax burden based on your canton and municipality of residence.
What role do collective wage agreements play?
Collective agreements, in force for specific sectors and often for companies, bind the employer to salary parameters, increases for seniority, bonuses, holidays and other working conditions. If your employer applies a collective bargaining agreement, your salary must be included in the collective bargaining agreement. You can search for collective agreements in your industry on the SECO website.
