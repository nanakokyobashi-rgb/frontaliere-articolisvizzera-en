Operational details

The practical analysis of cantonal taxes requires a clear understanding of the deduction mechanism. In addition to income tax, the employee is subject to compulsory social contributions: AHV/IV/EO at 5.3% for the employee, AD/AC at 1.1% (with a ceiling calculated on an income of CHF 148,200), and variable quotas for UVG (0.7–1.5%) and BVG (7–18% depending on the age group, from the age of 25). These deductions reduce the tax base, but represent a fixed cost that must be considered in the national comparison. Compared to the Italian system, which is characterised by IRPEF brackets (23% up to €28,000, 35% up to €50,000 and 43% above this threshold), the Swiss system offers greater fragmentation. ### Operational comparison For a taxpayer assessing the tax burden between different cantons, including the canton of Vaud, the comparison should not be limited to the nominal rate. Deductions for professional expenses, social security contributions and the impact of the municipal multiplier must be considered. Legitimate financial planning involves optimising these elements. For example, the choice of health insurance company (KVG) plays a crucial role: the right of option for cross-border commuters allows them to manage the insurance premium, with deductibles ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 for adults. This is not a 'health tax', but a mandatory coverage that affects the family budget. The tax impact in 2026 will also be influenced by the possible revision of the cantonal rates, which each canton decides independently. For those who work in the canton of Vaud, consulting the official calcolatore tables is the first step towards a realistic net projection. The stability of the Swiss system, despite its cantonal diversity, allows for long-term planning, provided that it knows its rights and duties under federal and cantonal legislation, avoiding confusing the competences between the bodies that deal with social security and those that manage taxation.

Frequently Asked Questions
How is the border tax handled in 2026?
For frontier workers, income tax is withheld at source directly in Switzerland. The employer applies the rates established by the cantonal administration of reference. This system, in combination with the tax credit provided for by the EC framework of 730 in Italy, avoids double taxation.
What are the applicable deductibles for frontier workers?
The New Frontier Agreement, in force from 1 January 2024, establishes a deductible of 10,000 euros for new frontier workers. For the so-called 'old frontier workers', i.e. those who were already such before 17 July 2023, an exemption of 7,500 euros is provided, managed within a transitional regime that will last until 2033.
Who sets tax rates in Switzerland?
Tax rates are defined by federal and cantonal laws. The Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV) manages the principles at the federal level, while cantonal tax administrations set specific rates and multipliers for their territory. The UFAS and the BFS have no competence in setting such rates.
