The offering at the center

The Federal Administration presents work within its structures as an activity with a tangible impact on Switzerland: the talent, ideas and commitment of those who work there contribute to the country’s high quality of life. The offering therefore invites people to view employment also as a contribution to the smooth functioning of Switzerland.

At the Federal Administration, work is described as meaningful. Those who perform it can assume responsibility, work for the well-being of the population in Switzerland and develop innovative solutions within multidisciplinary teams. The teamwork dimension is part of the proposition for those who want to put their talent and ideas to good use.

For recent graduates, the Federal Administration offers a wide range of internships. The experience makes it possible to gain valuable practical experience, expand one’s network of contacts and use one’s knowledge to help shape Switzerland’s future. The internship is thus presented as a concrete experience, linked both to learning and to personal contribution.

Alongside internships, there is vocational training. The offering comprises 50 vocational training programs: those who choose one work on concrete tasks and projects, learn with the support of their trainers and acquire skills for their future. There are also 7 weeks of holidays, with time to devote to relaxation, hobbies, travel and friends.

The declared common thread is contributing to Switzerland’s future. In work at the Federal Administration, this happens through responsibility and innovative solutions; in internships, through practical experience, networking and knowledge; in training, through real tasks, guidance and skills.

Finally, the Federal Administration presents its offering as an employer with flexible working arrangements, performance-based remuneration, continuing education opportunities and additional proposals. Those who wish to broaden the comparison can set this offering alongside annunci di lavoro, without losing sight of the three paths indicated by the source.

Operational details

How to read the three opportunities

The practical value of the proposal becomes clear when the three paths are separated, because each option addresses a different need. The question to ask is simple: are you looking for meaningful work, experience after your studies, or vocational training? The answer guides the reading of the characteristics indicated by the source.

| Path | Elements to assess | | Work in the Federal Administration | responsibility, population well-being, innovative solutions and multidisciplinary teams | | Internship | hands-on experience, network of contacts and knowledge | | Vocational training | 50 pathways, real tasks and projects, trainers, skills and 7 weeks of vacation |

For a recent graduate, the internship is presented primarily as a step toward gaining experience. It is not only about learning: the network of contacts and the use of one's knowledge are part of the same path. Its distinctive feature, according to the source, is the relationship between what one already knows and what one can experience in the field. The proposal thus combines experience with contributing to Switzerland's future.

Those seeking vocational training, on the other hand, find a broader offering, organized into 50 options. The presence of real tasks and projects makes the practical component concrete; the support provided by trainers indicates the role of guidance; skills development is forward-looking. The 7 weeks of vacation add to the training a period explicitly intended for relaxation, hobbies, travel and friends.

For those considering the Federal Administration as an employer, the point of comparison is different. Flexible working arrangements, performance-based remuneration and continuing education make up the overall offering. They should not be confused with internships or the 50 training programs: they describe how the Administration presents itself as an employer. Those who want to broaden the comparison can also look at the aziende che assumono, keeping the path, objective and offering separate.

Key points

A four-step operational path

The choice can be turned into a simple process, using only the elements provided by the Federal Administration.

1. Start from your starting point. If you are a recent graduate, focus on the wide range of internships. If you are looking for training, explore the 50 professional options. If you are considering an employer, instead look at flexible forms of work, performance-based pay and continuing education.

2. Define the result you want to achieve. For an internship, the reference points are hands-on experience, networking and applying knowledge. For vocational training, the focus should be on real tasks and projects, support from trainers and skills development. For work at the Federal Administration, the text highlights responsibility, the well-being of the population and innovative solutions in multidisciplinary teams.

3. Check what place everyday life occupies. In the vocational training path, the 7 weeks of vacation are associated with relaxation, hobbies, travel and friends. This element should be considered together with work on real projects and learning from the trainers, not as a separate detail.

4. Complete your research with a comparison. The annunci di lavoro help broaden your perspective, while the Federal Administration page makes it possible to identify the vocational training that is right for you. For those considering work as an employer, the reference remains the combination of flexibility, compensation and continuing education.

This sequence avoids mixing different objectives: experience, training and meaningful work. It also makes it possible to use the available information in an orderly way and to keep together personal contribution, growth and the conditions offered by the employer. Frequently Asked Questions
What kind of work does the Federal Administration propose?
The Federal Administration describes the work as significant and linked to a concrete contribution to Switzerland. Those who work there can take responsibility, commit to the well-being of the population and develop innovative solutions in multidisciplinary teams. The offer also includes flexible forms of work, remuneration according to performance, continuous training and other offers.
Who are practitioners for?
Interns are aimed at recent graduate students. The Federal Administration offers a wide range of these opportunities, which allow you to gain experience in the field, expand your network of contacts and put your knowledge at the service of Switzerland's construction of tomorrow. The route thus combines experience, contacts and personal contribution to Switzerland.
How much vocational training is available?
Vocational training includes 50 courses. Those who choose one of these trainings collaborate on real tasks and projects, learn from the trainers who work alongside them and develop skills for the future. The route also includes 7 weeks of holidays, with time for relaxation, hobbies, travel and friends.
What conditions do you offer as an employer?
Among the characteristics indicated for the Federal Administration as an employer are flexible forms of work, a remuneration according to performance, continuous training and other offers. The source presents these elements as part of the attractiveness of the Federal Administration for job seekers. They are associated with meaningful work and the ability to take responsibility.
