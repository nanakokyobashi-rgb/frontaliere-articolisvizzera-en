The Swiss system separates tax and social security

Switzerland organises the tax levy on three levels: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. Each Canton applies its own law and its own multiplier; the municipality uses a multiplier on the cantonal tax. For cross-border teleworking, this architecture is the starting point so as not to confuse the responsibilities of the offices with the job position.

The AFC/ESTV deals with direct federal tax and VAT. Cantonal administrations, on the other hand, manage cantonal and municipal taxation. The UFAS/BSV covers social security, including AVS/AI and LPP/BVG, but does not set taxes. The UST/BFS produces statistics and does not establish rates. The distinction is also useful when a worker reads a contract or a paycheck: a social contribution and a tax are not the same item nor are they at the same institutional level.

On salary, AVS/AI/IPG provide for 5.3% to be borne by the employee and 10.6% overall with the employer. The AD/AC is equal to 1.1% up to the annual ceiling. LAINF/LAA ranges from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector. For the LPP/BVG, applied to the coordinated salary, the indicated rates are 7% between 25 and 34 years, 10% between 35 and 44, 15% between 45 and 54 and 18% from 55 years up to the reference age. These parameters are distinct from the municipal tax and the sick pay premium.

Residence, health and permits

LAMal/KVG is the mandatory health insurance for residents. It must be stipulated within three months of arrival; the premium is per capita, not a tax and not a salary contribution. Premiums vary by Canton and region, while premium reduction is a cantonal subsidy. For adults, the deductibles are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 or 2500.

According to the SEM, the L title covers short-term stays, up to one year, while the B permit concerns the residence and is renewable. Permit C certifies domicile and permit G is intended for frontier workers. In general, the C permit is obtained after ten years of residence; for EU/EFTA citizens the ordinary period is five years. Permits define residence status, without determining tax and social security obligations on their own.

Operational details

Comparing a deduction with a premium

The most useful difference for those working across the border and teleworking concerns the basis on which the burden arises. AVS/AI/IPG, AD/AC, LAINF/LAA contributions and LPP/BVG are expressed as percentages of the salary or coordinated salary. LAMal/KVG, however, follows per capita premiums, differentiated by canton and region. Putting everything under the generic heading 'taxes' can alter the reading of remuneration and cost of living because the system uses different bases and authorities.

The G permit identifies the category of cross-border workers, but the provided framework does not associate a specific teleworking percentage with this designation. Therefore, the mere residence permit does not allow deriving a tax or social security amount other than what is indicated in the available data. The distinction between residence, work, and social security must be kept separate even when the activity is distributed across multiple locations.

The house follows a different logic

Tenancy is regulated by federal law, uniform in every canton, through CO art. 253 et seq. The security deposit cannot exceed three months' rent and must be paid into a blocked account in the tenant's name. The landlord's notice of termination is valid only if it uses the official cantonal form. The tenant can contest it within 30 days before the conciliation authority.

This comparison shows a recurring combination in the Swiss system: the basic rule may be federal, while the form, subsidy, multiplier, or competent office may depend on the canton or municipality. For cross-border teleworking, reading these areas separately avoids turning a payslip data point into an automatic conclusion about housing, health, or taxes.

Key points

The employment relationship has a common basis, but no federal minimum wage

In Switzerland there is no federal minimum wage; some cantons have their own. The maximum working week is 45 hours in industry, offices and retail, or 50 hours in other sectors. The minimum vacation entitlement is four weeks, rising to five for those under 20.

The national rules indicated also include 14 weeks of maternity leave at 80% through IPG and two weeks of paternity leave. For termination, the reported notice periods are one month in the first year, two months from the second through the ninth, and three months from the tenth. These parameters describe the employment relationship and are not a measure of the share of work carried out remotely.

Services that vary from one canton to another

As a rule, social assistance is administered by cantons and municipalities according to need. Motor vehicle tax, energy incentives, building permits and contributions to continuing education are likewise governed by cantonal rules and offices. Requirements, deadlines and amounts must therefore be checked with the relevant official source, rather than inferred from a single national rule.

Several services are regulated at the territorial level. Building insurance may be mandatory or organized differently depending on the canton. Civil protection, although it falls within the framework of federal law, is coordinated at cantonal level: call-ups and allowances must be checked with the competent authority.

Indexed figures should not be set without a year

Some values change every year: the maximum for the 3a third pillar, the LPP coordination threshold or deduction, the AD/AC maximum, average LAMal premiums and the median salary. The verified framework does not provide precise amounts that can be applied without specifying the year, so it is not correct to turn these quantities into permanent figures.

The same limitation applies to cross-border telework. The available data do not include a single percentage threshold, nor a general amount or deadline that would make it possible to automatically calculate the tax and social-security impact of the portion performed remotely. The responsibilities, contribution bases, the LAMal rule for residents, permit categories and cantonal differences are defined instead. Frequently Asked Questions
What tax levels does the Swiss system include?
The system includes direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. Each Canton has its own law and its own multiplier; the municipality applies a multiplier on the cantonal tax. The AFC/ESTV deals with IFD and VAT, while the cantonal administrations deal with the cantonal and municipal part. The UFAS/BSV follows social security and does not set tax rates.
Is LAMal a payroll tax?
No. For residents, LAMal/KVG is the mandatory health insurance and must be taken out within three months of arrival. The premium is per capita, varies by Canton and region and is neither a tax nor a wage contribution. For adults the deductibles indicated are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500; the reduction in premiums is a cantonal subsidy.
What contributions are shown on the salary?
AVS/AI/IPG amount to 5.3% borne by the employee and 10.6% overall with the employer. AD/AC is 1.1% up to the annual ceiling, while LAINF/LAA varies from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector. For the LPP/BVG on the coordinated salary, rates of 7%, 10%, 15% and 18% are indicated according to age.
Is there a single percentage for cross-border teleworking?
The framework provided does not indicate a percentage threshold, a date or a single amount for teleworking across borders. Instead, the tax competences, contributions, LAMal and permits are specified. For this reason, tax or social security treatment cannot be deduced only from the fact that part of the work is carried out remotely.
