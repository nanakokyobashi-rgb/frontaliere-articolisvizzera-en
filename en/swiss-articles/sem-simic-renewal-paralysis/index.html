The project and the relationship

In the report published on Monday evening, the Federal Financial Control warns that the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) could be paralysed if it does not obtain additional resources to transform its database. The system in question is SIMIC, now outdated, which collects the data of foreign citizens residing in Switzerland.

With the aim of reducing costs, Parliament had allocated a budget four years ago to finance the transformation of the database. However, estimates have increased: in 2024 SEM revalued the cost of the renewal to 193 million, while the initial estimate amounted to about 66 million.

SIMIC has 30,000 users between the Confederation, Cantons and Municipalities. The data shows that the database involves the three levels of administration indicated in the report, without the source dividing the users among them or describing what operations they carry out through the system.

The report warns that the problem is not only about the transformation of the database: SEM's ability to guarantee its activities is also at risk.

Operational details

Comparing the reported figures requires keeping the estimate, the reassessment, and the outlook on delays distinct. The first point of reference is the budget of approximately 66 million allocated by Parliament four years ago. The second is the estimate of 193 million prepared by SEM in 2024. The report adds that the renewal should cost four times as much as initially planned. The source does not provide a breakdown of expenditure items and does not assign separate amounts to the Confederation, the cantons, or the municipalities.

Distinguishing between the two pieces of information avoids confusing the cost of the project with the potential cost of postponing it.

The database also has a defined administrative scope: it collects data on foreign citizens residing in Switzerland and is used by 30'000 users across the Confederation, the cantons and the municipalities. The text does not specify how the transformation will change users' access, which functions will be transferred, or which services could be disrupted. The only explicitly stated effect is the risk to SEM's ability to carry out its activities.

Key points

The report then shifts attention from the figures alone to SEM's organizational capacity. The agency will have to meet by 2028 the requirements arising from the Switzerland-EU package. To meet that deadline, the document states that the necessary human resources must be ensured with the utmost urgency. However, the text does not specify either the size of the staff or the individual activities included in those requirements.

Funding and institutional response

As part of the 2027 relief plan, the Federal Council reduced funding to renew the system. The report points to further reductions planned for 2029 and 2030. In the document, these cuts are associated with the revision of the project requested by the Federal Audit Office, which considers the workload and costs of the various projects to have been underestimated.

The recommendation is to completely revise SEM's planning. In its response, the Secretariat assures that it has already initiated the necessary review, so that it can proceed with the work as soon as the decision on the Switzerland-EU package is adopted. The source therefore documents an internal review that has begun and a decision-making step that is still necessary, but does not indicate when the decision will be adopted, what additional resources will be allocated, or what timetable the work will follow.
