Flight suspension in Kägiswil until 20 nov 2026

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 30 September 2026

UFAC has ordered the suspension of flights from 9 October 2026 at least until 20 November 2026 at Kägiswil airfield, pending the PSIA-Rega public filing from 15 October to 16 November 2026.

Context

At a Glance

  • Kägiswil suspends flights until at least November 20, 2026
  • The FOCA has ordered the closure of the airfield
  • The public notice period begins on October 15, 2026
  • The public notice period ends on November 16, 2026

The federal government’s press release, published on September 30, 2026, announces the suspension of flight operations at the Kägiswil Airfield. The FOCA ordered the closure because the requirements for continuing operations are no longer met. The measure will remain in effect at least until November 20, 2026.

The decision stems from the FOCA’s denial of a building permit on July 13, 2026, as part of the change-of-use procedure. The Obwalden Airfield Cooperative (FGOW) has filed an appeal with the Federal Administrative Court. The proceedings are pending, and the change of use for civilian purposes has not yet become final.

Why the Runway Is Being Shut Down

The surface rights agreement with armasuisse, which allows FGOW to use the airfield and hangars, expires at the end of September 2026 and has not yet been extended. Federal authorities have offered the cooperative the opportunity to negotiate a temporary continuation of operations. However, after determining that the requirements are no longer being met, the Federal Office of Civil Aviation (FOCA) has ordered the closure.

Prior to October 9, only flights intended for relocation to another location are authorized. From October 9, 2026, and at least until November 20, 2026, all flight operations must be suspended for safety reasons. During that period, Rega will need to install the necessary infrastructure in preparation for the planned new construction near the runway.

Plans for the site’s future are proceeding in parallel. Rega has submitted an application to the FOCA to establish operations at the former military airport in Kägiswil, in the municipality of Sarnen (OW). A civilian heliport is planned to replace operations with fixed-wing aircraft. The project includes a hangar building, five helicopter parking pads, and a multi-story administrative complex with an underground garage.

The public review period for the PSIA documents and Rega’s application will begin on October 15, 2026, and last for 30 days, ending on November 16, 2026. The Confederation will then evaluate the comments received and determine the next steps in the process. Regarding general mobility, the press release does not announce any changes to the mezzi pubblici regulations.

Operational details

A practical reading of the dossier requires distinguishing between the operation of the airfield and the proposed heliport project. The former concerns the current use of the runway; the latter requires amendments to the PSIA and an assessment by the Confederation.

A Project Still Pending Evaluation

Public submission does not equate to a green light. The FOCA is submitting the proposed amendments to the PSIA and Rega’s application to the public; after consulting with the authorities and reviewing the comments received, the federal government will conduct a comprehensive assessment of the interests involved regarding Kägiswil Airport. Only then will it determine how to proceed. The consultation period opening on October 15, 2026, is therefore an opportunity for public participation, not an announcement of the heliport’s approval.

The FGOW appeal also leaves open the question of the originally planned change of use for civilian purposes. The Rega project is based on a different configuration than that of current operations with fixed-wing aircraft: it envisions a civilian heliport intended for technical and work flights during business hours. The source rules out a Rega operational base. Instead, it refers to the technical maintenance of the helicopter fleet and the new headquarters. For Rotex Helicopter AG, based in the region, the possibility of a base for work flights is envisaged in the long term.

Two Time Horizons

Rega’s timeline includes a separate deadline: following the planned construction of taxiways to bypass the runway, Rega must vacate its headquarters at Zurich Airport by the end of 2030. This date pertains to the relocation of the headquarters and does not, by itself, determine when Kägiswil will be able to operate as a heliport. The press release provides no data on jobs, salaries, costs, or economic impacts; therefore, it does not allow the project to be linked to new annunci di lavoro.

For the public, the practical implication is to distinguish between what has already been decided regarding operations and what remains to be defined in terms of infrastructure. The suspension of operations and the PSIA consultation are related steps, but they are not the same: the former addresses the requirements for the use of the airfield, while the latter concerns the future civil use of the site. Whether operations will continue after November 20, 2026, also remains under negotiation, with no outcome indicated in the source.

Key points

To follow the case without getting the deadlines mixed up, it is best to organize your attention in a specific sequence. The procedure described by the Confederation provides a public access point, while the airfield’s operations follow a separate schedule.

Steps for Review

1. Starting October 15, 2026, review the PSIA documentation and the application submitted by Rega at the Sarnen municipal administration office. The PSIA-related documents are also available on the participation page indicated by the FOCA. 2. During the 30-day public review period, review the SIAP amendments and Rega’s construction project separately. The former concerns the aviation planning framework; the latter describes the building with a hangar, the five helicopter parking pads, and the administrative complex with an underground garage. The application calls for technical and operational flights during business hours. 3. Set November 16, 2026, as the deadline for the public consultation. Anyone wishing to submit a statement must do so within the consultation period; the Confederation will then evaluate the statements received and the consultation with the authorities. 4. After the submission period closes, monitor the outcome of the comprehensive assessment of interests and the next steps in the procedure. The source does not anticipate either the outcome or an authorization date.

Steps Toward Operationalization

Those currently using Kägiswil must distinguish between temporary authorizations and safety suspensions. Prior to October 9, 2026, only flights intended for transfer to another location remain authorized. From October 9, 2026, until at least November 20, 2026, all flight operations must be suspended. After that date, any continuation will depend on negotiations between the Confederation and FGOW.

FGOW’s appeal before the Federal Administrative Court remains pending, while armasuisse’s lease agreement expires at the end of September 2026 and has not yet been extended. For long-term planning, the target date is the end of 2030: by then, Rega must vacate its headquarters at Zurich Airport. Frequently Asked Questions
Why did UFAC order the suspension of flights at Kägiswil?
UFAC has ordered the suspension because, following the expiry of armasuisse's surface right at the end of September 2026, the requirements for continuing to operate the airfield are no longer met. The decision stems from the denial of the building permit on 13 July 2026 and from the fact that the surface-right contract was not extended, making it necessary to halt operations for safety reasons.
What are the key dates of the suspension and the public deposit?
The suspension of flights is scheduled from 9 October 2026 and will remain in effect at least until 20 November 2026. The public deposit of the PSIA documents and Rega's application begins on 15 October 2026 and lasts 30 days, ending on 16 November 2026. During this period, the population may consult the documentation and submit comments.
What does the Rega project envisage for the site after the suspension?
The Rega project envisages the construction of a civil heliport at the former military airfield of Kägiswil. It includes a building with a hangar, five helicopter parking stands and a multi-storey administrative complex with an underground garage. The helicopters would be used for technical and work-related flights during working hours, without constituting an operational base for Rega.
