Recent graduates, rising unemployment: UST DATA (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 August 2026

Master UNI at 6.4%, bachelor sup at 4.9%. UST: between 2023-2025 the difficult insertion in the market grows. Affected sectors and scenarios.

The Federal Statistical Office (FSO) today released data on unemployment among graduates of universities (UNI) and vocational colleges (SUPs) measured one year after graduation. The picture shows a clear upward trend between 2023 and 2025, with significant variations depending on the type of training and the field of study.

For master's degrees obtained at universities, the unemployment rate rose from 3.9% to 6.4% over the same period. Holders of a sup bachelor's degree, on the other hand, registered an increase from 3.4% to 4.9%. The methodological basis used by the UST follows the definition of the International Labour Organization (ILO), ensuring comparability with international employment data and reliability

Operational details

Increasing difficulty in finding work 'in line with aspirations'

Formal unemployment numbers are only part of the story. The UST also has an increasing share of graduates who report difficulties in finding employment consistent with their training and career expectations. For university master's degrees, this percentage rose from 30.0% (2022) to 44.1% (2024), an increase of over 14 percentage points in two years. Among sup bachelor's degrees, the jump was from 24.2% to 36.9%, an even more marked increase in terms of relative speed, equal to 12.7 points.

This gap between the availability of 'some work' and 'the desired job' reveals a structural tension in the Swiss labour market. Many young graduates accept jobs below their qualification level, in secondary sectors, with reduced hours or with precarious contracts, simply to stay employed and support housing and living expenses. Ticino and the other Swiss regions are no exception: employment pressure affects the national territory uniformly.

This dynamic means that conventional unemployment statistics underestimate the true discomfort of recent graduates. Key points

How to enter the job market after graduation in Switzerland

For recent graduates, UST data suggests concrete practical orientations. First and foremost, the choice of sector remains decisive: the data shows that healthcare, medicine, pharmacy and social work offer significantly lower unemployment rates compared to arts, design, general economics and applied technologies. If you are still in the decision phase, evaluate not only your passion for the discipline, but also the national employment reality which registers 1.6% unemployment for doctors and pharmacists, compared to 10.8% for musicians and artists at SUP level.

Second crucial element: accumulate practical experience during your studies. Internships, apprenticeships, laboratory projects and collaborations with companies are not optional, but by now practically mandatory to compensate for the dynamics of Swiss employers' selection. Given that experience is still the number one barrier cited by graduates in difficulty, every week of practice counts for your CV.

Positioning strategies in the national market

Don't wait for formal unemployment to act. If you are still temporarily employed or underutilized, start immediately looking for your next opportunities on specialized job portals. In Switzerland, the main job search channels for university and professional level are visible through dedicated platforms and professional networks specific to sector and canton.

Frequently Asked Questions
What percentage of recent graduates find work within one year of graduation in Switzerland?
According to the UST, official unemployment rates (ILO definition) one year after graduation rose to 6.4% for university master's degrees and 4.9% for sup bachelor's degrees between 2023 and 2025. However, over 44.1% of UNI masters and 36.9% of sup bachelors report difficulties in finding employment in line with expectations, indicating that most do find employment, but not always at the desired level of qualification.
What are the sectors with the most opportunities for recent graduates in Switzerland?
The strongest sectors remain medicine, pharmacy, healthcare and social work, with unemployment rates of 1.6%, 1.7% and 1.5% for SUP bachelor's degrees respectively. High pedagogical schools show an even lower rate, still at 0.7%. In contrast, design, music, theatre and general economic sectors suffer from rates above 8-10%.
What is the main obstacle recent graduates face in finding work?
Lack of professional experience remains the factor most cited by graduates. However, between 2023 and 2025, the economic situation became a growing barrier, indicated by 42.5% of UNI master's graduates in 2025, almost doubling from 23.8% in 2023, reflecting the impact of business cycles and inflation on the Swiss labour market.
What can I do to increase my chances of entering the market?
According to UST data, accumulating practical experience during studies (internships, internships, laboratories) is essential. In addition, considering sectors with structural demand (healthcare, pedagogy) and acquiring additional certifications can reduce the experience gap. Don't wait to become formally unemployed: start your active search on specialised portals right away.
How has unemployment increased among UNI Masters in Economic Sciences?
The unemployment rate for UNI Masters in Economic Sciences grew from 3.5% (2023) to 8.5% (2025), an increase of 5 percentage points. This suggests a saturation of the management and financial market at the national level and requires more marked differentiation strategies for recent graduates in this sector.
