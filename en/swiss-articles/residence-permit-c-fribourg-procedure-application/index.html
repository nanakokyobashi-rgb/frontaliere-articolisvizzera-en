Residence permit C Freiburg: years and application procedure (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 24 August 2026

Residence permit C in the Canton of Freiburg requires 10 years of residence (5 for EU/EFTA citizens). Learn about national requirements, the step-by-step procedure, and the next steps towards naturalization.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Permit C requires 10 years continuous residence (5 for EU/EFTA)
  • Issued by SEM in coordination with canton Fribourg
  • Requires social-cultural integration and economic stability
  • Paves the way for ordinary naturalization

Key facts

  • What: Federal residence permit (SEM)
  • Who: Canton of Freiburg on instructions from the SEM
  • Where: Canton of Freiburg, Switzerland
  • When: After 10 years residence (5 for EU/EFTA)
  • Document: Permanent residence permit

Residence permit C represents the highest level of federal authorization for foreigners wishing to reside permanently in Switzerland. Unlike the B permit (residence, renewable every 2–5 years), the C permit offers long-term housing security, typically valid for ten years.

Who can apply? As a rule, a period of continuous residence of 10 years on Swiss territory is required. However, citizens of the European Union and the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) have access to an accelerated path: a 5-year residency is sufficient. After the issuance of the C permit, it is also possible to apply for ordinary naturalization after a further 10 years (during which 8–18 years of residence count twice), plus the specific cantonal and municipal requirements.

Operational details

The step-by-step application procedure

The application for the C permit is not automatic: after 10 years (or 5 for EU/EFTA) of residence, the foreigner can apply to the competent office of the canton of Freiburg.

Step 1 — Preparation of the application. Collect the required documentation: extract from the criminal record, certificate of residence, recent pay slips (last 12 months), proof of professional integration (contract or letter from the employer), self-certification of cultural and social integration, and dichiarazione dei redditi (if self-employed). The cantonal authorities of Freiburg will publish the exact list of documents on their official website, which will vary slightly depending on the region.

Step 2 — Presentation to the cantonal migration office. In Fribourg, the application must be submitted to the competent office (address available on the website of the Fribourg State Chancellery). You can submit it on paper or online if the canton offers this option. No registration fee is due at the federal level, but the canton may require a small administrative fee (amounts being defined).

Step 3 — Cantonal preliminary investigation (approximately 2–4 months). The migration office of the canton of Fribourg checks the documents, contacts any bodies (employer, municipalities of previous residence, tax office) for confirmation, and drafts an opinion. During this stage, you may receive questions for clarification.

Key points

What to do next: next steps

If you are approaching (within 6–12 months) the completion of 10 years (or 5 if EU/AELS) of residence, it is advisable to start preparing the documentation immediately.

First step: Contact the migration office of the Canton of Fribourg (state chancellery website of the canton of Fribourg) to receive the complete list of required documents. The exact procedure is defined at the cantonal level; each canton may have variations.

Document control: Check that your judicial record is clean. If you have convictions, even minor ones, inform the office proactively: transparency facilitates the process. Request an updated extract from the competent authority.

Employment stability: Ensure you have a stable job (permanent contract or consolidated self-employment project). The SEM values positively those who have maintained the same employer for years. If you are looking for a stable job opportunity, consult our job postings; frequent job changes can raise questions during the investigation.

Language proficiency: If you have not yet certified your level of French or German (depending on the Fribourg region), you can certify it through a course or an official test (e.g. DALF, Goethe). The canton often does not require a formal certification, but sufficient practical competence for daily life; however, it is not bad to have it written in the file.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between permit B and permit C?
The B permit (residence) is renewable every 2–5 years and does not offer a permanent guarantee; the C permit (domicile) is indefinite, offers housing security and represents a higher level of authorization. A minimum of 10 years of continuous residence (5 for EU/EFTA) is required to access the C permit. Once obtained, the C permit is the step towards ordinary naturalization.
Can I apply for the C permit before the age of 10 if I have an important job offer?
No, the C permit is not available in advance only for job offers. However, if you are an EU/EFTA citizen, the term is reduced to 5 years. The SEM assesses exceptions in very specific cases (e.g. marriage to a Swiss citizen or family reunification), but the standard path remains tied to 10 years of continuous residence.
What are the criteria for language integration in the Canton of Freiburg?
The Canton of Freiburg is bilingual (French and German). The SEM and cantonal authorities assess a practical proficiency in the language of the region of residence (usually level A2–B1); a formal certification for the C permit is not required, but having one (DALF, Goethe) in the application dossier strengthens the position.
How long does it take overall to obtain the C permit?
The administrative procedure (from the time of application to approval) takes an average of 3–6 months, depending on the workload of the cantonal migration office and the completeness of the documentation submitted. Longer times arise if documents are missing, if the employer delays in providing certifications, or if a face-to-face conversation is required to assess integration.
Having obtained the C permit, can I automatically apply for naturalization?
No, the C permit is a prerequisite for ordinary naturalization, but not automatic. After the issuance of the C permit, you must wait another 10 years of continuous residence (or 5–8 years if part of the previous residence counts double), plus meet the integration criteria and obtain federal, cantonal, and municipal approval. Total time: 6–12 months from application.
