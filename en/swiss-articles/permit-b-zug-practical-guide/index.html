Guide to residence permit B in Zug: how the renewable permit works, what federal and cantonal requirements to follow, documents and renewal procedure.

Key facts

  • What: Residence permit for long-term residence in Switzerland
  • Type: Category B renewable without maximum limit of years
  • Where: Canton of Zug (like all Swiss cantons)
  • Who manages: Federal State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) and cantonal administration
  • Validity: Renewable on request at the scheduled deadlines
  • Requirements: Check at the cantonal migration office of Zug

Residence permit B is a federal category that allows permanent residence in Switzerland and is renewable according to the procedures of the canton of competence. In the Canton of Zug, as in all Swiss cantons, management follows both federal and cantonal regulations. The State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) sets the national criteria, while the cantonal administration applies them locally. Those who hold a B permit enjoy long-term residence and work rights, unlike the L permit which allows short stays of up to 1 year.

Residence permit B in Switzerland

The B permit is a national category that allows long-term residence. It differs from the L permit (short duration, maximum 1 year) and the C permit (domicile, usually granted after 10 years of continuous residence for non-European citizens, 5 for EU/EFTA citizens).

Operational details

Social and tax implications of the B permit

The B residence permit entails specific rights and obligations at the Swiss level. Those residing with a B permit must ensure mandatory health insurance coverage (LAMal) within 3 months of arrival in Switzerland; premiums vary according to the canton of residence, age, and chosen deductible. In the Canton of Zug, LAMal premiums are calculated in canton brackets, with the possibility of reduction through cantonal subsidies intended for low-income families. Additionally, the B permit requires the payment of social contributions: AVS/AI (Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance / Disability Insurance) for mandatory pension insurance, at a rate of 5.3% borne by the employee (to which is added the 5.3% of the employer, for a total of 10.6%). Those classified as stable employees also contribute to an LPP (Professional Pension) plan, according to progressive contribution tables: 7% for employees aged 25-34, 10% for 35-44, 15% for 45-54, 18% for 55+ until the reference age. It is not necessary to contribute on the entire salary: there is a coordination deduction that excludes an initial portion of the salary from LPP.

Key points

How to renew the B permit in Zug: practical procedure

To renew the B residence permit in the Canton of Zug, the first step is to check the expiry date on the document itself. It is recommended to contact the competent migration office at least 2-3 months before the expiry date, to allow time to gather the required documentation. The cantonal office will communicate the exact list of documents during the preliminary consultation; generally, it includes documentation related to the employment situation (employment contract or letter from the employer), housing (lease agreement, rent payments), income tax return for the previous year, and certificates of good conduct. It is necessary to submit a renewal application filled out according to the form provided by the cantonal administration, together with the above-mentioned documentation. The migration office of the Canton of Zug will evaluate the application and generally communicate the outcome within a few weeks of receiving the complete application. During the waiting period, the current permit remains valid if submitted within the prescribed deadlines; some cantons issue a provisional certificate to ensure continuity of rights during the evaluation procedure.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between permit B and permit C in Switzerland?
The B permit (residence) is renewable and allows long-term residence with periodic renewal deadlines. The C (domicile) permit is generally granted after 10 years of continuous residence (5 for EU/EFTA citizens) and is renewable without a defined limit, offering greater legal stability. Both allow you to work and live in Switzerland, but the C represents a more stable condition of permanent domiciliation.
When do I need to renew my B permit?
The expiry of permit B is clearly indicated on the document itself. It is advisable to contact the migration office of the Canton of Zug at least 2-3 months before the expiry date to start the renewal procedure and collect the required documents. By submitting the application within the established deadlines, the permit remains valid during the evaluation procedure.
What documents are needed for the renewal of permit B in Zug?
The migration office of the Canton of Zug provides the exact list of documents during the preliminary consultation. They generally include: employment contract or letter from the employer, lease agreement and rent payments, previous year's tax return, certificates of good conduct. Please contact the office directly for the complete and up-to-date list specific to your situation.
Does the B permit also cover access to healthcare and social security?
Yes, residence permit B requires you to take out health insurance (LAMal) within 3 months of arrival in Switzerland. In addition, those who work as an employee pay social contributions for AVS/AI (5.3% borne by the employee) and, if framed as a permanent employee, also in an LPP (Occupational Pension) plan. Contributions and premiums vary by age, salary and canton of residence.
Where can I find official information about the renewal in Zug?
The competent office is the Migrationsamt (migration office) of the Canton of Zug, which provides advice, forms and documentation. At the federal level, the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) publishes criteria and guidelines on the website www.sem.admin.ch. Contact the cantonal office directly for hours, locations and specific details of your personal situation.
