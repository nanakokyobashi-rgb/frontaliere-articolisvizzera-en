Scholarships represent a fundamental tool for guaranteeing access to education in Switzerland, regardless of the economic conditions of the family. Unlike federal taxation, managed by the Federal Customs Administration (AFC), or social security, coordinated by the Federal Office of Social Insurance (FSIO), study financing is a cantonal matter: each canton independently defines its own criteria, scholarship limits and application procedures.

In the Canton of Zurich, as in the other Swiss cantons, scholarships and study loans are administered by a dedicated cantonal office. This means that a student residing in the Canton of Zurich accesses a system distinct from that of other cantons, even if the general principles remain consistent: scholarships are awarded on the basis of two main factors: academic merit and the economic capacity of the family.

How the cantonal study financing system works

The Swiss school funding structure reflects the principle of subsidiarity: the decisions closest to the citizens remain with the cantons. This approach ensures that the allocation criteria take into account the local economic situation and living costs specific to each region. In the canton of Zurich, the cost of living situation is also monitored through national statistics from the Swiss Statistics Office (FSO), which helps to calibrate the benefit ceilings.

Students who wish to apply for a scholarship in the Canton of Zurich must generally demonstrate two elements: (1) a family economic situation that does not allow them to independently finance their studies, assessed on the basis of income thresholds defined by the canton, and (2) sufficient academic achievement, which in Switzerland indicates regular academic progress and without significant delays in the course of studies.

At the national level, the Swiss system does not have a federal minimum wage for adults (some cantons have their own), but the grants are calibrated to wages and incomes that the FSO monitors annually. This information is relevant because the economic capacity of a family is often defined on the basis of the income declared in federal and cantonal direct taxes: the canton evaluates whether the family unit falls within the relative poverty thresholds for access to the welfare service which are scholarships.

Operational details

Who is eligible for scholarships in the Canton of Zurich

The cantonal scholarships are aimed at students attending recognized training courses: compulsory teaching, upper secondary school (high school, vocational schools), higher education (universities, university of applied sciences, specialized higher schools). In the Canton of Zurich, as in other cantons, the service also covers students who complete a professional retraining course.

Access is subject to two main conditions. The first concerns academic achievement: the student must demonstrate that he is progressing regularly in his studies, without exceptional delays. This principle is common at Swiss level and aims to ensure that the cantonal contribution does not finance courses without concrete outcomes. The second condition concerns economic capacity: the canton evaluates the annual family income declared to the tax authorities, comparing it with thresholds defined internally by the canton itself.

Differences between cantons and implications for those who change residence

An important aspect for those who live or move to Switzerland: scholarships are not automatically recognized when changing canton of residence. If a student moves from the Canton of Ticino to the Canton of Zurich, he or she will have to submit a new application at the Zurich office, according to the specific criteria and deadlines of that canton. This is also relevant for those who move between cantons for work or family reasons, or to follow a training course. The procedure takes time: it is advisable to contact the cantonal office as soon as possible after the change of residence. Consult your tax return to check how your income data is evaluated by the new canton.

The cantonal administrations communicate with each other through standard protocols, but there is no automatic recognition of a grant already obtained in another canton. The reason is that each canton manages its own budget for study aid and applies its own criteria based on the resident population and annual financial availability.

Comparison of grants and loans

The Canton of Zurich, like other cantons, generally offers two tools: actual scholarships (non-refundable) and study loans (repayable after completion of training). The grants are awarded based on the merit and economic capacity of the family, often assessed by comparing the local cost of living data with the thresholds established by the canton; loans offer additional liquidity to cover living costs during your studies. This combination allows students with limited resources to access education while reducing the financial burden on the family unit.

Key points

How to present a request in the Canton of Zurich

The procedure for requesting a scholarship in the Canton of Zurich follows a standardized process at the competent cantonal office. Students must submit an application, as a rule, during defined periods every year, generally concentrated between February and March for the following school year, but the exact deadlines vary from year to year and must be checked directly with the cantonal administration.

To initiate the procedure, the following basic documents are required: (1) a copy of the previous year's school or university certificate, (2) a tax return declaration or a certificate of family income, (3) a completed and signed application form, available at the office itself or on the cantonal portal. Some cantons now accept digital requests, while others still maintain paper procedures: the Canton of Zurich specifies this in its internal regulations.

Where to find up-to-date and specific information

Since the maximum amounts, economic evaluation criteria, and deadlines change annually based on cantonal budgets, it is essential to consult the Canton of Zurich's website directly or contact the competent cantonal office (usually the social affairs, culture, or education office of the canton). The information published by the cantonal administrations is updated at the beginning of each academic year.

Students from border regions or with special housing situations (e.g., those residing in Switzerland but working abroad, or vice versa) can check if they meet the canton's residency requirements: generally, it is necessary to reside in the canton for at least one or two years to access cantonal welfare services, including scholarships, but this varies from canton to canton.

Additional support tools

In addition to cantonal scholarships, Swiss students can explore other forms of support: tax deductions for dependents (deductible from federal and cantonal direct taxes), reductions or exemptions from contributions to the mandatory health insurance (LAMal) for students with low income, and family allowances (Familiengelder) provided by the canton and companies. These tools, although not directly scholarships, reduce the overall family burden during educational paths.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who is eligible for a scholarship in the Canton of Zurich?
The scholarships are intended for students in recognised education (compulsory, secondary, university, professional) with regular academic merit and limited economic capacity. The economic assessment is based on the family income declared to the tax authorities, compared with cantonal thresholds. Students with significant delays in the course of study are excluded.
What is the difference between a scholarship and a study loan?
The scholarship is a non-refundable contribution, awarded based on the academic merit and financial need of the family. The loan is instead repayable, usually after completion of training, and provides additional liquidity to cover living costs during studies. The cantons offer both instruments, allowing students to combine them according to their needs.
What happens if I move to the Canton of Zurich from another canton?
You must submit a new application to the Zurich office according to the specific criteria of that canton. Administrations do not automatically recognise grants obtained elsewhere, as each canton manages its own budget. It is advisable to contact the competent office as soon as possible after the transfer of residence.
What documents do I need to apply?
Please provide the previous year's school or college certificate, the family income statement, and the completed and signed application form. The form is available at the cantonal office or online. Some cantonal administrations now accept digital applications through online portals, while others still maintain paper procedures: verified at the Canton of Zurich.
When to apply for a scholarship in the Canton of Zurich?
Submission deadlines vary from year to year, but are generally concentrated between February and March for the following academic year. It is essential to check the official calendar at the competent cantonal office or consult the Canton of Zurich web portal at the beginning of each academic year. Applications submitted after the set deadline are usually not accepted.
