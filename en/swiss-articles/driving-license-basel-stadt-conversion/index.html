Driving license Basel City: conversion and exams

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 19 August 2026

How to convert a foreign license in the Canton of Basel-City, theoretical and practical exams, compulsory courses and procedures at the traffic office.

Context

In brief

  • Converting a foreign driving licence requires a theoretical and practical test in the canton
  • The Road Traffic Office (Amt für Verkehr) manages the procedures
  • Temporary validity period: 12 months from taking up residence in Switzerland
  • Costs and deadlines vary according to the type of foreign licence

Key facts

  • What: Conversion of a foreign driving licence into a Swiss one
  • Where: Canton Basel-Stadt, at Amt für Verkehr
  • Who: Foreign nationals residing in Switzerland with a foreign driving licence
  • When: Within 12 months of registering with the municipality
  • Legislation: Federal Road Traffic Act (LCS), Art. 24

Anyone moving to Switzerland from abroad who holds a valid driving licence from their country of origin cannot drive indefinitely with the foreign document. Federal road traffic legislation requires foreign residents to convert their licence into a Swiss driving licence within specific deadlines. In Canton Basel-Stadt, this procedure is managed by the Road Traffic Office (Amt für Verkehr), which oversees the theoretical and practical tests required for approval.

The conversion depends primarily on the country that issued the licence. People coming from a member state of the European Union or the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) benefit from simplified procedures, thanks to the bilateral agreements and international treaties that Switzerland has signed. Licences issued by these countries are recognized more easily, although they are still subject to verification and registration. Those coming from non-EU countries, on the other hand, must undergo a more restrictive procedure, and recognition is subject to further assessments.

When is conversion required?

Conversion becomes mandatory when your residence permit in Switzerland changes from temporary (L permit) to long-term (B or C permit). During this transition, the cantonal Road Traffic Office informs you that you must replace your foreign licence with a Swiss one. The deadline for completing the procedure is usually 12 months from the date of registration in the local population register. Driving without completing the conversion within this period may result in administrative and criminal penalties.

Operational details

Theoretical and practical exam: how it works

The theoretical exam consists of a written or computer-based test on knowledge of the Swiss road code, road signs, safety regulations, and rules of conduct on the road. The exam is administered in Italian in the Canton of Ticino and in German in the Canton of Basel-Stadt (or in French, depending on the canton’s official language). The test covers specific topics such as right of way, safe distances, behavior in adverse weather conditions, regulations on loads, speed limits, and the use of onboard technology.

The practical exam is a road test with an official examiner who assesses your driving skills in real-life situations. They check coordination, compliance with regulations, hazard perception, and behavior in traffic. Both exams must be passed to obtain a Swiss driving licence.

The administrative procedure also includes a vision test and a review of the documentation. The Basel-Stadt Road Traffic Office requires you to submit an application with the following documents: a valid passport or identity document, a certificate of residence in Switzerland, the original driving licence from your country of origin, a medical certificate (if required based on age), and a passport-size photo meeting Swiss standards.

Parallel procedures and additional obligations

Some applicants from specific countries must complete mandatory driving courses before taking the exams. These courses, held at authorized driving schools, cover Swiss regulations and the specific aspects of driving in Switzerland. The number of lessons varies, but generally a minimum of 8-12 hours of driving accompanied by a certified instructor is required. The costs of these courses are borne by the applicant and are not included in the fees charged by the Road Traffic Office.

The temporary validity of a foreign driving licence is limited: you may not drive beyond 12 months from your registration in Switzerland. Once this period has elapsed without completing the conversion, use of the foreign driving licence becomes unlawful, even if the document remains valid in the country of origin.

Key points

Step by step: the complete procedure

Phase 1 – Registration with the municipality. Upon your arrival in the Canton of Basel-Stadt, register with the municipality of residence (Gemeinde). You will receive a certificate of residence within a few days. This document is the basis for the entire subsequent procedure.

Phase 2 – Contact with the Road Traffic Office. Contact the Amt für Verkehr in Basel-Stadt with your certificate of residence, passport, original foreign driver's licence and a passport-size photo. State your country of origin: this will determine whether you are subject to compulsory courses or can proceed directly to the examinations.

Phase 3 – Registration for the examinations. If required, enrol in a driving course at an authorised school. The course must be completed before you take the practical examination. Book the theory examination online or at the office. The theory examination takes place at the Road Traffic Office premises and is generally held weekly.

Phase 4 – Theory examination. Take the written test. The questions cover the Swiss Highway Code and are formulated according to federal standards. The time allowed is limited (usually 1-1,5 hours). If you pass the test, you will receive a certificate authorising you to take the practical examination.

Phase 5 – Practical examination. Once you have passed the theory examination, book the road test with an official examiner. The test lasts approximately 45 minutes and takes place on varied routes (urban road, rural road, motorway if applicable). You must demonstrate safe driving skills and compliance with the rules.

Phase 6 – Issuance of the driving licence. If you pass both examinations, return to the Road Traffic Office with the original documents and receive your Swiss driving licence (Führerschein). The document is valid for 10-15 years depending on the category.

The total costs include the registration fee at the office (variable by canton), driving lessons (8-12 hours x the school's rate, generally CHF 80-120 per hour), registration for the theory and practical examinations (CHF 150-250 in total), and issuance of the licence (CHF 50-80). The total procedure is estimated to take between 2 and 4 months, depending on the availability of examination dates and how quickly the compulsory courses are completed.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I drive in Switzerland with my foreign license for the first 12 months?
Yes, the foreign license remains valid temporarily for 12 months from your joint registration. Within this period you must start the conversion procedure at the Basel City Traffic Office. Once the deadline expires, the foreign license is no longer recognized and driving becomes illegal, even if the document is still valid in the country of origin.
Do I have to take a theoretical and practical exam even if I come from the EU?
Bilateral agreements between Switzerland and the European Union provide for facilities for residents of the EU and the European Economic Area, but the conversion procedure remains mandatory. In general, no additional courses are required, but you still have to take the theoretical and practical exams according to Swiss standards. Exceptions are assessed on a case-by-case basis by the Traffic Office.
How much does it cost to convert your driver's license in Basel City?
The total cost is estimated between CHF 300 and 600, depending on whether compulsory driving courses are required. The registration fee at the office is about CHF 50-80, the theoretical exam CHF 50-80, the practical exam CHF 100-120, and the issuance of the driving license CHF 50-80. If you have to take a course (8-12 hours), the additional cost is CHF 80-120 per hour at a licensed school.
What language is used for the theoretical exam in the Canton of Basel-City?
The theoretical examination in the Canton of Basel-City takes place in German (official language of the canton). If you are not fluent in German, you can request the use of a dictionary or, in exceptional cases, an interpreter (at your own expense). The Circulation Office evaluates each request individually.
What happens if I don't complete the conversion within 12 months?
After 12 months of joint registration, the foreign driver's license is no longer valid in Switzerland. Continuing to drive without having completed the conversion is illegal and subject to administrative (fine) and criminal penalties (revocation of the right to drive). The conversion procedure therefore becomes urgent. You must contact the Traffic Office immediately to regularize your situation.
