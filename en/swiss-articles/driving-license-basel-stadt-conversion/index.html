Driving license Basel City: conversion and exams (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 19 August 2026

How to convert a foreign license in the Canton of Basel-City, theoretical and practical exams, compulsory courses and procedures at the traffic office.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Foreign driver's license conversion requires theoretical and practical examination in the canton
  • Traffic Office (Amt für Verkehr) manages the procedures
  • Temporary validity period: 12 months from residence in Switzerland
  • Costs and deadlines vary according to the type of foreign license

Key facts

  • What: Conversion of foreign driving license in Switzerland
  • Where: Canton Basel-City, near Amt für Verkehr
  • Who: Foreigners residing in Switzerland with a foreign driver's license
  • When: Within 12 months of joint registration
  • Legislation: Federal Law on Road Traffic (LCS), art. 24

Those who move to Switzerland from abroad and have a valid driving license in their country of origin cannot drive indefinitely with the foreign document. The Federal Road Traffic Act requires foreign residents to convert their licence into a Swiss licence within specified time limits. In the Canton of Basel-City, this procedure is managed by the Traffic Office (Amt für Verkehr), which supervises the theoretical and practical examinations necessary for homologation.

The conversion mainly depends on the country of origin of the licence. Those who come from a Member State of the European Union or the European Economic Area (EFTA) benefit from simplified procedures, thanks to the bilateral agreements and international treaties that Switzerland has signed. Operational details

Theoretical and practical exam: how it works

The theoretical exam consists of a written or computer test on knowledge of the Swiss road code, road signs, safety rules and rules of conduct on the road. The exam is administered in Italian in the Canton of Ticino and in German in the Canton of Basel-City (or in French, depending on the official language of the canton). The test covers specific topics such as precedence, safety distances, behaviour in adverse weather conditions, load rules, speed limitations, and use of on-board technology.

The practical exam is a road test with an official observer assessing your driving skills in real-world settings. They check coordination, compliance with rules, perception of danger, and behavior in traffic. Both exams must be passed to obtain a Swiss licence.

The administrative procedure also includes a visual check and a review of the documentation. The Basel City Circulation Office requires that you apply with the following documents: passport or valid identity document, certificate of residence in Switzerland, original driver's license of the country of origin, medical certificate (if required according to age), and a photo card format compliant with Swiss standards.

Key points

Step by step: the complete procedure

Step 1 – Joint registration. Upon your arrival in the Basel-City Canton, register with the municipality of residence (Gemeinde). You will receive a certificate of residence within a few days. This document is the basis for all the subsequent procedure.

Step 2 – Contact with the Traffic Office. Contact the Amt für Verkehr in Basel City with your certificate of residence, passport, original foreign license and a photo in card format. Communicate your country of origin: this will determine if you are subject to mandatory courses or if you can take the exams directly.

Phase 3 – Registration for exams. If required, enroll in a driving course at an authorized school. The course must be completed before you take the practical exam. Book your theory exam online or at the office. The theoretical examination takes place at the premises of the Traffic Office and is generally weekly.

Phase 4 – Theoretical examination. Please take the written test. The questions cover the Swiss road code and are formulated according to federal standards. The time allowed is limited (usually 1-1.5 hours). If you pass the test, you receive a certificate that enables you to take the practical exam.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I drive in Switzerland with my foreign license for the first 12 months?
Yes, the foreign license remains valid temporarily for 12 months from your joint registration. Within this period you must start the conversion procedure at the Basel City Traffic Office. Once the deadline expires, the foreign license is no longer recognized and driving becomes illegal, even if the document is still valid in the country of origin.
Do I have to take a theoretical and practical exam even if I come from the EU?
Bilateral agreements between Switzerland and the European Union provide for facilities for residents of the EU and the European Economic Area, but the conversion procedure remains mandatory. In general, no additional courses are required, but you still have to take the theoretical and practical exams according to Swiss standards. Exceptions are assessed on a case-by-case basis by the Traffic Office.
How much does it cost to convert your driver's license in Basel City?
The total cost is estimated between CHF 300 and 600, depending on whether compulsory driving courses are required. The registration fee at the office is about CHF 50-80, the theoretical exam CHF 50-80, the practical exam CHF 100-120, and the issuance of the driving license CHF 50-80. If you have to take a course (8-12 hours), the additional cost is CHF 80-120 per hour at a licensed school.
What language is used for the theoretical exam in the Canton of Basel-City?
The theoretical examination in the Canton of Basel-City takes place in German (official language of the canton). If you are not fluent in German, you can request the use of a dictionary or, in exceptional cases, an interpreter (at your own expense). The Circulation Office evaluates each request individually.
What happens if I don't complete the conversion within 12 months?
After 12 months of joint registration, the foreign driver's license is no longer valid in Switzerland. Continuing to drive without having completed the conversion is illegal and subject to administrative (fine) and criminal penalties (revocation of the right to drive). The conversion procedure therefore becomes urgent. You must contact the Traffic Office immediately to regularize your situation.
