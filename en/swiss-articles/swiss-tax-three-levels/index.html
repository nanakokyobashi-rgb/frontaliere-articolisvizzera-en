Swiss tax structure: levels, authorities and social contributions

Switzerland operates a three-tier tax structure: direct federal tax, cantonal taxes and municipal taxes. This is the first point to establish when dealing with income earned abroad and the risk of double taxation. A foreign item cannot be correctly interpreted without knowing to which level the Swiss tax refers and which authority has jurisdiction.

At the federal level, the FTA/ESTV is the designated authority for direct federal tax and VAT. The cantonal authorities, on the other hand, administer cantonal and municipal taxation. Each canton has its own law and its own multiplier; for municipalities, the multiplier applies to the cantonal tax. To interpret the case, the key piece of information to be distinguished is therefore the competent authority, as opposed to simply the place where the payment is received.

The available material does not identify the type of foreign income, the period, the country or the procedure to be applied: it therefore does not allow us to state how any elimination of double taxation is calculated on a case-by-case basis.

The verifiable scope

In the available material, the reference to audits is not accompanied by an authority that has announced an audit, a date, a procedure or a penalty. It is not correct to attribute an operation to the FTA/ESTV, the cantons or the Federal Council without these elements. The verifiable part is the map of responsibilities, which is useful to avoid directing a federal matter to the wrong office or confusing a municipal tax with a social security contribution.

Even acronyms have a clearly defined scope. The UFAS/BSV deals with social security, including AVS/AHV, AI/IV and LPP/BVG, but does not set taxes. The FSO/BFS produces statistics and does not set rates. AVS/AHV, AI and IPG are therefore treated as social security contributions when analysing a salary; VAT, on the other hand, remains a federal competence of the AFC/ESTV. This separation helps to interpret the tax return without turning every deduction or payment into a foreign income tax.

Operational details

Separating tax from deductions

The risk of confusing double taxation with the total amount of salary deductions arises from the fact that items of a different nature may appear on the same payslip. Social security and insurance percentages are not rates of direct federal tax, nor do they replace the distinction between cantonal and municipal tax.

The AVS/AI/IPG figure is therefore distinct from the tax levy: the share indicated for the employee is 5.3%, while the total including the employer reaches 10.6%. AD/AC is 1.1% up to an annual ceiling. For LAINF/LAA, the indicated percentage ranges from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector. LPP/BVG, on the other hand, uses the coordinated salary and provides for different percentages by age bracket. The material does not provide an absolute amount to apply to these items.

The LAMal/KVG follows a different logic altogether. Health insurance is compulsory for residents and must be taken out within three months of arrival. It is private, with per-capita premiums, and is neither a tax nor a salary contribution. Premiums vary according to canton and region, while the reduction in premiums is a cantonal subsidy. For adults, the deductibles indicated are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500.

This separation is decisive for the issue of foreign income. A LAMal premium or an AVS contribution cannot be treated as evidence of tax already paid on income; it belongs to a different category. Likewise, an LPP/BVG deduction alone does not make it possible to reconstruct the tax due in Switzerland. The correct comparison must therefore start from the nature of the item, not merely from the amount appearing on Swiss payslip.

The source does not indicate a general mechanism for allocating a foreign tax to Swiss items. It is therefore not possible to turn these percentages into an automatic formula to avoid double taxation, nor to add social contributions and insurance premiums to obtain a single tax rate.

For the social security framework, the reference to [UFAS] is also available (https://www.bsv.admin.ch/it/telelavoro). The tax framework remains distinct: the UFAS/BSV deals with social security, but does not establish taxes.

For direct federal tax and VAT, the indicated jurisdiction remains that of the FTA/ESTV. For cantonal and municipal taxes, the institutional reference is instead represented by the cantonal administrations, because each canton has its own law and its own multiplier and the municipality applies the multiplier on the cantonal level. The FSO/BFS can provide statistics, but does not establish tax rates. A statistic, therefore, does not replace the rule applicable to an income.

Frequently Asked Questions
How many levels does the Swiss tax system have?
The Swiss tax system operates on three levels: federal, cantonal and municipal. At the federal level, the FTA is the designated authority for direct federal tax and VAT. Cantonal and municipal taxes are administered by the cantonal authorities.
What is the employee’s contribution to OASI/DI/IC?
For OASI/DI/UI, the employee’s contribution is 5.3%, while the total including the employer’s contribution is 10.6%. This social security contribution is separate from the rates of direct federal tax.
Which authority administers cantonal and municipal taxes?
Cantonal administrations administer cantonal and municipal taxation. Each canton has its own law and its own multiplier, which the municipalities apply to the cantonal tax. The FTA, on the other hand, administers direct federal tax and VAT.
Can payroll deductions be confused with foreign income tax?
No, it is essential to distinguish between the items: AVS/AI/IPG is a social security contribution, while VAT falls under the federal remit of the FTA/ESTV. LAMal/KVG premiums are neither a tax nor a payroll contribution, and an LPP/BVG deduction does not constitute the tax due in Switzerland.
