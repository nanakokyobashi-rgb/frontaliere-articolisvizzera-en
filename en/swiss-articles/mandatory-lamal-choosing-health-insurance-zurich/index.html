Compulsory health insurance for those residing in Switzerland: how to enroll in LAMal, choose the cashier and understand deductibles in 2026.

In brief

  • Mandatory health insurance for those residing in Switzerland
  • Three months to register after arrival
  • Minimum deductible CHF 300 per year, maximum CHF 2,500

Key facts

  • What: Mandatory health insurance (LAMal)
  • When: Within 3 months of taking up residence in Switzerland
  • Where: Every canton (including Zurich) has its own premiums and subsidies
  • Who: All residents of Switzerland, regardless of citizenship
  • Amount: Deductibles from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year

The LAMal system in Switzerland

Health insurance is one of the cornerstones of Swiss social protection. Unlike many European countries, there is no single national fund: the system is managed by private insurers operating at cantonal and regional level, with premiums that vary significantly from one area to another.

Anyone moving to Switzerland must take out a health insurance contract within three months of arrival. It is neither a tax nor a salary contribution: it is a private premium paid directly to the insurer, calculated on a per-capita basis. This means that each family member pays an individual premium, regardless of the family's income.

Deductible and coinsurance

The deductible is the amount the patient pays out of pocket before the health fund starts reimbursing. The options vary: CHF 300, CHF 500, CHF 1,000, CHF 1,500, CHF 2,000, CHF 2,500 per year. A higher deductible results in a lower monthly premium; a lower deductible means a higher premium. Once the deductible is reached, the patient shares medical costs (coinsurance) at rates defined by federal law.

The cantonal subsidy reduces the premium for those with low or medium incomes. Each canton, including Zurich, has its own eligibility criteria and subsidy amounts, reflecting local fiscal choices.

Operational details

Practical implications: the weight of the premium on the family budget

The choice of the deductible has a direct impact on the annual budget. Those who opt for the minimum deductible (CHF 300) will pay a higher monthly premium than those who choose CHF 2500. For a family with children, this decision is multiplied: three deductibles from CHF 300 cost more than three from CHF 1000, but the initial financial sacrifice is less. The logic is simple: set aside more money each month for more covered insurance, or risk paying more at the time of medical recourse.

Cantonal subsidy: eligibility and procedure

The premium subsidy is calculated based on your family's net income. In the canton of Zurich, as in the other cantons, eligibility depends on a fixed income threshold that varies annually. Those who are eligible can apply directly to the cantonal authorities, often online. The subsidy is calculated as a percentage of the gross premium and paid directly to the polizza di assicurazione.

Not applying for the subsidy when you are entitled means losing public money intended precisely to cover health costs for those with less ability to pay. Many do not know that the subsidy must be actively applied for — it is not awarded automatically. Applications are usually submitted in the spring, before the deadline of late May or early June, although the exact deadlines vary per canton.

How to register for LAMal: the practical steps

Anyone who has just arrived in Switzerland has 3 months to take out a health insurance contract. The procedure is simple but requires prompt action.

Step-by-step

1. Gather your documents: passport/ID, residence permit, cantonal registration certificate of your address (obtainable from the municipal office). 2. Choose a fund: use the cantonal online comparison tools (e.g. the Canton of Zurich portal) to compare premiums for your age and deductible. 3. Fill in the application: the registration form (paper or online) requires personal details, coverage start date, and type of deductible. 4. Pay the first premium: the fund sends an invoice. The first payment must be made within the deadline set by the contract (usually 30 days). 5. Coverage confirmation: the fund issues an insurance card and a coverage document. Only from that moment are you insured.

If you do not take out insurance within 3 months, the cantonal authority can assign you to a fund ex officio (default registration). In that case, you will pay premiums at the full rate with no choice of fund for the following twelve months, and you will have to reimburse any medical costs incurred in the previous 3 months.

Tools and resources available

Each canton makes available online premium comparison tools (with filters by age, type of coverage, deductible), registration forms (downloadable or digitally fillable), and a cantonal helpline for clarification on subsidies and premiums. In the Canton of Zurich, the official cantonal portal provides access to the comparison tool and up-to-date information. Do not rely on unverified third-party websites: information changes every year and only official portals guarantee accurate data.

Frequently Asked Questions
How long do I have to sign up for LAMal after moving to Switzerland?
You have 3 months from arrival in Switzerland to take out a health insurance contract. If you do not do so by this deadline, the cantonal authority may assign you a cashier ex officio. In that case, you will pay the premium at the full rate without choice, and you will have to reimburse the medical expenses incurred in the uninsured period.
What is the difference between deductible and co-insurance?
The deductible is the amount you pay out of your own pocket before the cashier begins to repay (varies from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 per year). After reaching the deductible, you pay a fixed percentage of the medical costs (co-insurance), defined by federal law. A higher deductible usually results in a lower monthly premium.
How do I access the cantonal grant for the LAMal award?
The subsidy depends on your family's net income and the amount of the premium. You must actively apply to the cantonal authority (usually online). It is not automatically assigned. Each canton has different income thresholds and percentages. Please check your canton's portal for eligibility criteria and application procedure.
Are LAMal prizes the same throughout Switzerland?
No. Awards vary greatly between cantons and regions. This variation does not reflect differences in health quality, but local choices on premium regulation and subsidies. A person could pay almost twice as much in one canton as in another, for the same age and deductible.
Can I change sick pay during the year?
The standard exchange window is November 30 of each year, with effect from the following January 1. Some cantons allow extraordinary changes if the premium increases beyond a specific threshold. Check the term in your canton, which varies. Present the cancellation form to the current cashier, not the new one.
