The labour market in the canton of Basel-City is governed by an overlap of federal rules and cantonal provisions that directly affect wages, social contributions and conditions of employment. Unlike neighbouring countries, Switzerland does not set a minimum wage at the federal level. Nor does the canton of Basel-City impose a uniform legal minimum wage: salaries are formed through individual contracts and, where provided, collective labour agreements (CCAs) in the various sectors.

Operational details

How wages are structured in Switzerland

In Basel City, as in the rest of Switzerland, there is no legal minimum wage obligation at the cantonal level. This means that the salary is negotiated directly between employer and worker, or is regulated by a collective agreement if the sector so provides. Some Swiss sectors (construction, catering, healthcare, construction) have CCAs that set minimum wages. Basel-City, being an industrial and commercial canton, has strong CCA presences in the manufacturing and service sectors.

The cost of work for the employer is lower than the gross salary of the employee: he must add the AVS/AI contributions (10.6%), the accident insurance premiums (0.7%–1.5% depending on the sector), and the LPP contribution (minimum 50% of the rate prescribed by the fund). These fixed costs directly affect the total salary that the company must allocate for each position.

On the worker side, annual gross salary is reduced by AVS/AI personal contributions (5.3%) and by LPP contributions (7%–18% depending on age). Federal and cantonal taxes are deducted from these. Basel-City has its own cantonal tax rate: the system is progressive, and the amount depends on salary, marital status and number of children. It is not uncommon for a worker in Basel City to have his annual gross reduced by about 25%–35% due to taxes and mandatory contributions.

Key points

What to do if you are looking for a job in Basel City

If you are looking for a job in the canton, it is useful to know in advance the salary structure that awaits you. When you receive a job offer, the gross salary indicated must cover your AVS/AI contributions (5.3%) and LPP (7%–18% depending on age). Always ask if the employer offers LPP contributions above the legal minimum (50%), as many companies in Basel City pay higher amounts to attract talent.

Step 1: Check the sector collective agreement Before accepting an offer, check if your industry (chemical, pharmaceutical, services, logistics, etc.) has a CCA. If yes, the minimum wage cannot fall below what is established. You can check with the trade association or contact the national consultation tool on CCAs to find out if the employer is bound.

Step 2: Calculate the net salary and financial availability Use a calcolatore di stipendio to estimate the net you will receive. Enter the gross offered, select the canton Basel City, your age group and marital status. The result will tell you how much you will actually have available per month, once you deduct AVS/AI, LPP, cantonal/municipal taxes and duties. Don't forget to add the LAMal cost (monthly premiums, available on the cantonal office website).

Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a cantonal minimum wage in Basel-City?
No. Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage, nor does the canton of Basel-City provide for a uniform one by law. Wages are formed through individual contracts or, where in force, collective bargaining agreements (CCAs) in the individual sectors. If you work in a sector with CCA (e.g. chemistry, pharmacies, construction), the minimum wage is binding; otherwise, the employer and the worker agree on the wage.
How much is withheld from my gross salary in Basel City?
On average, between 25% and 35% of the gross is withheld for AVS/AI (5.3%), LPP (7%–18% depending on age), federal and cantonal taxes. Basel City has its own cantonal tax rates. The exact calculation depends on salary, age, marital status and number of children. Use a salary calculator for an accurate estimate.
What is the LPP and why is it withheld from my salary?
The LPP (Occupational Pension) is a mandatory old-age insurance managed by companies (or pension funds) in Basel City as in all of Switzerland. Covers the supplementary pension to the AVS. The contribution varies by age group: 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54), 18% (55+). The employer pays at least 50%; the rest is covered by you. It is not a tax, but a mandatory saving for your future retirement.
What are the maximum working hours per week in Basel City?
The legal maximum is 45 hours/week in industry, offices and sales; 50 hours in other sectors. These limits apply throughout the national territory. Extra hours can be compensated with equivalent rest or, if the CCA provides for it, paid in excess.
How long do I have to wait to submit my resignation in Basel City?
The minimum notice period is: 1 month in the first year of employment; 2 months from the 2nd to the 9th year; 3 months from the 10th year onwards. These terms apply to the employer and the worker and are counted from the beginning of the month. The CCAs may provide for different terms, but increasingly favourable to the worker.
