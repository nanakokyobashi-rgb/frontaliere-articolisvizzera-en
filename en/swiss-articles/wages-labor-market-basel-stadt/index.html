The labor market in the canton of Basel-Stadt is regulated by an overlap of federal regulations and cantonal provisions which directly affect salaries, social contributions and employment conditions. Unlike neighboring countries, Switzerland does not set a minimum wage at the federal level. Not even the canton of Basel-Stadt imposes a uniform legal minimum wage: wages are formed through individual contracts and, where applicable, collective labor agreements (CCA) in the various sectors.

However, the mandatory contribution structure is standardized nationally. Each employee pays 5.3% of the gross salary in AVS/AI/IPG contributions (old age and disability insurance, compensation for loss of earnings). The employer pays an equivalent amount, bringing the total to 10.6%. These contributions are managed by the federal social security system and provide old age benefits (AVS), invalidity benefits (AI) and family benefits (IPG).

In addition to the AVS, there is accident insurance (AD/AC), which is mandatory for those who work in Basel City as in all cantons. The contribution is 0.7%–1.5% of the salary, depending on the risk sector.

Occupational pension provision (LPP)

BVG occupational pension provision is mandatory for wages above a coordinated threshold (the exact value for Basel-Stadt in 2026 has not yet been specified). Rates vary by age group: from 7% for those aged 25–34 up to 18% for those aged 55+. The employer covers at least 50% of the contribution.

The job market in Basel-Stadt benefits from a central geographic location in Europe: pharmaceutical, chemical, logistics and financial services sectors are present, although sector-specific hiring data is not standardized in this source. Employers operating in Basel-Stadt must respect the maximum working time of 45 hours/week if they work in industry, offices or sales, or 50 hours in other sectors.

The legal minimum holidays are 4 weeks per year. For those under 20 years old, 5 weeks are mandatory. These rights are guaranteed by the Federal Constitution and the Code of Obligations, and are valid throughout the country, therefore also in Basel-Stadt.

Operational details

How wages are structured in Switzerland

In Basel City, as in the rest of Switzerland, there is no legal minimum wage obligation at the cantonal level. This means that the salary is negotiated directly between employer and worker, or is regulated by a collective agreement if the sector so provides. Some Swiss sectors (construction, catering, healthcare, construction) have CCAs that set minimum wages. Basel-City, being an industrial and commercial canton, has strong CCA presences in the manufacturing and service sectors.

The cost of work for the employer is lower than the gross salary of the employee: he must add the AVS/AI contributions (10.6%), the accident insurance premiums (0.7%–1.5% depending on the sector), and the LPP contribution (minimum 50% of the rate prescribed by the fund). These fixed costs directly affect the total salary that the company must allocate for each position.

On the worker side, annual gross salary is reduced by AVS/AI personal contributions (5.3%) and by LPP contributions (7%–18% depending on age). Federal and cantonal taxes are deducted from these. Basel-City has its own cantonal tax rate: the system is progressive, and the amount depends on salary, marital status and number of children. It is not uncommon for a worker in Basel City to have his annual gross reduced by about 25%–35% due to taxes and mandatory contributions.

Key points

What to do if you are looking for work in Basel-Stadt

If you are looking for employment in the canton, it is useful to know in advance the salary structure that awaits you. When you receive a job offer, the gross salary indicated must cover your AVS/AI contributions (5.3%) and LPP (7%–18% depending on age). Always ask whether the employer provides LPP contributions above the legal minimum (50%), since many companies in Basel-Stadt pay higher amounts to attract talent.

Step 1: Verify the collective agreement for the sector Before accepting an offer, check whether your sector (chemical, pharmaceutical, services, logistics, etc.) has a CCA. If so, the minimum salary cannot fall below the amount established. You can check with the trade association or contact the national CCA consultation tool to find out whether the employer is bound.

Step 2: Calculate net salary and financial availability Use a salary calculator to estimate the net amount you will receive. Enter the gross salary offered, select the canton of Basel-Stadt, your age bracket and marital status. The result will tell you how much you will actually have available each month, once AVS/AI, LPP, taxes and cantonal/municipal charges have been deducted. Do not forget to add the cost of LAMal (monthly premiums, available on the cantonal office's website).

Step 3: Verify leave entitlements Make sure that the contract complies with the legal minimum: 4 weeks of vacation per year. If you are under 20, it is 5 weeks. Explicitly ask how many days of sick leave are covered by the employer (the legal minimum is 3 paid days, but many companies guarantee more).

Step 4: Take out LAMal within 3 months If you move to Basel-Stadt from another canton or from abroad, you have 3 months to take out health insurance. Ask the employer whether it provides information on the most affordable providers in the canton. Also check whether you are entitled to cantonal subsidies based on your income.

Step 5: Understand the tax structure Basel-Stadt has its own cantonal tax rate. Unlike other cantons, not all social deductions reduce federal taxable income. Use the tax calculator to get a realistic idea of how much you will pay. Bear in mind that there are standard deductions (rent, health insurance, mandatory contributions) and, if you are married or have children, additional tax relief.

Protection in case of unemployment

If you lose your job in Basel-Stadt, you are entitled to unemployment benefits (AD), paid for a maximum of 400 days (just over 52 weeks). The rate is 1.1% of the salary, up to an annual maximum that has not yet been specified in 2026. You must register with the cantonal employment office within 7 days of losing your job. The cantonal unemployment fund will calculate the benefit as a percentage of your last salary (generally 80% for the first 3 days of each week, followed by reduced percentages).

Finally, remember that the labour market in Basel-Stadt, as throughout Switzerland, is highly regulated by federal law. Supplementary cantonal rules (reduced working hours, additional rights for protected categories, special permits) may vary, but the core rights — vacation, contributions, protection in case of illness, minimum notice period — are uniform. Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a cantonal minimum wage in Basel-City?
No. Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage, nor does the canton of Basel-City provide for a uniform one by law. Wages are formed through individual contracts or, where in force, collective bargaining agreements (CCAs) in the individual sectors. If you work in a sector with CCA (e.g. chemistry, pharmacies, construction), the minimum wage is binding; otherwise, the employer and the worker agree on the wage.
How much is withheld from my gross salary in Basel City?
On average, between 25% and 35% of the gross is withheld for AVS/AI (5.3%), LPP (7%–18% depending on age), federal and cantonal taxes. Basel City has its own cantonal tax rates. The exact calculation depends on salary, age, marital status and number of children. Use a salary calculator for an accurate estimate.
What is the LPP and why is it withheld from my salary?
The LPP (Occupational Pension) is a mandatory old-age insurance managed by companies (or pension funds) in Basel City as in all of Switzerland. Covers the supplementary pension to the AVS. The contribution varies by age group: 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54), 18% (55+). The employer pays at least 50%; the rest is covered by you. It is not a tax, but a mandatory saving for your future retirement.
What are the maximum working hours per week in Basel City?
The legal maximum is 45 hours/week in industry, offices and sales; 50 hours in other sectors. These limits apply throughout the national territory. Extra hours can be compensated with equivalent rest or, if the CCA provides for it, paid in excess.
How long do I have to wait to submit my resignation in Basel City?
The minimum notice period is: 1 month in the first year of employment; 2 months from the 2nd to the 9th year; 3 months from the 10th year onwards. These terms apply to the employer and the worker and are counted from the beginning of the month. The CCAs may provide for different terms, but increasingly favourable to the worker.
