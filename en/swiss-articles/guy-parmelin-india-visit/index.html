Parmelin in India: focus on the economy and investment

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 October 2026

Federal Councillor Guy Parmelin in New Delhi to strengthen economic cooperation, intellectual property protection and migration agreements.

Context

TL;DR

  • Three-day state visit to India by Guy Parmelin.
  • Objective: strengthen bilateral economic cooperation.
  • Focus on intellectual property and investment agreements.
  • New agreements on migration, training and science.

Key facts

  • Who: Guy Parmelin, Federal Councillor
  • Where: New Delhi, India
  • Duration: 3 days
  • Partner: Narendra Modi, Indian Prime Minister
  • EFTA agreement: in force for one year

Federal Councillor Guy Parmelin arrived in New Delhi for a three-day official mission aimed at consolidating ties between Switzerland and India. The Swiss economy minister's agenda focuses on strategic pillars for the future of bilateral relations, with particular attention to trade and legal protection. During talks with Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, the federal councillor reiterated that the trade agreement between India and the EFTA countries represents an advantageous instrument for both parties. This agreement, which entered into force one year ago after 16 years of complex negotiations, aims to improve Swiss companies' access to the vast Indian market, guaranteeing greater legal certainty for domestic economic operators.

FDEA's strategic priorities

One of the mission's key points concerns the protection of intellectual property. Minister Parmelin emphasized that the results of discussions in this area are of fundamental importance for further stimulating Swiss investment in India. Bern hopes for the swift conclusion of a specific agreement on investment protection, a step considered essential to provide greater security to Swiss companies operating or intending to expand in the subcontinent. The visit is not limited to the economic and financial sphere alone, however: the delegations agreed on a declaration of intent covering various areas, including migration, talent mobility, specialized training and closer cooperation in the field of science. This integrated approach aims to create an ecosystem of shared opportunities, fostering the exchange of expertise between the two nations and paving the way for new synergies in high-value-added sectors, in line with federal internationalization strategies.

Operational details

The analysis of the economic impact of this mission reveals a clear interest on the part of the Indian government in strengthening specific sectors. Prime Minister Narendra Modi expressed the desire to attract new Swiss investments, explicitly citing the banking and insurance sectors, as well as food processing. This is a significant opportunity for Swiss companies, which can benefit from a more robust regulatory framework and easier access to local markets. The Indian authorities have also expressed their intention to increase exports to Switzerland of agricultural, pharmaceutical, textile, and engineering products, defining a trade framework that goes beyond the simple exchange of goods.

Synergies in key sectors

Cooperation extends to high-profile technological and industrial domains, such as defense production and the nuclear energy sector. This broadening of the areas of cooperation indicates a political will to elevate the partnership to a higher level, which also includes exchanges in the military sphere. For Swiss companies, particularly those active in financial services and the insurance sector, the stability of India's legal framework, supported by the EFTA agreement and the new discussions on intellectual property, represents a decisive factor for long-term planning. The management of costo della vita and global competitiveness require constant expansion into emerging markets with high potential. The alignment between Bern's federal policies and India's growth needs could translate into new jobs for specialized professionals and an increase in investment flows abroad. It is clear that, while the Federal Council works to protect national economic interests, the ability of individual companies to leverage these diplomatic channels will be crucial to the strategic success of the entire Swiss economic system, bringing indirect benefits also to the domestic labor market and the banking services sector.

Key points

For those operating in the Swiss economic context, monitoring developments in these agreements is essential to understand internationalization opportunities. Companies interested in exploring the Indian market can consult the official information provided by the Confederation regarding EFTA agreements and the legal protections available to investors. It is advisable to periodically check the progress of agreements on mobility and training, which could facilitate the secondment of staff or scientific collaborations with Indian partners. The procedure for companies intending to expand abroad always includes a preliminary analysis of the risks and benefits, supported by the advisory tools offered by chambers of commerce and consular representations.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main objectives of Parmelin's visit to India?
The visit's main objectives are to strengthen economic cooperation, protect intellectual property and conclude new agreements on migration, training and scientific cooperation, consolidating relations based on the EFTA agreement.
How does the EFTA agreement benefit Swiss companies in India?
The agreement, which entered into force one year after 16 years of negotiations, aims to improve Swiss companies' access to the Indian market and increase legal certainty, facilitating investment and trade.
What sectors did Prime Minister Modi mention for cooperation?
