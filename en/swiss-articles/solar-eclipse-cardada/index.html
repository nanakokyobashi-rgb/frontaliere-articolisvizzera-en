Operational details

The solar eclipse of August 12, 2026 was a unique and spectacular event observed by two hundred people in Cardada, a tourist resort located in the Canton of Ticino, about 1,500 meters above sea level. The effort of climbing to the top was rewarded by an extraordinary spectacle, with exceptional colors of the sunset.

The solar eclipse is a natural phenomenon that occurs when the moon is between the earth and the sun, blocking sunlight and creating a shadow on the earth's surface. This event was accurately predicted by astronomers, who calculated the position of the moon and sun to create a perfect shadow over the Ticino region.

The structure of the Ticino mountains has helped to create a unique atmosphere for the eclipse. The mountain tops blocked the sunlight, creating a deep and intense shadow that allowed you to observe the moon in all its splendor.

The solar eclipse is an event that requires some planning and preparation. Local authorities worked together with astronomers to create a contingency plan in case of bad weather or other adverse weather conditions. The emergency plan provides for the closure of roads and the creation of an evacuation plan in case of need.
