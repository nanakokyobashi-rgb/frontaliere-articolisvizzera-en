Key facts

GDP growth 2026 -> 1.7% GDP growth 2027 -> 1.6% Export forecast -> +1.5% Unemployment rate 2026 -> 3.1% Unemployment rate 2027 -> 3.0% Estimated inflation -> 0.6%

The forecasts on Swiss economic growth for the current year show an exceptionally strong upward revision, fueled by the favorable cyclical trend recorded in the second quarter. This was communicated by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO), whose officials emphasize that the national economy is poised to gain new momentum. Specifically, the head of SECO's Economic Policy Directorate, Roland Indergand, highlighted the magnitude of the revision compared to previous estimates. According to the updated data released by the federal agency, Switzerland's gross domestic product (GDP) should record an increase of 1.7% for the current year. This figure marks a clear improvement over the 0.9% that had been hypothesized in June. Regarding the medium term, the forecast of +1.6% already formulated for 2027 is instead confirmed in full. The positive trend of the Swiss economy finds confirmation in the dynamics of productive sectors and exchanges with foreign markets, offering an overall more solid macroeconomic picture for residents, businesses, and economic operators active on the national territory. This upward revision fits into a global context in evolution, where the Swiss industrial fabric's capacity to react continues to represent the main engine of national and cantonal economic development.

Operational details

L'evoluzione positiva della congiuntura economica nazionale si ripercuote in modo diretto anche sulle dinamiche del mercato del lavoro svizzero. Secondo le analisi e le proiezioni elaborate dalla SECO, il consolidamento della crescita economica determinerà una graduale ma costante diminuzione del tasso di disoccupazione su scala nazionale. In base alle stime ufficiali pubblicate, la disoccupazione dovrebbe attestarsi su una media annua del 3,1% per quanto riguarda il 2026. Per l'anno successivo, ovvero il 2027, le previsioni indicano un ulteriore calo, con il tasso destinato a scendere leggermente fino a raggiungere la soglia del 3,0%. Questa prospettiva di stabilizzazione e rafforzamento dell'occupazione offre un quadro rassicurante per la forza lavoro residente e per quanti operano nel mercato del lavoro elvetico, riducendo le tensioni occupazionali registrate in precedenti fasi di incertezza macroeconomica globale. Sul versante dell'andamento dei prezzi e del potere d'acquisto, i mercati a termine lasciano intravedere segnali favorevoli nei prossimi mesi, guidati in particolare da un atteso calo del corso del petrolio. Sulla base di questi elementi e dei dati attuali, la SECO conferma le proprie aspettative per quanto riguarda il carovita, stimando un tasso di inflazione fermo allo 0,6% sia per il 2026 sia per l'anno venturo. Tale livello di inflazione contenuto contribuisce a preservare il potere d'acquisto delle famiglie e dei consumatori in Svizzera, limitando l'impatto del rincaro sui beni di prima necessità e sui costi energetici generali.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the new forecast for Swiss GDP growth?
According to updated data released by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO), Switzerland's gross domestic product (GDP) is expected to increase by 1.7% for the current year. This figure marks a marked improvement compared to the 0.9% that had been assumed in June. As for the medium term, the 1.6% plus forecast already formulated for 2027 is fully confirmed.
What are the forecasts for the unemployment rate in Switzerland?
According to official estimates published by SECO, the consolidation of economic growth will result in a gradual but steady decline in the unemployment rate on a national scale. Unemployment is expected to average 3.1% per year by 2026. For the following year, or 2027, forecasts indicate a further decline, with the rate set to fall slightly to reach the 3.0% threshold.
What are the current estimates for inflation and exports?
SECO confirms its expectations with regard to life expectancy, estimating a firm inflation rate of 0.6% for both 2026 and the coming year, a level also supported by an expected decline in the oil price. As far as foreign trade is concerned, the scenario outlined speaks of an actual growth of 1.5% for Swiss goods exported, reversing previous forecasts that indicated a contraction.
