Totalisation of AVS and INPS contributions: how it works

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 6 July 2026

Practical guide to the aggregation of contributions between Switzerland and Italy: procedure, forms and calculation of the pension for those who have worked in both countries.

Context

TL;DR

  • Aggregation makes it possible to add together CH and IT contribution periods.
  • It applies to establish entitlement to a pension, not its amount.
  • The application must be submitted to the social security institution of the last country of residence.
  • The pension is calculated pro rata according to national laws.

Key facts

  • What: Aggregation of AVS and INPS insurance periods.
  • When: Upon reaching retirement age.
  • Where: Switzerland and Italy.
  • Who: AVS (Switzerland) and INPS (Italy).
  • Amount: Calculated pro rata based on the periods for which contributions were paid.

The procedure for aggregating social security contributions between Switzerland and Italy is governed by international social security treaties. For those who have pursued part of their professional career in Switzerland and part in Italy, this mechanism makes it possible to count periods of work completed in both territories toward meeting the minimum requirements necessary to obtain an old-age pension. It is important to emphasize that aggregation does not entail the physical transfer of the capital paid from one institution to the other, but rather a cumulative count of insurance periods to determine entitlement to the pension benefit.

How the pro-rata system works

The system is based on the principle of equal treatment and the preservation of acquired rights. When the worker reaches retirement age, each social security institution (AVS for Switzerland and INPS for Italy) calculates the theoretical pension that would be due if all periods had been paid exclusively into its own system. Subsequently, the pension is recalculated proportionally based on the actual duration of the insurance periods accrued in each of the two countries. In this way, the insured person receives two separate pensions, one Swiss and one Italian, which together provide overall social security coverage based on their entire working career on both sides of the border. Social security information is essential for properly planning the future.

Operational details

The practical analysis of aggregation requires careful attention to timing and proper documentation. Unlike the second pillar (LPP/BVG), which may sometimes be paid out as a lump sum under specific conditions, AVS contributions paid in Switzerland remain in the system until reaching the statutory retirement age. For those who have worked in Switzerland, AVS/AHV represents the first pillar, mandatory and based on the pay-as-you-go principle. Coordination with INPS requires the insured person to be able to prove their employment history through official contribution statements issued by the competent authorities of both countries.

Differences between entitlement requirements and benefit calculation

It is essential to distinguish between entitlement to a pension and its amount. Aggregation is used primarily to overcome the minimum contribution-years threshold required to qualify for a pension. If, for example, a worker has not reached the minimum number of years of contributions required under Italian legislation for an old-age pension, they may add the Swiss periods to meet that requirement. However, adding the periods does not artificially increase the pension’s monetary value, since each institution pays only the share for which it is responsible. It is advisable to regularly consult your AVS contribution statement to verify that all periods worked in Switzerland are correctly recorded. Coordination procedures prevent periods of employment from being counted twice, in compliance with the provisions of the Italian-Swiss agreement, which ensures the proper allocation of liabilities between the national pension funds.

Key points

To initiate the totalization procedure, the applicant must submit the formal application to the social security institution of the country in which they reside at the time of retirement. If the worker resides in Italy, the pension application must be submitted to INPS, clearly indicating that they have worked in Switzerland. INPS will be responsible for contacting the competent Swiss compensation fund to request certification of the insurance periods accrued abroad. The application must include all documentation attesting to the periods worked in Switzerland, including salary certificates and AVS account statements received over the years.

Operational retirement checklist

1. Request the AVS account statement from the relevant compensation fund. 2. Check your insurance record with INPS in Italy. 3. Submit the pension application in Italy (if resident there), specifying the foreign periods. 4. Keep all documentation relating to Swiss employment contracts. 5. Check the deadlines established by the applicable regulations to avoid delays in payment.

The procedure may take several months due to cross-checks between the institutions. It is useful to be prepared to provide clarifications or supplementary documents if requested by the offices. Frequently Asked Questions
Does the aggregation of AVS and INPS contributions increase the amount of the pension?
No, the aggregation mainly serves to reach the right to pension, adding the contribution periods accrued in Switzerland and Italy to meet the minimum requirements. The amount of the pension is calculated pro rata by each institution on the basis of the contributions actually paid into the respective system, without the aggregation modifying the value of past payments.
To whom do I submit the totalization application?
The application must be submitted to the social security institution of the state in which you reside at the time of retirement. If you reside in Italy, the application must be forwarded to INPS, indicating that you have worked in Switzerland; if you reside in Switzerland, the application must be submitted to the relevant AVS Compensation Fund.
What happens to the contributions paid in the second pillar (LPP) during the totalization?
The totalization concerns the first pillar (AVS). Contributions paid in the second pillar (LPP/BVG) follow different procedures. In many cases, LPP capital can be liquidated or transferred at the time of retirement, depending on the rules of the pension institution where you are insured and the laws in force.
