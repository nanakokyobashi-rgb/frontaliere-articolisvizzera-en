The air traffic system failure

On Tuesday afternoon, a technical problem affected Skyguide's systems, causing disruptions to Swiss air traffic. Tages-Anzeiger was the first to report the news: at Zurich Airport, some flights were grounded.

Takeoffs and landings at the airports in Zurich and Geneva were temporarily suspended. The interruption was brief, and operations then resumed at both airports. Skyguide nevertheless confirmed that it was operating at 50% of its capacity, a precautionary measure.

According to the company's statement, the reduction in capacity was caused by a failure in the system that provides controllers with a radar representation of air traffic. The statement also ruled out a cyberattack and assured that safety in Swiss airspace was guaranteed.

“Safety in Swiss airspace is guaranteed.”

Skyguide warned that reduced capacity could lead to delays. The text does not indicate the number of flights involved, the exact duration of the suspension or the number of delays recorded.

The company said it had mobilized a crisis team and intended to restore service as soon as possible, limiting the repercussions for air traffic. Skyguide also apologized to its partners and passengers.

Key points

Restoration and information still missing

Skyguide's communication describes a two-stage process. First, the precautionary reduction in capacity, followed by the resumption of operations at Zurich and Geneva airports. In parallel, the company activated a crisis team to address the service disruption and limit the impact on air traffic.

Skyguide stated that it aims to restore full capacity in the coming hours. The source therefore reports an operational objective, but does not state that full restoration has already occurred. This is also why the warning about possible delays after takeoffs and landings resume remains.
