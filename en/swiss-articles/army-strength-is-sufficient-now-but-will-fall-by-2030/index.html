Army strength is sufficient now but will fall by 2030

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 October 2026

As of March 1, 2026, the army numbers 147 017 personnel, enough for the statutory 100 000, but a decline below 130 000 is expected by 2030.

Context

In brief

  • 147,017 soldiers enlisted as of March 1, 2026
  • A decline below 130,000 units is expected by 2030

On October 6, 2026, in Bern, the 2026 Army Census was published. The survey photographs the forces as of March 1, 2026 and indicates that the formations today have a sufficient number of soldiers for the army to deliver its performance.

A snapshot as of March 1

The total is 147,017 soldiers enlisted in the formations. The number is enough to guarantee, in case of deployment, the regular strength of 100,000 soldiers. The picture changes on the 2030 horizon: the strength should drop to 130,000 units. The shift is linked to the discharge from service in 2028 and 2029 of the last two age classes subject to a 12‑year mandatory military service.

A short‑term indicator instead offers a positive signal. The number of soldiers available for ongoing training has increased by 2.5% compared to the previous year, with a particularly positive effect on the effective numbers of repeat courses.

In recruitment the picture is mixed. The total number of recruitments is falling, while the number of female soldiers still grows slightly. The source links this trend to the great interest that women continue to show for a career in the officer corps, which entails a longer mandatory military service, and considers a further slight increase possible.

The Federal Council adopted the related message in the session of September 9, 2026. The proposal to introduce a mandatory information day for women will now go to Parliament; the related popular vote should take place in 2028.

With the current 70 percent eligibility rate for military service and moderate demographic growth among those subject to the obligation to serve, it would be possible to guarantee the recruitment of new recruits. However, almost 12,000 people subject to the obligation leave service early, especially for civil service. The indicated answer is greater flexibility of training and career models.

In 2025 Parliament instructed the Federal Council to implement the «obligation to provide security service» to ensure personnel for the army and civil protection. On June 24, 2026 the Federal Council defined the reference parameters; consultation of the Cantons is underway.

Operational details

The challenge is the personnel timeline

The news distinguishes between two levels: the army's current ability to deliver its services and personnel availability in future years. For those living or working in Switzerland, the practical takeaway is to distinguish the current situation from forecasts and measures still undergoing an institutional process.

The 2.5% increase in personnel available for training does not offset the projected trajectory through 2030. These two data points measure different aspects: the first relates to current training compared to the previous year, with a positive impact particularly on refresher courses; the second concerns the evolution of overall personnel following the discharge of the last age classes under a 12-year obligation.

The growth in the number of female soldiers also unfolds in two stages. Interest in leadership careers is already noted as a positive factor, while the mandatory information day is still part of a message adopted by the Federal Council and submitted to Parliament. A popular vote is expected to take place in 2028; therefore, its outcome cannot be treated as a settled decision.

The nearly 12,000 early departures show that the issue is not solely about recruiting new members. Retention within the system is another factor noted by the source, particularly regarding the shift to civil service. More flexible training and career models are mentioned as a solution, but the press release does not yet specify how they will function, who will be eligible, or when they will take effect.

The 'obligation to provide security service' follows a separate path: the parliamentary mandate dates back to 2025, the Federal Council's parameters were set on June 24, 2026, and the consultation with the Cantons is still ongoing. The press release does not provide a final text or a new individual deadline. Those interested in the professional side can supplement this information with job advertisements, without treating that catalog as census data.

Key points

How to follow the dossier

To read the updates without confusing numbers, forecasts, and decisions, it's best to use a simple sequence.

The sequence to follow

  • Start from the reference date. The 2026 Army Census captures the actual numbers as of March 1, 2026, while the publication date is October 6, 2026. These two dates describe different moments.
  • Separate the actual strength from the regulatory strength. The first indicates the military personnel present in the formations; the second is the level of 100,000 military personnel to be guaranteed in case of deployment. This distinction helps to correctly understand any subsequent communications.
  • Keep the 2028 and 2029 calendars separate from the forecast for 2030. The first two dates concern the discharge of the last age groups with a 12-year military service obligation; 2030 is the horizon indicated for the expected strength.
  • Follow the path of the mandatory information day for women. The message was adopted by the Federal Council on September 9, 2026, must be submitted to Parliament, and the popular vote should take place in 2028. Until the process is completed, the proposal should not be presented as a definitive rule.
  • Check the progress of the "security service obligation." Parliament tasked the Federal Council in 2025, the reference parameters were defined on June 24, 2026, and the consultation with the cantons is underway. The source does not yet describe the individual effects of a possible final model.

For those subject to the "service obligation," the press release provides useful indicators but does not list forms, questions to be submitted, or personal deadlines. The 70% suitability rate, the nearly 12,000 early departures, and the intention to make training and career models more flexible help to follow the topic, but do not replace any future instructions.

The professional plan can be kept separate from the strength count: to explore available opportunities, you can consult the hiring companies, while the census remains the reference for army data.
