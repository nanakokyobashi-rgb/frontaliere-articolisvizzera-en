AI and Automation: A Plan for Federal Efficiency

The Federal Council Adopts the Report on October 2, 2026: Five Priorities for Streamlining Procedures and Reducing Wait Times.

On October 2, 2026, in Bern, the Federal Council adopted the report “Increasing the Efficiency of Administrative Processes through Process Automation and Artificial Intelligence (AI),” in fulfillment of Postulate 24.3582 Silberschmidt. The document sets out guidelines for a more efficient use of process automation and AI within the Federal Administration.

The goal is to simplify procedures, speed up the processing of applications and cases, and make more effective use of available resources. The report assigns these technologies a central role in the fulfillment of tasks: they should reduce administrative burdens, improve quality, and free up resources for more important tasks.

This push stems from demographic changes and the increasing volume of data. The Federal Administration will have to carry out the same or an increasing number of tasks with more limited human resources.

The Federal Vision

The Federal Council identifies automation and artificial intelligence as strategic digital resources for fulfilling legal mandates in an effective, efficient, and resilient manner. Automation should optimize processes and performance, support managers and staff, and pave the way for new solutions. However, the approach remains people-centered, reliable, and sustainable.

The five priorities are business management, with integrated and transparent processes; technology-assisted translation; the review of applications and cases, to reduce wait times and accelerate decision-making; data processing, through extraction, standardization, and harmonization; and business support processes, which also address the monitoring of financial transactions and procurement. For back-office operations, the report also mentions verifying the contractual basis, budget compliance, and internal regulations.

The report therefore envisages an initial internal phase: improving the overall view of the systems developed, purchased, or used by the Confederation; accelerating synergies between solutions; and promoting their large-scale implementation. Resources must be used sparingly and focused on cases with high scalability potential.

Operational details

Practical Impact on the Population and the Economy

The report outlines a course of action for the Federal Administration: to streamline the internal processes involved in handling applications and cases. For those who live or work in Switzerland, the potential benefit described is therefore linked to the quality and speed of their interactions with the Federal Administration.

From Application to Decision

The most concrete result highlighted in the report concerns processing times. If automation is applied to the review of applications and cases, it should help reduce wait times and speed up the decision-making process. The wording describes an objective: the potential benefit should be understood as a path toward improvement, not as a result already achieved for every individual or business.

Integrated case management can impact the continuity of the process and the transparency of the steps between offices. The report refers to a process that extends from the submission of a parliamentary motion to its implementation. The same logic of integration is linked to common solutions for translation: the expected effect is to simplify processes and support the multilingualism of the Federal Administration.

Another effect concerns the information infrastructure. Data extraction, standardization, and harmonization must create a reliable foundation for dependable automation. The stated goal is therefore to prepare more consistent data for automated processes; the press release does not provide figures or results that have already been achieved.

For the back office, the impact primarily concerns business support processes: the verification of financial transactions and purchases can be standardized and accelerated, taking into account accuracy, contractual basis, budget, and internal regulations. The decision to focus resources on scalable cases indicates that the rollout will prioritize solutions with greater scalability. Finally, the framework sets a clear limit on the pursuit of speed alone: automation and AI must support managers and staff, remain people-centered, reliable, and sustainable. According to the report, administrative efficiency therefore encompasses timeliness, quality, resilience, and the prudent use of resources.

Key points

How to Navigate the Operational Phase

The press release focuses on the guidelines and the initial internal phase. To follow the implementation without adding details not contained in the source, it is best to read the report as a roadmap of the processes that the Confederation intends to make more efficient.

Four Steps to Follow the Issue

1. Identify the type of process involved. The source distinguishes between case management, translation, review of applications and files, data processing, and business support. This classification helps clarify which outcome is being pursued.

2. If the topic is an application or a case, focus on the two indicators mentioned in the report: wait times and decision speed. These are the expected effects of automation; the text does not provide a one-size-fits-all deadline or a specific number of days to expect.

3. If the process involves multiple languages, consider computer-assisted translation as one of the common solutions that should streamline the steps and support the Federal Administration’s multilingualism. If it involves data, look instead at extraction, standardization, and harmonization: these are the foundations identified for reliable automation.

4. If the process involves financial transactions or procurement, verify which controls the report aims to make more standardized and faster: accuracy, contractual basis, budget compliance, and internal regulations. For those managing processes, this is the point to compare with existing repetitive tasks.

The first phase remains internal to the Confederation. It includes an overview of the AI and automation systems that have been developed, purchased, or are in use, as well as the acceleration of synergies and large-scale implementation. The stated criterion is frugality: resources should be concentrated on applications with high scalability potential.

The press release does not list documents to be submitted, offices to contact, or operational deadlines for the public. It is therefore not possible to derive a new personal procedure from the report. Frequently Asked Questions
When did the Federal Council adopt the report on automation and AI?
The report was adopted by the Federal Council in Bern on 2 October 2026. The document is entitled "Increasing the efficiency of administrative processes using process automation and artificial intelligence (AI)" and fulfils postulate 24.3582 Silberschmidt.
What are the five priority sectors identified in the report?
The five priorities are: business management, technology-assisted translation, examination of applications and practices, data processing (extraction, standardization and harmonization) and business support processes, including control of financial transactions and purchases.
What is the main goal for citizens and businesses?
The aim is to simplify procedures, speed up the processing of applications and files, and reduce waiting times. The report indicates that automation should contribute to faster decision-making while maintaining the quality and reliability of services.
