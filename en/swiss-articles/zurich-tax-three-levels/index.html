Taxes in Zurich: the three levels and how it works

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 16 October 2026

The Swiss tax system is structured at three levels: federal, cantonal and municipal.

Context

TL;DR

  • Each Canton has its own law and multiplier
  • The Municipality applies a multiplier to the cantonal tax
  • AFC/ESTV administers direct federal tax and VAT; the Canton administers the rest

Key facts

  • Levels → federal direct tax (IFD), cantonal tax, municipal tax

  • Cantonal authority → cantonal and municipal taxes
  • Municipal rule → multiplier applied to cantonal tax
  • Indexed figures → updated every year

Three levels make up the Swiss tax system: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. For the Canton of Zurich, rates, brackets and deductions must therefore be read within this framework, distinguishing what belongs to the Confederation from what depends on the Canton and the municipality.

Each Canton has its own law and its own multiplier. The municipal component uses the municipality's multiplier on the cantonal tax. Therefore, the municipal figure must be read together with the cantonal rule, without replacing it with a federal parameter.

The federal level is handled by AFC/ESTV, which is responsible for direct federal tax and VAT. Cantonal administrations, on the other hand, oversee cantonal and municipal taxes. This distinction also indicates the first point of reference: for the rules of the Canton of Zurich and for the municipal component, consult the cantonal tax administration, while AFC/ESTV remains the reference for IFD and VAT.

Responsibilities not to be confused

The tax framework exists alongside social security and health insurance, but does not coincide with them. UFAS/BSV is responsible for social security, including AVS/AHV and LPP/BVG, and does not set taxes. UST/BFS produces statistics and does not determine tax rates.

LAMal/KVG is mandatory health insurance for residents, with per-capita premiums and a premium reduction managed at cantonal level. It is neither a tax nor a wage contribution. The distinction serves to prevent including in the tax calculation items that belong to social security or healthcare.

For the rates, brackets and permitted deductions in the Canton of Zurich, the operational reference remains the official documentation of the cantonal tax administration. The same channel indicates the submission deadline to meet and the online portal to use. Figures subject to indexation must instead be verified for the reference year, because some amounts change every year.

Rates, brackets and deductions: how to avoid incorrect comparisons

The most concrete way to assess the tax burden in Zurich is to separate the three components before comparing rates or deductions. A value referring to direct federal tax does not automatically describe the cantonal share, while the municipal multiplier is applied to the cantonal tax.

The item to be applied must be verified in the relevant cantonal rules and not derived from a list referring to another Canton. The same care is needed for brackets: each Canton has its own law and the municipal result depends on the multiplier established for the Municipality.

Hypothetical scenario: a person takes a rate or deduction found for another Canton and transfers it into the Zurich tax return. The problem is not merely the number selected, but the tax level to which that number belongs. Before using it, it must be related back to the cantonal rule, the municipal component and, where necessary, direct federal tax.

Comparing disposable income and the cost of living then requires a second filter. AVS/AHV and LPP/BVG belong to social security and must not be confused with taxes. LAMal/KVG also follows a different logic: it is compulsory for residents, has per-capita premiums and provides for premiums that vary by Canton or region, in addition to a cantonal premium reduction. It is not a tax and is not a payroll contribution.

This separates the tax calculation from the family’s overall budget. A tax deduction, a social security contribution and a health insurance premium are not the same item, even when they all affect available funds. For accurate comparisons, the three components must therefore be read according to the authority and rule governing them.

Key points

How to proceed with the tax return in the Canton of Zurich

The practical procedure begins with correctly identifying the jurisdictions involved. Generic figures valid throughout Switzerland are not needed: what is needed is the cantonal law, the municipal multiplier and the official information for the year in question.

Five operational steps

1. Separate the three tax components: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. This distinction prevents using a federal figure for a cantonal or municipal component.

2. Identify the correct authority. For IFD and VAT, the reference is AFC/ESTV; for cantonal and municipal taxes, consult the cantonal tax administration.

3. Check the official source of the Canton of Zurich for the rates, brackets, permitted deductions and filing deadline. The date should not be reconstructed using a generic calendar or a rule from another Canton.

4. Use the online portal of the cantonal tax administration to follow the tax return procedure. The portal is the operational point of reference for the information and steps required by the cantonal authority.

5. Before submitting, check the reference year of the figures used. The figures indexed annually include the pillar 3a maximum, the LPP/BVG coordination threshold or deduction, the AD/AC maximum, the LAMal/KVG average premiums and the median salary.

Figures and deadlines to check

In tax planning, it is not advisable to automatically carry over amounts already used in a previous year. For indexed figures, the practical rule is always to verify the value applicable in the year in question with the official source. The same check applies to the filing deadline and the instructions on the cantonal portal.

If the calculation starts from the payslip, AVS/AHV, LPP/BVG and LAMal/KVG must be kept separate from taxes. This labeling makes the transition from income to available budget easier to read without turning premiums or contributions into tax rates.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the three levels of the Swiss tax system?
The Swiss tax system consists of three levels: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. Each Canton has its own law and its own multiplier, while the municipality applies a multiplier to the cantonal tax. It is essential to distinguish these components for a correct assessment of the tax burden.
Who administers the Direct Federal Tax (IFD) and VAT?
Direct Federal Tax (IFD) and VAT are managed at federal level by the Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV). For cantonal and municipal taxes, the point of reference is the competent cantonal tax administration, which provides specific information on tax rates, brackets and deductions.
How should deductions and tax brackets be taken into account in the Canton of Zurich?
In the Canton of Zurich, deductions and brackets must be verified in the relevant cantonal regulations. It is not correct to apply values or rules from other Cantons. The municipal multiplier is applied to the cantonal tax, making a combined reading of the cantonal and municipal rules essential, separate from the federal component.
Which elements should not be confused with tax calculation?

What are the operational steps for filing a tax return in Zurich?
For the tax return in Zurich, it is necessary to separate the three tax items (federal, cantonal, municipal), identify the correct authority for each, check tax rates, brackets, deductions and deadlines using the official cantonal source, use the online portal of the cantonal tax administration and verify the reference year for the indexed figures.
