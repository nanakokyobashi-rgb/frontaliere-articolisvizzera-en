Key facts

Permit: Settlement Permit C Standard residence: 10 years EU/EFTA residence: 5 years Canton: Glarus Requirements: Integration and language

The settlement permit C represents the highest level of residential stability for foreigners residing in the Canton of Glarus. Unlike short-term permits such as the L permit or the B residence permit, the C permit is not linked to a specific purpose and does not expire, offering the holder almost the same rights as Swiss citizens, with the exception of the right to vote and be elected.

Temporary residence criteria

According to the SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) guidelines, obtaining the C permit generally requires a period of uninterrupted residence in Switzerland. For citizens from third countries, the standard term is 10 years. However, for citizens of European Union (EU) member states or the European Free Trade Association (EFTA), this period is reduced to 5 years of residence.

The cantonal administration of Glarus verifies that the applicant has maintained a stable residence and has had no significant interruptions during the period of stay. This step is fundamental for transitioning from permit B to permit C, ensuring that the individual is stably integrated into the social and economic fabric of the canton. The verification of residence times is the first procedural filter before moving to the analysis of qualitative integration requirements.

Operational details

Integration and language skills

Obtaining the residence permit C in the Canton of Glarus does not depend exclusively on the time spent in the territory, but also on the degree of integration of the applicant. The cantonal authorities assess whether the foreigner is able to properly fit into the local community. A key element of this assessment is language skills. The applicant must demonstrate sufficient competence in the official language of the canton to be able to interact independently in daily and professional life.

Integration is also analysed through the absence of problems with justice and compliance with Swiss laws. A profile with criminal penalties or serious administrative issues may have their application rejected or deferred. In economic terms, financial independence plays an important role: those who are no longer dependent on social aid are more likely to obtain permission.

Early release scenarios

There are situations where the C permit can be issued before the 5 or 10 year deadlines are reached. These cases are defined on the basis of particularly high integration criteria. For example, an individual who demonstrates a perfect command of the local language, exemplary social integration and a stable employment contract could benefit from a fast-track procedure.

Key points

Application Procedure and Documents

To apply for the C residence permit in the Canton of Glarus, the interested party must submit a formal application to the cantonal migration office. The process usually begins with the completion of a specific form declaring the years of residence and any educational qualifications or language certifications held. It is necessary to attach documentation proving continuous residence, such as previous B permits and certificates of domicile.

Once the application is submitted, the cantonal authority begins the investigation. Social security records and criminal records are checked to ensure the applicant has no legal pending issues. If language requirements are not documented, the applicant may be required to take an official exam or present a recognized certificate of proficiency. Processing time varies depending on the completeness of the documentation provided.

Next Steps and Naturalization

Obtaining the C permit is a fundamental step for those who wish to apply for ordinary naturalization in the future. To start the citizenship process, possession of the C permit is required along with a total residence period of 10 years. In this calculation, some years of residence (between 8 and 18 years, depending on the case) may count double, facilitating the path toward Swiss citizenship.

C permit holders enjoy greater freedom of internal movement and simplified renewal procedures, as the permit is no longer linked to the validity of the employment contract. Frequently Asked Questions
How many years of residence are required for the C permit in Glarus?
EU/EFTA citizens are generally required to have 5 years of uninterrupted residence in Switzerland. For third-country nationals, the required period is normally 10 years.
Is it possible to obtain the C permit before the deadline?
Yes, early release is possible in cases of particularly high integration, demonstrated through excellent command of the local language and exemplary social and economic inclusion.
What are the language requirements for the C permit?
The applicant must demonstrate sufficient linguistic knowledge in the official language of the canton for daily and professional integration, as verified by the cantonal migration authorities.
