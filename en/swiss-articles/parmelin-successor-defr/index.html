Parmelin Leaves the Government: Race to Succeed Him in the SVP

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 October 2026

Guy Parmelin announced his resignation from the government on October 2, 2026; the SVP succession race closes on October 23, with the election scheduled for December 9, 2026.

In a statement on October 2, 2026, Guy Parmelin announced his resignation from the government. His departure kicks off the race for his successor, which will go to a vote in December during the winter session of Parliament. The election is scheduled for December 9. The contest will therefore play out among the candidates, the federal departments, and Parliament.

The seat is expected to remain with the SVP, Switzerland’s largest party. The Center, in two posts shared on X, acknowledged the SVP’s right to retain its two seats on the Federal Council. The PLR, on the other hand, expressed the hope that, for the succession to the Vaud representative, the party would consider candidates from French-speaking Switzerland.

The Internal Struggle Within the SVP

Albert Rösti already holds one of the two SVP seats. Parmelin’s presence in the government represented the SVP’s expansion from German-speaking Switzerland to French-speaking Switzerland and Ticino. Now the balance between a Federal Councilor from German-speaking Switzerland and one from French-speaking Switzerland is back at the center of the debate.

A few minutes after the announcement, the Zurich SVP chapter stated that it intends to consider fielding its own candidate. The chapter is described as the party’s most influential; among the names mentioned in the past is SVP State Councilor Natalie Rickli. National Councilors Thomas Aeschi and Céline Amaudruz have also been mentioned.

The succession is intertwined with a possible reshuffling of the departments. Beat Jans, the Socialist from Basel, could set his sights on the Department of Economic Affairs—currently led by Parmelin—while the source points to previous shifts between the departments of Justice and Police, and the Department of Home Affairs and Finance, which involved Jans and Karin Keller-Sutter.

The DEFR leaves open the agreements with the EU—approved by the Council of States but still pending in the National Council—trade negotiations with the United States following the Trump administration’s tariffs on Swiss exports, and issues related to agricultural policy and free trade. Candidates have until October 23 to apply; this will be followed by hearings, proposals from the selection committee, and a final decision by the parliamentary caucus. The winner will become the 124th member of the Federal Council in history.

Operational details

The potential succession at the helm of the DEFR should not be interpreted as a decision that has already been made regarding economic issues. The source highlights open questions, not their outcome: for those who live or work in Switzerland, the concrete reality is therefore the institutional timeline and the stage each matter has reached. No specific amounts or immediate measures for families are announced; automatically linking Parmelin’s resignation to an impact on personal finances would go beyond the text.

Three Scenarios to Keep Separate

The first concerns internal representation. A potential candidacy from the Zurich SVP would bring the question of the balance between German-speaking and French-speaking Switzerland back to the forefront. A candidate from the French-speaking region would make the wish expressed by the PLR a current issue. Neither of these two possibilities is presented as a decision that has already been made.

The second scenario concerns Beat Jans. If he were to aim for the Department of Economic Affairs, it would trigger a new reshuffling of departments; however, the source does not indicate what the final structure would be. The third scenario concerns the policy dossiers: the new head of the DEFR would inherit unresolved issues, but the text does not anticipate a solution regarding agreements with the EU, trade negotiations, agriculture, or free trade.

Key points

Operational Timeline

The procedure outlined by the UDC allows one to follow the process step by step, without confusing a name that has been mentioned with a formal candidacy. For the reader, the key is to note the deadlines and distinguish between those who propose and those who decide.

1. By October 23. This is the window set by the UDC for interested parties who wish to come forward. Until that time, the names mentioned remain part of the race, not the final outcome.

2. After the deadline. The process of candidate hearings begins. This is the step that precedes the selection committee’s proposals and allows for a distinction between the hearing phase and the final political decision.

3. Proposal phase. The selection committee formulates its proposals. The final say rests with the parliamentary caucus, which decides which candidate—or candidates—to field.

4. December 9. The election of the successor takes place. Whoever wins the support of Parliament becomes the 124th member in history of the Federal Council.

In the meantime, the reader can create a two-column checklist: the date of the procedure and the decision that was actually reached. The first row should list the October 23 deadline; the second, the start of the hearings; the third, the committee’s recommendations and the parliamentary group’s choice; and the last, the December 9 vote. This outline helps ensure you don’t jump from one evaluated candidacy to a final outcome.

After the vote, the next step involves reviewing the departmental structure: the source suggests the possibility of Beat Jans moving to the Department of Economic Affairs and, consequently, a reshuffling of portfolios. Only then will it be possible to assess the new appointment alongside the DEFR’s ongoing initiatives, without anticipating decisions that have not yet been announced.

Frequently Asked Questions
When is the deadline for submitting nominations to succeed Parmelin?
The deadline is October 23, 2026, by which time interested parties must come forward, as specified by the UDC. After this date, the process of candidate interviews will begin, followed by the selection committee’s recommendations and the final decision by the parliamentary caucus.
Who currently holds the DEFR, and which cases remain open?
Guy Parmelin currently heads the Department of Economic Affairs, Education, and Research (DEFR). Pending issues include agreements with the EU (approved by the Council of States but still pending approval by the National Council), trade negotiations with the United States following the Trump administration’s tariffs, future agricultural policy, and free trade—all of which are cited as matters still to be resolved.
When will the election for the new Federal Councilor take place, and what number will he or she hold in the history of the Federal Council?
The election is scheduled for December 9, 2026, during the winter session of Parliament. The winner will become the 124th member in the history of the Federal Council, taking office immediately after the parliamentary vote.
