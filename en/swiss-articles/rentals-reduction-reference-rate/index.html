Rents: rate down but the reduction is not automatic (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 September 2026

In 2025, the reference mortgage interest rate fell twice. Only 12% of tenants requested the reduction. The ASI reminds that it pays to actively request the reduction.

Context

In brief

  • In 2025, the key interest rate fell twice: to 1.5% (March) and 1.25% (September)
  • Every drop of 0.25% results in about a 3% reduction on the rent
  • Only 12% of tenants requested the second lowering (April 2026)
  • Automatic adjustment works one-way: owners raise, rarely lower

Key Facts

  • What: Rent reduction due to the fall in the reference mortgage rate
  • When: March and September 2025
  • Where: Throughout Switzerland (all regions)
  • Who: Swiss Tenants' Association (ASI); data from Zürcher Kantonalbank (ZKB)
  • Amount: Approx. 3% rent reduction for every 0.25% interest rate drop

In 2025, the reference mortgage rate fell twice, creating the conditions for rent reductions. However, the reality of Swiss tenants tells a different story: the majority have not yet submitted a formal request to the landlord. According to the Swiss Tenants' Association (ASI), it is time to remember a fundamental principle: the rent adjustment does not automatically take place downwards.

The rate used to calculate rents has remained stable at 1.25%, according to a statement from ASI today. But the road traveled so far has been anything but linear. In 2023, the rate had risen from 1.25% to 1.75%, generating price increases for millions of Swiss tenants. Then, in March 2025, the rate fell to 1.5%. Finally, last September it reached 1.25%, the pre-crisis level. Two cuts in one year: a real opportunity to obtain rent reductions.

Operational details

Why the system favors owners

The asymmetry between price increases and reductions is not accidental. When the rate rises, the owner has an immediate economic incentive: passing on the increase to the tenant protects his margins. The owner's statement arrives on time. When the rate falls, the incentive disappears: reducing the rent means lower income, and the owner without regulatory constraints can simply ignore the situation.

The ASI notes that landlords, while not applying the rent reduction, often compensate with flat-rate increases on ancillary costs (heating, maintenance, insurance). The net result for the tenant is a minimal or non-existent reduction, despite the fact that the interest rate has fallen. In other cases, the landlord is simply late, waiting for the right time to negotiate other contractual conditions.

For this reason, the ASI reminds tenants that it is not enough to wait: they must act. The same association calls on landlords to proactively pass on the reductions without compensating them with flat-rate increases on ancillary costs. But, in practice, the association argues, tenants have no choice but to take action themselves.

The calculator as a tool for awareness

Recognizing this reality, the ASI has made available a free calcolatore online, available on the www.asi-infoalloggio.ch website, which allows you to check in a few clicks if you are entitled to a discount and to quantify it. The tool asks for a few data: the current rent, the rate at the time of signing the contract, the current rate, and the date of the last adjustment. In seconds, the calculator provides the exact amount of the reduction due.

Key points

How to request and obtain the reduction

The first step is always the contract. Check to see if it contains an adjustment clause based on the reference mortgage rate. Not all contracts include it; if there is not, a reduction becomes more difficult (but not impossible) to enforce. With the contract at hand, access the ASI calculator and collect the necessary data: the original monthly fee, the rate at the time of subscription, the current rate. The calculator will do the rest.

Once the amount due has been calculated, document the result. Screenshots, printouts, notes: everything that proves the right to the reduction. Then, contact the owner. The ASI recommends sending a written request — by registered email or simple letter — that clearly mentions the change in the reference rate (from 1.75% to 1.25%, passing through 1.5%), the amount of the calculated reduction (about 3% for every 0.25% decrease), the date from which the reduction must take effect, and the date by which the owner should respond.

If the landlord does not respond, or refuses, contact the regional branch of the ASI in your canton. The association offers free advice and, if necessary, support in conciliation procedures. Many cantons have a conciliation authority — a public office that attempts to resolve disputes between tenant and landlord before the case reaches the court. It is a quick procedure, often free of charge for the tenant, and resolves most cases.

Don't put it off: your rights

Remember that the right to a reduction is retroactive. If the rate fell as early as March 2025, and we are now in September 2026, you may be entitled to an 18-month reduction in arrears. It is an additional incentive not to postpone: each month of inaction corresponds to months of full rent that you should have paid less.

Frequently Asked Questions
If the rate has fallen by 0.5% (from 1.75% to 1.25%), how much reduction am I entitled to?
Every 0.25% drop in the rate results in a reduction in rent of around 3%. A drop of 0.5% (two increments of 0.25%) is equivalent to a reduction of around 6% on the rent. If you pay CHF 1,000 per month, you would be entitled to a discount of around CHF 60 per month. Use the ASI calculator to check the exact amount based on your contract.
How do I check if the reference rate has really fallen?
The reference mortgage rate is recalculated every quarter on the average of the rates charged by the banks. The ASI and the www.asi-infoalloggio.ch website publish regular updates. In 2025, the rate fell twice: in March it fell to 1.5%, in September it fell to 1.25%. If your contract mentions the reference rate, these drops affect you directly.
What happens if the owner refuses to apply the reduction?
If the landlord does not respond or refuses, contact the regional branch of the ASI in your canton. The association offers free advice. Many cantons have a conciliation authority: a public office that resolves disputes between tenant and landlord without the need for a court. The procedure is often free of charge and resolves most cases quickly.
Can I request the reduction retroactively, for the past months?
Yes. The reduction is retroactive from the time the rate fell. If the rate fell in March 2025, and you apply for the reduction today (September 2026), you are entitled to an arrears discount of about 18 months. This makes it even more convenient to act without delay: each month of inaction corresponds to full rent that you would have paid less.
My contract does not mention the reference rate. Can I still ask for a reduction?
It depends. If the contract does not contain an explicit adjustment clause on the basis of the rate, a reduction becomes more difficult to enforce formally. However, the Tenancy Act (Code of Obligations, Art. 253 et seq.) allows adjustments on the basis of changes in the rate. Contact the regional section of the ASI for specific advice on your case.
