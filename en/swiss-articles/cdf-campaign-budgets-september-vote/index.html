CDF: 7.86 million for voting campaigns 27 September (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 28 August 2026

The Federal Financial Controller publishes the budgets: 5.51 million for neutrality, 2.35 million for food. Blocker 2+ mln, Economiesuisse 1.2 mln.

Context

In a nutshell

  • CDF: CHF 7.86 million total campaign budget vote 27 September
  • Neutrality initiative: 5.51 million (3.84 yes, 1.67 no)
  • Food initiative: 2.35 million (0.72 yes, 1.63 no)
  • Blocker 2+ mln, Economiesuisse 1.2 mln against neutrality

Key facts

  • What: Federal Voting Campaign Budget Statement
  • When: Votes 27 September, data communicated today
  • Where: Switzerland, federal level
  • Who: Federal Financial Control (FFC)
  • Amount: 7.86 million francs in total

The Federal Financial Control (FFC) has announced the budgets declared by the referendum committees in view of the federal votes on September 27. The total figure stands at CHF 7.86 million, with a clear prevalence of resources concentrated on the Neutrality Initiative, which alone absorbs CHF 5.51 million. The Food Initiative collects the remaining 2.35 million. The data, as specified by the CDF, are not definitive: the authority has yet to carry out additional checks and recalls that sometimes the same payment can be declared twice, first as own funds and then as a donation to another organisation.

Marked asymmetry on the Neutrality Initiative

The "yes" side of the initiative "Safeguarding Swiss Neutrality" has 3.84 million, financed by the "Preservation of Swiss Neutrality" committee. Two donors weigh in decisively: former UDC Federal Councillor Christoph Blocher paid out of pocket

Operational details

The disparity of resources between the two fronts on each initiative offers a concrete glimpse into the balances of economic power behind the Swiss referendum campaigns. On neutrality, the ratio is about 2.3 to 1 in favor of "yes", driven by two well-known private actors; on food, the ratio is about 2.3 to 1 for "no", with the weight of agricultural and agro-industrial organizations. This asymmetry is not a new phenomenon, but the CDF numbers make it measurable and transparent to the electorate.

Transparency and limits of the current system

The reporting mechanism managed by the CDF represents the main instrument of financial accountability in the Swiss direct democracy. However, the CDF itself points out two structural limits: the figures are not definitive at the time of publication and the double counting of certain payments can artificially inflate the totals. For the citizen, this means that the data should be read as a provisional photograph, not as a certified balance sheet. The Federal Statistical Office (UST/BFS) does not intervene in this survey, which remains the exclusive competence of the CDF.

Key points

For voters who want to orient themselves before September 27, consulting the CDF's online register is the first operational step: it lists the names of all donors above the legal threshold and their allocation to the respective committees. The procedure is accessible without authentication and allows searches by initiative, committee or donor. Those who wish to delve into the content of the two texts under vote can find the official explanations from the Federal Chancellery and the opinions of the Federal Council and Parliament on the ch.ch portal.

What to do concretely in the coming weeks

1. Verify the updated data on the CDF website in the weeks leading up to the vote, as supplementary declarations may change the totals. 2. Read the explanatory leaflets sent to all eligible voters: they contain the full text, official arguments, and voting recommendations. 3. Compare the positions of the political forces represented in Parliament and of the reference economic and social organisations. 4. For citizens abroad, ensure you have received the voting material in time; otherwise contact the competent Swiss diplomatic representation.

How much is the total budget declared for the votes on September 27?

How are the funds distributed between the two initiatives on the ballot?
The Neutrality Initiative absorbs 5.51 million (compared to 3.84 million, compared to 1.67 million). The Food Initiative raises 2.35 million (compared to 720,000 francs, compared to 1.63 million). On each object, a ratio of about 2.3 to 1 is observed between the opposing fronts.

What are the legal thresholds for the declaration of funding?
The law imposes the declaration for campaigns with a budget of more than 50,000 francs and for individual donations of more than 15,000 francs. The names of donors above the threshold are published online by the CDF in a register accessible without authentication.
