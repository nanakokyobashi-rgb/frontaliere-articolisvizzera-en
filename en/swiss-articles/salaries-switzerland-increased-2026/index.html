Travail.Suisse calls for a general increase in wages in Switzerland (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 17 August 2026

Travail.Suisse is asking for a 2% increase for all workers, but the negotiations are going to be difficult.

Context

The trade union organisation Travail.Suisse opened the dances by calling for an overall increase of 2% for all workers. For its part, the Swiss Union of Entrepreneurs (USI) believes an average increase of 1% is plausible. The 2% claim is an average and varies across sectors, Travail.Suisse explained.

The president of Travail.Suisse, Mario Fehr, said that “wage increases are necessary to maintain workers' purchasing power.” According to Fehr, a 2% increase would be enough to cover inflation and ensure greater economic stability.

According to estimates by Travail.Suisse, an increase of 2% would result in an increase of about CHF 1,200 per year for a worker with a salary of CHF 60,000 per year. This means that a worker earning 60,000 francs a year could receive an increase of about 1,200 francs a year, bringing his salary to 61,200 francs a year.

USI's president, Heinz Karrer, expressed concerns about rising costs for businesses. According to Karrer, a 2% increase would be too high and could result in reduced working hours or reduced staff. USI proposed an average increase of 1%, which would be more sustainable for businesses.

Operational details

The wage situation in Switzerland is complex and constantly evolving. Travail.Suisse's request for a 2% increase was met with skepticism from USI, which believes an average increase of 1% is plausible. The issue is further complicated by the fact that the negotiations promise to be difficult.

Switzerland is known for its stable economy and high wages, but demand for growth is growing due to inflation and a lack of affordable housing. According to 2022 data, the cost of living in Switzerland increased by 2.5% compared to the previous year, while average wages rose by 2.2%.

Travail.Suisse's request for a 2% increase was submitted in response to the growth in wage inequality. According to 2022 data, 10% of Swiss workers earn more than CHF 10,000 per month, while 50% of workers earn less than CHF 5,000 per month. Wage inequality is a serious problem in Switzerland, and the call for a 2% increase is an attempt to reduce inequality and ensure fair wage growth.

Key points

The Swiss Confederation is known for its economic stability and its ability to attract foreign investment, but Swiss households are facing an increasingly precarious financial situation. The demand for wage increases made by Travail.Suisse, the main Swiss union, is a clear sign of the difficulty of coping with daily expenses.

To justify its claim, Travail.Suisse highlights a stagnation in real wages over the last ten years. According to data from the Federal Statistical Office (FSO), Swiss wages rose by only 10.4% between 2010 and 2020, while inflation rose by 25.6% over the same period. This situation has led to a deterioration in the financial situation of households, which have to deal with an increase in prices and a reduction in their purchasing power.

USI, the Swiss Association of Entrepreneurs, presents a different analysis, judging the trade union demand disconnected from the economic reality, characterized by weak growth and geopolitical uncertainties. According to USI, Switzerland is a country where competition is high and demand for labour is low, leading to a labour market characterised by strong competition among economic operators.
