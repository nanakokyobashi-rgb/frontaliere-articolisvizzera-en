Context

In a nutshell

  • Swiss B permit: renewable residence for those working in Switzerland
  • Managed by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) and the cantons
  • Allows occupational flexibility unlike the L permit (short duration)

Key facts

  • What: Residence permit B (legal SEM category for renewable stay)
  • Competent authority: Migration Office of the Canton of Appenzello Esterno
  • Nature: Renewable, linked to a work activity in Switzerland
  • Permitted difference L: L limited to 1 year and a specific employer
  • Permit difference C: C requires 10 years residence for non-EU nationals (5 for EU/EFTA)

Residence permit B is a legal category of Swiss residence managed by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) that allows foreign nationals to live and work in Switzerland on a renewable basis. At cantonal level, the migration office of Appenzello Esterno is the competent authority for the issuance, renewal and modification of B permits for those residing in the territory. Unlike other categories of residence, the B permit offers a balance between employment flexibility and administrative stability in the medium term.

Operational details

Basic requirements for permit B

To obtain a B permit in External Appenzell, the applicant must demonstrate that they possess three essential elements: a valid employment contract with an employer registered in Switzerland, a regular housing situation, and compulsory health insurance coverage (LAMal). The employment contract must be authentic and confirm an employment that allows the applicant to support themselves during their stay. There is no federal minimum wage in Switzerland, although some cantons have introduced their own. The amount of the salary is not in itself decisive, but must be sufficient to cover the cost of local living and to pay health insurance premiums.

With regard to accommodation, the federal lease law (Articles 253 et seq. of the Code of Obligations) applies uniformly in all Swiss cantons. The rental contract must be regular, the security deposit must not exceed 3 months of rent and must be paid into a restricted account in the tenant's name. This protection also applies to foreign citizens holding B permits.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure for the initial application

Phase 1: Preparation of documentation Before going to the migration office, gather all necessary documentation in original or certified copy. Verify that the employment contract is signed by both parties, that the rental agreement contains the details of the landlord and the tenant, and that the LAMal (health insurance) is effectively subscribed (the insurance company must issue a proof of enrollment). Also prepare a copy of your passport and fill out the application form available at the office or on the cantonal website.

Phase 2: Submission of the application Contact the Appenzell Ausserrhoden migration office directly to check opening hours and whether an appointment is necessary. There is no unified national online portal; each canton manages applications independently. Submit the application in person along with the complete documentation. The office will provide a submission receipt with the date and case number.

Phase 3: Processing of the application The migration office will begin examining the documentation. Generally, the processing time varies from 3 to 6 weeks, depending on the complexity and completeness of the documentation. If any document is missing or if additional checks are required (such as contacts with the employer), the processing time will be extended. The office will notify the applicant if additional documents are needed.

Phase 4: Decision and collection of the permit Once the application is approved, the office will communicate the positive outcome and set a date for the collection of the permit. The holder must collect the permit within the indicated deadline. The permit is an official document that must be kept with care; in case of loss, a duplicate must be requested from the migration office.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the main difference between an L, B and C permit in Switzerland?
The L permit is a short duration valid for up to 1 year for a specific work activity at a single employer. Permit B is a renewable residence that allows the change of employer during the period of validity. The C permit represents permanent residence and requires a minimum residence of 10 years in Switzerland for third-country nationals (5 years for EU/EFTA nationals). Permit B offers intermediate flexibility between L and C.
What documents are needed to apply for a B permit to Appenzello Esterno?
Authentic employment contract, rental agreement or document of ownership of the accommodation, proof of registration with LAMal (compulsory health insurance), passport or valid identity document are required. The migration office may require additional documents such as a certificate of good conduct, extract from the criminal record, or tax returns based on the individual profile.
How long does it take to get a B permit?
The processing time usually varies from 3 to 6 weeks, depending on the completeness of the documentation and the need for additional checks. It is advisable to contact the migration office of Appenzello Esterno directly to check the specific times and see if an appointment is necessary. Complete and correct documentation speeds up the process.
What if I lose my job while I have a valid B permit?
The B permit remains valid until it expires, but it is bound to the exercise of a work activity. If you remain unemployed for an extended period without finding new employment, the migration office may refuse to renew your permit or request repatriation. It is important to inform the office promptly of any change in the work situation.
How much does LAMal cost and how can I reduce the premium?
LAMal premiums vary by canton and region and are based on individual per capita premiums; there are no fixed rates. The deductible can vary from CHF 300 to 2,500 depending on the choice. The canton of Appenzello Esterno, like all Swiss cantons, offers subsidies (premium reductions) for those with moderate or low incomes. Check with the cantonal social office to see if you have access to benefits.
