Context

In short - The G permit is required for border workers working in Switzerland. - Provides for specific residence and work requirements. - Duration and renewal vary according to cantonal regulations. ## Key facts - What: Residence permit for frontier workers - When: in force according to current regulations - Where: all of Switzerland, with cantonal differences - Who: frontier workers from abroad - Amount: not yet specified The G permit represents the official document that allows workers residing abroad but employed in Switzerland to regularly enter the country for professional reasons. Federal legislation defines the criteria for obtaining this permit, which must be requested from the competent cantonal authorities. The validity of the permit is generally annual, with the possibility of renewal based on the employment contract and the personal situation of the applicant. ### Requirements and validity of the G permit The G permit is intended for those who work in Switzerland but reside in a neighbouring country or in a State that allows regular transit. Among the main requirements is proof of a valid employment contract and official residence outside Swiss territory, but with daily or at least weekly return. Operational details

Obtaining and managing the G permit has considerable practical implications for frontier workers and for the Swiss authorities at national and cantonal level. The permit regulates not only the possibility of working, but also affects tax and social security aspects, such as registration with the AVS/AHV, the payment of LPP/BVG contributions and LAMal/KVG health coverage. ### Tax and social security implications The worker with a G permit is subject to tax at source, which varies according to the canton of employment. The rates and methods of taxation are often subject to updates, with direct effects on the net income received by border crossers. In addition, the G permit entails the obligation to register with the Swiss social security, thus guaranteeing the rights regarding AVS, LPP and other social coverage. ### Comparison with other types of permits Compared to the B permit, intended for residents in Switzerland, the G permit is characterized by its cross-border nature, with an obligation to return regularly to the country of residence. This involves a series of specific requirements, both bureaucratic and practical, which vary slightly from canton to canton, making it essential to carefully check local provisions to avoid compliance problems.
