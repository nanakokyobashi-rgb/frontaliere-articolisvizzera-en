2027 Budget: A Deficit of 98.5 Million and Controversy

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 30 September 2026

The State Council presents a budget with a deficit of 98.5 million. The Greens criticize the measure, calling it electoral and lacking investment.

On September 30, 2026, in Bellinzona, the 2027 budget presented by the State Council was described by the Greens of Ticino as an “election budget.” The proposal projects an operating deficit of 98.5 million francs. This assessment was communicated that evening in a statement signed by Samantha Bourgoin, the party’s co-coordinator.

According to the environmentalist party, the document relies primarily on spending cuts and does not provide sufficient investments to address future challenges. The statement also criticizes the fact that no tax increases are planned, not even for those who could afford the burden. Instead of addressing revenue, the budget focuses on spending cuts that, according to the Greens, affect services, staff, asylum, vulnerable individuals, and municipalities one by one.

In terms of investments, the party points out the absence of measures aimed at helping citizens and businesses face two explicitly identified challenges: the climate crisis and the aging population.

The Postponed Shift

The Greens cite studies comparing Ticino’s spending with that of other cantons. In their view, these comparisons have revealed the limitations of the approach attributed to the majority parties—summarized in the refrain of tax breaks for the wealthy and cuts to wasteful government spending. Unilateral solutions, they argue, are not enough. The statement adds that the State Council had already begun last year—albeit tentatively—to take action on the revenue side as well.

The shift in course has been postponed until the start of the next legislative term. The government plans a package to rebalance the 2028–2030 Financial Plan for current operations. It will need to address spending through additional structural measures and sectoral reforms, consider revenue sources, and explore possible adjustments to financial relations with the Confederation and the municipalities. The Greens say that, for now, they will analyze the budget in detail, seeking to invest in the future and protect the most vulnerable people, given the Grand Council’s limited room for maneuver. Operational details

When reviewing the budget, it is important to distinguish between the short term and the reforms promised for the future. The 2027 plan is criticized as a temporary measure; the shift in policy is postponed until the start of the next legislative term; the announced package concerns day-to-day management for 2028–2030. This sequence shifts the focus from annual figures to decisions on fiscal rebalancing.

For those living in Ticino, the most concrete point is the list of sectors that the Greens say will be affected: services, personnel, asylum, vulnerable individuals, and municipalities. The source does not assign a separate amount to each item. It is therefore not possible to convert the overall deficit into a figure per household, business, or municipality without adding data that is missing from the note.

The report takes two directions. The government outlines spending, reforms, and financial reports; the Greens call for a review of the tax system and deem unilateral solutions insufficient. The debate thus pits spending cuts against the search for new revenue, while investments in climate action and aging remain an unquantified political demand.

The absence of a tax increase in 2027 does not, according to the source’s analysis, mean that the revenue issue has been resolved: that topic is included in the chapters of the next phase. This distinction is also useful to avoid confusing the canton’s budget with the costo della vita in Svizzera, which follows a different framework.

The Greens consider it difficult, in the short term, to change financial flows with the municipalities—following the total failure of Ticino 2020—or with the Confederation, because a revision of fiscal equalization is not imminent. For the purposes of this analysis, these channels must be kept separate from internal spending measures.

Key points

The operational part involves following the path outlined by the source, without jumping ahead to decisions that the text addresses later on. A resident, a business, or a municipality can create a monitoring report in five steps.

Five Operational Steps

1. Set the 2027 target. Record the budget as an annual report and separate the expense items listed in the note: services, personnel, childcare, vulnerable individuals, and municipalities.

2. Note what is not planned. The Greens’ note states that no tax increases are planned, not even for those who could bear the burden. Therefore, do not include any tax changes in this phase other than those indicated.

3. Isolate the investment section. In the political debate, verify whether the proposals address support for citizens and businesses in light of the climate crisis and the aging population.

4. Follow the parliamentary process. A useful point of reference is the leeway granted to the Grand Council, which the Greens consider insufficient; take note of the positions that emerge from the analysis of the budget.

5. Set the schedule for the next phase. At the start of the next legislative term, review the 2028–2030 budget package across the four areas indicated: spending, sectoral reforms, revenue, and financial relations with the Confederation and municipalities.

How to Avoid Premature Interpretations

This sequence helps keep measures potentially under discussion for 2027 separate from the structural measures announced for the 2028–2030 budget period. It also avoids turning the overall deficit into an individual cost: the document lists areas and guidelines but does not assign a specific figure to each service, person, or municipality. On the fiscal front, a discussion of revenue remains distinct from the absence of tax increases envisaged in the budget outlined by the Greens.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the amount of the 2027 budget deficit?
The 2027 draft budget, presented by the Council of State on 30 September 2026 in Bellinzona, forecasts an operating deficit of 98.5 million francs. The document was criticized by the Greens of Ticino, who called it an 'election-year budget' based predominantly on spending cuts rather than structural investments to address future challenges, such as the climate crisis and the ageing population.
Which sectors will be affected by the cuts according to the Greens?
According to the statement signed by Samantha Bourgoin, co-coordinator of the Greens of Ticino, the spending reductions planned by the Government will affect several sensitive areas. Specifically, the cuts indicated concern benefits, staff, asylum, vulnerable people and municipalities. The environmentalist party also contests the lack of tax increases, including for income brackets that could have borne a greater burden.
When is a change of pace in cantonal finances expected?
The Government has postponed the change of course until the beginning of the next legislative term. In fact, a rebalancing package is planned as part of the Financial Plan for current operations 2028-2030. This plan will have to address spending through structural measures and sectoral reforms, as well as provide for a review of revenue and an assessment of financial relations with the Confederation and the Municipalities.
