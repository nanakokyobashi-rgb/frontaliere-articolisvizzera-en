Compass Initiative: the Government proposes to reject it

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 August 2026

The Federal Council recommends that Parliament reject the Compass Initiative without counter-proposals. At the center is the node on future Bilaterals III.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The Government rejects the Compass Initiative without counterproposal.
  • The proposal aims to extend the mandatory referendum to international treaties.
  • Delivered over 111,000 valid signatures in 2025.
  • Central node: voting procedures on future Bilateral agreements III.

Key facts

  • What: Recommendation to reject the Compass Initiative.
  • When: Decision in principle confirmed on Wednesday.
  • Where: Bern, Federal Council.
  • Who: Federal Council, Parliament, initiative promoters.
  • Amount: Over 111,000 valid signatures delivered.

The Federal Council has officially recommended to Parliament to reject the so-called "Compass Initiative", without advancing any counter-proposal, neither direct nor indirect. The decision, matured and reiterated last Wednesday on the basis of an orientation already consolidated on 26 November last year, marks a clear break point between the Executive and the promoters of the initiative. Thelatter, launched in 2024 and deposited in 2025 with the support of over 111,000 valid signatures, proposes to modify the constitutional rules relating to the process of approving international treaties. In particular, the text aims to impose a mandatory referendum for all agreements that provide for the transposition of rules of law. Operational details

The analysis of the practical implications regarding the rejection of the 'Bussola' Initiative reveals a deep conflict between the need for greater direct democratic participation and the necessity of maintaining efficient governance in international relations. The promoters' proposal is not limited to a procedural change, but touches the very essence of managing international treaties that have a direct impact on the cost of living and the labor market in Switzerland. Had the initiative been accepted, every future normative international agreement would have undergone a much more burdensome and uncertain ratification process. For the citizen, this would mean periodically facing calls to the polls with qualified majorities, making the negotiation of long-term agreements extremely complex. The Government fears that such a constraint would turn every agreement, even technical ones, into a field of total political confrontation, paralyzing diplomatic activity. From the perspective of economic stability, the Federal Council's choice to favor the optional referendum allows for better management of the transposition of European regulations that influence key sectors such as the free movement of persons or the recognition of professional qualifications. Without the 'Bussola' Initiative, current procedures remain unchanged: the salary and tax calculator will not undergo direct variations, but the overall regulatory framework remains anchored to existing practices. It is important to consider that, while the promoters argue for the need to vote first on the 'rules of the game' (the initiative) and only subsequently on the contents of the Bilateral III agreements, the Executive believes this sequence is a pretext. The major concern relates to the transitional provision included in the text of the initiative, which specifically and targetedly aims to block or condition the approval of the EU package.

Future scenarios and legislative impact

If Parliament were to follow the Government's recommendation and reject the text, the current ratification practice would be confirmed. This scenario guarantees continuity in bilateral relations but keeps political tension high between forces calling for stricter control over international agreements and those that see European integration as an indispensable engine for national economic prosperity. The management of dossiers, including those related to pension and labor regulations, will not see immediate procedural changes.

Frequently Asked Questions
What exactly did the Compass Initiative propose?
The initiative called for amending the Constitution to extend the use of compulsory referendums to all international treaties that provide for the transposition of rules of law. This change, which would require the approval of the people and the Cantons, was explicitly aimed at future Bilateral III agreements with the European Union.
Why is the Federal Council against it?
The Government considers the initiative unclear and instrumental, since it was formulated to influence a specific case (Bilateral III). According to the Executive, the mandatory referendum is a democratic pillar that should not be changed for short-term reasons, preferring to keep the referendum optional to manage relations with the EU.
What will be the next step for the initiative?
After the Federal Council's recommendation for rejection, it will be up to Parliament to discuss and vote on the proposal. The Executive has asked to reject the initiative without proposing counter-projects, a position that will be at the center of the debate in the next sessions of the Federal Chambers.
