Volkswagen cuts another 50,000 jobs (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 September 2026

The Volkswagen Group has announced a restructuring plan that envisages the cutting of an additional 50,000 jobs by 2030.

Context

In brief

  • Volkswagen to cut 50,000 jobs by 2030
  • Total job losses: 100,000 (15% of global workforce)
  • Restructuring plan approved by management and unions

Key facts

  • What: Volkswagen to cut 50,000 jobs
  • When: By 2030
  • Where: Production sites in Emden, Zwickau, Hannover (VW) and Neckarsulm (Audi)
  • Who: Volkswagen Group
  • Amount: 100,000 jobs in total

Volkswagen has announced a harsh restructuring plan that involves cutting an additional 50,000 jobs by 2030. This brings the total job losses to 100,000, approximately 15% of the global workforce. The plan was approved on Thursday and agreed upon by management and unions.

The most affected production sites will be those in Emden, Zwickau, Hannover for VW, and Neckarsulm for Audi. There is no talk of closing these plants at the moment, but the possibility remains on the table.

Implications for the Volkswagen Group

In addition to reducing the workforce, the “Future Plan 2030” aims to halve its model range by 2035. According to the economic magazine Wirtschaftswoche, Volkswagen could withdraw the Spanish brand Seat from the market, with its closure expected by 2029 at the latest.

Chairman of the Supervisory Board Hans Dieter Pötsch stated that the implementation of the new plan “will ensure the long-term sustainability and competitiveness of the Volkswagen Group.”

Operational details

Practical Analysis

Volkswagen's restructuring plan has significant implications not only for the group's employees but also for the global automotive industry. The reduction in the range of models and the possible closure of the Seat brand could have repercussions on the electric and hybrid vehicle market, sectors in which Volkswagen is heavily investing.

Impact on Production Sites

The production sites in Emden, Zwickau, Hannover, and Neckarsulm will be the most affected. Although immediate closures are not being discussed, the possibility remains on the table, which could lead to further uncertainty for workers and local communities.

Future of Brands

The possible closure of the Seat brand by 2029 could have a significant impact on the market for compact and affordable vehicles. Seat is known for its economical and innovative models, and its disappearance could leave a void in this market segment.

Sustainability and Competitiveness

Chairman of the Supervisory Board Hans Dieter Pötsch emphasized that the new plan will ensure the long-term sustainability and competitiveness of the Volkswagen Group. This implies that the group is seeking to better position itself in the global market, despite current difficulties.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before the announcement of the restructuring plan, Volkswagen had already cut 50,000 jobs. The current plan brings the total to 100,000, representing a significant reduction in the global workforce. This comparison shows the extent of the measures taken by the group to address current economic challenges.
