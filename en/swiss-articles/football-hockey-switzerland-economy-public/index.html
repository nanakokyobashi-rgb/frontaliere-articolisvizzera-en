The Swiss ice hockey championship officially kicks off on Tuesday evening, a sport that sparks great passion in Switzerland but has to contend with the competition of football, a discipline that has won over a few more fans in recent years. The radio broadcast SEIDISERA compared the two sports, hockey and football, analyzing financial and audience data. In terms of revenue, football outperforms hockey. The Swiss Football League published data for the 2024-2025 season, in which the twelve Super League clubs generated 440 million francs in turnover. For hockey, not all financial data from the clubs is available, but an estimate can be made taking into account that there are fourteen participating clubs: this brings the direct turnover to around 300 million francs.

…

Operational details

On the front of public attendance and stadiums, economic and statistical analysis offers insights of great interest to understand the bond between fans and their respective sports disciplines within the Swiss national context. In the football championship, the recorded average was 13,000 people per match, while in ice hockey the average stands at 7,500 spectators per game. Despite the difference in the average per single event, during the examined season the overall total of spectators was practically equal, reaching 2.7 million attendances for both football and hockey. However, this tie in total audience numbers must be read in light of the competition calendar: ice hockey in the season under review played as many as 364 matches, compared to the 228 matches played in the football championship.

…

Key points

A determining aspect that profoundly differentiates the two sporting worlds concerns the sense of territorial belonging and the perceived national identity of fans in the stands. The hockey fan finds a strong national and local identity in their favorite team. This bond is also favored by the composition of the rosters: the majority of players fielded in hockey teams are of Swiss nationality, as regulations establish that foreign players who can take the field are limited to a maximum of six units. Conversely, in the football championship, the percentage of foreign players in the squads can reach up to 60%. The situation takes on even more marked contours when analyzing the geographical origin of athletes in club teams.

Connection with the territory and economic perspectives

Many ice hockey teams in Switzerland regularly feature players coming directly from the region where the club is based. Taking the realities of Lugano and Ambrì Piotta as examples, both can boast the presence of several Ticinese players in starting roles, while by comparison FC Lugano does not have a single one in its squad. In summary, as highlighted by the economic and sports analysis, football turns out to be more powerful from a strictly economic standpoint and enjoys greater international appeal, but hockey proves decidedly more popular and rooted thanks to its visceral bond with the territory and local communities.
