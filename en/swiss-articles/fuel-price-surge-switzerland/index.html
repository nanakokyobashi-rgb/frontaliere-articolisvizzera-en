The gap at the pump

On 24 September 2026, in Zurich, the Swiss fuel picture shows an already significant gap. Those who own a petrol car have so far had only relative relief: there has been an increase in prices, but the price of unleaded 95 remains lower than diesel.

According to the TCS fuel radar, diesel in Switzerland reached an average of 2.46 francs per liter, with peaks above 2.50. The unleaded 95 instead travels on the median, however high, of 2.14 francs. For those who use a car, the comparison shows how the advantage of petrol is still visible today, but already in an increased market.

That advantage could soon disappear, however. This is the perspective reported by Blick in a recent report on high fuel prices in Switzerland, after the alarm contained in a Goldman Sachs report reported by Bloomberg. The thesis concerns production: today diesel produces more and refineries are shifting production from petrol to diesel. If the quantity of petrol in circulation decreases, its price may rise.

The logic indicated by analysts is that of supply: less product available means greater pressure on prices. Diesel may continue to grow, but gasoline now has more room for upside. For this reason, the investment bank advises investors to focus on an increase in European petrol by mid-2027. The forecast concerns European petrol and places the possibility of an increase in the middle of next year.

In Switzerland the risk is recognized by professionals. Olivier Fantino, director of Avenergy Suisse, the distributors' association which includes, among others, Agrola, Migrol, Socar and Tamoil, links the lack of refining capacity to a further increase in costs. The problem is structural: numerous refineries have been closed in Europe in recent years; in Russia and the Middle East several plants have been damaged or destroyed by war.

So far the shortage has mainly affected diesel, which has grown more than petrol. For those living in Switzerland, the transition from a price difference to a possible increase in the price of unleaded 95 translates into an item to follow in the personal budget. Key points

A four-step control routine

To face a possible increase in prices without relying on a future figure that is not yet available, it is advisable to set up a small control routine. It does not require a personal forecast: it serves to distinguish the observed data from the analysts' hypotheses and to report only what has actually been paid in the financial statements.

1. Start from the TCS fuel radar. Note the reference value for the fuel used, keeping diesel and unleaded 95 separate. In this way the comparison remains anchored to the data indicated for Switzerland.

2. Record actual spending. Keep the date, type of fuel, price in francs per liter and total amount for each refueling. The personal table allows you to distinguish the price actually incurred from the national median.

3. Separate the data from the forecast. Keep the signals from the Goldman Sachs report picked up by Bloomberg and the expert assessments on a separate line. A possible increase in European petrol should not be treated as a quotation already applied to every Swiss distributor.

4. Check the external factors indicated in the source. The developments of the war in Iran and the one in Ukraine affect oil; the Rhine level concerns the transportation of imported fuel; Donald Trump's hypothesis concerns US diesel exports. They are different elements and should be noted separately.

The next step is to update the budget with the observed spending, without replacing it with a hypothetical increase. If the cost of refueling changes, the recording immediately shows which item has changed. If it remains stable, the analysts' forecast remains a signal to follow, not an outlay already incurred.

Since the source reports little hope of clearing soon, a periodic check is more useful than a decision based on a single day. The comparison between TCS radar, personal spending and oil updates allows us to keep the current situation distinct from future scenarios.
