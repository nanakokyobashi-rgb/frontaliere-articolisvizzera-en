Bern rejects the initiative on quality education

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 28 September 2026

With 59.9% voting no and a turnout of 45.3%, the people of Bern reject the right to a quality education in the cantonal constitution.

Context

In brief

  • Bern rejects the initiative with 59.9% voting no
  • Voter turnout: 45.3%
  • The right will not be included in the cantonal constitution

Key facts

  • Outcome → 59.9% no
  • Turnout → 45.3%
  • Proposal → right to quality education in the cantonal constitution
  • Obligation → Canton and municipalities would have guaranteed high standards
  • Territory → schools throughout the territory

With 59.9% voting no, Bernese citizens rejected the popular initiative calling for the right to quality education to be enshrined in the cantonal constitution. Voter turnout was 45.3%. The result therefore rules out the constitutional strengthening of the obligation assigned to the Canton and municipalities in the school sector.

The proposal put to the vote

The proposal called for the Canton and municipalities to guarantee high-quality teaching throughout the territory. It also provided for the necessary means to be supplied and for teachers and specialized school staff to be qualified. The core of the text therefore brought together three elements indicated in the source: quality of teaching, means and qualifications.

The reference to the entire Bernese territory made the obligation common to the Canton and municipalities. The initiative did not merely evoke a general objective: it linked quality education to public responsibilities and to the characteristics of school staff. The vote thus concerned a constitutional strengthening of the obligation in schools.

The requested right will not appear in the Bernese cantonal constitution. The decision concerns the constitutional strengthening of the obligation to guarantee high standards in schools, not the introduction of a new wording approved by the electorate.

The authorities had argued that the initiative's demands were already present in the law and being implemented. By rejecting the proposal, the Bernese followed this argument. The vote therefore delivers a precise outcome: the requested constitutional guarantee will not be added, while the position that the same objectives are already contained in the law and are being implemented remains central.

Operational details

What changes at the cantonal level

The practical significance of the outcome can be understood by separating the amendment requested by the initiative from the situation described by the authorities. On the one hand, the proposal called for the right to a quality education to be enshrined in the cantonal constitution. On the other hand, the authorities considered that the demands were already present in the law and being implemented. The no blocked the first step, while voters followed the second argument.

Key points

How to read the outcome operationally

To follow the issue without confusing the proposal with the result, the reader can use a simple sequence based on the content indicated in the source.

Four steps for following the decision

1. Separate the constitutional level. The question concerned including the right to quality education in the cantonal Constitution. The first check is therefore to distinguish the requested right from the broader issue of education.

2. Identify the responsible parties indicated. The proposal called on the Canton and municipalities. The text must keep these two entities distinct from schools, teachers and specialized school staff as a whole.

3. Verify the quality criteria. The initiative linked high-quality teaching to the availability of the necessary resources and the qualifications of teachers and specialized school staff. These are the concrete elements to consider together.

4. Compare the request with the authorities' reasoning. The authorities considered that the requests were already included in the law and implemented. This step makes it possible to distinguish the proposed constitutional guarantee from the description of the legislative framework already applied according to that position.

The sequence avoids two opposing but equally imprecise interpretations: considering the no as a rejection of the objective of quality education, or considering it as approval of the new constitutional guarantee. The result reported by the source is more limited: the proposal was rejected and the requested right will not appear in the Bernese cantonal Constitution.

Frequently Asked Questions
What was the outcome of the vote in the Canton of Bern?
The citizens of Bern voted 59.9% against the popular initiative calling for the inclusion of the right to quality education in the cantonal constitution. Participation in the vote was 45.3%. Consequently, that right will not appear in the Bernese cantonal constitution.
What did the education initiative provide for?
The proposal called for Cantons and Municipalities to guarantee a high level of education throughout the territory. It also provided for the provision of the necessary means and the assurance that teachers and specialised school staff were qualified. Its objective was to strengthen the obligation to guarantee high standards in schools at the constitutional level.
Why did the authorities support the rejection?
The authorities believed that the requests of the initiative were already present in the law and implemented. Rejecting the proposal, Bernese citizens followed this argument. The result therefore leaves out of the cantonal constitution the right required, while the position remains that the objectives indicated were already provided for and applied in the law.
