The idea of deferring the health tax for border restaurants is taking shape through diplomatic talks between Switzerland and Italy. According to leaks from the Cdt, the authorities of Bern and Rome have opened a specific dialogue on this fiscal issue, which could lead to a freeze of the tax.

A strategic meeting has already been scheduled in Rome by the end of the current month. The meeting will feature Federal Councillor Karin Keller-Sutter, head of the Federal Department of Finance (DFF), and the Italian Economy and Finance Minister, Giancarlo Giorgetti. The purpose of the summit is to assess the work done and set the next steps.

Bilateral Dialogue on Fiscal Issues: The authorities have confirmed the start of a targeted conversation to examine the reciprocal fiscal dossiers. From Roman government sources, it is known that the input for the face-to-face meeting came directly from Keller-Sutter, whose relations with Minister Giorgetti remain good and cordial.

Among the hot topics on the table are the operational aspects of the 2020 Border Agreement, along with additional dossiers related to the border area.

Operational details

Implications for cross-border workers

The issue of the health tax takes on particular significance in light of a legal opinion commissioned by the Canton of Ticino. According to this document, the tax is effectively classified as a levy and not merely a contribution. Consequently, imposing it on cross-border workers would constitute a blatant violation of the tax agreements in force between Switzerland and Italy.

This legal interpretation strengthens Ticino's position, which views the tax as an Italian action harmful to international agreements. The Ticino State Council, intending to exert pressure, has long been considering the suspension or reduction of cross-border workers' reimbursements as a response to the health tax.

Current scenario and possible developments

The current situation is characterized by a standoff between Ticino authorities and federal authorities. On one hand, Ticino seeks to react to what it considers a violation of agreements; on the other, Bern fears that any unilateral moves could compromise diplomatic relations with Italy.

The Canton of Ticino's decision, which must be made by June 30, could have significant repercussions on the reimbursement system for cross-border workers. The Ticino Government will meet in the coming days, with a decision theoretically expected during the ordinary session on Wednesday, June 24.

Comparison with previous regulations

Unlike in the past, when reimbursements were automatically granted based on precise agreements, the current situation requires a careful assessment of fiscal and legal implications. The health tax, if confirmed, would represent a significant novelty in the fiscal treatment of cross-border workers in Switzerland.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the exact health tax for cross-border workers?
The health tax is an Italian tax that the Italian authorities intend to apply to cross-border workers, those who work in Switzerland but reside in Italy. According to a legal opinion commissioned by the Canton Ticino, this tax would be an impost and not a contribution, thus violating the bilateral fiscal agreements between Switzerland and Italy.
What are the restaurants for cross-border workers and how do they work?
The restaurants for cross-border workers are mechanisms of fiscal compensation that the Canton Ticino intends to apply in response to the health tax. They are a countermeasure that could involve suspending or reducing the restaurants provided to cross-border workers, as a form of diplomatic pressure on Italy.
What is the deadline for the decision of the Canton Ticino?
The Ticino State Council must take a decision by June 30, 2026. The government of the canton will meet in the coming days, with a decision expected for the regular meeting on Wednesday, June 24. This deadline is political and linked to the need to respond to the situation of the health tax.
What will happen at the summit between Switzerland and Italy in Rome?
By the end of the current month, a strategic summit will be held in Rome between the Swiss federal councilor Karin Keller-Sutter and the Italian minister Giancarlo Giorgetti. The meeting aims to outline the work done and set the next steps on the bilateral fiscal dossier, including the health tax for cross-border workers.
