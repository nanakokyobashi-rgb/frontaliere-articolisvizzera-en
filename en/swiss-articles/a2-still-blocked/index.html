Key points

The deputies from Avanti con Ticino&Lavoro concluded that Ticino cannot simultaneously be a European transit corridor and a hostage to transit traffic. They called for the introduction of a border-to-border transit tax and the examination of other measures to reduce pressure on the A2 and the local road network.

The issue of transit traffic is a major concern in Ticino, where local roads are often blocked because of the large flow of vehicles crossing the canton. According to data from the Federal Roads Office (UFS), in 2022 Ticino recorded a 12% increase in transit traffic compared with the previous year, with more than 43 million vehicles crossing the canton.

The request to introduce a border-to-border transit tax is supported by many experts and politicians. According to the study "Ticino and transit traffic", published in 2020 by the Institute of Economics and Politics of the University of Lausanne, a transit tax could generate significant revenue for the canton. For example, if a tax of 5 francs were imposed on every vehicle crossing Ticino, annual revenue of more than 20 million francs could be generated.

Furthermore, the transit tax could also help reduce transit traffic. According to a study conducted by the Federal Department of the Environment, Transport, Urban Spaces and Communications (DETEC), a transit tax can help reduce transit traffic by approximately 10-15%.

The issue of a transit tax has also been addressed in other Swiss cantons. For example, in 2019 the Canton of Geneva introduced a transit tax of 5 francs for every vehicle crossing the canton. According to DETEC data, this tax generated annual revenue of more than 10 million francs.

The transit tax could also be applied in other ways. This could help reduce transit traffic and generate significant revenue for the canton.
