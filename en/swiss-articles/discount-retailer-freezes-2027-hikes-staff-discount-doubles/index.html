BERN — Denner has decided to forgo general wage increases for 2027. The discount retailer belonging to the Migros Group is instead focusing on strengthening benefits for its staff.

Starting next year, the employee discount will rise from 10% to 20%. The measure was announced the day after Migros had decided to award its employees ten times the Cumulus points on purchases, providing an equivalent benefit to a 10% discount. The two companies are therefore pursuing different strategies: Migros intends to invest further in wages, while Denner is not planning a general increase.

Unchanged wages and minimum already set

The company justifies the decision by pointing to the particularly difficult market environment. The minimum wages introduced at the beginning of 2026 will be maintained and will continue to be paid thirteen times a year; according to Denner, the minimum wages guaranteed to employees will remain above the industry average.

The comparison provided by the company concerns 2026: Denner's minimum wages were higher than those of Migros and Coop, but slightly lower than those of Aldi and Lidl. The source does not report the amounts, but indicates the discount retailer's relative position compared with the competitors mentioned.

Pensions and new promotions

Denner will strengthen pension benefits to compensate for foregoing the increases. Thomas Kaderli, the company's spokesperson, explained that, in the second pillar, the employer contributions paid into the pension fund will increase by 1,2 percentage points. The employee share will remain unchanged.

The doubling of the discount and the strengthening of occupational pension benefits will be supplemented by rotating monthly promotions, in addition to the benefits already in place. The choice announced for 2027 therefore shifts support for employees from general pay to a combination of benefits and pension provisions.

Operational details

To read the announcement from the perspective of someone who works at Denner, it is useful to separate three elements: pay, benefits and pension provision. They do not have the same effect on daily life and cannot be added together as though they were a single salary component.
