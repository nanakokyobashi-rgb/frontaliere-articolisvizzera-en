Hotel Schweizerhof hacker attack: 10,000 customers alerted (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 September 2026

The Hotel Schweizerhof in Lenzerheide was the victim of a hacker attack. Approximately 10,000 customers were alerted to possible fraud attempts with a fictitious offer of CHF 250.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Hotel Schweizerhof in Lenzerheide hacked, 10,000 customers notified
  • Fraudsters ask for personal data with a fictitious credit offer of 250 francs
  • Attack could be linked to vulnerabilities in Austrian IT provider

Key facts

  • What: Hacker attack on the booking systems of the Schweizerhof Hotel, a four-star hotel
  • Where: Lenzerheide, Canton of Graubünden (GR), Switzerland
  • When: Date of attack not specified; hotel had tested vulnerabilities in spring
  • Who: Andreas Züllig (owner); about 10,000 interested customers
  • Damage: Personal data exposed; fictitious offer of 250 francs to attract victims

The Hotel Schweizerhof in Lenzerheide was allegedly the victim of a hacker attack on its booking systems. The news, first published by the Swiss weekly SonntagsBlick, was confirmed to Keystone-ATS by the hotel's owner, Andreas Züllig. The management of the four-star facility sent a safety notice to around 10,000 customers.

Fraudulent messages in circulation

In recent days, customers have started receiving messages via WhatsApp and other platforms from scammers. The hotel's official notice warns against replying to these messages and clicking on any links. Criminals ask for personal data and credit card numbers, using a very precise decoy: a credit of 250 francs.

Operational details

Recognizing Fraudulent Messages

Messages received by Schweizerhof clients follow a specific pattern: they arrive via WhatsApp. The hotel's official warning explicitly cautions against responding to these messages and clicking on links contained within them. The message offers a credit of 250 francs to encourage the recipient to provide sensitive data or credit card numbers.

No legitimate hotel offers unexpected credits to random clients via private messages. This is a red flag that should immediately raise suspicion.

How to Protect Yourself from Fraud Attempts

If you have booked at the Hotel Schweizerhof and receive suspicious messages, the golden rule is simple: do not click on links and never provide banking information via messaging. Always contact the hotel directly using the official phone number from the website, not the one potentially indicated in the fraudulent message. The hotel's official warning emphasizes exactly this defensive approach.

Verify the sender's identity using reliable sources. A legitimate contact attempt from the hotel will arrive via official email or phone to the numbers published on the website, not via WhatsApp with sudden offers.

The Risk for Other Hotels

The attack on Schweizerhof is not an isolated case in the Swiss cybersecurity landscape. The vulnerability of the Austrian IT provider suggests that other hotels using the same booking platform may be exposed to similar risks. Those booking hotels in Switzerland and abroad should maintain continuous vigilance when receiving unexpected offers or communications.

Key points

Immediate actions if you are a hotel customer

If you booked at the Hotel Schweizerhof, take the following precautions without delay:

Don't reply to suspicious WhatsApp messages: The hotel's official notice is crystal clear on this point. Completely ignore messages that offer sudden credits and do not click on links within them.

Block senders: Use the blocking feature built into your messaging app to prevent further contact attempts from suspicious numbers.

Monitor your bank account carefully: Check your credit card or bank account regularly. If you detect unauthorized transactions, contact your bank immediately without delay.

Report phishing: Many messaging platforms allow you to report fraudulent messages directly to the platform moderators.

Contact the hotel and file a complaint

If you have suffered a real fraud (money stolen from the account, unauthorized purchases with your card), contact the Hotel Schweizerhof directly through the official number from the website to communicate the incident and possibly receive official assistance.

Subsequently, file a complaint with the local police in your canton. In Switzerland, cybercrime and online fraud are punishable under the Criminal Code, even if the perpetrators are abroad as in the case of this investigation.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the hallmarks of a fraudulent message linked to the Schweizerhof attack?
Messages arrive via WhatsApp, NOT the hotel's official email. They offer an unexpected 250 franc credit and ask you to click on a link or provide personal data. The hotel's official notice recommends that you do not reply or click. To verify, always contact the hotel directly using the number from the official website.
I booked at the Hotel Schweizerhof. Am I automatically at risk of fraud?
Yes, your contact details were likely exposed to scammers in the attack. However, the real risk of fraud is only high if you reply to fraudulent messages or click on links. Regularly monitor your bank account and ignore suspicious messages, as recommended by the hotel.
What should I do immediately if I clicked on a link from the suspicious message?
Do not provide additional sensitive data. Block the sender, change the password of your online bank account if you used it, and monitor transactions in the following days. Contact your bank to alert them of a possible fraud attempt, so they can increase vigilance on your account.
Can I report the crime to the police even if the perpetrators are abroad?
Yes. Please contact the local police or canton command where you reside. Cybercrime and online fraud are punishable in Switzerland under the Criminal Code. Even if the perpetrators are abroad, as in the case of this attack, a complaint remains important for official documentation and coordination between authorities.
Could other hotel facilities in Switzerland be affected by the attack of the Austrian IT provider?
Possibly. The attack on the Schweizerhof was linked to a vulnerability in an Austrian IT service provider. Other hotels using the same booking platform may be exposed to similar risks. Stay alert when you receive unexpected offers from hotels you've booked in the past.
