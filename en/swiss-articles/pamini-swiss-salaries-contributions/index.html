Operational details

Contributions, taxes and premiums on three levels

The relevant comparison is not between isolated percentages, but between different bases. An item may be calculated on salary, tied to a cap, adjusted according to sector or age, or set as a per-capita premium. Putting everything under the word contributions creates a misleading comparison.

The first level concerns deductions linked to employment. AVS/AI/IPG, AD/AC, LAINF/LAA and LPP/BVG are presented with different mechanisms. The first three items are expressed within the framework of salary contributions; LPP, by contrast, uses the coordinated salary and an age-bracket scale. LAMal/KVG does not belong to this same base, because the premium is per capita and does not depend on a percentage of salary.

The second level is fiscal. Switzerland provides for three levels: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. Each Canton has its own law and multiplier. The municipality applies a multiplier to the cantonal tax. A figure for the AVS/AHV percentage, therefore, does not replace the calculation of direct federal tax or that of cantonal and municipal taxes.

Institutional responsibilities

The offices also have distinct areas of responsibility. AFC/ESTV is responsible for direct federal tax and VAT. Cantonal administrations manage the cantonal and municipal levels. UFAS/BSV concerns social security, including AVS, AI and LPP, but does not set taxes. UST/BFS produces statistics and does not establish tax rates. This separation prevents assigning to one body a decision that belongs to another level.

For someone reading a Swiss payslip, the distinction is concrete: a contribution deduction, an insurance premium and a tax burden are not interchangeable. The same applies to the cost of living. A LAMal premium may affect monthly expenses, but it does not thereby become a percentage of salary. The minimum wage follows yet another logic: there is no federal minimum wage, while some Cantons have their own. This parameter, too, cannot be confused with social-security contribution rates.

Cross-border commuters, cantons and documented limits

The word cross-border commuter is not enough to determine how each item is treated. Within the framework indicated by SEM, foreign permits are distinguished by function: permit L concerns short-term stays of up to one year, B is a renewable residence permit, C is a settlement permit normally linked to 10 years of residence or to 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens, while G identifies the cross-border commuter. The work framework

Salaries and contributions also fall within the general rules governing the employment relationship. There is no federal minimum wage, although some cantons have their own. The maximum working time is 45 hours per week in industry, offices and retail, or 50 hours in other sectors. The minimum vacation entitlement is four weeks, five for those under 20. Maternity leave lasts 14 weeks at 80% through IPG; paternity leave lasts two weeks. The stated notice periods are one, two or three months, respectively, during the first year, from the second to the ninth year and from the tenth year.

These rules define the employment relationship, but do not demonstrate what intervention concerning contributions was proposed. Cantonal benefits and services also follow different rules: social assistance is generally administered by the cantons and municipalities based on need, while motor vehicle taxes, energy incentives, building permits and contributions to continuing education depend on the competent cantonal offices. Requirements, deadlines and amounts are not uniform throughout the country.
