Cyberattack hits Zurich’s pension fund

PKZH personal data stolen in an attack on a software provider Pension funds and annuities were not affected The attack dates back to the end of September

On Thursday, PKZH, the pension fund of the City of Zurich, announced that personal data of its insured members had been stolen. The cyberattack targeted an external software provider, without affecting pension assets or pension payments. The statement thus distinguishes the theft of information from the payment of benefits.

The cyberattack dates back to the end of September. Since a switch to a new management system was planned, the fund had already sent its data to the company. PKZH filed a criminal complaint. In the meantime, it is trying to determine precisely what information was stolen and how many people were affected.

Reconstructing the attack

The worst-case scenario listed by the fund includes personal contact details: name, address, email and telephone number. The same list includes the AVS number, marital status, information about partners, the IBAN number and pension data such as insured income and assets. The wording is conditional: the source does not present these categories as a list already confirmed for every person.

According to the statement, the administrative system used by PKZH to manage assets and pay pensions does not appear to have been involved in the attack. For the fund’s operations, this is therefore the limit indicated by the source. What remains unresolved, however, is reconstructing the full set of copied data and determining the number of insured members affected.

The case follows announcements from other Swiss pension funds. In the preceding days, other funds, including the public-sector fund in Bern, had announced that they had fallen victim to the cyberattack. The text does not specify which data were involved in the Bern case.

On Thursday, possible involvement by the Posta pension fund had also been raised. The source clarifies that this has not been established. Warnings appeared on its website, but this does not turn the hypothesis into a confirmation. The PKZH statement, by contrast, explicitly links the transmission of the data to the new management system project and the breach at the external provider.

Operational details

The practical implications for insured persons

For those who live or work in Switzerland and are insured with PKZH, the news has two distinct aspects: continuity of pension provision and the confidentiality of information. Regarding the former, the fund says that assets and pension payments have not been affected; the administrative system used to manage assets and pay benefits would also not have been affected. Regarding the latter, the potential leak concerns more than mere contact details, because the worst-case scenario includes an AHV number, an IBAN and pension data. The source does not report any money transfers or a suspension of pension payments. The available information concerns the theft of information.

2. Treat the list of data as a worst-case scenario, not as individual confirmation. PKZH is verifying which information was stolen and how many people are involved.

4. Record the only formal act indicated: PKZH has filed a criminal complaint. This is the procedural step reported by the source; no other requirements for insured persons are described.
