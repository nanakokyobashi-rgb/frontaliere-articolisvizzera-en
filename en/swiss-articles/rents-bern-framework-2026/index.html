Rentals in the canton of Bern and federal tenancy rules

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 October 2026

Analysis of the real estate market in the canton of Bern and of the federal regulations currently in force concerning security deposits and rental agreements.

Context

In brief

  • Rental law is federal and uniform across cantons
  • Security deposit: maximum three months rent
  • The deposit must be placed in a blocked account in the tenant's name
  • Bern is compared with average values per canton

Key facts

  • Period → 2026
  • Focus → Switzerland and Canton of Bern

  • Law → federal, uniform in every canton
  • Security deposit → maximum 3 months rent
  • Account → blocked and in the tenant's name

The national reference for renting is CO art. 253 et seq.: the matter is governed by federal law which is the same in every canton. For those comparing the rental market in 2026, this distinction separates the price dimension from tenant protection. The focus on the Canton of Bern does not alter the basic legal framework.

The comparison between the Canton of Bern and the Swiss average must keep two levels distinct. The first is the market, with average prices organized by canton; the second is legal protection, which remains federal. For this reason, a national average can guide the picture, but it does not replace the cantonal value when evaluating accommodation in the Canton of Bern. Similarly, the difference between cantons on the market does not mean that each canton has its own federal rental law.

The security deposit as a first check

The most concrete verification concerns the security deposit. The rule indicated for renting sets a maximum of three months rent. The money must be placed in a blocked account in the tenant's name. These are two elements to check immediately in the contract: the requested amount and the account ownership.

This step serves not to confuse the deposit with ordinary rent. The rent is the periodic cost of the accommodation; the deposit is a separate sum subject to the indicated limit. Therefore, when comparing offers, it is not enough to look at the monthly price: one must also consider the initial outlay required by the contract, without assigning it a different cap than the federal one.

The common rule is the fixed point for those moving between cantons or evaluating Bern compared to the rest of Switzerland. To read housing expenses within the cost of living in Switzerland, it is best to start with the available cantonal rent and then verify that the contractual conditions comply with the federal framework. The economic comparison can change from canton to canton; the deposit limit and the blocked account remain instead the national references of the federal framework.

Operational details

The practical comparison does not end with the listed rent. To understand the burden of rent in the canton of Bern compared with the national picture, it is necessary to separate housing costs from items that depend on income, residence, or the level of government. This is the clearest way to understand the cost of living: an insurance premium or a tax follows rules different from those governing a lease agreement.

Three levels not to be confused

The first column of the budget is the rental market, sorted by average cantonal prices. The second covers expenses linked to residence. LAMal/KVG is mandatory for residents, must be taken out within 3 months of arrival, and has per-capita premiums by canton or region. The premium reduction is a cantonal subsidy. The third column concerns income and taxation.

In a hypothetical scenario, someone comparing a home in the canton of Bern with an offer in another canton should first enter the rent figure, then residence-related costs, and finally disposable income after social-security and tax charges. If all the items are moved together, it becomes difficult to understand whether the difference comes from the real estate market, premiums, or taxation. LAMal/KVG, for example, is neither a tax nor a payroll contribution: it is mandatory insurance for residents, with per-capita premiums and a possible cantonal reduction.

For the tax component, the comparison must respect the three levels: direct federal, cantonal and municipal tax. Each canton has its own law and multiplier, while the municipality applies a multiplier to the cantonal tax. Key points

To turn the comparison into a practical decision, it is advisable to follow a fixed sequence. The procedure keeps contract, insurance, contributions, and taxes separate, so the rent in the canton of Bern remains comparable with that of the other cantons.

Five-step procedure

1. Set the scope. Indicate the canton of Bern and place the average value per canton alongside the national comparison. The two reference points serve to read the local market and the Swiss context without confusing them.

2. Check the financial clause. Note the rent, the deposit, and the account holder. The deposit may not exceed three months' rent, and the account must be blocked and in the tenant's name.

3. Record the health-insurance deadline. If you are a resident, LAMal/KVG must be taken out within 3 months of arrival. Bear in mind that premiums are per capita and vary by canton or region; also check the cantonal premium reduction.

4. Reconstruct the payslip. To separate disposable income from rent, consider the deductions indicated: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% for the employee and 10.6% in total with the employer; AD/AC 1.1% up to the annual maximum; LAINF/LAA 0.7–1.5% depending on the sector.

5. Assign each item to the correct authority. AFC/ESTV concerns direct federal tax and VAT; cantonal administrations handle cantonal and municipal taxation. UFAS/BSV concerns social security, does not set taxes, while UST/BFS concerns statistics, not tax rates. For the tax part, you can consult dichiarazione delle imposte.

Frequently Asked Questions
How does rent deposit work under federal law?
The deposit for the lease sets a maximum of three months' rent. The money must be placed in a escrow account in the tenant's name. It is essential to immediately check the requested amount and the exact account header in the contract to avoid confusion with the ordinary rent.
What are the differences between the canton of Bern and the Swiss average for rentals?
The comparison between the canton of Bern and the Swiss average distinguishes the market, with average prices organized by canton, and the legal protection, which remains federal according to the Code of Obligations art. 253 ff. A national average orients the picture, but does not replace the cantonal value.
How does LAMal fit into the monthly budget for those living in Bern?
LAMal or KVG is a mandatory insurance for residents that is taken out within three months of arrival. It provides for per capita premiums per canton or region and the possibility of requesting a reduction in premiums via cantonal subsidy, while remaining distinct from rent and tax expenditure.
