At school only 4 days, the experiment (for now) convinces (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 September 2026

For three weeks, in Belp, in the Canton of Bern, a new pilot project has been underway with 4 days of school and 6 weeks of vacation.

Context

In a nutshell

  • School experiment in Belp, Canton Bern
  • 4 days of school, 6 weeks of vacation
  • Parents and children enthusiastic

Key facts

  • What: School experiment in Belp
  • When: For three weeks
  • Where: Belp, Canton Bern
  • Who: 21 children and their families
  • Amount: Not specified

A new pilot project has been underway for three weeks in Belp, Canton Bern. Less holiday-related stress, less attendance issues during after-school hours, and fewer trips between school, lunch, and home. For the 21 children of the Bernese school, the traditional lessons leave room for continuous supervision, even during the lunch break. Children attend school four days a week, from Monday to Thursday, instead of five but, in return, have only six weeks of vacation per year instead of thirteen.

The testimony of two mothers

"It was a good start, my daughter likes to go there," says Lisa, mother of one of the 21 children. Almost every day his daughter comes home with "a lot of experiences". And there's room for kids' ideas, too.

"She can't wait to attend the cooking class." For her daughter, in fact, one of the most pleasant moments is cooking together with the others. Since the children stay at school during the lunch break, they also help prepare the food: "She is always happy when she is assigned to the cooking group".

Longer days, but more energy.

Manuela, whose six-year-old daughter also participates

Operational details

Practical Implications

The school experiment at Belp could have significant implications for the Swiss education system. Reducing school days to four days could lead to better time management for families, reducing stress related to after-school hours and holidays. However, it is important to monitor the impact of longer days on children, especially younger ones, to ensure they do not become overly fatigued.

Comparison with the traditional model

The traditional model includes five days of school and thirteen weeks of vacation. The new experiment, on the other hand, offers four days of school and only six weeks of vacation. This change could affect the daily routine of families, allowing them to have one day off per week for activities and relaxation. However, it is necessary to assess whether the shorter holidays will be sufficient for rest and unstructured family time.

Possible scenarios

If the experiment proves positive, it could be adopted by other schools in Switzerland. This could lead to a reform of the education system, with a greater focus on the quality of daily life of children and their families. However, it is important to conduct further studies to better understand the long-term effects of this new model.
