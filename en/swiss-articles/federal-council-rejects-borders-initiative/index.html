Federal Council rejects the UDC initiative on borders (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 19 September 2026

The Federal Council recommends rejecting the UDC initiative on borders. High costs, damage to the economy and risks for Schengen have been detected.

Context

At a glance

  • The Federal Council rejects the SVP border initiative
  • High costs and damage to the Swiss economy identified
  • The executive warns of risks to Schengen and Dublin
  • Alternative measures on the way through the European Pact

Key facts

  • Initiative: Stop abuses in asylum
  • Daily border crossings: 2.2 million
  • Cost of leaving Schengen: 5.8 to 16.1 billion
  • Maximum asylum limit: 5,000 people

The Federal Council has officially recommended that Parliament reject the popular initiative promoted by the SVP for border protection, formally named Stop abuses in asylum. The Swiss executive expressed a clear rejection of the proposal, highlighting a series of significant critical issues that could affect the entire national territory. According to the government's assessment, the implementation of the new measures would entail very high costs, a deterioration of the country's welfare, a weakening of internal security, and severe damage to Switzerland's traditional humanitarian policy. Furthermore, the executive reiterated that the text would not even be suitable for effectively curbing the phenomenon of irregular migration and cross-border crime. Specifically, the initiative requires systematically controlling all incoming people, fully manning border crossings, and turning back those who do not meet entry conditions, while introducing a maximum cap of 5,000 asylum grants per year. For those staying irregularly, the text requires a notification obligation, the nullity of employment contracts, exclusion from social and health insurance, and the obligation to leave the territory within 90 days.

Impacts on daily mobility

The repercussions of permanent and systematic controls would inevitably reflect on traffic and the management of Swiss customs checkpoints. The Confederation registers approximately 2.2 million border crossings daily and the presence of many hundreds of thousands of cross-border commuters. Consequently, the execution of fixed and systematic checks would heavily burden the national economy and traffic fluidity, generating billions of francs in congestion costs especially in border regions. The government emphasizes that it would be essential to significantly increase border protection personnel, generating a further increase in public spending. Current customs procedures, the executive recalls, are already based on checks tailored to the situation and risks, which can be intensified as needed without resorting to permanent blockades of internal borders. Operational details

Security and Schengen Consequences

A central aspect of the analysis conducted by the Federal Council concerns the strategic link between Switzerland and European cooperation agreements. Accepting the initiative would concretely jeopardize the Confederation's association with the Schengen and Dublin system. The potential abandonment of this regulatory and security framework would weaken the country's internal protection, as Switzerland would lose immediate access to shared European search databases. Furthermore, Swiss authorities would find themselves in a position of having to independently re-examine all applications rejected by other Dublin member states, without retaining the power to transfer applicants to the competent European countries. This situation would lead to a significant and predictable increase in the number of applications handled directly at the national level. To support this assessment, the government cites an official report commissioned by the National Council, according to which the annual costs resulting from the abandonment of Schengen and Dublin would range between a minimum of 5.8 billion and a maximum of 16.1 billion Swiss francs.

Analysis of Quantitative and Humanitarian Limits

The proposal to set a maximum limit of 5,000 asylum grants per year conflicts with obligations arising from international law. To avoid violations of signed treaties, the Confederation would still be required to examine each individual asylum application separately, making it impossible to proceed with automatic rejection and repatriation of applicants. People fleeing armed conflict zones would no longer be able to obtain provisional admission, thus calling into question Switzerland's historic humanitarian tradition. On the integration front, the text under examination would make it almost impossible for individuals with prolonged stays to achieve financial independence, with the concrete risk of heavily overburdening cantonal structures dedicated to emergency relief. Nevertheless, the executive recognizes the need to intervene in the migration sector, aiming to curb secondary migration movements within the Schengen area through the coordinated application of measures provided for by the European Pact on Migration and Asylum, in which Switzerland actively participates.

Key points

Legislative Outlook and Cantonal Interventions

Despite the strongly negative opinion expressed by the Federal Council regarding the SVP popular initiative, the executive branch and cantonal administrations agree on the need to implement targeted reforms in the migration management sector. In this scenario, the Federal Department of Justice and Police has initiated preparatory work to define, in close collaboration with the Cantons, new legislative amendments. The primary objective of these regulatory interventions is to tangibly relieve the national asylum system and overall raise security levels within the sector. The Cantons and federal authorities therefore continue to monitor the evolution of the regulatory framework, evaluating alternative administrative tools to address the challenges related to migration flows without compromising economic balances and the free movement of cross-border commuters. Final decisions will now be submitted to the Federal Parliament, which will have to discuss the rejection recommendation formulated by the executive and schedule the next institutional steps.

Operational References for Citizens

For those who live and work in Swiss territory, the evolution of the political and regulatory debate on security, cross-border commuting, and public finance requires constant monitoring of federal provisions. The reforms announced by the Federal Department of Justice and Police and the Cantons could impact administrative management and public expenditure planning in the medium term.
