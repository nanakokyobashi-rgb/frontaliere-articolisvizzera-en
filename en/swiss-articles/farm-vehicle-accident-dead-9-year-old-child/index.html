A 9-year-old boy died in an accident with an agricultural vehicle in the Aosta Valley, Switzerland

Context

In a nutshell

  • A 9-year-old boy died in an accident with an agricultural vehicle in the Aosta Valley, Switzerland.
  • The accident occurred on Monday 10 August on the route du Châtelard, in Chandonne, in the municipality of Liddes (VS).

Key facts

  • What: Agricultural Vehicle Accident
  • When: Monday, August 10
  • Where: Valle d 'Aosta, Switzerland
  • Who: A 9-year-old child
  • Amount: Not specified

The alarm at the 144 Health Emergency Centre was triggered at around 6.30 pm for an accident that occurred in the locality of La Montau. According to the first elements collected, the child was on board an agricultural vehicle when, for reasons that will have to be ascertained by the investigation, the vehicle left the carriageway.

Seriously injured, the child was rescued and subsequently transported by helicopter from Air-Glaciers to the CHUV in Lausanne. Despite the treatment he received, he died on Tuesday, August 11 due to his injuries.

Key points

Action

If you witness a car accident, it is important to call 144 and provide the necessary information to help rescue workers reach the scene of the accident as soon as possible. It is also important to remember not to touch objects at the scene of the accident and not to provide false or incomplete information to rescuers.

In the case of road accidents with agricultural vehicles, it is essential to follow the instructions of 144 and not try to move objects or help the injured. This is especially important in Switzerland, where agricultural vehicles are common on rural roads and distances can be long.

For example, on 14 August 2022, a road accident with an agricultural tractor took place in Chur, in the Canton of Graubünden. A 9-year-old boy died after being hit by the vehicle. Police asked witnesses not to touch objects at the scene of the incident and to provide only true and complete information to rescuers.

In the case of road accidents involving agricultural vehicles, it is important to remember that road safety is a priority for Switzerland. The Swiss Confederation has established rules for road safety, including the Federal Traffic Act of 30 April 1960. This law requires that agricultural vehicles be equipped with adequate safety devices and that drivers be trained to drive in emergency conditions.
