OECD assigns positive assessment to Switzerland (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 10 August 2026

The Swiss National Contact Point for Responsible Business Conduct was positively assessed by the OECD.

Context

On 17 June 2026, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) published a report in which it positively assessed the Swiss National Contact Point for Responsible Business Conduct. The report underlines the professional and efficient way of working of the Swiss National Contact Point and considers it competent, fair and reliable. In addition, the OECD appreciates the development of dialogue and mediation procedures aimed at finding solutions.

The Swiss National Contact Point was positively evaluated for its ability to handle disputes in a fair and transparent manner. According to the report, the Swiss national contact point resolved 85% of disputes in 2025, with an average resolution time of 120 days. This is an increase from 2024, when only 70% of disputes had been resolved by the Swiss national point of contact.

The report also highlights the collaboration between the Swiss national contact point and local authorities, such as the Federal Council and cantonal authorities. For example, the Federal Council has allocated CHF 10 million for 2025 to support the activity of the Swiss National Contact Point. In addition, the State Council of the Canton of Bern has signed an agreement with the Swiss National Contact Point for the management of disputes relating to workers' rights.

Operational details

The report published by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) rated Switzerland positively for its ability to implement the guidelines for transparency and financial cooperation. The report also contains recommendations for further strengthening the Swiss National Contact Point, which is responsible for promoting and implementing the guidelines at the national level.

The OECD recommendations include greater collaboration of the Swiss National Contact Point with civil society organisations and workers' representations in promoting the guidelines. This could include setting up a discussion forum with social partners to discuss implementation strategies for the guidelines and ensure they are consistent with national objectives.

The OECD also recommends more targeted support to small and medium-sized enterprises in the implementation of the guidelines. This could be done through the creation of training and support programmes for SMEs, such as the Swiss SME Support Programme in Finance, which was launched by the Federal Department of Finance (DDF) in 2020 with a budget of CHF 10 million.
