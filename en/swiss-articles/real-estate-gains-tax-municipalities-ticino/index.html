Real estate profits tax: 40% share to the municipalities of Ticino (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 12 August 2026

A parliamentary initiative presented by Patrick Rusconi calls for allocating 40% of TUI's revenue to the municipalities where the property is located.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Request for redistribution of 40% TUI to Municipalities
  • Submitted by Patrick Rusconi (PLR)
  • 31.6 million francs expected in 2025
  • No increase in the tax burden

Key facts

  • What: Parliamentary initiative on TUI
  • When: Reference year calculations 2025
  • Where: Canton of Ticino
  • Who: Patrick Rusconi (PLR), Michela Ris, Luca Renzetti
  • Amount: 31.6 million francs estimated for the Municipalities

In recent decades, there has been a progressive imbalance in financial relations between the Canton and the municipalities of Ticino. On the one hand, significant tax revenues have been concentrated at the cantonal level, on the other hand, the burdens borne by local authorities have increased. This situation of imbalance is at the heart of a new parliamentary initiative elaborated and presented by Patrick Rusconi, the first signatory, and signed by the PLR deputies Michela Ris and Luca Renzetti. The deed aims to change the distribution of income deriving from the real estate profit tax, known as TUI, which currently ends up entirely in the Canton's coffers. The objective stated by the promoters is to correct the financial disparity that penalizes many local authorities, which continue to be confronted with growing budgetary difficulties while contributing decisively to the creation of the wealth generated by tax revenues in the territory.

Financial analysis and impact on local budgets

The economic impact of the measure proposed by the radical Liberal MEPs is reflected in the forecast calculations made on the basis of recent financial data. With figures in hand, a redistribution of forty percent of TUI's revenue would have brought about 31.6 million francs into the coffers of the Ticino Municipalities during the year 2025. At the same time, the Canton would continue to retain a preponderant share of the tax revenues deriving from these transactions, leaving over CHF 47 million in the cantonal coffers. This scenario shows that the proposal does not intend to drain cantonal finances, but rather aims to rebalance the flow of resources between the different institutional levels of the territory, offering a breath of oxygen to municipal budgets under increasing pressure due to citizenship services and compulsory expenditure.

Efforts and difficulties of local authorities

The gap between the revenues concentrated in Bellinzona and the amount of tasks managed locally represents a significant criticality for the financial stability of the municipalities of Ticino. Many local authorities find themselves managing infrastructure, maintenance and social services whose cost has risen over time, while traditional sources of income struggle to fully cover expenses. How to follow the evolution of the proposal and verify your situation

The institutional path of an elaborated parliamentary initiative involves the initial examination by the Council of State and subsequently the discussion within the Grand Council, where MEPs will discuss in detail the regulatory outlines and the overall financial impact of the change on the allocation of the TUI. For taxpayers, property owners and operators in the real estate sector in Ticino, following the evolution of this debate is essential to understand any future updates in the management of transactions and related cantonal and municipal tax charges. Although the initiative does not provide for tax increases, monitoring the decrees and decisions of the cantonal parliament makes it possible to anticipate local economic dynamics.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does the parliamentary initiative on TUI provide for?
The initiative presented by Patrick Rusconi (PLR) asks to redistribute 40% of the income from property tax (TUI) to the municipalities where the fund being sold is located, leaving the rest to the Canton.
What would be the financial impact for Ticino Municipalities?
On the basis of forecast data, a redistribution of 40% of TUI's revenue would have brought about CHF 31.6 million into municipal coffers in 2025, while over CHF 47 million would have remained in the Canton.
Does the initiative include the introduction of new taxes or tax increases?
No, the parliamentary act does not introduce any new tax and does not provide for any increase in the tax burden for either citizens or companies, merely modifying the existing revenue distribution key.
