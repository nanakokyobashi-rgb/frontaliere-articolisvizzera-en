Operational details

The drought is causing psychological stress and family tensions among farming families. Economic difficulties related to failed harvests and the shortage of fodder for animals are aggravating the situation.

According to data provided by the Swiss Confederation, 70% of farming families in Switzerland are experiencing economic stress due to drought. In particular, the cantons of Ticino and Valais were among the most affected, with a 25% increase in requests for aid to farmers. In Bern, the capital of Switzerland, there were more than 50 requests for help from farmers in July alone.

Drought is causing problems for even small farms. According to data from the Federal Office of Agriculture (FOAG), 40% of small farms are at risk of closing due to drought. In particular, farms in the canton of Jura were among the most affected, with a 30% increase in aid requests.

The Swiss Confederation has allocated 10 million francs to help farming families affected by the drought. However, according to data from the Federal Office for Agriculture, 70% of these funds have already been used. In addition, the Swiss Confederation has also allocated 5 million francs to help small farms.

Key points

Farming families in Switzerland are facing economic hardship linked to failed harvests and a shortage of fodder for animals. It is important that the authorities provide measures to help these families. The drought, which has affected several regions of the country, is causing serious problems for farmers, who are facing a reduction in harvests and a decrease in the quality of fodder for animals.

According to data from the Federal Office of Agriculture (FOAG), 70% of the cultivated land in Switzerland has been affected by drought. The Canton of Ticino, for example, suffered a loss of more than 30% of maize and rice harvests, while the Canton of Graubünden recorded a loss of more than 20% of potato harvests. The shortage of fodder for animals is also causing problems in other regions of the country, such as the Canton of Bern, where 40% of the cultivated areas have been affected by drought.

The situation is particularly critical for peasant families who depend largely on crops for their livelihood. According to data from the Federal Office for Agriculture, 60% of peasant households in Switzerland have an annual income of less than CHF 50,000. These families are particularly vulnerable to drought and animal feed shortages, which can cause serious economic problems.
