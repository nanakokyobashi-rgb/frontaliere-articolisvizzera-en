Chronext closes: Zug luxury watches bankruptcy (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 August 2026

Bankruptcy declared on 19 August by the Cantonal Court of Zug. Luxury e-commerce company Chronext had 150 employees and revenues of hundreds of millions. By 2022, it had already laid off 40 people.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Bankruptcy declared on 19 August by the Cantonal Court of Zug
  • Chronext sold luxury watches online, founded in 2013
  • It had 150 employees, 14 offices in Europe and Hong Kong, millionaire revenues

Key facts

  • What: Bankruptcy of Chronext AG, a luxury watch and luxury goods e-commerce company
  • When: 19 August 2026 (declaration of bankruptcy)
  • Where: Zug, Switzerland (head office)
  • Who: Cantonal Court of Zug
  • Occupation: 150 employees at the time of bankruptcy
  • Locations: 14 distributed in Europe and Hong Kong
  • Founded: 2013

Chronext AG raises white flag. The cantonal court of Zug opened the bankruptcy of the company on 19 August, as can be seen from the cantonal trade register. The liquidation also concerns Chronext Group AG, the holding company that managed shareholdings in other companies, which was also declared bankrupt on the same day.

The company, founded in 2013, operated in the luxury watch sector, selling used and new models through an online portal, as well as jewellery and luxury goods of various kinds. Throughout its history, Chronext had developed a significant network with 14 offices distributed in Europe and Hong Kong, consolidating itself as one of the main players in international luxury e-commerce.

# Growth and decline

In the initial years, Chronext recorded strong economic growth, reaching revenues in the hundreds of millions of francs.

Operational details

The economic context of the luxury sector in Switzerland

Chronext has not been the only Swiss luxury company to experience difficulties in recent years. The Zurich company's failure reflects broader trends in the premium goods e-commerce market, where global competition has grown enormously and margins have narrowed. Switzerland, historically strong in the watches and jewellery sector, has seen the emergence of new online players who have sought to redefine the way luxury is sold worldwide.

Impact on the Swiss labour market

The failure of Chronext has direct implications for the Swiss labour market. With 150 employees at the time of liquidation, the company represented a significant operation in the luxury e-commerce and retail sector. Employees who have lost their jobs — initially 40 dismissed in 2022, and potentially many more since today's liquidation — will need to find new opportunities in a competitive market.

Chronext had built a complex operation, with physical locations and online platforms, that required personnel specialized in logistics, customer service, luxury product evaluation, and expensive inventory management. Key points

What happens now: resources and next steps

Chronext's liquidation will proceed according to Swiss bankruptcy procedures. The Zurich Regional Court will oversee the process, with the aim of liquidating the company's assets and distributing the proceeds to creditors in accordance with the order established by law. Employees, as privileged creditors under Swiss law, have specific rights compared to other non-privileged debtors.

Opportunities for former employees

The 150 Chronext employees who had not already been laid off in 2022 now face the search for new employment. This process can be facilitated by accessing public employment services and specialized job search platforms. In Switzerland, those who lose their job can request support from the competent cantonal authorities, which offer advice and assistance in finding a new position.

Assistance and support during transition

Cantonal authorities offer resources for those in occupational transition. Those who were Chronext employees may be entitled to unemployment benefits, depending on the duration of employment and previous income — a matter to verify by calculating your benefits or directly with the competent cantonal offices.

Suppliers and creditors of Chronext will need to monitor announcements from the Zurich Regional Court to learn about creditor dates and procedures for participating in the claims process. In Switzerland, bankruptcy procedures are published and accessible through cantonal commercial registers.

Frequently Asked Questions
What exactly does it mean that Chronext is in liquidation?
Liquidation means that the Cantonal Court has opened the bankruptcy of the company. A director or trustee will be in charge of selling the company assets and distributing the proceeds to creditors in the order established by Swiss law. During this process, the company no longer operates normally and employees terminate their employment.
What rights do Chronext employees have after bankruptcy?
In Switzerland, employees are considered privileged creditors in a bankruptcy proceeding, which means they are entitled to payment of their back wages and benefits due before other joint creditors. In addition, they can access cantonal unemployment benefits if they meet the requirements.
Where can former Chronext employees find work?
Former employees can access Swiss job search platforms and cantonal placement services. Sectors such as e-commerce, logistics, retail and customer service continue to look for profiles with the experience gained by Chronext employees.
Chronext also had offices at the European level. How does international failure work?
Chronext was a Swiss company headquartered in Zug, so the bankruptcy is handled by the Zurich Cantonal Court. For foreign locations and international activities, the process is more complex and may require coordination with the judicial authorities of the countries where Chronext operated, according to international insolvency treaties.
What will happen to customers who had pending orders?
Customers who had undelivered orders are at risk of not receiving them. In the bankruptcy process, unexecuted orders are considered receivables from the company, and customers will have to participate in the procedure to eventually recover their money, with lower priority than privileged creditors such as employees.
