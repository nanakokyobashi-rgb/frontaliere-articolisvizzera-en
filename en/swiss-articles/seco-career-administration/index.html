Working at SECO: Opportunities and Federal Career (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 September 2026

The Secretariat of State for the Economy employs over 900 people in three locations. Discover apprenticeships, university internships and opportunities in Swiss federal public administration.

Context

In brief

Key facts

  • What: Employment opportunities at SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs)
  • Where: 3 main locations in Switzerland + approximately 30 overseas detachments
  • Who: Federal Swiss State Secretariat for Economic Affairs
  • Employees: Over 900 staff members and managers
  • Sought-after profiles: Commercial employees, digital business developers, IT specialists
  • Opportunities: Vocational apprenticeships (AFC), SMC traineeships, university internships

The State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) stands as a significant employer in the Swiss federal public administration sector, with over 900 staff members and managers distributed across three main locations in the country. In addition to this national base, the federal institution has approximately 30 professionals stationed in various countries, demonstrating an active role in economic diplomacy and Swiss representation on the international stage.

For those seeking career opportunities in the federal public sector, SECO offers a concrete option: the entity provides apprenticeship positions (AFC), SMC traineeships, and university internships, in addition to traditional roles. SECO's organizational philosophy is based on recognizing the responsibility, open-mindedness, and personal initiative of its employees, creating what the institution describes as a "stimulating work environment."

Operational details

What awaits new hires

The SECO work environment is built around three declared pillars: responsibility, open-mindedness, and personal initiative. This is not mere organizational rhetoric, but an operational model that concretely influences the daily lives of employees. SECO, the federal agency responsible for managing the country's economic policy, requires its staff to actively participate in defining and implementing strategies that impact the entire Swiss economy.

For apprentices and trainees, this philosophy translates into the assignment of 'diverse responsibilities' and involvement in 'concrete projects.' These are not purely administrative or back-office positions, but roles where young talent acquires real operational skills, facing the actual challenges of national economic management. Those who enter SECO through an AFC apprenticeship or a university internship do not perform typical 'secretarial work,' but participate in assignments that require understanding market dynamics, analyzing economic data, and providing direct support to federal decisions on trade and industrial policy.

International opportunities and linguistic specialization

A relevant but lesser-known aspect is the international dimension of work at SECO. The approximately 30 professionals seconded to various countries represent a gateway to European and global careers for those starting in the Swiss public sector. An apprentice who demonstrates potential and dedication in their role could see, in the medium term, opportunities for temporary transfer abroad, acquiring skills in economic diplomacy and commercial negotiation.

Key points

How to apply and selection procedure

The official source of SECO does not specify a detailed application procedure (opening dates of calls, recruitment portal, exact timelines). However, as with any Swiss federal entity, interested candidates should consult the official SECO website (seco.admin.ch) or the national platform for federal jobs.

Those wishing to apply for an AFC apprenticeship must meet the standard requirements for Switzerland: completion of compulsory school certificate, minimum language skills in one of the official languages (Italian for the Ticino site, French/German for other sites), and, for digital and IT profiles, basic knowledge in ICT. For university internships, student status at a federal university or polytechnic school (SUPSI, USI, ETHZ, etc.) and regular enrollment at the time of application are required.

Salary context and benefits

The SECO source does not provide explicit information on salaries, allowances, insurance premiums, or additional benefits for its employees. As with most Swiss federal entities, it is reasonable to expect that salaries follow the federal salary scales of the public administration (levels by function, seniority, grade), mandatory LAMal/KVG coverage, AVS/AHV and LPP/BVG contributions according to federal standards, and possible benefits such as meal vouchers and subsidized transportation. Those wishing to understand in detail how a federal payslip works or the calculations of pension (AVS/LPP) can consult the dedicated guides. For precise details on remuneration, it is advisable to contact the SECO Human Resources service directly or consult the normative documents on the remuneration of the federal public administration published by the Federal Department of Economic Affairs (DFE), of which SECO is a part.

Frequently Asked Questions
What skills are required to apply for an AFC apprenticeship at SECO?
SECO researches apprentices for three main profiles: commercial employees, digital business developers and IT specialists. A trade apprenticeship requires a certificate of compulsory education and language skills in an official Swiss language (Italian, French or German depending on the location). ICT basics are required for digital and IT profiles. The source does not further specify technical requirements; please refer directly to the SECO site for full descriptions of open positions.
How many people work at SECO and where are they located?
SECO employs more than 900 employees and executives, distributed in three main offices in Switzerland. The institution also has about 30 professionals posted in different countries, witnessing an active role in the international economic representation of Switzerland and in federal trade diplomacy.
What international career opportunities does SECO offer?
SECO's approximately 30 employees located abroad represent a gateway to economic diplomacy and commercial negotiation experiences. Although the source does not specify the selection mechanism for international postings, an apprentice or young professional who demonstrates potential could access these positions, acquiring European and global skills in the context of federal economic policy.
Does SECO hire translators and interpreters or offer language collaborations?
SECO has specialized language services with a network of freelance translators working in the official Swiss languages (Italian, French, German) and other languages. Translation mandates for external collaborators are provided on a regular basis. For those interested in the fields of translation or multilingual communication, this facility represents a potential opportunity for collaboration as a direct employee or specialised freelancer.
What are the salary advantages and benefits of a career at SECO?
The official source of the SECO does not specify detailed wages, allowances or benefits. As a federal entity, SECO presumably applies federal public administration pay scales, LAMal/KVG coverage, AVS/AHV and LPP/BVG contributions, and potential benefits such as transportation benefits and meal tickets. For precise information, please contact the SECO Human Resources Service or the Federal Department of Economics.
