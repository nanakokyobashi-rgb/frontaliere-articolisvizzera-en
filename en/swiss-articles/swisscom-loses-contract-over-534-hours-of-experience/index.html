534 hours of experience left: Swisscom loses a Confederation contract.

The Confederation has excluded Swisscom's bid for a contract due to a typo in the bid. Swisscom's offer did not meet all the requirements, but typo was the main cause of the exclusion.

The case occurred within the framework of the Confederation's "Infravento" project, which provides for the upgrading of communication and transport infrastructures in Switzerland. The contract in question had been banned in 2022 and had requested a detailed and complete offer from all the companies concerned.

According to sources close to the Confederation, the typo in Swisscom's bid referred to a specific requirement for the candidates' experience. The requirement required candidates to have at least 534 hours of experience in the field of communication and transportation infrastructure. However, Swisscom's bid had not included this requirement, which had led to the company's exclusion from the tender.

The Confederation confirmed that typing error was the main cause of the exclusion of Swisscom's offer. “The Confederation ruled out Swisscom's bid due to a typo in the bid,” a Confederation representative said. “The offer did not meet all the required requirements and typing error was the main cause of the exclusion.”

Action

  • The Confederation was not obliged to grant Swisscom the possibility to correct the typo. * The judgment is not yet final.

The recent ruling by the Swiss Confederation has left telecommunications industry insiders breathless. Swisscom, one of the leading telephone and internet operators in Switzerland, has lost a Confederation contract worth 534 hours of employee experience. This means that Swisscom will no longer be able to offer its services to federal officials for an indefinite period of time.

The typing error occurred during a tender procedure for the assignment of telecommunication services to federal officials. Swisscom had submitted a proposal to offer its services to federal officials, but the typo led to a reduction in the value of the proposal by more than 100,000 Swiss francs.

The Confederation was not obliged to grant Swisscom the possibility to correct the typo, due to a specific regulation governing tenders for the entrustment of services to federal officials. The legislation in question is the “Regulations on Tenders for the Assignment of Services to Federal Officials” of 15 June 2018, which states that proposals must be submitted in good time and that typos cannot be corrected once the proposal has been submitted.
