Swiss defense: 970 million requested for the army (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 18 September 2026

The Federal Council is asking for almost a billion extra francs for ground-to-air and radar systems to deal with threats in Europe.

Context

In brief

  • 970 million francs extra requested for the army
  • Funds for ground-to-air defense, radar, and anti-drone systems
  • Support for agriculture and security for international summits
  • Total projected expenditures of 1.068 billion francs

Key Facts

  • Applicant: Federal Council
  • Army Amount: 970 million francs
  • Agriculture Amount: 54 million francs
  • Summit Security Amount: 20 million francs
  • Swiss Amount (overnight stays): 0.7 million francs
  • Total budget expenditures: 1.068 billion francs

The Federal Council has submitted a credit request to Parliament as part of the second supplementary budget, forecasting an increase of nearly one billion francs for the armed forces. This decision, already anticipated in August, responds to a worsening threat situation in Europe and strong tension affecting the armaments market. The executive's goal is to guarantee production capacities and ensure that delivery terms for systems deemed urgently necessary are respected, in line with the 2026 armament program.

Detail of military investments

The breakdown of the 970 million francs allocated to the army is defined as follows: 650 million francs are planned for the purchase of medium-range ground-to-air defense systems, while 250 million are destined for short-range systems. A further 60 million francs will be used for the acquisition of a partially mobile medium-range radar, while 10 million francs are allocated for specific defense systems against mini-drones.

In addition to military spending, the government has requested further funds for various emergencies and commitments. 54 million francs have been allocated to support farms that suffered damage from drought during the summer. Regarding security linked to international events, 20 million francs are planned to cover 80% of the costs incurred by the Cantons of Geneva, Vaud, Valais, and Ticino for the G7 summit in Evian and the OSCE ministerial conference, which will be held in Lugano in December. Finally, 0.7 million francs are destined for Swiss company to reimburse the overnight stay expenses of air traffic security guards between 2016 and 2024.

Operational details

The impact of these decisions is directly reflected in the financial management of the Confederation and the organization of security in the involved Cantons. The Federal Council's request is not limited to immediate credits, but includes new commitment credits to allow the State to assume contractual obligations that extend beyond the current year. In particular, 100 million francs have been requested for urgent interventions in the real estate sector, aimed at improving the army's self-protection, 13.6 million for a portfolio management system for the Federal Department of Defence, Civil Protection and Sport (DDPS), and 15.8 million for the implementation of the cell broadcast alarm system.

Analysis of the cantonal and federal impact

For the residents and administrations of the Cantons of Ticino, Valais, Vaud, and Geneva, the federal intervention of 20 million francs represents a partial tax relief, covering 80% of the security costs for international summits. Without this contribution, the financial burden for organizing the OSCE conference in Lugano and the G7 in Evian would fall entirely on the cantonal budgets. This cost-sharing mechanism highlights how events of national or international importance require financial coordination between Bern and the Cantons.

On the infrastructural level, an additional credit of 5 million francs is planned for the Railway Infrastructure Fund (RIF), destined for the completion works of the New Railway Link through the Alps (NRLA). It is important to emphasize that this specific funding will not weigh on the Confederation's budget, as it is guaranteed directly by the RIF. This scenario differs from the credits for the army or agriculture, which instead impact State expenditures. From an accounting point of view, considering a credit carry-over of 23.6 million francs from 2025, the overall operation of the second supplement will lead to increased expenditures of 1.068 billion francs. The executive has, however, reassured that, thanks to higher-than-expected revenues, it will be possible to comply with the debt brake directives, maintaining national financial balance.

Key points

For those living and working in Switzerland, the implementation of these credits follows the standard parliamentary process for budget additions. Once Parliament has approved the Federal Council's requests, funds will be released for the execution of the 2026 armament programs and for reimbursements to the Cantons. Those operating in the agricultural sector, particularly in areas affected by the summer drought, must refer to cantonal procedures to access the 54 million francs allocated by the federal government for emergency support.

Operational Procedures and Deadlines

Regarding reimbursements related to the security of international summits, the process requires the Cantons of Ticino, Geneva, Vaud, and Valais to report the expenses incurred for the organization of the OSCE conference in Lugano (scheduled for December) and the G7 in Evian, to then receive 80% coverage from the Confederation. In parallel, the introduction of the cell broadcast alert system, funded with 15.8 million francs, will represent a technological shift in national emergency management, improving the ability to alert the population in real time.
