Operational details

The context of rising costs

Parliament's concern is well founded. With cassa malati's annual premium announcement just weeks away, specialist doctor salary data confirms a long-standing trend: Swiss healthcare costs continue to rise, underpinned by an incentive structure that favours high-value performance. For 2027, the comparisons site Comparis forecasts an increase in premiums of 3.7%, a percentage that is in addition to the increases of previous years and that will affect millions of policyholders throughout Switzerland.

This increase does not affect everyone in the same way. Low-income earners can rely on cantonal subsidies to reduce premiums (the so-called cost compensation system), but the situation for the middle classes remains critical: premiums rise, while salaries stagnate. Moreover, the increase is not evenly distributed across the cantons — each canton applies its own criteria in determining premiums and disbursing subsidies. The result is growth perceived as unfair and unsustainable by millions of residents.

Key points

How to move as a citizen: three concrete actions

The parliamentary debate takes a long time — a popular initiative requires at least 18 months to collect signatures, and regulatory changes even more. Meanwhile, the rewards keep going up. Here's how you can protect your wallet based on current data.

First step: check the cantonal subsidy. Not all policyholders know they are entitled to a premium reduction. Each canton independently manages the subsidy system (the so-called cost compensation system, financed by the State, the cantons and the municipalities). If your premium exceeds a certain percentage of household income — the threshold varies by canton, but hovers around 7-8% — you are entitled to ask for a reduction. Contact the relevant cantonal office (usually the Department of Health or Finance in your canton) and provide your tax return. The subsidy is retroactive: if you are entitled to a reduction and have not yet applied for it, you can recover the premiums of the last few years.

Second step: compare the sick coffers. The LAMal (Federal Health Insurance Act) requires each insurer to offer the same basic compulsory coverage, but premiums vary considerably between cantons, regions and cities, sometimes as much as 20-30%. Frequently Asked Questions
What do the three policy proposals ask for?
The Swiss Federal Parliament is debating three proposals. The CDU (Rémy Wyssmann) proposes direct negotiations between insurers and suppliers, without State intervention. The PS (Sarah Wyss) calls for a ban on bonuses for doctors. Liberal Greens (Patrick Hässig) want to recalibrate incentives to reward efficiency, not volume. The three proposals reflect different approaches to the problem of rising healthcare costs.
By how much will premiums increase in 2027?
According to Comparis, health insurance premiums will increase by 3.7% in 2027. This increase is in addition to the increases of previous years and will affect millions of policyholders throughout Switzerland. The impact varies for different households, but each franc of increase represents a significant additional expense.
How much do Swiss radiologists earn?
Some Swiss radiologists receive salaries in excess of CHF 300,000. These figures have fuelled the parliamentary debate on health costs and prompted several politicians to call for corrective action to counter the growth in health costs.
Who is entitled to the prize subsidy?
Low income earners can count on cantonal subsidies to reduce premiums (cost compensation system, financed by the State, cantons and municipalities). If your premium exceeds a certain percentage of household income (around 7-8%), you are entitled to claim a reduction. Contact the Department of Health or Finance of your canton with your tax return.
How can I reduce my expenses now?
You can check the cantonal subsidy if the premium exceeds 7-8% of income. In addition, you can compare sickness funds: LAMal imposes the same mandatory coverage, but premiums also vary by 20-30% between cantons and regions. The exchange is free from 1 November every year.
