How the premium reduction works

The reduction in premiums for compulsory health insurance in the Canton of Zug is based on the insured's income bracket. This means that those with lower incomes will pay less for their insurance. The deductible, i.e. the sum that the insured must pay before being able to access medical services, varies from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 based on the income bracket.

Exemplification with real data

Suppose a worker from Zug has an income of CHF 50,000 per year and a family of two. The deductible for them would be CHF 500, as they fall into the lowest income bracket. If the worker has an income of CHF 80'000, the deductible would be CHF 1500. This means that the worker with a higher income will pay more of the deductible than the worker with a lower income.

Rules and regulations

The reduction of premiums for compulsory health insurance in the Canton of Zug is regulated by the Federal Law on Health Insurance (KVG) of 1996. The law establishes that premiums for health insurance must be reduced according to the income bracket of the insured. Furthermore, the law establishes that the deductible cannot exceed CHF 2500.

How premium reduction works

The allowance for adults is CHF 300, CHF 500, CHF 1000, CHF 1500, CHF 2000 and CHF 2500. Premiums are reduced by income bracket, as follows:

  • Adults with an income below CHF 3500 pay a deductible of CHF 300.
  • Adults with income between CHF 3500 and CHF 4000 pay a deductible of CHF 500.
  • Adults with income between CHF 4000 and CHF 4500 pay a deductible of CHF 1000.
  • Adults with income between CHF 4500 and CHF 5000 pay a deductible of CHF 1500.
  • Adults with an income above CHF 5000 pay a deductible of CHF 2000.
