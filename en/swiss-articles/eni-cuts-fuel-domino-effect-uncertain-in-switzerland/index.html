Eni cuts fuel, domino effect uncertain in Switzerland

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 28 September 2026

Eni sets a price cap at 2,19 €/l for diesel and 1,99 €/l for petrol (2,05 CHF and 1,88 CHF) for one month, with a possible extension until the end of the year; 30 Enilive stations in Ticino.

Context

In brief

  • Eni cuts petrol and diesel starting Monday 28 September
  • Cap: 2,19 euros/litre for diesel, 1,99 for petrol
  • Price cap valid for one month, possible extension until year-end
  • 30 Enilive stations in Ticino, Swiss extension uncertain

Key facts

  • What → Eni price cap on petrol and diesel
  • Prices → 2,19 euros/litre for diesel; 1,99 euros/litre for petrol
  • Amounts in CHF → 2,05 for diesel; 1,88 for petrol
  • Duration → one month, possible extension until year-end
  • Average saving → 9,35 euros for diesel; 8,45 euros for petrol on the regular network
  • Switzerland → 30 Enilive stations in Canton Ticino

On Monday 28 September, in Rome, Eni launched discounts on petrol and diesel. The price cap sets diesel at 2,19 euros per litre, equivalent to 2,05 Swiss francs, and unleaded petrol at 1,99 euros, equivalent to 1,88 francs. The stated reduction is around 17 cents compared with current average levels.

The cap will remain in effect for one month, with the possibility of an extension until the end of the year. Eni presented it as a new contribution of solidarity to the country, consumers and its own customers. The Italian government thanked the company, linking the decision to the need to curb high fuel prices which, because of the difficult international context, are affecting families.

The decision has already prompted a first reaction. Socar, an Azerbaijani energy company that acquired Italiana Petroli from Api Holding last May, announced a limit on petrol and diesel selling prices across its network. The discounts will start at IP stations and will then be extended to Esso stations. Experts, however, still consider the spread of the measures among other operators uncertain and point to the presence of obstacles.

Differences between the initiatives

The two initiatives do not coincide. IP has not set, at least for now, a price cap with precise values: it has committed to containing prices, also defining them based on the sustainability of the entire supply chain. One possibility is the initial involvement of IPmatic stations, which are fully automated and have no attendant, but the methods and timing have not been made official. Eni's mechanism appears broader and could involve Esso stations and other operators that purchase fuel from IP to market it under different brands.

The response for Switzerland remains open. It is not yet possible to know whether the measure will reach Enilive and Socar's Swiss network. Enilive Suisse, which operates 30 filling stations in Canton Ticino alone, has been contacted but has not yet replied. The source indicates that lowering prices in the border area should not be enough to trigger a real rush by people from Ticino to fill up in the provinces of Como and Varese. The situation may be reassessed in the coming days.

Operational details

What changes for those who refuel in Ticino

For those who live in Ticino, the operational fact is not yet a new Swiss price, but the uncertain scope of the Italian announcement. The values in euros and Adusbef's estimates describe the network affected by the Italian measures: they do not authorize considering 2,19 euros for diesel or 1,99 for petrol as prices automatically applicable to an Enilive station in Switzerland. The distinction is crucial for assessing the cost of living without turning an announcement into savings that are already available.

Three scenarios to distinguish

| Scenario | What is known | Practical interpretation | | Eni in Italy | Precise cap and defined duration | The figure does not confirm a price for Switzerland | | Enilive and Socar in Ticino | Possible extension, but no response from Enilive Suisse | Prices and timing remain to be verified | | IP and Esso | IP launch and subsequent Esso extension announced by Socar | The domino effect remains possible, but not certain |

The first useful comparison concerns the route taken. Adusbef calculates an average saving of 9,35 euros on a full tank of diesel and 8,45 euros on a full tank of petrol on the ordinary road network; on motorways, the situation is different. For a Ticinese user, therefore, the figure is not a universal amount: it depends on the fuel and the type of network, as well as on whether it is applied in Switzerland. The source does not provide an equivalent amount for Ticino stations. The comparison between the figures in euros and the values expressed in francs can be followed using comparatore CHF/EUR, without confusing currency conversion with the extension of the discount.

The IP model also deserves attention. Since no numerical cap has yet been set, the containment would depend on the sustainability of the entire supply chain; the possible involvement of IPmatic adds an operational hypothesis, not a certain date. For families following costo della vita in Svizzera, the point to measure therefore remains the actual price at the pump in Ticino. The simple Italian announcement, on its own, does not yet indicate how much will be saved in Switzerland.

Key points

How to assess the impact over the next few days

The situation can be reassessed over the next few days, as the source indicates. To avoid confusing Italian announcements with a measure already operational in Switzerland, it is advisable to follow five steps.

A five-step check

1. Separate the date and territory. Monday 28 September is the start date of the Eni discount announced for the Italian network. It should not be read as automatic confirmation for the Swiss network.

2. Identify the fuel. Diesel and petrol have different caps: 2,19 euro and 1,99 euro per litre, with the equivalents in francs indicated by the source. The comparison must be made on the product actually purchased.

3. Distinguish the network. For savings estimates, Adusbef refers to the ordinary road network and treats the case of motorways separately. A calculation referring to the former should not be transferred to the latter.

4. Verify any extension to Ticino. Enilive Suisse operates 30 stations in Canton Ticino, but no response has arrived on whether the measure also applies there. As long as this point remains open, the Italian price cap is not a guarantee for a Swiss distributor.

5. Follow the domino schedule. Socar indicated a start at IP stations and a subsequent extension to Esso; for IP, however, the arrangements and timing have not been made official. The situation must therefore be updated when indications emerge regarding the networks involved.

This sequence makes it possible to use the available figures without turning them into promises. The source does not announce a rush by people from Ticino to fill up in Como and Varese: it says that the reduction in the border area should not be sufficient, but leaves open a new assessment. The practical figure to monitor will therefore be the actual price charged, not just the cap announced in Italy.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the price cap set by Eni for petrol and diesel in Italy?
Eni has set a ceiling of 2.19 euros per litre for diesel and 1.99 euros per litre for green petrol, corresponding to 2.05 francs and 1.88 francs, valid for one month with possible extension until the end of the year, presented as a contribution of solidarity and welcomed with thanks by the Italian government.
Does Eni's provision also apply to fuel stations in Switzerland, specifically in the Canton of Ticino?
The source indicates that it is not yet known if the discount will be extended to the 30 Enilive stations managed by Enilive Suisse in Ticino; the company has not yet responded, so prices and times remain to be verified and the Italian price cap cannot be considered as a guarantee for a Swiss distributor.
What other operators have announced similar measures and what are the uncertainties?
SOCAR has stated that it will apply a limit to prices along its network, starting from IP stations and then extending it to It; however, for IP a precise numerical price cap has not yet been defined, neither the methods nor the times have been officialised, making the domino effect possible but not certain.
