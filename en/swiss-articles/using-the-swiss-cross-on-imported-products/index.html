How to use the Swiss cross on foreign products? (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 12 August 2026

Switzerland has rules for using the Swiss cross on foreign products.

Context

In a nutshell

The Swiss cross is a symbol of quality and authenticity that can be used on foreign products if combined with specific indications. The IGE (Institute of Labelling and Quality Management) monitors compliance with the 'Swissness' legislation, which establishes the rules for the use of the Swiss cross.

Key facts

The Swiss cross can be used if at least 60% of the production costs are incurred in Switzerland. The essential phase of processing must take place in the country. For food products, at least 80% of the weight of the raw materials must come from Switzerland. The head office of the company and the place of actual management must be located in Switzerland.

Concrete examples

Suppose a watch production company has a subsidiary in Switzerland and produces watches that are assembled in Switzerland, but with components from other countries. In this case, the company could use the Swiss cross if at least 60% of the production costs are incurred in Switzerland. For example, if the cost of production is 100,000 Swiss francs, at least 60,000 Swiss francs must be incurred in Switzerland.

The legislation governing the use of the Swiss cross on foreign products is established by the Federal Office of Switzerland (OFOS) and the Code of Indices of Provenance (CIP) of 20 June 2019. The Code provides that products wishing to use the Swiss cross must meet certain production and processing requirements.
