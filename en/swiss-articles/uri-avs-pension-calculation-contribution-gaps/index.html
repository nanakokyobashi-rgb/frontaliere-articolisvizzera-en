  • Each missing year of contributions reduces the monthly pension by about 2-3%

Key facts

  • What: First pillar AVS and supplementary benefits in the Canton of Uri
  • Who: Active residents and pensioners who contribute or receive pensions
  • Contributions: 5.3% employee (10.6% total with employer)

  • Minimum: Supplementary benefits for those who do not reach the cantonal minimum subsistence level
  • Gaps: Years without contributions reduce the calculated pension

In the Canton of Uri, as in every Swiss canton, the first pillar of the pension system — the AVS (Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance) — rests on three pillars: the systematic collection of mandatory contributions, the faithful recording of each year of contribution, and the integration through supplementary benefits for those with insufficient income. 📊 All this is coordinated by an institution often unknown to contributors: the cantonal compensation fund, the local administrative office that acts as a link between the hundreds of thousands of active workers and the pensioners who depend on their future pension.

Operational details

# How Contribution Gaps Affect Rent Calculation

The monthly AVS income is proportional to three factors, all managed by the cantonal compensation fund:

1. Number of years of contribution

The formula provides for a “standard” number of years: 44 for men, 43 for women (stylized, evolving data). Those who have worked all these years receive an income calculated on a full average salary. Those who have less (e.g. 40 years instead of 44) receive a proportionally reduced amount. Each missing year subtracts about 2-3% from the total.

2. Average career salary

The fund calculates the average gross income for all years of contribution. A higher average salary = higher income. This average income is revalued each year according to the federal wage index (revaluation factor of the AVS, updated annually by the Confederation).

3. Impact of Gaps: Bridging vs. Accepting Reductions

If there have been years of non-contribution during the career, the fund offers two paths: voluntary contributions (retroactively paying contributions for the missing years, calculated on the average income of that period — this fills the gap in the record and increases the calculated income), or supplementary benefits (if the voluntary payments are not economically sustainable, the cantonal supplementary benefits supplement the income up to the living minimum).

Key points

Access to Supplementary Benefits: Step-by-Step Operational Guide

Cantonal supplementary benefits are the safety net for those with insufficient AHV pension. In the Canton of Uri, they are governed by cantonal ordinances that define amounts, eligibility criteria, and application procedures. The cantonal compensation fund manages the applications.

Step 1: Check General Eligibility

You are entitled to supplementary benefits if: your monthly AHV pension (alone) is below the minimum living standard defined by the Canton of Uri (specific amount not available in this guide; consult the fund directly), your personal assets (bank accounts, additional real estate, investments) do not exceed the maximum threshold set by cantonal law, you reside in the Canton of Uri (official residence for >90 days per year).

Step 2: Gather Documentation

Before submitting your application, prepare: AHV account statement (request a complete copy from the fund or print from the federal portal myAVS with federal credentials); estimated pension calculation (the fund sends this to you automatically 4 weeks before retirement; if you don't have it, request it); income declaration for the last 2 years (cantonal forms or copy of the declaration to the municipality); residence certificate (issued by the municipality of residence, valid for 3 months); bank account statement (balance as of December 31 of the previous year for all accounts in Switzerland and abroad); documentation of extraordinary expenses (rents, medical care not covered, legitimate creditor debts, if relevant).

Frequently Asked Questions
What exactly is the cantonal clearinghouse?
It is the administrative office that collects AVS contributions (5.3% employee, 10.6% total with employer), maintains the register of individual contributions, verifies the completeness of the contribution record, and communicates cantonal supplementary benefits. It does not manage money like a bank, but manages the flow of data and social rights for all active and retired residents in the canton.
Can I fill a contribution gap before retirement?
Yes, through voluntary contributions paid to the compensation fund. If your record is missing a year, you can pay the relative contribution (calculated based on the average income for that period). The cashier updates your record and recalculates the presumed annuity. Do this at least 1-2 years before retirement for proper planning.
If my AVS pension is low, am I entitled to supplementary benefits?
Yes, if your monthly income is less than the cantonal minimum living wage (specific figure varies for URIs) and your assets do not exceed legal thresholds. Go to the cantonal compensation fund, fill in the application form, and attach documentation of income and assets. The fund verifies eligibility within 2-4 weeks.
Where can I find details on URI-specific supplemental benefit amounts?
The Canton Uri Clearing House is the official office. You can also consult the website of the State Chancellery of Uri for the cantonal ordinance on supplementary benefits (includes amounts and criteria updated annually).
Do voluntary contributions really increase my income?
Yes, each year of voluntary contributions fills a gap in your record and increases the calculated total monthly AVS annuity (proportionally: 1 year ~2-3% more). If you have many gaps, the priority is to fill the years with the highest income first to maximize the effect on the final income.
