Childcare in Switzerland is not a federal responsibility but a cantonal one. In the canton of Bern, families have several options to balance professional commitments with childcare: daycare centers (Kindertagesstätten), Tagesfamilien (day families), and after-school programs (Hort). The fees for these services are not fixed at the national level but vary from canton to canton and, within the canton of Bern, from municipality to municipality.

A distinctive feature of the Bernese system is the calculation of fees based on family income. This means that the cost of childcare depends on the family's economic situation: parents with higher incomes pay higher contributions, while those with lower incomes benefit from reduced fees. It is a progressive mechanism aimed at making the service accessible to all segments of the population.

The cantonal administration of Bern also offers dedicated subsidies to reduce the economic burden. These contributions are intended for families that meet specific income criteria and represent a crucial element of the cantonal and communal welfare system.

Types of available services

In the canton of Bern, the range of solutions is wide. Daycare centers welcome children from birth to 4-5 years old, with flexible hours (full-time, part-time, by days). Tagesfamilien are professionals who welcome a limited number of children at their home, under the supervision of the cantonal administration and the municipality. After-school programs cater to school-age children and offer afternoon care and care during holidays. Each service has its own cost structure and specific access criteria defined by the municipality of residence.

How fees are calculated

In the canton of Bern, the cantonal administration sets the general principles, but it is the municipalities that define the specific fees for childcare services in their territory. The calculation takes into account mainly the gross family income: the higher the income, the higher the contribution tends to be. This progressivity ensures that the cost remains accessible even to families with limited economic resources. In addition to income, other factors can influence: the number of children in care simultaneously, the hours and days of attendance, the age of the child. There is no single fee for the entire canton; each municipality has management autonomy.

Cantonal and municipal subsidies are added to the progressive fee system. A family that meets certain income criteria can benefit from contributions that cover part of the costs. The goal is to ensure that childcare does not represent an economic obstacle for those who work and have a family.

Procedure to access services

Families looking for a place in a daycare center or after-school program should contact the competent office of their municipality of residence. Each municipality in the canton of Bern has a contact person for childcare. It is advisable to inquire in advance about available places, as in many municipalities the demand exceeds the supply. The registration procedure and times vary from municipality to municipality, as do the priority criteria.

Operational details

Economic implications for families

The cost of childcare has a significant impact on the family budget. In the canton of Bern, as in the rest of Switzerland, a family with both parents working and a young child in daycare faces the central question: is professional childcare economically sustainable? The answer depends mainly on two factors: the family's gross income and the amount of available subsidies.

How subsidies reduce the burden

Imagine a family with a substantial total annual gross income. Without subsidies, the fee calculated on income could result in a significant monthly contribution to daycare. However, if the municipality applies subsidy criteria and the family meets the parameters (number of children, financial situation, etc.), the actual contribution could be significantly reduced. This is where the cantonal and municipal policies supporting families make a concrete difference: subsidies are the main tool to ensure fair access to childcare.

Comparison between services

Another factor to consider: which service to choose between daycare, Tagesfamilien, and after-school care? Daycares often have more standardized fees, regulated by both the canton and the municipality. Tagesfamilien can sometimes offer slightly different fees and greater hourly flexibility, but they vary greatly depending on the individual provider. After-school care, being a service for older children and therefore with lower costs than daycares, can represent a less costly solution for those with school-aged children.

Tax deductions

An often overlooked aspect: external childcare expenses can have tax implications. In many Swiss cantons, including the canton of Bern, documented childcare expenses are partially or fully deductible from taxable income. In the Bernese context, it is advisable to verify with the cantonal tax office or the municipality which expenses are recognized as deductible and to what extent. This element further reduces the net burden on the family and represents a concrete opportunity for tax savings.

When to start the search

Experience in Bernese municipalities shows that the demand for daycare places often exceeds the available supply. It is advisable to start the search well in advance (even 6-12 months before the desired start date). At the same time, it is recommended to request information on available subsidies for your situation: some municipalities publish online simulators, others require direct contact with the office.

Key points

How to access childcare services in the canton of Bern

Those interested in enrolling a child in a childcare service in the canton of Bern must follow a series of concrete steps. The procedure begins with the search and selection of the facility, proceeds with the registration, and concludes with the signing of the contract and the start of the service.

Step 1: Inform yourself at the municipality

The first step is to contact the municipal office responsible for childcare (often part of the family services office or the social services department). Here you can obtain a list of accredited facilities — public and private daycare centers, Tagesfamilien, after-school programs. Each municipality usually also publishes information on tenders, registration deadlines, and waiting lists, often available online.

Step 2: Check access criteria and simulate costs

Before registering, it is essential to request a fee simulation. The municipal office can calculate the required contribution based on the declared family income. This is the time to ask which subsidies the family might be eligible for and how to apply for them. This data allows for a realistic economic evaluation and a conscious choice.

Step 3: Apply and wait for the response

Once the preferred facility has been identified, the family submits the registration application according to the municipality's procedures (paper form or online platform). Many municipalities have specific registration periods (e.g., spring for the following school or calendar year). If the application is accepted, the contract with the facility follows, where payment methods and attendance conditions are defined.

Step 4: Apply for cantonal subsidies

Parallel to the registration with the facility, the family must apply for a subsidy with the municipality and/or the canton if they believe they are entitled to it. The criteria and required documentation (income certificates, bank statements, tax returns) vary, but the municipal office provides the complete checklist. The subsidy approval times can range from a few weeks to a few months; it is best to submit the application as soon as possible.

Step 5: Annual planning

Once registered and receiving the subsidy, the family must stay informed about any changes in fees (usually annual) and subsidy criteria. It is advisable to recertify the income each year and check if the economic situation allows the subsidy to be maintained. Timely planning and quick requests to the municipalities significantly increase the chances of obtaining a place in a quality facility and fully benefiting from the available subsidies in the Bernese system.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who sets custody fees in the canton of Bern?
The cantonal administration of Bern sets the general principles and guidelines, but each municipality of the canton has autonomy in the specific definition of fees for custodial services in its territory. This ensures that the calculation remains progressive based on household income, while maintaining equity and accessibility.
How are custody fees calculated in the canton of Bern?
Rates are primarily based on the household's annual gross income. The higher the income, the higher the contribution required. Additional factors include the number of children in custody, attendance times (part-time vs. full-time), and the age of the child. Each municipality applies its own formula, so it is essential to ask your municipality of residence for a simulation.
What subsidies are available to reduce custody costs?
The canton of Bern and the municipalities offer dedicated subsidies for families that meet specific income criteria. The amount and criteria vary from municipality to municipality, but the objective is to significantly reduce the economic burden of custody. You must submit an application to the competent municipal office with income documentation.
Where to go for information on nurseries, Tagesfamilien and afterschool in my municipality?
Contact the municipal office for family services or social services (often also called the social councillor or welfare office). Each municipality publishes an updated list of accredited facilities, registration forms, schedules and deadlines. Many municipalities also have online portals dedicated to childcare.
Are Custody Fees Tax Deductible?
In many Swiss cantons, including the canton of Bern, documented custodial expenses may be deductible from taxable income in the cantonal and municipal tax return. The maximum deductible amount and conditions vary per canton; we recommend checking with your municipality's cantonal tax office for exact details.
