PS launches initiative against gender-based violence in Thun

On Saturday, during the congress of the Swiss Socialist Party in Thun, the delegates unanimously approved launching a popular initiative to strengthen the protection of women from violence and combat gender-based violence. The project is not limited to calling for more prevention: it also proposes closer cooperation between the Confederation, the cantons and specialized services.

The financial demand is explicit. The PS is calling for at least 500 million additional francs to be made available each year to combat gender-based violence. The party argues that support for victims still depends too much on their place of residence and considers the progress made in this area insufficient.

To support its position, the PS recalls that 27 femicides were recorded in Switzerland in 2025 and that there have already been 18 cases since the beginning of this year. Tamara Funiciello, National Councillor and co-president of Socialist Women, described femicides as merely the tip of the iceberg, also citing rapes, beatings, humiliations and discrimination. “Protection costs money, but violence costs lives,” she said.

The institutional step

Federal Councillor Beat Jans also spoke at the congress. He argued that domestic and gender-based violence are not private matters, but a responsibility of the state governed by the rule of law and society as a whole.

Thun’s green light does not conclude the process: before the PS launches the signature collection, scheduled for spring 2027, the text will have to undergo legal review by the Federal Chancellery. In her speech, Socialist co-president Mattea Meyer accused the UDC of having “a structural problem with violence,” citing visits to Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping and tributes to Donald Trump.

Operational details

The territorial issue

The proposal's most concrete implication concerns the relationship between place of residence, services and institutional levels. The SP starts from the observation that support for victims still depends too much on their place of residence. Viewed from a national perspective, this criticism shifts the focus: it is not enough to demand a response to violence; it is necessary to ask to what extent prevention and assistance are coordinated among the Confederation, Cantons and specialized services.

More resources, but with what structure

The request for at least 500 million additional francs each year introduces a precise financial dimension, but the available text does not define how the resources should be distributed. The source does not specify amounts for individual Cantons, allocation criteria, programs to which the funds should be allocated or a control mechanism. For this reason, it is not possible to translate the figure into a concrete benefit for an individual or an expense attributable to a territory. The key issue to watch in the debate will be the link between funding and objectives: strengthened prevention, institutional cooperation and a response less dependent on place of residence.

For anyone living or working in Switzerland, the distinction between a proposal and a measure currently in force is decisive. The available text does not describe a new service, a change to support arrangements or the immediate disbursement of 500 million. The source places the project in a preliminary phase: the text still has to undergo legal review by the Federal Chancellery, and signature collection is scheduled to begin only in spring 2027. Until that stage, the operational fact is therefore political and procedural, not a change that has already been implemented.

Beat Jans's intervention is also indicative. Defining domestic violence and gender-based violence as a responsibility of the rule-of-law state and society as a whole broadens the scope of the discussion, but does not indicate new responsibilities or concrete obligations. Mattea Meyer's position on the SVP belongs to a different level: it is a political criticism of the party and its leadership, not a decision on the initiative. Separating these levels helps avoid confusing the requested funding, the organization of services and the debate between parties.
