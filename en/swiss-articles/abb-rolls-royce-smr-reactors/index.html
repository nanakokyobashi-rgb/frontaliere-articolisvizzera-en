A strategic alliance for next-generation nuclear energy

Swiss electrical engineering giant ABB and British company Rolls-Royce SMR have signed a memorandum of understanding to explore automation and electrification solutions designed for small modular reactors. The agreement represents a strategic move to accelerate the spread of next-generation nuclear energy on a global scale.

The small modular reactors (SMRs) at the heart of the alliance are capable of generating 470 MW of low-carbon electricity, a capacity sufficient to power one million homes in the United Kingdom for 60 years.

Rolls-Royce SMR has already launched design activities for the first three plants in North Wales, which will create thousands of jobs, as ABB reports in today's press release. The British company is also active in the Czech Republic, where it collaborates with partner ČEZ at Temelín. More recently, it was selected by Videberg Kraft to build three SMRs on the Swedish peninsula of Värö.

Stacy O'Brien, senior executive at Rolls-Royce SMR, highlighted the value of the standardized approach: 'Rolls-Royce SMR is transforming the way nuclear projects are implemented, ensuring greater certainty on costs and timelines through a standardized approach and factory-built manufacturing'.

Operational details

Implications for the global energy strategy

ABB has been operating in the industrial automation and energy sector for decades, with a consolidated presence in Switzerland and internationally. A partnership with Rolls-Royce SMR represents a strategic positioning for ABB in the emerging market for small modular reactors, which analysts consider one of the most promising solutions for the global energy transition.

Small modular reactors differ from large traditional nuclear plants in flexibility, potentially reduced construction costs and compatibility with distributed energy networks. Rolls-Royce SMR has developed a standardised, factory-built approach, elements that contribute to greater certainty in costs and lead times — critical factors that have always held back investment in nuclear energy.

ABB contributes to this partnership with its expertise in automation, process control and electrification. These elements are essential for the efficient and safe operation of nuclear plants, both small and large. The company said it took the opportunity to "further strengthen our implementation team" in this collaboration, indicating a potential increase in investments in human and technological resources.

Key points

What this partnership means for the sector

The collaboration between ABB and Rolls-Royce SMR is not only a commercial partnership, but a crucial market signal: the main players in the global energy transition believe SMRs are mature for significant scale-up in the coming decades. ABB, with over a century of history in industrial automation and providing critical solutions for energy infrastructure, would not have signed this memorandum without seriously evaluating the commercial growth potential of the SMR sector.

For European governments and regulators, this news communicates a clear message: the private sector is ready to invest in small modular reactors once regulatory conditions become predictable and stable. The critical variable remains political support: reducing regulatory approval times, providing regulatory clarity on standards and licenses, and in some cases providing initial incentives or investment guarantees.

Next steps and the role of regulation

Rolls-Royce SMR is currently in the final design stages for the first three plants in North Wales. If the projects proceed according to announced plans, they will represent the first commercial-scale test of the British company's standardized and factory-built approach. Parallel projects in the Czech Republic (Temelín with ČEZ) and Sweden (Värö with Videberg Kraft) will follow the same technical and commercial model, accumulating operational experience and building a credible track record for investors and financiers.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the advantages of small modular reactors (SMR) compared to traditional nuclear plants?
According to Rolls-Royce SMR press releases, SMRs offer a standardized, factory-built approach, which ensures greater certainty in costs and lead times. The modular reactors have lower capacity (470 MW in the Rolls-Royce SMR solution), compatibility with distributed energy networks and are built according to replicable models, reducing technical and commercial risk.
Where will the first ABB-Rolls-Royce partnership SMR plants be built?
Rolls-Royce SMR has started design activities for the first three plants in North Wales (UK), where they will create thousands of jobs. At the same time, the company is active in the Czech Republic with partner ČEZ in Temelín and has been selected by Videberg Kraft for three SMRs in the Swedish peninsula of Värö.
What generative capacity do the SMRs of the Rolls-Royce SMR-ABB partnership have?
The small modular reactors have a capacity of 470 MW of low-carbon electricity. This capacity is enough to power one million homes in the UK for 60 years, as indicated in the data provided by Rolls-Royce SMR.
What is ABB's role in the partnership with Rolls-Royce SMR?
ABB brings its expertise in providing automation and electrification solutions for energy infrastructure. The company contributes skills in process control, industrial automation and safety systems, essential elements for the efficient and safe operation of small modular reactors.
What are the International Energy Agency's forecasts for the spread of SMRs?
According to the International Energy Agency, with the current regulatory frameworks the global capacity of SMR will reach 40 GW by 2050. However, with targeted policy support and leaner regulations, capacity could reach 120 GW — a significant increase that hinges on governments' willingness to streamline regulatory approval.
