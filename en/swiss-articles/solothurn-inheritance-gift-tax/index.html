Canton of Solothurn: inheritance and gift tax

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 30 September 2026

Swiss taxation at three levels: for the Canton of Solothurn, kinship, exemptions, declaration and deadlines matter.

Three tax levels define the Swiss framework: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. To understand inheritance and gift tax in the Canton of Solothurn, this distinction comes before comparing rates. Each Canton has its own law and multiplier; for municipalities, the multiplier is applied to the cantonal tax. A rule relating to Solothurn should therefore not be treated as a national rule.

The framework not to conflate

The topic indicated by the source focuses on four elements: tax rates by degree of kinship, exemptions for spouses and descendants, declaration and deadlines to be observed. These are the fields to keep separate when analyzing an inheritance or gift. The family relationship guides the search for the applicable rate; being a spouse or descendant guides the verification of the exemption. The declaration component completes the check and requires following the deadline set by the applicable procedure.

The territorial factor and the family factor do not serve the same function: the former establishes the cantonal and municipal framework; the latter structures the search for the rate and exemption. The declaration connects the two levels and brings the review to the operational level.

UFAS/BSV concerns social security, namely AVS/AI/LPP, but does not set taxes; UST/BFS produces statistics and does not set rates. The analysis therefore starts with the tax domicile, moves through the family relationship and reaches the compliance requirements. The Canton of Solothurn is the jurisdiction to examine; the three levels and the map of offices are the filter for avoiding confusion among rules, multipliers and responsibilities.

Operational details

From the framework to the practical decision

The concrete implication of the cantonal model is that the name of the tax alone is not enough to interpret the result. In a hypothetical scenario, whoever has to assess an inheritance or a gift starts with the transaction and the degree of kinship. Only afterward do they check whether the recipient falls into the category of spouse or descendants, because the editorial perspective requires a specific review of the relevant exemptions. The declaration and deadline are part of the same sequence, not a separate step.

Three filters to avoid incorrect calculations

The first filter is family-related. The tax rate must be sought in relation to the degree of kinship indicated by the Solothurn rule; the exemption must be verified for the categories mentioned, spouse and descendants. The second filter is territorial. Each canton has its own law and its own multiplier: a result referring to Solothurn cannot automatically be transferred to another canton. The municipal framework must then remain visible, because the municipality's multiplier is applied to the cantonal tax.

The third filter is institutional. AFC/ESTV concerns direct federal tax and VAT, while cantonal and municipal taxation falls within the cantonal administrations. UFAS/BSV is linked to AVS, AI and LPP, not to setting taxes; UST/BFS produces statistics, not tax rates. This separation prevents using a social-security or statistical reference to resolve a tax issue.

If one item remains mixed in with the others, it becomes difficult to understand whether one is reading a cantonal rule, a municipal step or a federal competence.

A calcolatore stipendio/imposte can help organize a general numerical check, but the interpretation must remain anchored in the law of the canton and the parameters of the case. The practical advantage is having a sheet in which kinship, exemption, tax level and deadline remain legible without overlapping different areas of responsibility.

Key points

How to organize the verification

The concrete action consists of turning the topic into a short, traceable sequence. The worksheet must start from the tax situation of the Canton of Solothurn and keep the cantonal level, the municipal level and any federal reference separate. This way, the search for tax rates is not mixed with the verification of exemptions or with the deadline for the return.

Five operational steps

1. Classify the transaction. Indicate whether the case concerns an inheritance or a gift. 2. Define the kinship. State the relevant degree of kinship and separately verify the status of a spouse or descendant. 3. Set the territorial scope. Use the law of the Canton of Solothurn and consider the municipality without confusing its multiplier with the cantonal one. 4. Choose the correct point of contact. For direct federal tax and VAT, the point of reference is AFC/ESTV; for cantonal and municipal taxation, the cantonal administrations are responsible. 5. Complete the procedural part. Include the return and the deadline to be observed in the same verification of the tax rate and any exemption.

The sequence also serves to rule out two shortcuts. The first is to look for a single answer for all Cantons, when the source establishes that each Canton has its own law and multiplier. The second is to ask an entity that performs another function for a tax rate: UFAS/BSV concerns AVS, AI and LPP, while UST/BFS deals with statistics and does not set taxes.

The criterion also applies to communication between levels: a request concerning cantonal or municipal taxation must be kept separate from a request concerning direct federal tax and VAT. Separating the functions makes the case file more readable and makes it possible to check that kinship, exemption and deadline have been addressed within the same Solothurn framework.

For the return-filing step, dichiarazione delle imposte can be used as an organizational aid, precisely stating the tax level and the applicable deadline. The final check must leave four items visible: transaction, kinship, territorial jurisdiction and procedure. Frequently Asked Questions
Which tax levels must be distinguished for the Canton of Solothurn?
The Swiss taxation indicated by the source is divided into three levels: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. Each Canton has its own law and its own multiplier. For Municipalities, the multiplier is applied on the cantonal tax. The reading of the case of Solothurn must therefore keep the cantonal level and the municipal level separate, without treating the rule as national.
Which elements affect the assessment of inheritances and gifts?
The editorial perimeter includes the rates linked to the degree of kinship, exemptions for spouses and descendants, the declaration and the terms to be respected. The procedure must be read in this sequence: first the family relationship is identified, then any exemption occurs and finally the declaration and term are checked. These categories remain distinct in the practical verification.
Who manages cantonal and municipal taxation?
The cantonal administrations manage cantonal and municipal taxation. AFC/ESTV is associated with direct federal tax and VAT. UFAS/BSV concerns social security, AVS/AI/LPP, and does not set taxes; UST/BFS produces statistics and does not establish rates. The distinction serves to direct each step towards the competent level.
