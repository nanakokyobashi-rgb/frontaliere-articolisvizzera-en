Apprenticeship in Lucerne: a practical guide (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 September 2026

Search for internship, salary, social contributions and professional maturity: here's how to orient yourself in Swiss dual training in Lucerne.

Context

In brief

  • Dual apprenticeship: 3-4 years between company and vocational school
  • Maximum hours 45-50 hours/week, 4-5 weeks of guaranteed vacation
  • Increasing salary and social contributions from the first day

Key facts

  • What: Professional training (AFC) in a dual system
  • Where: Canton of Lucerne, companies and vocational schools
  • Who: Young people from 15 years old with compulsory education completed
  • Duration: 3-4 years depending on the profession
  • Start: Search 1-2 years before the start date
  • Protection: Federal labor law applies to all apprentices

Apprenticeship in Switzerland is the most widespread professional training path after compulsory schooling. In the Canton of Lucerne, the system operates on a dual basis: the young apprentice spends 60-70% of the time in the company (practical training with a qualified trainer) and 30-40% in a vocational school (theoretical lessons and general culture). This model combines practical skills and knowledge, ensuring that the labor market has figures ready for employment.

The search for the internship usually begins during the penultimate or last year of middle school. The main channels are the official job search sites, direct contact with companies in the sector of interest, training fairs organized by the canton, and school guidance offices. In the Canton of Lucerne, the cantonal vocational training office provides support to both young people and host companies in the matching process between demand and supply.

Operational details

Compensation and Salary Dynamics

The apprentice's compensation is not set at the federal level: each canton, sector, and company determines the monthly amount independently. In the Canton of Lucerne, as in other Swiss cantons, the salary range for apprentices varies depending on the profession and the chosen VET pathway. Generally, compensation increases year by year: the first year is lower, the second and third years increase progressively, reaching amounts closer to the salary of a qualified professional in the fourth year. Some sectors (e.g., crafts, manufacturing industry, retail trade) have collective wages agreed upon between employers' associations and unions, which establish uniform minimums for the category.

During the apprenticeship, compensation is subject to federal, cantonal, and municipal taxation, according to the Swiss three-tier system. Each canton has its own tax rates, and each municipality acts as a multiplier on the cantonal level. An apprentice residing in the Canton of Lucerne pays federal direct tax based on income (with deductible allowances for young people and students), then Lucerne cantonal tax, and finally a municipal tax on top of the cantonal base.

Key points

Professional Maturity: The Upgrade After AFC

Upon completion of the AFC apprenticeship (3-4 years), the apprentice receives the professional apprentice certificate (Lehrbrief), a nationally recognized document that allows them to practice their profession with verified qualifications. Many apprentices choose to complete a professional maturity program, which can be obtained in parallel during the internship (integrated part-time model) or after the AFC certificate (post-diploma professional maturity). Professional maturity adds theoretical skills and opens access to specialized university studies at universities of applied sciences (UAS), or in some cases, cantonal universities. In the Canton of Lucerne, institutions such as the Berufsfachschule Luzern offer integrated or post-AFC professional maturity programs.

Other apprentices continue with post-apprenticeship specializations (continuing education), international certifications in their field, or directly enter the job market with a qualified apprentice salary. Opportunities for further education depend on the individual's profile, professional interests, and access requirements for subsequent training.

Step-by-Step Procedure: From Search to Completion

The search and application process begins about 18 months before the actual start. Consult the cantonal internship search portal, visit training fairs, and contact companies of interest directly. Prepare a CV and cover letter in line with industry expectations. The company evaluates the candidate and, if suitable, proposes a start date and details of the internship contract.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the maximum working time for an apprentice in Switzerland?
The maximum hours are 45 hours per week in the industrial, office and commercial sectors, and 50 hours in other sectors. Weekly breaks and rest periods are also guaranteed according to the Federal Labor Law. The Canton of Lucerne applies these federal rules; consult the cantonal vocational training office for any more restrictive regulations at sectoral level.
How many weeks of holiday does an apprentice have?
Minimum 4 weeks per year for apprentices aged 20 and over, and 5 weeks for those under 20. Some collective sectors in the Canton of Lucerne and trade union agreements could provide additional weeks for deserving young people or additional benefits according to the internship contract.
How are social contributions calculated on the apprentice's salary?
The AVS/AHV and AI contributions are 5.3% borne by the apprentice (the employer pays an additional 5.3%), unemployment insurance (AD/AC) is 1.1% up to the annual ceiling, and accident insurance (LAINF) is borne by the employer (0.7–1.5% according to sector). The total has an impact of about 12–15% on gross salary. Consult the cantonal office for updated amounts and details applicable to 2026.
What happens if the apprenticeship is terminated before the end?
During the first year, the notice of cancellation is 1 month for both parties. From the second year onwards, the deadline increases to 2 months. The trainer must document the reason for the termination. In the event of a serious breach, immediate termination is possible. Consult the internship contract and the cantonal labour law of Lucerne for specifications.
Can I attend professional maturity during the apprenticeship?
Yes. In the Canton of Lucerne it is possible to follow the professional maturity path in parallel to the AFC (part-time integrated model) or complete it later (postgraduate). Professional maturity opens access to specialized university studies at professional university schools and advanced continuing education courses.
