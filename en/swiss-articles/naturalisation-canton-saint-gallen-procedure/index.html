Naturalization in St. Gallen: requirements and procedure (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 12 September 2026

Find out about the requirements for years, integration tests, language and fees for ordinary naturalisation in the canton of St. Gallen.

Context

In brief

  • Ordinary naturalization in St. Gallen requires integration and language tests
  • There is a naturalization fee to be paid
  • The procedure can take several months, depending on the municipality

Key Facts

  • What: years requirements, integration test, language, fees
  • Where: Canton of St. Gallen, municipality of residence
  • Who: applicant with C permit
  • Amount: naturalization fee (not yet specified)

The Canton of St. Gallen has published guidelines for ordinary naturalization. The procedure is divided into several stages: the applicant must prove that he or she has lived for a period determined by both the canton and the municipality of residence, pass an integration test and a language test, and pay the required fees. The necessary residence permit is the C permit, which is usually obtained after ten years of residence (with a double count for the years between the 8th and 18th).

The cantonal and municipal authorities work together to check the residence time requirements: the canton sets a minimum number of years, while the municipality can request an additional period. Only after meeting these criteria can the applicant access the integration test, which assesses knowledge of Swiss culture, institutions and norms. At the same time, it is mandatory to pass the language test, which varies depending on the level required by the municipality.

Operational details

Practical analysis

The naturalization process in St. Gallen has concrete implications for those who live and work in Switzerland. First of all, the requirement of years of residence imposes long-term planning: those who have just obtained a C permit must take into account that the counting of years begins from the moment the permit is issued and that, for individuals between 8 and 18 years of age, the years count double. This can reduce the time it takes to reach the cantonal threshold, but at the same time requires a careful verification of the periods recognized by the municipality.

The integration test, although not specified in detail, is a common element in all cantons. Its assessment includes knowledge of Swiss democratic institutions, the values of neutrality and the system of direct democracy. Preparation may require specific courses offered by cantonal bodies or private schools. At the same time, the language test is essential: the canton of St. Gallen requires mastery of German, with a level that varies depending on the municipality. Those coming from a French-speaking or Italian-speaking area will have to invest time in language courses to meet this requirement.

From an economic point of view, the naturalization fee represents an additional cost. Although the amount is not indicated, it is an expense to be included in the family budget, especially for those who already have commitments related to direct federal taxes, VAT and AHV/AHV contributions. In addition, the duration of the procedure – several months – means that applicants will have to maintain full KVG insurance coverage throughout the process, since citizenship does not alter insurance status.

Key points

Step-by-step action

1. Check your residence permit: make sure you have a C permit, a fundamental requirement for ordinary naturalization. 2. Calculate the years of residence: count the years of presence in Switzerland since the C permit was issued; remember that the years between 8 and 18 count double. If the total does not yet reach the cantonal minimum, continue to reside regularly. 3. Gather documentation: integration certificates, language certificate (German), extracts from the population register, proof of payment of fees, and any employment certificates. All documents must be translated into German if necessary and notarized. 4. Submit the application to the municipality: send the complete dossier to the competent municipal office. The municipality will carry out an initial check and may request additions. 5. Take the tests: Take the integration test and the language test organized by the canton or municipality. Prepare yourself with specific courses or teaching materials provided by the authorities. 6. Pay the naturalisation fee: pay the amount required by the canton; keep the receipt for the next step. 7. Wait for the cantonal decision: the canton examines the file, verifies the results of the tests and the regularity of the payments. The response time varies, but it is normal to wait several months. 8. Final registration: Once citizenship has been granted, register your new status with the tax office and update your KVG insurance.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the residence time requirements for naturalization in St. Gallen?
The canton requires a period of residence established by the canton itself and by the municipality of domicile. The applicant must hold the C permit and have lived in Switzerland for the expected number of years, with double counting for the years between the 8th and 18th.
What kind of tests do I have to pass to get citizenship?
Two tests are mandatory: an integration test, which verifies knowledge of Swiss institutions and values, and a German language test, with the level required by the municipality of residence.
How long can the entire naturalization procedure take?
The duration varies depending on the municipality and canton, but it is common for the procedure to take several months from the submission of the application until the final decision.
