How to open a business in Svitto: cost guide (cross-border guide)

Registration in the trade register, legal forms, minimum capital and insurance obligations: everything you need to start a business in the canton of Schwyz.

  • What: registration with the Schwyz Cantonal business register
  • Where: Schwyz Canton (central Switzerland)

  • When: before starting the business

Starting a business in the Canton of Schwyz involves a series of well-defined administrative and financial steps. The choice of legal form — sole proprietorship, general partnership, joint-stock company (AG) or limited liability company (GmbH) — represents the first crucial step, as it determines both the entrepreneur's civil liability and tax and pension obligations. Registration with the cantonal business register is mandatory before starting the business and constitutes the legal foundation of the enterprise.

Legal forms and liability

The choice of legal form is not merely bureaucratic: it directly impacts the entrepreneur's asset protection and management costs. A sole proprietorship, the simplest and most common form, makes the owner personally and unlimitedly liable for business debts. By contrast, a GmbH or AG separate personal assets from business assets, limiting liability to the capital contributed. This distinction is essential for assessing the enterprise's risk profile during the planning phase.

Operational details

Registration in the commercial register: procedure and documents

Registration in the commercial register of Canton Schwyz is the fundamental administrative act. Depending on the legal form chosen, the canton requires specific documentation: articles of association for companies, declaration of business for sole proprietorships, and identification of the entrepreneur. Registration costs and processing times vary based on the complexity of the structure; it is advisable to consult the competent cantonal office for updated details on forms, fees and specific timeframes.

Minimum capital and initial deposits

Minimum capital is not standardized at the national level for all legal forms. A sole proprietorship does not require formal minimum capital, offering maximum flexibility for small entrepreneurs. A joint-stock company (SA) generally requires at least CHF 100,000 (half of which must be paid at the time of incorporation), while a limited liability company (SAGL) requires at least CHF 20,000. However, cantonal rules may vary; Schwyz may have specific provisions that must be verified with the competent cantonal authority before proceeding with incorporation.

Key points

Practical Procedure: From Idea to Registration

1. Define the legal form based on the risk profile, complexity, and business growth plans. 2. Gather the documents required by the canton: articles of incorporation (for companies), personal identification, property documents or premises, and any sector-specific authorizations. 3. Contact the Schwyz canton's commercial register office to verify canton-specific requirements, updated forms, and registration fees. 4. Submit a complete registration application with documentation and pay the required registration fees. 5. Wait for registration confirmation (timelines vary; typically from a few days to several weeks). 6. Register with the Federal VAT Register at the Federal Customs Administration (if your turnover requires it). 7. Register with the cantonal tax office and communicate your business start date for income tax purposes.

Ongoing Obligations and Compliance

After registration, the entrepreneur must comply with a series of obligations: maintain records and accounting (in accordance with the Swiss Code of Obligations), annual cantonal tax returns, regular payment of AVS/AI/IPG and LPP contributions (for yourself or employees, depending on the structure), maintenance of professional insurance, and, if employing staff, registration with SUVA and compliance with federal labor regulations (maximum working hours of 45 or 50 hours per week depending on the sector, minimum vacation of 4 weeks, probationary period and termination with notice periods of 1-3 months according to seniority).

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the cost of registering in the Swiss trade register?
The costs vary according to the legal form chosen and are set by the Canton of Schwyz. There is no single national amount. It is necessary to consult directly with the commercial register office of the Canton of Schwyz to obtain the updated rates and any additional administrative costs applicable in your specific case.
Do you need minimum capital to open a business in Svitto?
The minimum capital depends on the legal form. A sole proprietorship does not require minimum formal capital. A public limited company (SA) requires at least CHF 100,000 (half paid at the time of incorporation). A limited warranty company (SAGL) requires at least CHF 20,000. Please check the specific provisions of the Canton of Schwyz, as they may have additional regulations.
What insurance is mandatory when I open a business?
Professional liability insurance is mandatory. If you employ employees, you must enroll in SUVA and accident insurance (LAA/LAINF). In addition, all residents in Switzerland, including employees, must have health insurance (LAMal). For more details on health coverage, check out our [guida alla sanità](nav:health).
How is the cantonal tax burden calculated for an activity in Switzerland?
Svitto has its own income and capital tax rates. The amount depends on the gross income of the asset, the legal form chosen, and any allowable deductions (operating costs, social security contributions, interest on debts). Check with the cantonal tax office for an accurate estimate based on your specific situation.
Do I need to register for VAT if I open a business in Svitto?
Registration in the Federal VAT Register is mandatory if your annual turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 in the previous twelve months. If the turnover is lower, you can apply for optional VAT registration. Contact the Federal Customs Administration to see if your business falls within the threshold.
