Rösti promises compensation for waste storage (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 September 2026

Rösti presents the project for the Lägern Nord depot between Zurich and Aargau in Fisibach. Promised compensations, decision towards 2030, storage from 2050.

The Fisibach event

Albert Rösti, federal councilor and head of DETEC, appeared in Fisibach (Aargau) to illustrate the project for a deep repository for the safe storage of Swiss radioactive waste in front of over 400 people. The structure should be built in the Lägern Nord area, between the cantons of Zurich and Aargau.

During the meeting, Rösti assured the population of a "transparent, progressive and jointly conducted" approach and announced compensation for the region. He stressed that the safe disposal of radioactive waste is a national responsibility that cannot be delegated to future generations, with safety as the top priority. He also thanked the local population for taking on "an important project for the whole of Switzerland".

The decision-making process towards 2050

The institutional path is complex and extends over several decades. The Federal Council should decide on the final choice of the site around 2030, considering the various objections received during the consultative phase. Subsequently, it will be up to Parliament to give its approval to the project.

Operational details

The Lägern Nord site: geology and precise location

The depot is planned to the north-west of Bülach, in the canton of Zurich, in a strategic position between the two cantons involved. The structure will descend hundreds of meters deep within a geological layer of opaline clay, chosen for its natural insulation properties and long-term stability.

The main access to the complex will be located in Stadel, canton of Zurich. However, the waste treatment facilities will not be built directly at the deep repository, but at the already existing intermediate repository in Würenlingen, Aargau. This geographical separation between the access location, the processing space and the deep storage reflects an optimization of logistics and safety processes dictated by national technical needs.

Nagra and the federal administrative process

Responsibility for the entire project falls on the Cooperative Society for the Storage of Radioactive Waste (Nagra). In November 2024, Nagra submitted the official application for the construction of the depot to the Confederation, marking the formal start of the technical, administrative and political evaluation by the federal authorities.

Nagra's application represents the fruit of years of geological research and technical evaluations, culminating in the proposal for a site that combines safety requirements, geological stability and logistical feasibility. Approval by the Confederation (expected around 2030) will not be a formality: the Federal Council will have to assess the technical suitability of the site, the adequacy of the safety plans and the response to the objections raised by local communities and cantonal bodies.

Key points

The promised compensations: what will be defined

Rösti has announced compensation for the region affected by the project, but details regarding the amount, how it will be distributed and the specific beneficiaries have not yet been made public. This is a crucial issue for local communities: the presence of a national infrastructure of this kind brings national benefits but also territorial impacts such as land occupation, prolonged construction activities and long-term monitoring.

The compensations represent a recognition of this disparity: the Confederation recognizes that the cantons and municipalities concerned (Aargau and Zurich) support a project of national interest that does not generate immediate local economic benefits. Details on the composition, size and distribution of compensation will need to be negotiated and defined in the years leading up to the federal decision around 2030.

One of Rösti's key commitments was to emphasize the role of local people in decision-making. It guaranteed a "transparent, progressive and conducted together with the population" approach. This means that affected communities will not simply be informed ex post, but will have the opportunity to express concerns, criticisms and proposals during the consultative phases.

In Switzerland, federal decisions of this magnitude follow a principle of direct democracy involving both Parliament and citizens. The popular vote towards 2030 will be the crucial moment in which all Swiss will be able to express their opinion, not just residents of the affected cantons. This opens up broader debates on national responsibility for the nuclear waste cycle and on the fair sharing of infrastructure burdens.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the site chosen for the radioactive waste repository?
The site chosen is Lägern Nord, located northwest of Bülach, between the cantons of Zurich and Aargau. The deposit will be built hundreds of meters deep in a layer of opaline clay. The main access is planned in Stadel, in the canton of Zurich, while the treatment plants will be located at the intermediate repository in Würenlingen, Aargau.
When will the final decision on the deposit take place?
The Federal Council is expected to decide on the final choice of the site around 2030, taking into account the objections received. After that, Parliament will have to give its go-ahead. A popular vote is expected around 2030, and the first storage of radioactive waste is expected to start in 2050.
What did Rösti announce about compensation?
Albert Rösti, Head of DETEC, announced compensation for the region affected by the project. However, the amount and distribution methods have not yet been disclosed. Rösti has ensured a transparent and progressive process conducted together with the local population.
Which company is responsible for the repository project?
The Cooperative Society for the Storage of Radioactive Waste (Nagra) is the responsible body. In November 2024, Nagra submitted the official application for the construction of the deep repository to the Confederation, formally initiating the federal decision-making process.
How will the local population be involved in the project?
Rösti has ensured a process that is "transparent, progressive and conducted together with the population". Local communities will be able to express concerns and proposals during the federal consultation phases. The popular vote towards 2030 will allow all Swiss people to express their opinion on the project.
