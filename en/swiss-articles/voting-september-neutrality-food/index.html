Gfs survey: neutrality close to no, power 50-50 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 21 August 2026

The first gfs.bern poll on the votes of 27 September: neutrality towards rejection (54%), hovering food (49% vs 47%). Regional differences.

Context

In a nutshell

  • gfs.bern survey: 3-16 August 2026, 17,212 people
  • Neutrality: 54% against, 42% in favour (73% decision-making)
  • Power supply: 49% against, 47% in favour, 4% undecided
  • Vote on September 27, 2026

Key facts

  • What: Survey on two Swiss federal initiatives (neutrality, food)
  • When: 3-16 August 2026, vote 27 September 2026
  • Where: Switzerland (national champion)
  • Who: Institute gfs.bern for SRH, 17,212 people
  • Amount: Neutrality 54% no / 42% yes; Power 49% no / 47% yes

The first SSR gfs.bern survey of federal votes on September 27 paints a clear picture for the neutrality initiative and uncertain for the food initiative. Conducted from 3 to 16 August on 17,212 people, the survey shows the rejection of the initiative promoted by the UDC with 54% against and 42% in favour, while for the food initiative a head-to-head emerges with 49% against, 47% in favour and 4% undecided. A margin of error of 2.8 percentage points underscores the uncertainty about power.

Neutrality Initiative

On neutrality, 73% of respondents already have clear ideas — a sign of advanced opinion formation. Within the parties, the gaps are marked: among the supporters of the UDC (promoter of the initiative), 86% support yes. In the other parties, opposites clearly prevail, indicating a deep fracture in the Swiss political coalition.

Operational details

The margin of error of 2.8 percentage points on the food initiative highlights how close the vote is. With 49% against and 47% in favor, a shift of just 2-3 points would change the outcome. The fact that 40% of voters have not yet formed a clear opinion leaves room for campaigns, media, and communication in the months leading up to September 27. The initiative on neutrality, on the other hand, shows a polarized electorate: 73% have clear ideas, which reduces the space for significant changes in opinion. However, Switzerland's Italian-speaking region with its 55% in favor stands out from the national rejection (54%), reflecting different visions of Swiss sovereignty and the role of the Confederation in diplomacy and international relations. For the food initiative, the lines of political fracture are evident: Green Party voters (84%) and those of the Social Democratic Party (64%) converge, while members of FDP, UDC, and the Centre remain skeptical. The 18-39 age group and women, with a support of 54% and 55%, respectively, show that the theme of food autonomy and sustainability resonates with these demographic segments. The French-speaking region (57%) appears more sensitive to the issue than the national average (47%), while the Italian-speaking region (53%) remains moderately favorable but with lower consensus than the French-speaking region. ### How votes change in the last months The August surveys are a snapshot. In the 30 days leading up to September 27, the picture can evolve significantly. Media campaigns, public debates, and party communications will influence undecided voters on food — the 40% who have not yet formed a stable opinion — and may consolidate or weaken the 'no' on neutrality. The UDC, as promoter of the initiative on neutrality, will have an incentive to mobilize its members (86% in favor internally) and reach moderate undecided voters; on the other hand, supporters of a more flexible Switzerland in international politics will work to expand the current 54% of opponents. On food, the Greens and the Social Democrats will have to convince the Centre's moderate electorate — currently skeptical — that a 70% self-sufficiency target is realistic and manageable, not an isolated ideological ideal. A theme that touches on the cost of living and the sustainability of Swiss supply chains.

Key points

For those who wish to delve deeper into the two texts before the vote, official sources such as the portal Parlament.ch and the websites of the cantons offer the complete texts of the initiatives, explanations of the Federal Chancellery and evaluation material. Swiss media outlets — particularly Radio Swiss Italian, Neue Zürcher Zeitung, Tages-Anzeiger, Le Temps and other quality organs — will publish in-depth analyses, debates and expert positions in the coming months. On September 27, every resident in Switzerland with the right to vote will have the opportunity to express their opinion on both initiatives. For border residents and expatriates, consular offices and foreign voting organizations will facilitate participation. It is essential to check, before the date, that one's registration on an electoral roll is up-to-date and functioning. The gfs.bern source provides a snapshot of opinion by mid-August, but the following weeks will see evolution. Consult high-quality sources — Parlament.ch for official texts, Swiss newspapers of substance (NZZ, Tages-Anzeiger, Le Monde for French-speaking Switzerland, Corriere del Ticino for the Ticinese context) — to form a well-founded opinion. Many media will publish concise summaries of the economic, environmental and fiscal impacts of each initiative, essential to understand the real weight of a choice. In the case of food, reaching 70% self-sufficiency could influence consumer prices and the structure of living costs in the medium to long term. ### Why these votes matter Swiss federal initiatives are a direct expression of popular sovereignty. With several initiatives every year put to a popular vote, Swiss citizens have the power to set the country's legislative agenda. The result of September 27 will shape Swiss politics on two crucial fronts: neutrality, central to national identity and the Confederation's international stance, and food self-sufficiency, which affects sustainability, prices and independence from imports. Monitoring polls in the coming weeks, discussing with other citizens and consulting reliable sources on the Swiss cost of living to understand the economic impact of choices helps to exercise the right to vote in an informed and conscious manner. On September 27, it will be the test of the Swiss people's will on issues that will shape the Confederation for years to come.

Frequently Asked Questions
When do you vote on the two initiatives?
The vote is set for 27 September 2026. All Swiss citizens with the right to vote will be able to participate. The cantons shall publish modalities and deadlines for early voting and correspondence within their jurisdictions.
What exactly is the food initiative asking for?
The initiative calls for bringing Switzerland's food self-sufficiency rate to 70% within ten years. This implies a significant increase in domestic production and a reduction in import dependence, with potential effects on consumer prices.
What does the neutrality initiative ask for?
The neutrality initiative, promoted by the UDC, calls for the strengthening and preservation of Swiss neutrality in the context of international relations. According to the gfs.bern survey, 54% of respondents are against and 42% in favour, with 73% already making decisions.
Which parties support each initiative?
On food: Greens (84%), PS (64%), Liberal Greens (55%). On neutrality: UDC registers 86% internal consensus. Centro, UDC and PLR are skeptical about food. Italian Switzerland favours neutrality (55%), while French Switzerland favours food (57%).
Is the gfs.bern survey final or can it change?
The survey is a snapshot of August. The margin of error on power is 2.8 percentage points, and 40% of voters still do not have a firm opinion. Campaigns, debates and communication in the months before 27 September will influence the final results.
