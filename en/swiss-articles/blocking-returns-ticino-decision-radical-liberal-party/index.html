Context

In brief - The Swiss Radical Party welcomes the freeze on cross-border rebates. - The decision is considered serious and carefully considered. - The Swiss Council of State announced the freeze on rebates on 30 June. ## Key facts - What: Blocking cross-border rebates. - When: June 30, 2024. - Where: Ticino. - Who: Swiss Radical Liberal Party. - Amount: Not specified. The Swiss Radical Liberal Party has welcomed the decision of the Swiss Council of State to freeze cross-border rebates for Lombardy. The choice was announced on June 30 and the party considered the decision "serious and carefully considered, based on solid legal foundations and consistent with what we have been saying for some time". Since the announcement of the so-called "health tax", State Councillor Christian Vitta has also pointed out that the new levy applied to old cross-border commuters was incompatible with the tax agreements between Switzerland and Italy. The Plr had drafted an inter-party motion, presented by President Alessandro Speziali and supported by the Centre, the League and the UDC, with which it had asked the Government "to prepare for the total or partial freeze of rebates, as a measure to protect Ticino's interests". ### The decision of the Plr The Plr continued to maintain that the government acts on the basis of an independent expert report that confirms the Italian measure as a violation of international agreements. Defending Ticino also means demanding that the commitments signed are respected. For this reason, the party has increased the dose, arguing that the Italian tax would even be advantageous for Ticino means shifting the debate away from the real problem. The Ticino labor market does not depend on potential new taxes on workers. The core of the matter is that Ticino cannot continue to be neglected on the subject of rebates, which for decades has seen us pay hundreds of millions of francs. Ticino cannot afford to remain inert on this without exerting legitimate pressure on Bern. ### The criticism of the UDC The decision of the Plr has aroused criticism from the UDC, which has attacked in a decidedly contradictory way a decision that goes exactly in the direction previously hoped.
