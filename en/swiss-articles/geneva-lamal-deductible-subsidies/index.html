The focus on Geneva

For Swiss residents, the KVG is compulsory health insurance. The 2026 guide to health insurance premiums takes this rule as a starting point and focuses on the canton of Geneva, placing it in the national comparison between cantons and regions.

The decisive figure for reading the premium is not an indistinct average: the premiums are indicated by canton and region. When looking for the 2026 value for Geneva, it is therefore necessary to use the correct geographical reference and the updated official publication. A national figure does not automatically replace the cantonal or regional figure.

Obligation and choice of deductible

For those arriving in Switzerland, the insurance must be taken out within three months of arrival. The deadline accompanies the insurance obligation and must be distinguished from the moment the options are compared: first the correct perimeter is checked, then the most consistent combination with canton, region and deductible is evaluated.

For adults, the available deductibles are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500. These are the six thresholds to be compared in a 2026 guide, together with the applicable official premium. The deductible should also be read in the personal budget, keeping any cantonal premium reduction separate.

The KVG premium is a private premium per capita. It is not a tax and is not a wage contribution. The distinction avoids confusing it with direct federal tax or VAT, which fall within the scope of the FTA/ESTV, or with salary-related AHV/AHV and BVG/BVG contributions. Operational details

Cantonal differences and staff budget

The comparison between Geneva and the other cantons has a practical function: it shows that the place of residence enters the reading of the premium. There is no single national value to be applied to everyone; the correct reference starts from the canton and region indicated for the insured. Financial support also follows a cantonal logic, because the premium reduction is a cantonal subsidy.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is the KVG mandatory for people living in Switzerland?
Yes. LAMal is the mandatory health insurance for those residing in Switzerland. Anyone arriving must take it out within three months of arrival. The premium is private and per capita: it is neither a tax nor a payroll contribution. For 2026, the comparison must use the official figure for the canton and region, so in Geneva it is necessary to verify the value relating to that area.
What KVG deductibles are available for adults?
The deductibles indicated for adults are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500. The choice must be compared with the official 2026 premium of the canton and region and with the personal budget. It is not correct to automatically use the value of another canton, because the KVG premiums are indicated according to the cantonal or regional reference.
Is the KVG bonus a tax or a deduction from salary?
No. The KVG premium is a private premium per capita. It is to be distinguished from taxes and salary contributions. It is therefore not the same as direct federal tax or VAT, which fall within the scope of the FTA/ESTV, nor with AHV/AHV and BVP/BVG contributions. It must be recorded as a separate item in the budget, while any reduction is a cantonal subsidy.
When does the KVG have to be concluded after arrival?
For those arriving in Switzerland, the policy must be taken out within three months of arrival. The deadline concerns compulsory health insurance for residents. Those considering changing health insurers should instead check the procedure and any deadlines in the current official documentation for their coverage, without applying a single date that does not relate to their own case.
