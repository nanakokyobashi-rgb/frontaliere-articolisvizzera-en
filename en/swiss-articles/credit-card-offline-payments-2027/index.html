Offline credit card payments since 2027 in Switzerland (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 24 August 2026

From 2027, Swiss stores will be able to process credit and debit card payments even without the internet. DEFR, the SNB and the Federal Council announce the solution for financial resilience.

Context

In a nutshell

  • From 2027 offline payments with credit cards in Swiss stores
  • DEFR announces solution for internet blackouts and network outages
  • Terminal records offline transaction, transmits when the connection is restored
  • No card swapping: simply reconfigure readers in stores

Key facts

  • What: Offline payments with physical cards and PINs at stores
  • When: Gradual implementation; wide diffusion by the end of 2027
  • Where: Switzerland (first Central European country with a shared solution)
  • Who: DEFR, Swiss National Bank (SNB), Federal Council, business representatives
  • Mode: Local recording of the transaction, synchronization when the network is restored
  • Requirements: Physical cards with PINS; terminals powered by current or external battery in blackout
  • Impact: No paper replacement necessary, just reconfigure the readers

The Federal Department of the Economy (DEFR) announced today in Bern a statement of intent aimed at ensuring payments in stores even during internet outages or network blackouts. The solution was presented by business representatives at an event attended by the president of the Confederation Guy Parmelin, the president of the Swiss National Bank (SNB) Martin Schlegel and the delegate to the Economic Supply of the Country Roland Pfister.

Operational details

Switzerland thus joins the list of European countries that have decided to invest in the resilience of the national digital payment system. Sweden has already implemented offline payments for some time, while other Northern European states such as Finland, Norway and Estonia are introducing similar measures. However, the DEFR stresses that Switzerland is the first Central European country to adopt a widely shared solution to strengthen the resilience of electronic payments.

The impact on consumers' daily lives

For those who live and work in Switzerland, the change will bring significant practical benefits. Until now, any internet outage — be it due to technical failures, bad weather or emergencies — could completely block the possibility of buying goods with paper, forcing customers to resort to cash. With offline payments, the store will continue to operate normally, recording the transaction locally and processing it later.

This also has important repercussions on food security and essential services. During a blackout, basic necessities can still be purchased with a credit or debit card, keeping the costo della vita stable without forcing dependence on cash. The consumer will no longer have to carry significant amounts of money in times of infrastructure crisis.

Key points

How to prepare for offline payment

The transition will be almost invisible to Swiss consumers. You will not have to do anything to join the system: your cards currently in circulation will continue to function normally, without the need for a replacement request. What will change is the configuration of the card readers at the stores.

From the end of 2027, when the spread is expected to be wide, you can expect most stores in Switzerland to support offline payments. Some factors not yet specified — such as regional priority or by product category — will continue to be defined by the federal authorities during implementation.

What the merchant needs to do

The primary responsibility rests with the point of sale operators and the operators providing the payment terminals. These will have to reconfigure the card readers to activate in offline mode, ensure that the device has backup power (external battery or generator) in the event of a total blackout, and fine-tune the processes of synchronization and deferred transmission of transactions.

For customers, the experience remains the same: you'll enter your card, type in your pin, and the payment will be processed as usual. Frequently Asked Questions
When can I pay offline with my credit or debit card?
The solution will be phased in by the end of 2027. The Federal Department of the Economy (DEFR) has announced that the first implementations will start again in the course of the next year, but a wide diffusion in Swiss stores is only foreseen by the end of 2027. Not all stores will have the feature at the same time; implementation will proceed in stages and by regions.
Will I have to replace my credit or debit card?
No, the cards currently in circulation will continue to operate unchanged. What will change is the configuration of readers in stores: payment devices will have to be reconfigured by operators and financial service providers to support offline mode. You won't have to do anything of your own; the transition will be automatic on the merchant side.
How exactly does offline payment work in stores?
In the event of an internet blackout, the payment terminal records the transaction locally and keeps it in memory. As soon as the connection is restored, the device automatically transmits the transaction to the bank for processing and debiting. If the blackout is total (including current), the terminal must have an external battery or generator to continue operating.
What is the reason for this decision?
Switzerland intends to strengthen the resilience of the national digital payment system. The ability to pay even in the event of internet outages is critical to the continuity of essential services, economic life and food security in the country. Sweden, Finland, Norway and Estonia have already taken or are taking similar measures.
Is Switzerland the first country in the world to introduce offline payments?
No, Sweden has already implemented this solution. However, the DEFR stresses that Switzerland is the first Central European country to adopt a broadly shared and nationally coordinated solution, involving the Federal Council, the Swiss National Bank (SNB) and business associations in a formal and structured way.
