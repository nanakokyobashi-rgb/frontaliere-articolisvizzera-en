Permit C Internal Appenzell: requirements and application (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 September 2026

Permanent residence in Switzerland: 10 years residence (5 EU/EFTA), integration, German language. SEM procedure, cantonal criteria and naturalisation.

Context

In brief

  • Permit C = federal permanent residence, automatically renewable in Switzerland
  • Requirements: 10 years continuous residence (5 if EU/EFTA citizen) + economic stability
  • Each canton adds its own integration and language knowledge criteria
  • Ordinary naturalization requires permit C + another 10 years + cantonal/communal criteria

Key facts

  • What: Permanent residence permit (category C, federal SEM)
  • Required residence: 10 continuous years (or 5 for EU/EFTA)
  • Competence: Federal State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) + cantonal migration office
  • Integration: Evaluated by the canton (Appenzell Innerrhoden has its own criteria)
  • Language: Generally Swiss German knowledge at level A2/B1 required
  • Renewal: Automatic every 10 years (unlike permit B)
  • Documents: Passport, certificate of good conduct, employment contract, proof of residence

The permanent residence permit, category C, represents the permanent residence status in Switzerland for foreigners. Unlike the B permit (residence), which requires periodic renewals, the C permit renews automatically and is the first legal step towards ordinary Swiss naturalization.

To obtain it at the federal level, the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) requires a continuous period of residence of 10 years in Switzerland. Citizens of the European Union and the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) benefit from a reduced term: only 5 years. However, the mere passage of time is not sufficient. Each Swiss canton — including Appenzell Innerrhoden — has developed additional integration and language knowledge criteria that the applicant must meet. These criteria vary significantly between the 26 cantons and are managed by the respective cantonal migration offices.

Operational details

Integration: how the cantons evaluate it

Integration is not a vague concept. When the migration office of Appenzello Interno receives an application for a C permit, it concretely examines some elements. First: economic stability. Does the person have a permanent employment contract or a consolidated self-employed activity? Do you have enough income to support yourself without social assistance? Second element: family and social ties. Do you have a family permanently resident in the canton? Do you participate in associations, local initiatives, religious communities, sports clubs? Third: communication skills and compliance with the law. During the interview with the migration office, does the person demonstrate understanding of administrative instructions in Swiss German? Do you have a criminal record, labor law violations, building code violations, or other significant infractions?

There is no numerical grid: the office evaluates the set of factors. In Inner Appenzell, assessment is less formalised than in larger cantons (such as Zurich or Bern), where standardised language tests have sometimes been developed. Appenzello Interno applies semi-structured interviews and a holistic assessment. This means that two people with identical years of residence and the same income could receive different answers if one clearly demonstrates local integration and the other does not.

Key points

How to submit the C permit application: step-by-step procedure

Step 1: Preparation and collection of documents

Before contacting the Appenzell Innerrhoden migration office, ensure you have completed 10 years (or 5 if EU/EFTA) of continuous residence. Gather the required documents: a valid and readable passport or national ID, a certificate of good conduct and police record (available from your current canton of residence), employment contracts and the last three pay slips to prove economic stability, a copy of your lease agreement and proof of rent payments, and certificates of participation in language or integration courses if available.

Step 2: Submitting the application to the cantonal office

The application is submitted to the migration office (Migrationsbehörde) of the canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden. In Switzerland, there is no single online procedure; each canton has its own system. You can fill out a paper form or a digital form according to the cantonal office's instructions. The form asks for personal data, residence history, work information, family situation, and any legal records.

Step 3: Evaluation by the cantonal office

The office will review your documents and conduct an interview (usually in person). During the interview, you will demonstrate your knowledge of Swiss German through direct conversation (no official test is required, but you must be able to understand instructions and answer basic questions). The office will also assess your integration and economic stability according to local criteria. The average time for this phase is 2-3 months, although times may vary.

Frequently Asked Questions
How long do I have to stay in Switzerland before applying for the C permit?
The State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) requires 10 years of continuous residence in Switzerland. If you are a citizen of the European Union or the European Free Trade Association (EFTA), the term is reduced to 5 years. The count starts from the first L or B permit registered at the municipal office. Keep in mind that just spending time is not enough: you must also meet the integration and linguistic criteria of the canton where you reside.
Does Appenzello Interno require official language certificates for the C permit?
Appenzello Interno does not normally impose official certificates such as TELC, goethe-Zertifikat or similar. The migration office assesses your knowledge of Swiss German through a direct interview during the application process. A level A2/B1 (basic comprehension and conversation) must be demonstrated. If you want to prepare, you can attend local language courses; the certificates obtained are a plus that facilitates the assessment, but they are not mandatory.
Can I apply for a C permit before I have completed exactly 10 years?
No. The SEM requires that the period of residence be completed before the formal issuance of the permit C. Some cantons allow the application to be submitted in month 11 of the tenth year, but the actual issuance of the permit occurs after the completion of the 10 years. Check with the migration office of Appenzello Interno the exact calendar for your situation.
Are C-Permit and Swiss Naturalization the same?
No. They're two different statuses. The C permit is a federal permanent residence permit, which is automatically renewed every 10 years. Ordinary naturalization is the acquisition of Swiss citizenship, which requires the possession of the C permit plus another 10 years of additional residence, in addition to the cantonal and municipal naturalization criteria. Only after naturalization will you get your Swiss passport and the right to vote.
I have a B permit in Switzerland: can I 'convert' it directly into a C permit?
No, it's not a direct conversion. Permit B remains valid until renewed. If you have completed 10 years of total residence (counted from the first L or B permit), you can apply for permit C. You will inform the cantonal office that you wish to move from permit B to permit C. The evaluation procedure (integration, language, stability) remains the same as for those who apply from scratch. Permit B expires when permit C is issued.
