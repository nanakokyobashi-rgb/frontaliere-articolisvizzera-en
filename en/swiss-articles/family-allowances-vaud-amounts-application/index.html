Vaud Family Allowances: Amounts and How to Apply (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 September 2026

Practical guide to family allowances in the canton of Vaud: how to apply, beneficiaries' rights, required documents and competent compensation fund.

Context

In brief

  • Family allowances: monthly benefit for dependent children, amounts vary by canton
  • Canton Vaud: specific rights and amounts defined at the cantonal level, can be requested from compensation funds
  • Procedure: application to be submitted with supporting documents to the competent fund for the economic sector

Key facts

  • What: Family allowances (Swiss social benefit for families with dependent children)
  • Where: Each Swiss canton (including Vaud) organizes the system autonomously
  • Who: Compensation funds for family allowances (cantonal/parity bodies)
  • Beneficiaries: Parents/guardians of dependent children up to the legal age defined by the canton
  • Amounts: Vary by canton Vaud and number of children (consult the competent Vaud office for current 2026 data)

Family allowances are a fundamental institution of Swiss social security structured at the cantonal level. Each canton, including Vaud, manages the disbursement of these monthly benefits aimed at parents and guardians of dependent children autonomously. In Vaud, as in all Swiss cantons, family allowances represent an important supplement to family income for those with dependent charges and are an integral part of the social protection system dedicated to families.

The family allowances system in Switzerland operates through specialized compensation funds, public or parity bodies that collect contributions from employers and pay benefits to eligible beneficiaries. Unlike other social benefits managed at the federal level (such as old-age and survivors' insurance AVS/AHV), family allowances remain primarily a cantonal competence. This means that amounts, eligibility conditions, procedures, and age brackets vary from canton to canton, with Vaud having defined its own specific rules and amounts.

Operational details

In the canton of Vaud, as in the rest of Switzerland, family allowances are managed by compensation funds (caisse de compensation pour allocations familiales in French). These are specialized entities, generally with a public or paritarian structure (with representations of employers and employees), operating at the cantonal, supra-communal, or sectoral level. Their function is to collect contributions from employers (for each employee) and pay monthly allowances to eligible beneficiaries.

Key points

How to apply: the first step

The procedure to request family allowances in Vaud begins with identifying the compensation fund competent for your economic sector. The first step is to contact your employer or the relevant trade union: they can provide the name, address, and contacts of the fund designated for your sector. Alternatively, direct recourse to the Vaud cantonal office responsible for social security (Office cantonal de la famille, or equivalent structure of the competent department) allows you to receive complete information and be directed to the correct fund.

Required documentation

For the submission of the application, the standard documentation usually includes: birth declaration of the children or updated civil status certificate; valid identity document of the applicant; proof of parenthood and cohabitation (e.g., school medical expense receipts, school enrollments, official letters with home addresses); income and employment proofs (recent pay slips, employment letters) — sometimes required by the employer; copy of the Vaud cantonal tax decision of the previous year (in the case of self-employed workers).

Timeline and deadlines

The application can be submitted at any time of the year, but the allowances are generally backdated to the first day of the month in which the child was born or the applicant started working in the Vaud canton. The processing time normally varies from 2 to 8 weeks from the receipt of the complete application with documentation.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the monthly amount of family allowances in Vaud for 2026?
The amounts of family allowances in the canton of Vaud vary according to the number of dependent children and are indexed annually. Since the source does not specify the exact 2026 values, we recommend that you consult directly the compensation fund competent in your economic sector (private, public, independent employees, farmers) or the Office cantonal de la famille Vaud for current and customized data.
Which children are considered 'dependent' for family allowances?
In the Swiss system, family allowances are paid to the biological, adoptive or affiliated children of the applicant (or his/her spouse), who live with the applicant and do not have their own income above a certain threshold. The maximum age varies by canton, but generally checks continue until the end of apprenticeship, university studies, or in any case until the age of 25. Check the specific cantonal Vaud limit with the competent cashier.
How is the competent clearinghouse identified in the Vaud?
The relevant fund depends on the economic sector of the applicant. Employees in the private sector turn to the Vaud parity fund for private employees. Cantonal and municipal civil servants have a dedicated cashier. Self-employed workers and farmers belong to specialised coffers. The employer or union can provide the exact name. Alternatively, contact the Office cantonal de la famille Vaud.
Can family allowances from two different cantons be combined?
No. Family allowances cannot be combined between different cantons. If the applicant has worked in several cantons or has children entrusted to him in different cantons, the 'compensation' rule applies: the allowances are paid by the canton in which the child resides or in which the applicant carries out the main work activity. Complex situations (e.g. spouses working in different cantons) require clarification with the competent fund.
What documents are required for the application for family allowances in the Vaud?
Standard documentation includes birth certificates of children, identity document, proof of filiation and cohabitation (official addresses, school enrolments), and sometimes income documents of the applicant and employment confirmation from the employer. For self-employed persons, the Vaud cantonal tax decision may be required. The complete list must be confirmed by the relevant clearinghouse at the time of the request.
