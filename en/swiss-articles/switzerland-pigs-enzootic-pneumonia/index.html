Switzerland: 10'500 pigs killed due to enzootic pneumonia

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 October 2026

Health emergency in 14 cantons: over 10'500 pigs culled or slaughtered to stop the spread of a contagious lung infection.

Context

TL;DR

  • Over 10'500 pigs culled or slaughtered
  • Measures in 60 farms in 14 cantons
  • Epidemic emerged on September 9
  • The disease is not dangerous to humans

Key facts

  • Disease → enzootic pneumonia (EP)
  • Outbreak → September 9
  • Affected farms → 60 in 14 cantons
  • Total depopulation → 20 breeding farms in six cantons
  • Fattening farms → 40 in 14 cantons
  • Lucerne → 5'200 animals culled or slaughtered
  • Bern → 1'700 animals in three breeding farms
  • Compensation → for farmers who lose pigs by order of the authorities

On September 9, enzootic pneumonia (EP), a contagious lung infection in pigs, emerged within a large inter-cantonal pig supply chain. The announcement was made by the Veterinary Service of the canton of Lucerne, which clarified an important point for the public: the disease poses no danger to humans.

To stop the chains of transmission, the authorities ordered the complete depopulation of 20 breeding farms spread across six cantons. So far, the measure has led to the slaughter or culling of approximately 10'500 animals. At the 40 affected fattening farms, located in 14 cantons, restrictions on animal movements remain in force.

The cantonal distribution shows a strong concentration in certain areas. Lucerne is the worst-hit canton: ten breeding farms and one fattening farm have been depopulated, for a total of 5'200 animals culled or slaughtered. In Bern, three breeding farms were affected, involving 1'700 animals. In Solothurn, 930 pigs were culled at two farms; others were slaughtered and approximately 1'200 young animals were exported. Other affected breeding farms were located in Schwyz, Thurgau and Vaud. The latter canton reported the culling of 950 pigs.

In Bern, the restrictions imposed on four fattening farms were lifted following negative tests, while other farms are still under investigation. The restrictions imposed on breeding farms remain in force for the time being and may be lifted only after the barns have been completely cleaned and disinfected and have remained empty for at least ten days.

The risk of spread is assessed for each farm. Veterinarians also visit neighboring farms to check the health status of the pigs. So far, there is no indication that farms outside the affected supply chain have been infected. The report does not quantify effects on prices, consumption or wages; it is therefore not possible to derive an estimate of costo della vita in Svizzera from the report for these data.

Operational details

Operational impact on the supply chain

Breeding and fattening follow different rules

The report clearly separates two operational pathways. For breeding farms, the measure is complete depopulation and the restriction remains tied to specific health conditions. For fattening farms, by contrast, the measure indicated is restrictions on animal movements, with checks still underway at some farms. It is therefore not a single provision applied in the same way throughout the supply chain.

This comparison helps assess the situation farm by farm. A breeding farm cannot consider the restriction ended simply because checks have been carried out: the source also specifies complete cleaning and disinfection, followed by at least ten days with no animals. For a fattening farm, a negative test result has, in the reported case in Bern, already allowed the restriction to be lifted; other farms remain under investigation. The communication does not establish that the same result automatically leads to the lifting of the restriction in every canton.

The presence of the supply chain in 14 cantons does not, according to the available data, amount to a generalized infection of Swiss farms. The risk is assessed individually, and veterinarians also inspect nearby farms. So far, no indications of contagion outside the affected supply chain have emerged. For those living in Switzerland, the health message is equally limited: the Lucerne Veterinary Service states that PE poses no danger to humans. The source does not, however, provide data on meat prices, availability in stores or consequences for the cost of living.

Compensation should also be read without adding figures. It is provided for farmers who lose their pigs by order of the authorities, but no amount, calculation, timing or procedure is specified. Key points

Operational steps

What the communication indicates

For pig farmers, the first indication is to remain vigilant and consistently apply biosecurity measures. The source does not list individual biosecurity practices, so it is not possible to turn this recommendation into a list of products, checks or documents.

1. Consider the risk of the individual farm. The authorities assess it individually, while veterinarians also visit nearby farms to check the health status of the pigs. 2. Comply with the restrictions on animal movements where they are still in force. The text links these restrictions to the affected finishing farms. 3. Await the outcome of the checks and tests. In the case reported by the canton of Bern, four restrictions at finishing farms were lifted after negative results, while others remain under investigation. 4. For breeding farms subject to restrictions, complete the cleaning and disinfection of the barns and keep them empty for at least ten days before a possible lifting of the restrictions.

From an economic standpoint, farmers who lose their pigs by order of the authorities will receive financial compensation. The communication does not specify the amount, the calculation method, the deadline, the forms or the office to which an application should be submitted. Adding these details would mean going beyond the source. The same applies to any tax or accounting effects: they are not described and must not be inferred.

For those who do not operate a farm, the news offers only two verifiable indications. PE was detected in an inter-cantonal pig-production chain and, according to the Veterinary Service of the canton of Lucerne, poses no danger to humans. In addition, so far there are no indications of farms outside the chain being infected. The source does not prescribe health-related actions for the population or announce general restrictions for consumers.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the pulmonary disease disease that has affected pigs in Switzerland?
The disease is enzootic pneumonia (EP), a contagious lung infection of pigs that emerged on September 9 within a large inter-cantonal pig farming chain. According to the Veterinary Service of the canton of Lucerne, this infection poses no danger to humans.
How many animals were culled and in which areas are they most affected?
So far, approximately 10'500 animals have been slaughtered or culled. The canton of Lucerne is the worst-affected area, with 5'200 animals removed from ten breeding farms and one fattening farm. In Bern, three breeding farms with 1'700 animals were affected, while in Solothurn, 930 pigs were culled at two farms.
What control measures are the breeders to the breeding and fattening farms?
For breeding farms, the authorities ordered the complete clearance of 20 farms in six cantons, with mandatory cleaning, disinfection and at least ten days with the barn empty before the lockdown is lifted. For fattening farms, in 14 cantons, restrictions on the movement of animals were imposed, with lifting possible after negative tests, as occurred for four farms in Bern.
Will the affected breeders receive compensation?
Yes, financial compensation is provided for farmers who lose their pigs by order of the authorities. However, the source does not specify the amount, calculation method or application procedures.
