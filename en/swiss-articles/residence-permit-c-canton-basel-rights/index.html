Permit C Basel City: rights and naturalization requirements (cross-border guide)

Residence permit C in the Canton of Basel City: 10 years residence (5 EU/EFTA), federal integration, cantonal procedure and path to Swiss ordinary naturalization.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The C permit is the federal permanent residence permit for foreigners in Switzerland
  • Minimum requirement: 10 years continuous residence (5 for EU/EFTA citizens)
  • Basel City manages local integration procedure and criteria according to federal law
  • Permit C paves the way towards ordinary Swiss naturalisation

Key facts

  • What: Residence permit C (federal permanent residence permit)
  • When: Release normally after ≥10 years residence (5 for EU/EFTA)
  • Where: Canton Basel City (cantonal competence for procedure and integration)
  • Who: Federal SEM Administration + cantonal authorities Basel City
  • Requirements: Continuous residence, absence of public safety hazards, basic integration

Residence permit C is the most important federal title for foreigners who wish to settle permanently in Switzerland. At the national level, federal law normally provides for issuance after 10 years of continuous residence, or after 5 years for citizens of the European Union and the European Free Trade Association (EFTA). In the Canton of Basel-City, as in all Swiss cantons, this federal competence is administered in synergy with cantonal requirements, which include integration criteria and compliance with local regulations.

Operational details

Cantonal procedure in Basel-City: how it works

In the Canton of Basel-City, the C permit is administered by the competent cantonal migration office (part of the cantonal police structure or department of justice). The application is not automatic: after meeting the minimum residency requirement, the resident foreign national must submit a formal application to the local authorities.

The cantonal procedure, although following federal principles, includes specific verification steps: the cantonal administration checks municipal registries to certify 10 years (or 5 for EU/EFTA) of uninterrupted residency. Subsequently, the SEM and cantonal authorities verify whether the applicant has a clean criminal record and does not pose a security threat. Basel-City, like other cantons, evaluates integration through specific criteria: knowledge of German (the official language of the canton), participation in the labor market, and absence of dependence on social welfare. Following complete verification, the canton issues the C permit, normally valid for 10 years and renewable.

Generally, the procedure takes between 3 and 6 months, depending on the cantonal administrative workload. Federal law provides for cases of early issuance: the C permit may be granted before 10 years (or 5 for EU/EFTA) in special situations such as marriage to a Swiss citizen (required residency reduced to 3-5 years in cantons that allow it), children of Swiss citizens (more favorable criteria), or employees of companies of federal significance (rarely). Basel-City may apply these exceptions, but the details remain in the hands of the local cantonal administration.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure to apply for a C permit in Basel-City

Step 1: Check the prerequisites. Ensure you have reached 10 years of uninterrupted residency in Switzerland (or 5 if you are an EU/EFTA citizen). Check your municipal registry for the exact registration date and keep official documentation.

Step 2: Gather documentation. You will need to submit a valid passport or identity card, criminal record extracts (from your country of origin and Switzerland, if applicable), a recently issued municipal residence certificate, an employment document (employment contract) or tax return, and proof of language proficiency (diplomas, certificates, or a simple declaration, depending on the canton of Basel-City practice).

Step 3: Contact the competent office. The exact address depends on the cantonal administration of Basel-City. Generally, it is the Cantonal Police (Department of Justice and Security) or a specific immigration office. Check the official portal of the canton.

Step 4: Submit the application. It can be submitted in person at the office, by mail, or via the cantonal online portal (if available).

Step 5: Wait for verification. The canton will carry out residency, security, and integration checks. Remain available for any interviews or requests for additional documents. It is normal to receive requests for clarification during this period.

Step 6: Receive the issuance. Once approved, you will receive the C permit in the form of a federal legitimization card or digital document according to cantonal procedures.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between permit B and permit C?
Permit B (residence) is temporary, renewable usually every year or two years, and is linked to a specific reason (work, study, etc.). The C (domicile) permit is permanent and requires no reason, allowing for lasting legal stability. In addition, the C is not subject to annual review and facilitates access to social rights and geographical mobility within Switzerland.
If I am an EU citizen, how long do I have to wait for the C permit?
Citizens of the European Union and EFTA (European Free Trade Association) can apply for the C permit after only 5 years of continuous residence in Switzerland, instead of the 10 years required for third-country nationals. This advantage reflects the bilateral agreements between Switzerland and the EU on freedom of movement.
Can I lose the C permit after I get it?
Yes, the C permit can be revoked if you commit serious crimes, if you return to dependence on social assistance without valid reasons, or if you leave Switzerland for an extended period. However, revocations are rare and require specific violations of the law.
What is the first step to apply for the C permit in Basel City?
Verification on the municipal register that you have reached 10 years of residence (or 5 if EU/EFTA). Then contact the cantonal migration office in Basel-City to receive the application form and the exact list of required documents. Specific details vary slightly from canton to canton.
Does the C permit guarantee me the right of naturalization?
The C permit is a necessary prerequisite for ordinary Swiss naturalization, but does not automatically guarantee recognition. You will also need to meet additional cantonal and communal requirements (language proficiency, civic testing, no criminal record, etc.), and the process typically takes 6-18 months.
