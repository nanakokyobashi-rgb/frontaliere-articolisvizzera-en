Matteo Buzzi, meteorologist and leader of the Greens in the Grand Council, launched an appeal for the safeguard of the Cantonal Energy and Climate Plan (PECC). The policy discussion fits into a context of deep concern about ongoing climate change. According to the above, the summers, which were once considered extreme events with extremely low probability of occurrence, are repeating with a much higher frequency. If in the past an extreme summer occurred over a period of 200-1400 years, today, thirtyyears later, this event can repeat every 10-25 years. In a world that is heading for a warming of +3°C, intense heat conditions risk becoming the norm.

Operational details

The challenges of cantonal climate policy

The debate on the ECCP represents a pivotal moment for Ticino politics. The proposed obligations to reduce pollutant emissions and to incentivise solar photovoltaic energy are presented as measures proportionate to the gravity of the situation. Buzzi highlights how, in a context where hydroelectric power suffers the effects of drought and nuclear power encounters operational difficulties due to high temperatures and water scarcity for reactor cooling, photovoltaics is assuming a fundamental support role for the Swiss electricity supply. Consequently, the State Council's proposals should be supported rather than scaled down by short-term accounting calculations.

The need for a robust adaptation plan is underscored by the fact that environmental threats are already having a real impact on public health, with hospitals facing spikes in heat stroke patients and an increase in premature deaths. The political contrast between those who demand more incisive measures and those who prefer a more cautious approach is reflected in the work of the Grand Council. The invitation launched by the leader of the Greens is to look at future generations with foresight. Responsibility and Future Prospects

The management of the climate emergency requires a step change in the decisions that will be made within the Grand Council of Ticino. For those who live or work in the Canton, following the PECC process means monitoring the decisions that will influence local energy policy and adaptation measures for the years to come. The discussion is not just about technical data, but defines the spending priorities and public intervention in a sector, the energy sector, which is a pillar of the Swiss economy. The choices made today in parliament will have direct impacts on the resilience of the territory against global warming. It is necessary to be informed about the proposals that will be put to the vote, as these will determine the technological standards and regulations that will guide the private and public sectors in the adoption of renewable energies and in a careful management of water resources.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the role of the ECCP in Ticino?
The Cantonal Energy and Climate Plan (PECC) is the instrument through which the Canton intends to address the climate emergency, proposing mitigation measures to reduce emissions and stimulate the use of solar photovoltaic energy, as well as defining adaptation strategies to climate change.
Why are summers considered a critical issue?
The current summers have extreme temperatures that, due to climate change, occur with a much higher frequency than in the past. This has direct impacts on public health, water availability and ecosystem stability.
What is the position of the Greens on the ECCP?
The Greens, represented by the group leader Matteo Buzzi, consider the ECCP an essential but already insufficient plan. They urge political forces not to further weaken the State Council's proposals, stressing the need to act responsibly and with foresight.
