Interim: weather allowance after six months (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 24 September 2026

The National Council approves the protection of temporary workers employed for at least six months in continuous relationships against interruptions due to bad weather.

Context

In brief

  • The National Council approves the motion on temporary workers
  • The threshold is set at at least six months
  • The Council of States had amended the text
  • The act passes to the government for implementation

Key facts

  • Decision → National Council
  • Requirement → at least six months
  • Relation → continuous employment
  • Process → transmission to the government

The parliamentary decision

The National Council approved the motion of its own Social Security and Health Committee, already amended by the Council of States. The move concerns the current exclusion of the temporary work sector from bad‑weather allowances: temporary workers employed for at least six months in continuous employment relationships should no longer remain outside the scope envisaged by the text.

According to the promoting committee, the exclusion is not justified either by considerations relating to workers’ social security or by the intention to avoid dismissals. The motivation put before Parliament therefore concerns the treatment of temporary workers vis‑à‑vis interruptions of activity caused by bad weather.

The crux of the discussion was introduced by the Council of States during the examination of the motion, which took place last June. Senators deemed it appropriate to better protect long‑term temporary workers from work interruptions due to weather conditions. For this reason they narrowed the application of the text to continuous relationships with at least six months of employment.

The National Council, returning to the parliamentary act, followed the opinion of the Council of States. Thomas Rechsteiner (Centre/AI), speaking on behalf of the committee, indicated the temporal threshold as the point of balance of the amendment:

«il limite di sei mesi crea una chiara distinzione tra incarichi di breve durata e rapporti di lavoro più stabili»

In the reasoning given in the chamber, short‑term engagements are characterised by greater flexibility. Weather‑related interruptions can, in part, be compensated by other assignments; for this reason, the entitlement to the allowance is not extended in a blanket manner to the whole temporary‑work sector. The approved protection is therefore linked to the duration and continuity of the relationship, not merely to belonging to the sector.

With the vote of the People’s Chamber, the parliamentary process described by the source completes the decisive step. The parliamentary act has been transmitted to the government, which must implement it. The news therefore concerns a decision approved by the National Council and an implementation entrusted to the subsequent governmental phase.

Operational details

The criterion chosen by the States and accepted by the National Council creates two distinct perimeters. The decision does not treat every temporary assignment in the same way: it links protection to the combination of two elements indicated in the text, at least six months of employment and a continuous employment relationship.

Two different situations

SituationExpected effect according to the text
Continuous temporary relationship of at least six monthsThe worker is no longer excluded from the bad‑weather allowance after the act is implemented.
Short‑term assignmentThe extension does not become general for the whole sector.

The before‑after comparison must be read with caution. Before the parliamentary intervention, the temporary‑work sector was excluded from this allowance. After the implementation of the decision, the change will concern the part of the sector defined by the six‑month requirement and continuity. Conversely, the text does not show an automatic extension to every temporary worker.

For an agency worker with a continuous relationship that reaches at least six months, the threshold becomes the central reference to understand whether their situation will fall within the foreseen protection. For those who carry out short assignments, the logic is different: greater flexibility and the possibility of partially compensating weather‑related interruptions with other assignments are the reason given for not extending the right in a generalised way.

This distinction also has a practical effect when reading job offers and employment relationships. A short assignment and a more stable relationship are no longer considered, in the approved text, as equivalent situations in the face of weather‑related interruptions. The decisive factor is not only that the work is temporary, but the indicated duration and the continuity of the relationship.

To orient themselves, anyone evaluating new job postings must therefore separate the description of a short assignment from the situation of a worker employed long‑term. However, the link with the allowance is not yet an operational procedure defined in the described text: the act has been transmitted to the government, which will have to implement it. The parliamentary decision sets the criterion; its concrete implementation belongs to the next step.

The point remains as explained by the committee: better protect those who work long‑term, maintaining a distinction from short assignments. It is a selective choice, designed not to turn the allowance into a generalised right for the entire temporary‑work sector.

Key points

Per il lettore, la decisione può essere trasformata in una verifica in cinque passaggi, senza attribuire al testo informazioni che non contiene.

Checklist di lettura

1. Individuare il tipo di rapporto. La misura riguarda il settore del lavoro temporaneo; il primo elemento da riconoscere è quindi se la propria situazione rientra in quel settore.

2. Controllare la durata indicata. Il requisito fissato dal testo è un impiego di almeno sei mesi. La soglia non va sostituita con una valutazione generica del fatto che l'incarico sembri lungo: è il limite temporale scelto nel passaggio parlamentare.

3. Verificare la continuità. I sei mesi devono riferirsi, secondo la modifica approvata, a rapporti di lavoro continuativi. La fonte precisa questo requisito, quindi la sola indicazione di un incarico temporaneo non descrive da sola il perimetro della modifica.

4. Separare l'evento considerato. La protezione riguarda le interruzioni del lavoro dovute alle intemperie o alle condizioni meteorologiche. Questo è il caso trattato dalla mozione; il testo non presenta una disciplina generale per ogni possibile interruzione.

5. Distinguere approvazione e attuazione. Il Consiglio nazionale ha seguito il parere degli Stati, ma l'atto parlamentare è stato trasmesso al governo, che dovrà attuarlo. La verifica personale deve quindi tenere separati il criterio già approvato e le modalità applicative che deriveranno dall'attuazione.

Questa checklist non può indicare un modulo, un ufficio, un importo o una scadenza, perché tali elementi non sono riportati nella fonte. Non è quindi corretto presentare la decisione come una procedura già completa. Il dato operativo disponibile è il perimetro: almeno sei mesi, rapporto continuativo, interruzione dovuta al maltempo, attuazione affidata al governo.

Chi vuole organizzare le informazioni sul proprio rapporto può usare la guida alla busta paga svizzera come strumento editoriale del sito, senza confonderla con l'atto parlamentare. La questione dell'indennità resta legata al testo approvato e al successivo intervento governativo.

Anche la lettura economica va tenuta distinta dall'esito parlamentare: il calcolatore stipendio può aiutare a inquadrare il reddito, ma non stabilisce se un rapporto soddisfa i requisiti della mozione. Per accompagnare la verifica della propria situazione reddituale, usa il calcolatore stipendio.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which temporary workers are covered by the protection?
The change concerns temporary workers employed for at least six months in the context of continuous employment relationships. The text therefore does not automatically extend the allowance to the entire temporary work sector. The threshold was introduced by the States and accepted by the National Council to better protect temporary employees in the long term from work interruptions due to bad weather.
Why do short assignments stay out of the general scope?
According to the committee's reasoning, there is greater flexibility in short-term jobs. Interruptions due to weather conditions may be partially offset by other assignments. For this reason, the right to compensation is not extended in a generalised way to the entire sector, but remains linked to continuous relationships of at least six months.
What happens after the approval of the National Council?
The parliamentary act has been transmitted to the government, which will have to implement it. The source therefore describes the approval of the criterion and the subsequent institutional step, but does not indicate a date, amount, form or office for the request. Concrete implementation will have to follow the government implementation phase.
