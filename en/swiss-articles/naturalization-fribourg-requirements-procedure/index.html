Naturalization Canton of Fribourg: requirements and procedure (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 24 August 2026

Complete guide to ordinary naturalization in the Canton of Fribourg: federal procedure, language requirements, taxes and municipal deadlines.

Context

In short

  • Ordinary naturalization requires a minimum of 10 years of residence with permit C
  • Mandatory German language test (level A2/B1)
  • Specific taxes and documentation for each Friborg municipality

Key facts

  • What: Ordinary naturalization (Swiss citizenship)
  • When: Federal procedure minimum 10 years residence
  • Where: Canton of Fribourg, local municipalities
  • Who: Foreigners with residence permit C
  • Requirements: C Permit, language, integration, good conduct

Ordinary naturalization in Switzerland is the standard path for foreigners who wish to obtain Swiss citizenship. In the canton of Fribourg, the process is managed by municipal authorities according to federal requirements, with additional cantonal criteria for language and integration.

The legal and federal basis: the law prescribes at least 10 years of legal residence in Switzerland with residence permit C. However, the canton of Friborg and its municipalities add their own requirements, both in terms of language and integration and administrative documentation.

The procedure is not automatic: each municipality personally evaluates the request, applying specific federal and cantonal criteria. This means that the times, required tests and fees may vary from one Friborg municipality to another. There is no universal deadline; municipalities operate with their own calendars.

C permit and years of residence

To access ordinary naturalization, the first requirement is to possess a C permit, also called residence permit. This is the most stable residence permit for foreigners in Switzerland, renewable every 5 years (or 10 years for EU/EFTA citizens). Federal law requires 10 years of uninterrupted residence with this permit, although some periods of absence from Switzerland (short stays abroad) do not interrupt the count.

Operational details

Language and integration requirements

Switzerland requires genuine integration into the local context. At the cantonal level, this usually translates into language tests and, in some municipalities, into general integration assessments. In the canton of Fribourg, where Swiss German is predominantly spoken, the required level is typically A2 or B1 according to the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages.

Specific tests vary by municipality: some require formal exams at certified bodies, others informal assessments through interviews with the authorities. Linguistic competence is evaluated as a factor of social and work integration. It is not necessary to be a native speaker, but the ability to communicate in the official language of the canton is essential.

Naturalization fees and costs

Ordinary naturalization fees are set by the Friborg municipalities. There is no single federal amount. Each municipality establishes its own administrative fee, generally proportionate to the income capacity and family situation of the applicant.

In addition to municipal taxes, additional fees may be required for: administrative certificates (birth certificates, criminal records), certified translation of documents, certificates of good conduct, criminal background checks.

Those with low incomes can sometimes request a reduction or deferral of payment from the competent municipality. It is advisable to explicitly ask for this possibility when submitting the application.

Importance of good conduct

Swiss federal law does not allow naturalization for those with serious criminal records or those who pose a risk to public order. Each canton and municipality verifies this aspect through certificates of good conduct issued by the police.

Key points

Practical step by step procedure

Step 1: Check prerequisites

Make sure you have residence permit C and have resided in Switzerland for at least 10 years. If the count is uncertain (for example if you have previously had L permits or other qualifications), contact the cantonal migration office of Friborg for a free consultation and verify your precise count.

Step 2: Contact your municipality of residence

The municipality of the area in which you live manages naturalization. Contact your local administrative office or migration office. Explicitly ask for: complete list of documents required in your case, exact amount of municipal taxes, deadline for submitting the application (if any), estimated date of the decision, delivery method (physical counter or registered mail).

Step 3: Gather your documents

Prepare certificates, register extracts and tax returns according to municipal instructions provided. If some documents are not available or require certified translation, organize the service in advance (official translators or specialized agencies). Some municipalities accept notarial translations; others require federally certified translators.

Step 4: Take any language tests

If the municipality requires a German language test (very likely in Freiburg), enroll in the official course or exam at certified institutions. Level A2 is generally the minimum accepted; B1 is recommended for a more solid application. Keep the passing certificate to attach to the application.

Step 5: Submit your application

Fill out the official form of the municipality in all its parts and present it together with the documents. Deliver directly to the administrative office during opening hours, or via registered mail, retaining proof of receipt. Request a presentation receipt for tracking.

Frequently Asked Questions
How many years of residence do you need for ordinary naturalization in Switzerland?
At the federal level, at least 10 years of legal residence with a C residence permit are required. In the canton of Fribourg, the canton and municipalities apply this federal standard as a minimum requirement. Contact your Fribourg municipality to confirm the exact timing in your specific case and check if there are any additional criteria.
Do I need to pass a language test to become a naturalized citizen in Freiburg?
Yes, the canton of Fribourg requires German language skills (the main language of the canton). The level required is typically A2 or B1 according to the Common European Framework. Some municipalities offer information on preparation courses or official exam dates. Check with your municipality which test is mandatory and how to prepare for it.
How much does naturalisation in the Canton of Fribourg cost?
Fees are set by each municipality in Fribourg and vary from municipality to municipality. There is no single federal amount. Contact your municipality's administrative office to find out the exact cost in your situation. Reductions or deferrals of payment may be available for low incomes.
Can I naturalize if I have a criminal record?
Each case is assessed individually by the authorities. Serious crimes compromise naturalization. Misdemeanors may not automatically rule out, but the canton and municipality have the power to assess each situation. Consult the Fribourg authorities if you have any doubts about your specific location.
What is the typical duration of the naturalization procedure in Freiburg?
From a few months to a year, approximately. The exact timing depends on the municipality's workload, the completeness of the documents, and any additional checks required. The municipality will communicate an informal estimate at the time of submission of the application.
