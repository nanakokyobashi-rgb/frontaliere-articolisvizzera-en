Swatch beats Samsung in court: 11.6 million in compensation (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 September 2026

Swatch wins in London against Samsung: the court orders the giant to pay $11.6 million for counterfeiting digital dials.

Context

In brief

  • Swatch wins lawsuit in London against Samsung: $11.6 million in damages
  • Samsung copied digital watch faces with logos of 10 Swiss brands
  • Approximately 160,000 illegal downloads in the UK and EU between 2015 and 2019

Key Facts

  • What: Counterfeit digital watch faces on Galaxy App Store
  • Where: London Court
  • Who: Swatch Group vs Samsung Electronics
  • When: Violations from 2015 to 2019
  • Amount: $11.6 million (vs. $170 million requested)
  • Brands: Breguet, Blancpain, Jaquet Droz, Glashütte Original, Omega, Longines, Tissot, Hamilton, Mido, Swatch
  • Users: Approximately 160,000 downloads

Swatch has won a significant victory in a case judged by a British court against South Korean tech giant Samsung. The London court has sentenced Samsung Electronics to pay $11.6 million to the Swiss watch company for trademark infringement. The ruling comes after years of litigation on a specific issue: the digital counterfeiting of watch faces.

The story concerns applications that can be downloaded from Samsung's Galaxy App Store platform, available between October 2015 and February 2019. These apps offered watch faces that mimicked protected designs from ten brands of the Swatch Group: Breguet, Blancpain, Jaquet Droz, Glashütte Original, Omega, Longines, Tissot, Hamilton, Mido and Swatch itself. The apps were available both for free and for a fee and recorded around 160,000 downloads in the UK and European Union.

Operational details

The $11.6 million award represents a tangible economic achievement for the Swatch Group in the current business environment. The Swiss company, which is listed on the Zurich stock exchange, has recovered in recent years after a difficult period in the watch industry. A favorable ruling on intellectual property helps to strengthen trademark protection in the global market and sends an important signal to competitors about the cost of counterfeiting.

The proliferation of counterfeit apps on app stores is a growing phenomenon in the digital economy. Unlike counterfeiting physical products, which requires manufacturing infrastructure, digital counterfeiting is facilitated by scalability and the difficulty in controlling millions of applications available on the platforms. In Samsung's case, the 160,000 downloads recorded in the UK and EU highlight the scale of the problem: a significant user base that has come into contact with fake versions of Swatch products.

The precedent in digital markets

The British court's ruling underlines the importance of local jurisdiction in protecting intellectual property rights. The UK and the EU have some of the strictest standards of protection in the world. This factor has presumably favored Swatch's position in legal negotiation. In addition, the court was able to assess the actual damage in terms of users reached, downloads, potential loss of sales, and reputational damage to the brand.

Key points

If you're a Swiss consumer interested in technology and watches, the London ruling offers an important practical lesson: the reliability of the source from which you download apps. Counterfeit apps that mimicked Swatch, Omega, Longines, and other brands were available on Samsung's Galaxy App Store, an official platform. This means that even app stores run by big tech manufacturers require careful user verification.

How to recognize counterfeit apps

The first defense is to verify the developer's name. Legitimate apps from Swatch, Omega, and other luxury brands are published directly by the proprietary companies or clearly identifiable authorized partners. If an app offers popular design watch faces but is not published by the official company, it is almost certainly counterfeit.

Second, look at reviews and the total number of downloads. Official apps from established brands have thousands or hundreds of thousands of downloads with a pattern of verified reviews. Counterfeit apps, even if they are mass-downloaded as in the Samsung case, often have a less professional profile.

Finally, consider the price and features. A legitimate watch face from a luxury brand generally costs between $0.99 and $4.99. If you find deals that are too affordable or apps that promise access to premium content without in-app purchases, be wary.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is digital trademark counterfeiting?
Digital counterfeiting consists of the unauthorized reproduction of protected logos, designs and brand names in digital format, such as apps, dials and virtual accessories. In the Swatch vs. Samsung case, counterfeit apps allowed users to replicate the design of luxury watches without buying the original product. It is an intellectual property infringement that damages both the perceived value of the brand and the sales of the original products.
Why is the $11.6 million award so less than the $170 million claimed?
The courts calculate compensation on the basis of the actual proven damage. In Samsung's case, the court assessed the actual downloads (about 160,000), the period of diffusion (2015-2019), and the reputational loss to the brands. Swatch claimed $170 million, but the court found that the actual damage was less. Samsung had initially offered $301, indicating a serious initial underestimation of the breach.
Will this compensation have an impact on the price of the Swatch stock on the stock exchange?
The $11.6 million is a positive sum for the company, but in proportion to Swatch Group's financial statements it does not represent a figure that would significantly change the annual financial results. However, the legal victory strengthens brand protection globally and sends a positive signal to investors about intellectual property control.
How can I verify that an app is authentic before downloading it?
Check three items: (1) the name of the developer — it must be the official company or a clearly identified authorized partner; (2) the number of downloads and reviews — legitimate apps have verifiable profiles; (3) the price — official quadrants cost a few euros. If in doubt, always download from the verified brand's official profile.
What are the other Swatch Group brands involved in the dispute?
Swatch Group owns 10 brands involved in the case: Swatch, Breguet, Blancpain, Jaquet Droz, Glashütte Original, Omega, Longines, Tissot, Hamilton and Mido. These cover segments from ultra-premium luxury (Breguet, Blancpain) to affordable positioning (Swatch), representing a global diversification of the Swiss watchmaking strategy.
