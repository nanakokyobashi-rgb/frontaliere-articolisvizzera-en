The verdict on food

72.5% of Swiss voters rejected the food initiative. The result rules out a profound structural change in Swiss agriculture over the next ten years. All Cantons voted against it, with a voter turnout of 47.03%. The last poll before the vote indicated a 64% rejection, but the final result was even clearer.

Ticino also said no, with 68.66% of votes against, one of the lowest percentages among the Cantons. The day in Ticino was marked by two rejections: Bellinzona rejected the Fortress project, and the food text was also turned down.

The Federal Council's response

Guy Parmelin, Federal Councillor and head of the Federal Department of Economic Affairs, Education and Research (EAER), clarified that the verdict does not mean leaving everything unchanged. The Federal Council remains convinced that the agri-food system must evolve to face future challenges.

The indicated direction includes more resilience, better use of natural resources, less food waste, innovation, and production methods that are more efficient in economic and environmental terms. This is the trajectory linked to Agricultural Policy 2030+, the consultation for which was launched last September 5. Parmelin also indicated the goal of creating more value with available resources, strengthening agricultural incomes, increasing the efficiency of production systems, and consolidating the country's ability to overcome crises. The method, he explained, must be based on scientific knowledge, dialogue with the sectors involved, and a long-term vision.

Neutrality after the vote

Regarding the second dossier, Ignazio Cassis stated that the vote was not against neutrality. The Ticinese Federal Councillor expressed satisfaction because the definition of the principle was not made rigid and the current practice can continue.

«We were neutral yesterday, we are today, and we will be in the future».

Cassis recalled that Swiss neutrality has been adapted to different periods, sometimes more restrictively and sometimes more openly, and that it protects security and independence. Switzerland will also be able to continue deciding on a case-by-case basis whether to apply economic sanctions to other countries, looking at its own interests. For Cassis, neutrality remains linked to Swiss history and identity. It was his first vote as the person in charge of a dossier, despite having served on the executive branch since 2017.

Operational details

The practical reading of the vote

An uneven cantonal map

The national result is clear-cut, but the cantonal percentages show different levels of intensity. In Ticino, 68,66% voted against, while in the Grisons they reached 75,01%. Rejection exceeded 80% of the votes against in Uri, at 80,82%, in Schwyz, at 81,02%, in Appenzell Innerrhoden, at 80,29%, and in Obwalden, at 80,26%. Nidwalden stopped just below that mark, at 79,85%.

The rejection was more limited in Geneva, at 62,63%, Basel-City, at 63,16%, and Neuchâtel, at 65,49%. All the cantons nevertheless rejected the initiative. The picture in Ticino adds a useful distinction: while the canton said no, the municipalities of Verzasca and Campo Vallemaggia accepted the text. The cantonal result and that of individual municipalities, therefore, do not always coincide in intensity or direction.

The agricultural dossier: vote and process

For those following Swiss agriculture, the practical point is to distinguish the ballot-box verdict from the path outlined by the Federal Council. The rejection rules out the far-reaching structural change envisioned for the next ten years, but it does not close the debate on the evolution of the agri-food system. The consultation on Agricultural Policy 2030+, launched on 5 settembre, is the concrete step referenced by the source.

The topics to watch are those identified by Parmelin: resilience, natural resources, food waste, innovation, the economic and environmental performance of production, agricultural incomes, and efficiency. These are not measures already described as having entered into force; they are objectives and working criteria presented by the Federal Council. This distinction avoids interpreting the no vote as a freeze on agriculture or as the automatic approval of a new program.

Neutrality as practice

On the neutrality dossier, the practical effect described by Cassis is continuity of current practice without a rigid definition. Neutrality may be more restrictive or more open depending on the period, while the decision on economic sanctions against other countries remains to be made on a case-by-case basis, with Swiss interests in mind.

How to follow the next steps

A practical sequence

To follow developments without confusing the two dossiers, it is advisable to adopt a four-step sequence.

1. Separate the verdict from the agenda. The food initiative was rejected, while Agricultural Policy 2030+ remains the path mentioned by Parmelin. The date to mark is the launch of the consultation, on 5 September. 2. Check the territorial level. On the food text, the no is national and cantonal, but the Ticino result includes the yes votes from the municipalities of Verzasca and Campo Vallemaggia. When comparing the data, Switzerland, Cantons and municipalities must therefore be kept distinct. 3. Use the keywords of agricultural policy. Resilience, natural resources, food loss, innovation, value, agricultural incomes, efficiency and the ability to cope with crises are the criteria invoked by the head of DEFR. They help to understand whether an update concerns the trajectory described by the source. 4. Keep neutrality separate. For each new announcement, distinguish the general definition of the principle from the concrete choice concerning any economic sanctions against other countries. The position reported is that of a case-by-case decision, taking Swiss interests into account.

What to check in the next communications

The method indicated by the Federal Council for agriculture places scientific knowledge, dialogue with the sectors concerned and a long-term vision on the same level. These are therefore the three elements to look for in communications related to the consultation, together with the objectives of achieving greater value with the available resources and strengthening agricultural incomes. The concrete reference remains the consultation launched on 5 September.

On the neutrality front, Cassis's statement urges people not to mistake the rejection of the initiative for an abandonment of the principle. Neutrality, according to the position expressed, can be adapted to the period and does not coincide with isolation or indifference. This key helps to read identity, security, independence and economic decisions separately.

Frequently Asked Questions
What was the outcome of the vote on the food initiative?
The food initiative was rejected by 72.5% of Swiss voters. The rejection was unanimous at the cantonal level, with a voter turnout of 47.03%. In Ticino, 68.66% of the votes were against, while in Graubünden the opposition share reached 75.01%. This result excludes a profound structural change in Swiss agriculture for the next decade, although the Federal Council intends to continue the evolution of the agri-food system through the 2030+ Agricultural Policy.
What did the Federal Council say about neutrality?
Federal Councillor Ignazio Cassis said the vote does not represent a rejection of the principle of neutrality. He stressed that the current practice is confirmed, allowing Switzerland to adapt its approach according to the historical period. The Confederation may continue to decide on a case-by-case basis the possible application of economic sanctions, assessing its national interests and maintaining neutrality as a central element of Swiss identity and security.
What are the next steps for agricultural policy?
Despite the rejection of the initiative, the path of reform does not stop. The Federal Department of Economy, Training and Research (DEFR), led by Guy Parmelin, launched the consultation on Agricultural Policy 2030+ on 5 September. Objectives include greater resilience of the system, optimized use of natural resources, reduction of food losses and innovation, aiming to strengthen agricultural incomes and production efficiency based on long-term visions and scientific data.
