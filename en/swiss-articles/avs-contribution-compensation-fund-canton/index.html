AVS and benefits: cantonal compensation fund (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 7 September 2026

How the cantonal compensation fund works for the AVS, the calculation of the pension and supplementary benefits for those who have contribution gaps.

Context

In brief

  • Cantonal compensation fund manages the first pillar AVS locally
  • Contribution gaps reduce the pension proportionally to the missing years
  • Supplementary benefits ensure the minimum living standard
  • AVS/AI/IPG contributions: 5.3% employee (10.6% with employer)

Key facts

  • What: AVS system with cantonal compensation fund
  • Where: Each Swiss canton manages its own fund
  • Who: Federal UFAS/BSV coordinates; cantonal fund applies
  • Legal basis: First pillar of mandatory pension
  • Function: Pension calculation, gap management, supplementary benefits

The cantonal compensation fund is the local body that concretely administers the first pillar of Swiss pension. Each canton, from Zurich to Geneva, from Bern to Ticino, has a fund that collects the mandatory contributions of employed workers and coordinates the calculation of future pensions. This multi-level system — federal, cantonal, communal — ensures that those who have paid regularly throughout their career receive an adequate pension, while those with contribution gaps can access supplementary benefits to guarantee the minimum living standard.

The contributions paid each month on the pay slip amount to 5.3% for the employee (with the employer paying an additional 5.3%, for a total of 10.6% according to federal rates). These funds flow into the compensation fund of the canton of residence of the worker, where they are recorded year by year in the individual archive. The fund does not set the rates autonomously — this is the competence of the UFAS/BSV at the federal level — but rigorously applies the national regulation, calculating case by case.

The administrative role of the cantonal fund

In addition to collecting contributions, the compensation fund performs three key functions. First: it maintains a complete archive of the contribution career of each person, recording year by year the amount of the determining income and the corresponding contributions paid. Second: it coordinates with the funds of other cantons when a worker changes location (for example, moves from Zurich to Lugano) or works temporarily abroad, ensuring continuity of the national contribution path. Third: it manages the calculation of the initial pension and, if necessary, access to supplementary benefits.

When a worker reaches the reference age (65 years for men, 64 for women, subject to legislative changes), the fund receives the pension request and proceeds with the final calculation based on the accumulated contribution career. If gaps emerge during this process — years without payments or with insufficient payments — the fund documents them and communicates to the applicant the impact on the final pension. It is at this stage that the possibility of voluntary buy-backs or access to supplementary benefits comes into play.

The cantonal compensation fund offers individualized consulting services. The advisory role is important because it allows the future retiree to understand exactly how their career influences the pension, and if there are margins for intervention (buy-backs, credits for care periods or unemployment, etc.).

Operational details

Contributory gaps: what they are and how they affect you

A contributory gap is a year in which the worker has not paid AVS contributions or has paid them insufficiently. The causes can be multiple: unemployment not covered by insurance, periods of inactivity, income below the minimum contribution threshold (currently very low, but communicated year by year by the fund), or periods spent abroad without international coverage. Each year of gap proportionally reduces the final pension: if the complete professional career would cover 44 years and only 42 are present, the pension will be calculated on 42/44 of the maximum base.

The calculation of the AVS pension follows a standardized national formula, defined by the federal UFAS/BSV but applied locally by the cantonal compensation fund. The formula takes into account the average annual income recorded throughout the career (with adjustments for inflation), multiplied by the number of actual contributory years. It is not a simple division: years of higher income weigh proportionally more, precisely to reflect the insurance concept of the first pillar.

Mechanisms for covering gaps

Not all gaps are equal from a regulatory point of view. Federal law provides automatic credits for socially relevant periods: years in which one has been a parent of minor children (credits for childcare), periods of compensated unemployment, or years of disability coverage. These credits do not reduce the final pension, as the law considers them "equivalent" to actual payments. The cantonal fund automatically identifies these periods in the contributory career extract.

For residual gaps not covered by credits, the law allows voluntary buy-backs. The applicant can pay a lump sum (calculated by the fund based on the missing years) to retroactively fill the gap. This is a useful option in the years preceding retirement, especially for those who have underestimated the future cost of years of inactivity. However, not all periods are buyable (for example, very remote periods of casual work are often not).

Supplementary benefits to cover the minimum living standard

When the calculated AVS pension — even after buy-backs — does not reach the federally defined minimum living standard, the compensation fund intervenes with supplementary benefits. This is not social assistance, but a structural element of the first pillar: anyone who has paid for at least one year is entitled to a basic pension, and if this is insufficient, the State guarantees the minimum.

The minimum living standard is set at the federal level (it includes housing, food, utilities, health insurance) but is administratively managed by the cantonal fund according to uniform criteria. Once the pension request is approved, the fund automatically verifies if an integration is necessary. It does not require an additional application: the verification is procedural. However, the applicant must communicate any other income or assets (e.g., real estate beyond the first home), as supplementary benefits take into account the overall asset situation.

Key points

How to check your contribution status

The first step is to request a contribution history extract from your cantonal compensation fund. This document, available online or on paper request, lists year by year from the first payment to the current year: the recorded determining income, the contributions paid, and any recognized credits or documented gaps. There is no cost to obtain this extract. The fund is obligated to provide it within reasonable times (usually 2-4 weeks, sometimes online in the personal portal of the contributor).

By reading the extract, you can check if there are years with zero amount (pure gaps) or years with amounts lower than the minimum (partial contribution). The fund notes next to each gap the recognized reason (e.g., "period of compensated unemployment, equivalent credit") or "pure gap". This distinction is crucial: equivalent gaps do not reduce the pension, pure gaps do.

Step-by-step procedure towards retirement

Phase 1 (4-5 years before retirement age): Request the contribution history extract. If non-equivalent gaps emerge, evaluate with the fund whether it is convenient to make voluntary buybacks in the next years.

Phase 2 (about 6-8 months before): The fund, automatically or upon request, provides an indicative estimate of the pension, possibly with the integration of complementary benefits. In this phase, it is still possible to modify buybacks.

Phase 3 (4-6 weeks before the retirement birthday): Submit the formal pension application, equipped with an identity document, bank account extract (for credit), marriage certificate if married (for survivorship). The fund specifies the required documentation in its form.

Phase 4 (within 2-3 months of the application): The fund notifies the final decision with the monthly amount, start date, and possible integration. If the pension is integrated by complementary benefits, the total contribution and the amount available for the minimum living are also communicated.

Organizational aspects and coordination

AVS contributions continue to be paid regularly even during unemployment, because the unemployment insurance fund (AD) and the insolvency compensation fund (AC) pay the AVS contributions on behalf of the unemployed (up to 1.1% annually up to a maximum, according to federal rates). It is essential to immediately communicate any interruption of work to the cantonal fund, otherwise the payments risk stopping and creating an unjustified gap.

If during the career you have worked in more cantons, the fund of the current canton coordinates with the others to reconstruct the complete national path. This does not cause any delay: the system is computerized and the funds exchange data within a few weeks. It is not necessary to make manual requests between funds.

How do voluntary contribution gap redemptions work?
Voluntary redemptions allow years to be retroactively filled with gaps not covered by credits. The cantonal fund calculates a lump sum based on the missing years and the average career income. Payment is optional and must be made before retirement. Not all periods are redeemable (e.g., very remote periods of casual work often are not). It is advisable to inquire at the cashier's office at least 1-2 years before retirement to assess whether it is economically convenient.
What are supplementary benefits and who is entitled to them?
The complementary benefits are additions to the AVS annuity provided by the cantonal compensation fund to ensure the minimum living. They are not social assistance, but a structural part of the first pillar. Anyone who has paid at least one year and whose AVS income is insufficient to cover essential expenses (housing, food, utilities, health insurance) is entitled. How do I access my contribution career data at the cantonal fund?
You can request your contribution statement directly from the compensation fund in your canton, free of charge. The request can be submitted online (if the cashier has a personal portal), by mail, or in person. The cashier must provide the document within 2-4 weeks. The extract lists year-by-year recorded income, contributions paid, credits recognized, and gaps. It is advisable to request it at least 4-5 years before retirement to check for any anomalies and evaluate redemptions.
Does the compensation fund change if I move to another canton?
Yes, your AVS deposit flows into the cashier of the canton where you work (or residence, depending on the situation). However, you don't have to do anything manually: the cantonal coffers coordinate with each other through a computerized system and national career tracking. When you request the annuity, the cashier of your last canton automatically reconstructs the entire contribution path, coordinating with all the other cashiers. There are no delays or complications for intra-Swiss transfers.
