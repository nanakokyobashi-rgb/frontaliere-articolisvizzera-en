The Confederation promotes de-escalation in cyberspace

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 30 September 2026

An international conference organized by the Swiss Confederation on de-escalation in cyberspace is taking place in Zug, focusing on preventive diplomacy, the OSCE, and artificial intelligence.

The Theater Casino in Zug is hosting the international conference through which the Swiss Confederation is promoting de-escalation in cyberspace. The event is described in the source as an initiative taking place yesterday and today, Wednesday, during Switzerland’s chairmanship of the Organization for Security and Cooperation in Europe (OSCE). The two-day event showcases Swiss diplomacy in the cyber realm, following recent events that have highlighted how the virtual world can become a source of tension between countries.

Martin Pfister opened the proceedings yesterday. In his remarks, he emphasized the importance of preventive diplomacy to build trust and of mediation to ease tensions, including in the digital world. The conference thus combines the theme of cyber security with the diplomatic tools already referenced in the opening remarks.

The Cases Cited and the Proposed Response

Cyberattacks, espionage operations, and artificial intelligence that spirals out of control form the backdrop for the discussion. Florian Schütz, director of the Federal Office for Cybersecurity, cited two cases: artificial intelligence that covertly infiltrates Australian government websites and a false report by U.S. intelligence agencies that creates tensions with China. For Schütz, these incidents demonstrate the limitations of artificial intelligence.

“This means that humans must verify the results, but it also means that security mechanisms are needed,” Schütz told SRF on the sidelines of the conference.

The director then referred to the confidence-building measures agreed upon by OSCE member states. The next question is how to implement them amid increasingly intense geopolitical tensions and to what extent countries are willing to share the information necessary for defense operations.
