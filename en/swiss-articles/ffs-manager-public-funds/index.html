In December 2024, the Office of the Attorney General of Switzerland opened an investigation into a former FFS department head, now 54 years old, who is alleged to have misappropriated around nine million francs between 2007 and 2024. The contents of the indictment were reported by CH-Media newspapers.

For the former executive, who had worked for the former federal railway since the age of 23 and held various positions, a multi-year prison sentence is being sought. The charges listed are criminal mismanagement, aggravated fraud, money laundering and document forgery.

The alleged scheme

According to reports by CH-Media newspapers, the defendant allegedly issued invoices for nonexistent orders and then directed the money into accounts of companies he controlled. The sister and three acquaintances are also accused of complicity.

The sister and the three acquaintances allegedly were unaware of the illegal nature of the dealings. The Office of the Attorney General of Switzerland nevertheless believes they should at least have harbored suspicions, given that they received around one million francs without doing any work.

FFS's accounting checks compared only the supplier, the order number and the amount. For several years, no invoice allegedly exceeded 50'000 francs; later, in some cases, the former employee allegedly misappropriated sums exceeding 100'000 francs. The defendant cited his «financial difficulties» as the motive.

The purchases and the trial

According to the indictment, with the money obtained illegally, the man allegedly purchased, among other things, a plot of land in the Canton of Solothurn valued at 450'000 francs, a house in Florida worth 435'000 francs and 13 vehicles. Among these was a Lamborghini Gallardo LP570-4 Blancpain costing 400'000 francs, of which only twelve examples exist worldwide.

The trial of the 54-year-old, who has confessed, and the four co-defendants will begin in late October. Since fraud is time-barred after 15 years, only around five million of the nine million francs cited as total damage are currently relevant under criminal law. Any procedural delays could reduce this amount further.
