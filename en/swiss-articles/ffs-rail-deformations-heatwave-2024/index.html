The data communicated by the SBB

In the response to Keystone-ATS, the SBB endorsed the data of 97 deformations and crushing of the tracks, cited by the SonntagsZeitung. The communication, dated Bern, concerns last summer, marked by exceptional temperatures. According to Sunday, the figure is almost four times higher than that recorded in the previous year.

The phenomenon includes deformations and expansions of the railway infrastructure. The company said the increase had no impact on safety. In the same newspaper, however, Patrick Braess, an expert at the Federal Polytechnic University of Zurich, warned of the risk of derailments caused by the heat.

SBB explained that it has a dense monitoring network. Events with safety implications are systematically transmitted to the Federal Office of Transport. The rails are regularly checked with measuring and diagnostic vehicles, so as to assess the situation in a targeted manner. Rigorous infrastructure maintenance has been identified as the most important preventive measure. The company also pointed out that zero risk never exists.

Monitoring, reporting and vehicle checks are described as tools already used by SBB. The task force is instead in charge of defining any additional measures for exceptional heat, if necessary.

Faced with the exceptional heat of summer 2026, the company has set up a task force to define additional measures, if necessary. The strategy also includes long-term interventions: concrete sleepers, considered more stable than those in wood, and a higher neutralization temperature during welding, to make the rails more resistant to temperatures that tend to be higher.

SBB also experimented with white paint. At first, cooling averaged about 4.5 degrees; after two years, the dirt had reduced the effect to about half. The more intense the traffic, the faster the effect fades. This is why SBB is evaluating, in an ongoing analysis, whether white coatings are sensible in certain cases.
