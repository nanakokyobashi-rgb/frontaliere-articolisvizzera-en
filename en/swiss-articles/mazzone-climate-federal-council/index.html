Lisa Mazzone, president of the Greens, issued a stark warning at the party delegates' assembly in Fribourg. The central theme of her speech? The Federal Council's inaction in the face of the heatwaves of the past summer, which left gaps in decisions and concrete measures for Switzerland's climate.

'The Federal Council must remove the environment department from Albert Rösti,' stated the Geneva native, addressing the federal government directly. According to the Greens' president, the Swiss executive has clear responsibilities and must 'change course' in a radical and immediate way.

The demand is broad and incisive. Mazzone stressed that the Federal Council must 'put climate first,' 'adopt urgent measures to adapt our cities and countryside,' and 'invest heavily in climate protection by ending the harmful dependence on fossil fuels.' Words that summarize a vision of profound transformation of federal policy.

Operational details

The Federal Climate Debate and the Role of DETEC

The challenge that Mazzone raises goes beyond a simple ministerial reorganization: it touches the heart of Swiss federal governance and how Bern intends to address the climate crisis in the coming years in a structural way.

Albert Rösti, director of the DETEC (Department of the Environment, Transport, Energy and Communications), has been a point of friction in the Swiss political debate for months. Attributing the climate dossier to a department that also manages transport, energy and communications creates priority conflicts: how to balance road expansion, rail efficiency, digital infrastructure and renewable investment with the imperative to reduce emissions and protect the climate?

The Greens' position suggests that the current institutional set-up is not sufficient to ensure coherent and truly priority climate protection. If the climate is managed within a cross-cutting department, it risks being subordinated to short-term pressures from other sectors.

Municipalities locally finance energy insulation, shaded parks, resilient water supply systems, and infrastructure reinforcements against floods and landslides. Employers face rising costs for climate control, workplace safety in extreme heat, and absenteeism due to thermal discomfort. Workers suffer from reduced productivity, health risks in exposed sectors (construction, agriculture, transport), and stress from climate vulnerability. Frequently Asked Questions
Who is Lisa Mazzone and what role does she play in the Greens?
Lisa Mazzone is the president of the Swiss Green Party. Originally from Geneva, she leads the party in carrying out criticism of the federal management of climate change, especially with regard to the decisions of the Federal Council and the allocation of public resources between defense and environmental protection. His interview before party delegates in Freiburg represents the official position of the Greens on federal climate policy.
What does DATEC mean in the context of Mazzone's request?
DATEC is the acronym for the Department of Environment, Transport, Energy and Communications. Albert Rösti heads this federal department. Mazzone asks that the Federal Council remove him from this post because he is not ensuring sufficient priority to climate protection within a department with very transversal competences, where the climate is subordinated to short-term pressures from transport and energy.
How much has the Federal Climate Council allocated to defence?
According to Mazzone, the Federal Council has allocated an additional 900 million francs to the army. In the same budgetary context, the government has not allocated even one additional franc for climate protection and climate adaptation. This direct contrast highlights the federal priority scale: defense before climate.
How does the lack of federal investment in the climate impact Swiss residents?
Without specific federal investments, municipalities and cantons must locally finance climate adaptation measures (energy isolation, urban greening, resilient water supply), increasing the local tax burden. Farmers, workers and consumers face rising costs related to extreme heat, droughts and food price volatility. Quality of life and productivity are directly impacted by heat waves.
What has the Federal Council decided about the Sustainable Financial Square initiative?
The Federal Council rejected the initiative on the sustainable financial square. According to Mazzone, this initiative could have had a significant impact on the global climate, directing international financial flows towards climate-sustainable investments. The refusal represents, for the Greens, a further sign of immobility and absence of climate ambition on the part of the federal government.
