Permit C Basel City: requirements and application (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 September 2026

Residence permit C Basel: years required, integration, language skills and application procedure at the canton.

Context

In brief

  • Permit C: long-term residence (10 years in general, 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens)
  • Requires integration: stable job, housing, language, local laws
  • Application at the cantonal migration office of Basel-Stadt
  • Renewable after 10 years, leads to ordinary naturalization

Key facts

  • What: Permit C (federal permanent residence permit)
  • Duration: 10 years for non-EU/EFTA foreigners; 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens
  • Issued by: State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) in coordination with the canton
  • Where: Migration Office of the Canton of Basel-Stadt
  • Requirements: Socio-professional integration, local language, Swiss laws

The Permit C is the Swiss federal residence permit for foreigners intending to reside long-term. Managed by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) in coordination with the cantonal administrations, the Permit C is issued after at least 10 years of continuous legal residence (5 years for EU/EFTA citizens).

Unlike the Permit B (residence, renewable every 5 years) or the Permit L (short-term, up to 1 year), the Permit C offers prolonged residential stability and represents a concrete step towards ordinary Swiss naturalization.

Integration and federal-cantonal criteria

To obtain the Permit C, federal and cantonal authorities assess the foreigner's integration into Swiss society. There is no rigid national standardized list, but the cantons — including Basel-Stadt — verify criteria such as:

  • Stable professional situation (employment, self-employment, adequate income)
  • Permanent and stable housing in the canton
  • Knowledge of the local language (German, in the case of Basel)
  • Compliance with federal and cantonal Swiss legislation
  • Regular payment of AVS, IVA, cantonal and communal taxes

Operational details

Integration: what does it mean in practice?

Integration is not a uniform national quantitative metric, but rather it is evaluated by the canton and the municipality where you reside. According to federal regulations, integration requires four main dimensions:

Linguistic: Knowledge of the local language (German in Basel). Authorities usually verify this through direct interviews or certification. There is no official federal CEFR level required, but cantons generally require basic communication (A2 or B1 CEFR) for daily life: understanding work instructions, reading documents, interacting with schools or administration.

Socio-professional: Stable employment and sufficient income to support yourself without relying on public subsidies. Your employer may be contacted to verify your employment status.

Legal and civil: Absence of significant criminal records, regular payment of taxes and social contributions, and general compliance with Swiss laws (does not require detailed knowledge of the penal code, but correct conduct).

Cultural: Integration into the local community (use of public services, civic participation, social interactions). It is not mandatory to be a volunteer in associations, but it facilitates a positive evaluation.

Application procedures: the role of the canton and the SEM

The formal procedure is divided between the federal SEM and the Basel-Stadt cantonal migration office:

Phase 1 (Canton): You or your employer submit the application to the Basel-Stadt migration office. The required documents generally include: valid passport, residence certificate, current employment contract, recent pay slips, Swiss federal criminal record certificate, tax declaration for the last 2-3 years, proof of accommodation (rental or ownership contract), passport photo.

Key points

How to apply: step-by-step procedure

Step 1: Check prerequisites

Before applying, verify: Do you have at least 10 years of continuous legal residence in Switzerland with a valid L or B permit? Are you an EU/EFTA citizen (in which case, count 5 years)? Do you currently and legally reside in the canton of Basel-Stadt? If the answer is yes to all questions, proceed to the next steps.

Step 2: Gather official documents

Contact the migration office of the canton of Basel-Stadt directly to obtain the complete and up-to-date list. Generally, the essential documents include:

  • Valid passport or identity card
  • Official certificate of residence (not older than 3 months)
  • Current employment contract or self-employment declaration
  • Last 2-3 pay slips (or self-employed balance sheet for the last 2 years)
  • Swiss federal criminal record certificate (requestable online via the federal portal)
  • Federal and cantonal tax declarations for the last 2-3 years
  • Rental contract or property certificate (proof of stable housing)
  • Official passport photo (if required)

Step 3: Submit application and attend interview

Request an appointment at the Basel-Stadt migration office. Some cantons require online booking, others manage it at the counter. During the interview: bring all original documents in certified copy, the interview is conducted in German (basic level, no linguistic perfection required), prepare answers on employment, language integration, respect for laws, future projects in Switzerland.

Frequently Asked Questions
How long does it take SEM to decide on the C permit application?
There is no federal standardized duration. It depends on the complexity of the personal situation, the quality and completeness of the documentation and the administrative burden of the cantonal migration office and SEM. On average: 2-4 months, but some dossiers take 6+ months. Contact the Basel City migration office for a realistic estimate based on the local situation and current processing times.
Does the C permit renew automatically after 10 years?
No, permit C does not renew automatically. After 10 years of validity, the permit expires. To keep it, you must submit a formal renewal application to the cantonal migration office, with updated verification of integration. However, if you maintain the integration criteria (stable work, civil behavior, regular payments), renewal is generally approved. It is advisable to submit an application for renewal 6 months before the deadline to avoid interruptions.
What level of German is required for the C permit in Basel?
There is no mandatory official federal CEFR level. However, the cantonal authorities of Basel-City require basic communication in German: understand instructions, read daily documents, converse with employer and colleagues. Typically A2-B1 CEFR is considered adequate. During the integration interview, the authorities will assess your practical fluency. If necessary, take a language course before applying to increase your chances of passing.
If I have a past criminal conviction, can I get the C permit?
The SEM does not automatically exclude candidates with criminal records. It assesses on a case-by-case basis the type of offence, the date, the penalty and the subsequent behaviour. Very outdated misdemeanors or those with proven rehabilitation are less of an obstacle. Serious crimes (violence, drugs, fraud) significantly reduce the chances. Consult a Swiss immigration lawyer before applying to evaluate your specific profile.
Having obtained the C permit, do AVS taxes and contributions change?
Yes, significant changes. The C permit stabilizes your permanent resident status, bringing worldwide taxation by the canton (not just Swiss income). AVS contributions continue at the standard employee rate (5.3% salary, employer adds 5.3%), but your social security accreditation consolidates: every year of contributions paid counts towards the 43 years (men) or 42 years (women) needed for full AVS income.
