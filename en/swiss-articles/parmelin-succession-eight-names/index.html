On Friday morning, October 23, 2026, the race to succeed Guy Parmelin on the Federal Council had reached eight names. The day before, with Marco Chiesa and the French-speaking Bernese Pierre Alain Schnegg entering the race, the number of candidates had risen to four. The list then lengthened with Valais National Council member Michael Graber, Valais State Councilor Franz Ruppen, Aargau State Councilor Jean-Pierre Gallati and Thomas Aeschi.

Thomas Aeschi was officially nominated by the SVP in Zug. The source presents him as parliamentary group leader and specifies that he has not yet resolved his personal reservations. The text therefore keeps two elements separate: the candidacy announced by the SVP in Zug and the personal reservations that Aeschi has yet to resolve.

The chronology is equally precise: the list rose from four to eight names on Friday morning, while the deadlines for the cantonal sections expire at noon that same day. The source therefore describes a phase that is still open in terms of candidacies, not the outcome of the vote.

Statements in Bern

Graber came forward in an interview with TagesAnzeiger, published shortly before the press conference in Bern. In front of the media, the 45-year-old described himself as “a safe bet in uncertain times” and pledged his commitment to ensuring that Switzerland protects its assets, alluding to a Europe described as engulfed in flames.

Ruppen, 55, instead focused on the experience he has accumulated at several levels. He recalled his local political career in Naters from 2005 to 2021, ten years in the Grand Council and the six years in the National Council, from 2015 to 2021, before being elected to the State Council. Today he heads a department responsible for mobility, spatial development and the environment; he presents himself as a representative of the mountain regions and has stated that he is ready to serve Switzerland and its population.

Key points

Withdrawals, representation and timetable

The expansion to eight names was not linear. During the same phase described by the source, the race also saw two withdrawals. Nicolas Kolly, a 40-year-old from Fribourg, withdrew; in an interview with La Liberté, he said he was interested in moving in 2027 from the House of Representatives to the Council of States. He explained that he did not want to reduce the chances of French-speaking Switzerland in the race for the Federal Council and recalled Céline Amaudruz's candidacy. He also said that he would have been ready for a ticket with the Genevan, whose profile he considered interesting. The source specifies, however, that the party did not choose to focus on a French-speaking Swiss candidate in any event.

The Zurich section of the UDC also announced a withdrawal on Friday morning. Thomas Matter, a 60-year-old member of the National Council, explained to the section that he had chosen not to run, given the significance of the decision and the short time available to apply. These developments are documented as communications from sections or statements by those concerned; they are not accompanied by a reported final internal vote in the source.

The chronology is twofold: Kolly and Matter's withdrawals were announced during the same phase in which the list was growing, before Friday's noon deadline. The reasons given, however, are different: Kolly refers to representation for French-speaking Switzerland, Matter to the significance of the decision and the short deadline.

The timetable includes three steps: on November 20, as Aeschi had already stated in his capacity as parliamentary group leader, the UDC should submit to the Chambers a ticket of at least two names; on December 9, the election of the new Federal Councillor will take place; taking office is set for January 1.
