Starting a business in the Canton of Nidvaldo involves a series of administrative, fiscal, and social security obligations that must be addressed from the outset. The choice of legal form represents the first decisive step: operating as an individual business, a partnership, a simple limited partnership, or a joint-stock company has different consequences in terms of personal liability, taxation, and social contributions.

Registration with the commercial register is mandatory before the start of the activity. The commercial register is managed at the cantonal level in Nidvaldo, and registration has a cost that varies depending on the type of business organization. Once registered, entrepreneurs must address the issue of taxation, which in Switzerland operates on three levels: federal, cantonal, and municipal.

Operational details

Switzerland's three-tier tax structure

The Swiss tax system is characterised by pronounced decentralisation: federal, cantonal and municipal taxes coexist and each level has its own calculation mechanisms and rates. Those who open a business in Nidvaldo must take this overlap into account.

Direct federal tax is levied on income and capital by a nationwide unit rate, administered by the Federal Tax Administration (FTA). The cantonal income tax, managed by the administration of the canton of Nidvaldo, has its own rate that varies over time. The municipalities then apply a multiplier on this cantonal rate: which means that the overall tax burden depends on the specific municipality within Nidvaldo.

For commercial activities, VAT is a crucial obligation if turnover exceeds pre-established thresholds. VAT is administered by the AFC at the federal level and is a value-added tax that the entrepreneur collects from customers and pays to the State. VAT returns must be submitted periodically according to the established schedule: monthly, quarterly or annually depending on the size of the activity.

Key points

Procedure practical: the concrete steps

Opening a business in Nidvaldo involves a sequence of administrative and bureaucratic obligations. Below are the main steps:

1. Choice of legal form: Before anything else, decide whether to operate as an individual business, a partnership or a joint-stock company. This choice affects personal liability, the minimum required capital and registration procedures.

2. Verification of capital requirements: Each legal form has a minimum capital requirement. An individual business does not require a formalized minimum capital; a joint-stock company requires a declared capital. Partnership and simple limited companies do not have a mandatory minimum capital by law, although commercial practice may require initial contributions. Consult the Commercial Register Office of the Canton Nidvaldo for details on specific cantonal legislation.

3. Registration with the Commercial Register: Contact the Commercial Register Office of the Canton Nidvaldo or your place of residence. You will need to submit the constitutive documents (constitution act for companies, start of activity declaration for individual businesses) and pay the registration fees. Deadlines are tied to the actual start of the activity.

4. Request for business number: The local authority will communicate the business number (cantonese exercise number), essential for all communications with the administration. This number will be needed for VAT, AVS and tax compliance.

5. Declaration to AFC for VAT: If your turnover exceeds the statutory threshold, you must register for VAT with the Federal Tax Administration. The VAT declaration is mandatory and involves periodic obligations (monthly, quarterly or annually depending on the case).

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum capital required to open a business in Nidvaldo?
It depends on the legal form chosen. A sole proprietorship does not have a statutory minimum capital requirement. A joint-stock company has a minimum share capital prescribed by Swiss law. Collective and limited partnerships do not have a minimum capital by law, although the commercial practice may require initial contributions. Please consult the commercial register office of the Canton of Nidvaldo for specific details of the cantonal legislation.
Do I need to register for VAT immediately after opening the business?
Only if the annual turnover exceeds the legal threshold. VAT registration is mandatory with the Federal Tax Administration (AFC) for those who reach the limit. If the turnover is lower, enrollment is optional. Once enrolled, you will need to file VAT returns periodically according to the schedule established by the AFC.
How do municipal taxes work in Nidvaldo?
Municipal taxes are calculated by applying a municipal multiplier to the cantonal income and capital tax rate. This means that the overall tax burden varies from municipality to municipality within Nidvaldo, although federal and cantonal tax remain uniform at the cantonal level. The relevant municipal administration can provide the specific multipliers and an estimate of the total taxation.
What are the obligations towards AVS/AI/IPG when I start a stand-alone business?
Those who start a self-employed business must register in the AVS/AI/IPG system and pay both the worker's and the employer's share of the net income of the business, since it assumes both roles. The cantonal administration of Nidvaldo manages the calculation and communication of contributions, which must be paid periodically on the basis of the annual tax return.
When should I subscribe to LAMal?
Within three months of the start of residence in Switzerland, it is mandatory to take out a private health insurance policy (LAMal). Premiums vary by canton and region; a cantonal subsidy can be claimed from Canton Nidvaldo to reduce the premium burden based on business income. Contact a private insurer to choose the policy that best suits your coverage needs.
