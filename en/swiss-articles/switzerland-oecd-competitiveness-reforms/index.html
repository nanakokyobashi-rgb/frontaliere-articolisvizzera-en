The report on Switzerland by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) was made public on 15 September 2026. In the communiqué issued in Bern, the OECD views the country’s economic policy approach favorably, but points to the need for new measures to safeguard its competitiveness and innovative capacity over the long term.

The OECD notes the resilience of the Swiss economy. In its view, the country’s resilience has been supported by stable institutions and prudent, moderate economic and financial policy, based on favorable framework conditions and sound public finances.

The reforms outlined in the report

The first set of recommendations concerns the sustainability of public finances. The organization calls for further reforms in healthcare and old-age provision. In healthcare, the report proposes increasing efficiency, including through better coordination in hospital and care planning. Regarding pensions, it highlights the need for sustainable solutions in light of demographic trends.

A second area for action concerns productivity. The OECD proposes further reducing the administrative burden on businesses, making better use of the workforce’s potential and improving competitive conditions, citing the energy sector as one example.

The international context remains complex. The issues identified include the development of relations with the European Union and uncertainties linked to trade policy with the most important destination markets, particularly the United States. The OECD therefore calls for stronger economic ties with the main trading partners and the promotion of new trade partnerships.

The document also focuses on policies to address climate change and the availability of housing in the country. On climate matters, the OECD calls for closer cooperation between the various levels of government and more effective incentives for prevention; on housing, it cites greater densification and more efficient and faster approval procedures among the possible solutions.

The OECD’s analyses, especially the conclusions on how to make productivity growth more robust, will be incorporated into the Federal Council’s work on the growth policy report, expected by the end of 2027. The communiqué therefore sets out a reform agenda and a subsequent institutional step, without indicating a specific implementing measure.

Operational details

The OECD recommendations do not set out, in the press release, amounts, rates, contributions or effective dates. For those who live or work in Switzerland, the practical takeaway is therefore the direction of the policy discussions, not a new obligation that is already applicable. The concrete impact will depend on any subsequent decisions and, when several levels of government are involved, on the work of the Cantons.

Healthcare and pensions: distinguish the policy areas

In the Swiss system, LAMal/KVG is compulsory health insurance for residents. The report, however, discusses efficiency and coordination in hospital and care planning: it does not indicate how premiums or actual care would change. As far as the cost of living is concerned, therefore, the text contains no quantification that can be turned into a forecast for health insurance premiums.

The same caution applies to old-age provision. AVS/AHV and LPP/BVG are distinct components of Swiss social security; the OECD document highlights sustainability and demographic trends, but does not specify ages, contribution rates, amounts or a timetable. Today, the before-and-after comparison is not between two rules: it is between a recommendation and a possible future measure.

Businesses, housing and territory

For businesses, reducing administrative burdens and strengthening competition, including in the energy sector, indicate possible areas for intervention in the economic framework. The text does not identify a specific sector, a procedure to be changed or an effect on costs, employment and wages. The reference to the potential of the workforce also remains a line of economic policy, not a labor-market statistic.

On housing, greater densification and faster approvals concern how supply can be expanded. The report does not indicate cities, Cantons, the number of homes or a timetable. For climate adaptation, by contrast, the emphasis is on coordination among levels of government and better incentives for prevention, without citing specific programs.

Relations with the European Union and the United States add a trade dimension: the OECD proposes strengthening ties with key partners and seeking new ones, but makes no forecasts about orders, employment or turnover. The most cautious reading is therefore this: the report identifies national priorities, while the effects on daily life will require subsequent decisions and implementing texts.

Key points

It describes an OECD report, presents recommendations, and points to a future step in the work of the Federal Council. For now, the concrete action is to establish orderly monitoring and not treat the organization's policy direction as a rule already in force.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the OECD's main recommendations for Switzerland?
In the report released in Bern on September 15, 2026, the OECD recognizes Switzerland's economic strength but points to the need for structural reforms. Priority measures concern the sustainability of healthcare, through more efficient hospital planning, and old-age provision in the face of demographic aging. On the productivity front, the organization recommends reducing administrative burdens for businesses, harnessing the potential of the workforce, and strengthening competition, especially in the energy market.
Are there any immediate changes to health insurance premiums or pension benefits?
No, the OECD document contains strategic policy guidelines and does not introduce directly applicable rules. The text does not define new amounts, increases in contribution rates for AVS/AHV or LPP/BVG, nor specific changes to premiums for compulsory health insurance under LAMal/KVG. Consequently, residents and workers in Switzerland do not have to comply with any new immediate obligations: any possible change will depend on future federal or cantonal legislative processes.
What are the next institutional steps planned by the Federal Council?
The recommendations formulated by the OECD, particularly with regard to improving productivity, will feed into the preparation of the growth policy report overseen by the Federal Council, which is scheduled to be presented at the end of 2027. Only from that point onward, and once the resulting government measures have been adopted, will it be possible to assess any specific bills and verify their actual impact on citizens, cantons and business activities in Switzerland.
