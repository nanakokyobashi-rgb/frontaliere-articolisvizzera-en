Junge Tat trial: Prosecutor asks for conviction, acquittal defense (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 September 2026

Six members of the far-right group 'Junge Tat' appeared before the Zurich justice on charges of discrimination and incitement to hatred.

Context

In brief

  • Trial in Zurich for six members of 'Junge Tat'
  • Charges of discrimination and incitement to hatred
  • Prosecutor seeks prison sentences for main defendants

Key facts

  • What: Trial for discrimination and incitement to hatred
  • When: Today
  • Where: Zurich
  • Who: Six members of 'Junge Tat'
  • Amount: Prison sentences requested

Today, six members of the far-right group 'Junge Tat' appeared before Zurich's justice system on charges of discrimination and incitement to hatred. During the hearing, the prosecutor requested prison sentences for the two main defendants, while the defense called for their acquittal. Outside the courthouse, about a hundred anti-fascist protesters gathered. The verdict will be pronounced only later.

This morning, the Public Prosecutor's Office requested a prison sentence of fifteen months, with six months to be served, against the two main defendants, Tobias Lingg (23) and Manuel Corchia (25). According to the prosecution, their actions exceeded the limits of freedom of expression. Both recidivists, they refused to answer the judge's questions.

In the afternoon, their defense lawyers instead asked for their clients to be acquitted of charges of discrimination, incitement to hatred, and violation of freedom of belief and worship. They invoked freedom of expression. According to Lingg's lawyer, the group's leader should only be sentenced to a conditional fine for minor offenses. For Corchia, complete acquittal was requested.

Operational details

Practical Implications

The trial in Zurich against members of 'Junge Tat' raises important questions regarding freedom of expression and discrimination in Switzerland. The prosecution requested prison sentences for the two main defendants, Tobias Lingg and Manuel Corchia, while the defense invoked their acquittal, arguing that their actions fall within freedom of expression.

The group's actions, such as interrupting a Pride Mass and disrupting a children's reading, caused material and psychological damage. The prosecution assessed these actions as discrimination based on sexual orientation and violation of freedom of worship and religion.

The case highlights the tension between freedom of expression and the need to protect the rights of minorities. The final ruling of the Zurich District Court will be crucial in establishing the limits of freedom of expression in Switzerland.

Unauthorized Rooftop Actions

The court also examined several unauthorized actions taken on the roofs of various buildings. In November 2022, some members of the group had climbed onto the roof of the main building of the Basel train station, unfurled a banner with the words' Expel criminals' and lit smoke bombs. They had acted in a similar way in April 2024 in Winterthur (ZH), where a banner dedicated to the theme of repatriation had been hung on a building.
