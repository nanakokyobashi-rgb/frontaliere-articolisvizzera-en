Scholarships in the Canton of Geneva: requirements and amounts (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 September 2026

Scholarships and study loans in the Canton of Geneva: merit and income requirements, maximum amounts, terms and competent cantonal office.

Context

At a glance

  • Grants and loans are managed at the cantonal level, based on merit and income criteria
  • Maximum amounts vary according to the level of education and family situation
  • Applications are submitted online before the start of the academic year
  • The competent office is the Service des bourses et prêts d'études of the Canton of Geneva

Key facts

  • What: Study grants and loans for students residing in the Canton of Geneva
  • When: Applications must be submitted before the start of the academic year; specific annual deadlines
  • Where: Canton of Geneva, addressed to the Service des bourses et prêts d'études
  • Who: Eligible students according to the cantonal law on study grants (LBSE)
  • Amount: Maximum amounts calculated on the basis of tuition fees, living/accommodation costs, and family situation; exact figures reviewed annually
  • Legal reference: Cantonal law on study grants and loans (Loi sur les bourses et prêts d'études, LBSE) and its implementing regulations
  • Deadline: Annual deadlines published on the official website of the Canton

Financial aid for education in Switzerland is a cantonal responsibility. Each Canton — including Geneva — defines by its own law the requirements, amounts, and procedures for obtaining study grants and loans. The Canton of Geneva regulates the matter through the LBSE (Loi sur les bourses et prêts d'études), which sets the cumulative conditions of academic merit and family income for accessing the subsidy.

Operational details

How the grant amount is calculated

The grant amount is not a fixed figure: it is determined by the difference between the recognized costs of education (enrolment fees, food, accommodation, teaching materials, transport) and the financial contribution considered reasonable from the family. The lower the parents' taxable income and assets, the higher the grant, up to the cantonal ceiling.

The Canton reassesses the maximum amounts and income thresholds every year. To avoid incorrect amounts, it is advisable to consult the website of the Service des bourses et prêts d'études each year: there you will find the updated caps per type of training, the table of deductions for dependent children and the income brackets. Large families or those with children already at university benefit from higher allowances, because the calculation takes into account the number of dependent persons.

Grant, loan or both

The LBSE distinguishes between study grants (non-repayable) and study loans (to be repaid after the end of studies, at a preferential rate and with an instalment plan). Some students receive only the grant, others only the loan, and others a combination of the two. The choice depends on the cantonal assessment of the residual financial need.

At national level, the Swiss Conference of Cantonal Directors of Public Education (CDIP) coordinates minimum standards between Cantons. This does not unify the amounts, but it ensures that no deserving and needy student is excluded solely on the grounds of domicile. For Ticinese residents and Swiss nationals moving to Geneva to study, this means that the grant follows the Canton of fiscal domicile — not the Canton of origin.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure

The procedure for applying for a scholarship in Geneva follows a precise sequence. Here is how to proceed.

1. Check the basic requirements

Before opening your application, make sure you meet the three cumulative conditions: being enrolled (or about to enrol) in a recognised post-compulsory education programme; having tax domicile in the Canton of Geneva (or effective residence for the cases provided for by the LBSE); having a family income level below the applicable cantonal thresholds.

2. Gather the documents

You will typically need: certificate of enrolment at the school or university, parents' tax return (or the student's own if independent), salary certificate, statement of any other scholarships or loans, proof of family situation (dependent children, separations, widowhood). Holders of B or C permits must also attach a copy of the permit and proof of domicile.

3. Online submission

The application is submitted via the Canton of Geneva's eBourse portal. It is possible to start the procedure before the beginning of the academic year, in order to speed up the disbursement. The ordinary deadline usually falls in the summer months (July-September) for the following academic year; the exact dates are published on the website of the Service des bourses et prêts d'études.

4. Follow-up and decision

After submission, the cantonal office reviews the file and may request additional documents. The decision is communicated by post, indicating the amount granted (scholarship, loan or both), the duration and the obligations for annual renewal. In the event of a rejection, there is a right of appeal with specific deadlines indicated in the communication.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the requirements to get a scholarship in the Canton of Geneva?
The main requirements are three: enrolment in recognised post-compulsory training, tax domicile in the Canton of Geneva and family income below the current cantonal thresholds. The assessment combines the educational merit and financial situation of the parents (or the independent student). The maximum amounts and thresholds are updated annually by the Service des bourses et prêts d 'études.
How do I calculate the amount of the scholarship in Geneva?
The amount is calculated as the difference between the recognized costs of training (taxes, food, accommodation, material, transport) and the reasonable contribution of the family. The lower the taxable household income, the higher the bag will be up to the maximum cantonal ceiling. The Canton re-evaluates the ceilings, deductions for dependent children and income brackets every year: for the current year it is necessary to consult the official website of the Service.
Can I receive both a scholarship and a student loan?
Yes. The LBSE distinguishes the non-repayable scholarship from the repayable study loan. Some students get only the scholarship, others only the loan, still others a combination of the two. The decision depends on the cantonal assessment of the residual financial need after subtracting the reasonable family contribution.
Can students with a B or C permit apply for the scholarship in Geneva?
In principle, yes, as long as the training is carried out in a recognized Swiss institution and the income and merit criteria are met. The financial aid for training is generally reserved for those who have their tax domicile in the Canton, not only for the G border permit. For specific cases it is advisable to check with the Service des bourses et prêts d 'études.
When should I apply for a stock exchange in Geneva?
Deadlines vary from year to year, but the ordinary term falls in the summer months (July-September) for the academic year beginning in the fall. The website of the Service des bourses et prêts d 'études publishes the exact deadlines every year and indicates any extraordinary windows for transfers from Canton or resumption of studies. Missing the deadline means waiting for the next academic year.
