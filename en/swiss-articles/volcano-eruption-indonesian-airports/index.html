Volcano erupts, six airports blocked in Indonesia (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 September 2026

Suspension of airport activities in Indonesia due to the eruption of the Anak Krakatoa volcano. Over 20,000 passengers involved.

Context

In brief

  • Six Indonesian airports suspend operations
  • Over 200 flights face cancellations or delays
  • Approximately 20,000 passengers affected by the disruption
  • Cause identified in the eruption of Mount Anak Krakatoa

Key facts

  • What: Airport operations suspension
  • When: Period of volcanic eruption
  • Where: Indonesia, including Jakarta
  • Who: Indonesian authorities and Transport Minister Dudy Purwagandhi
  • Amount: 20,000 passengers affected

Airport operations in Indonesia have been forced to halt due to a significant natural event. The competent authorities of the country have formally ordered the operational suspension of six airports following the recent eruption of Mount Anak Krakatoa. The phenomenon, located over 150 kilometers from the capital Jakarta, has made emergency measures necessary to ensure air traffic safety. According to indications provided by Transport Minister Dudy Purwagandhi, the measure affects the international airport of Jakarta, Soekarno-Hatta, along with three other airports in the capital area. Additionally, the international airport of Bandung and the airport of Radin, located on the island of Sumatra, have been closed. The scope of the disruption, confirmed by official sources, has already affected over 200 operational flights. Managing air traffic flows in conditions of volcanic emergency requires strict and immediate protocols. For Swiss travelers transiting through that region, the repercussions on global mobility are significant. The need to constantly monitor airline communications becomes essential for anyone who needs to return to Switzerland or continue to international destinations departing from Indonesia. The numerical impact, with over 20,000 passengers stranded or forced to revise their travel plans, highlights the vulnerability of air routes in the face of geological surprises. It is advisable for Swiss citizens in transit to check with their travel agencies or carriers the possibility of rebookings or refunds, in accordance with contractual policies and current regulations in the air transport sector, which often provide specific protections for passengers in case of flight cancellations due to force majeure.

Operational details

Implications for international mobility and delay management

The suspension of activities at six Indonesian airports raises practical issues for Swiss citizens and residents traveling for professional or tourist purposes in Asia. When a natural event suddenly disrupts air traffic, the management of the situation involves a series of standardized procedures by international airlines. For a passenger, the first step after the official communication of the closure of the Jakarta or Bandung airport is to check the status of their flight through the official channels of the airline. In emergency situations, uncertainty about returning to Switzerland can generate complications related to additional stay costs or managing work absences. It is essential to remember that, in case of prolonged delays or cancellations due to volcanic eruptions, passenger rights are regulated by transport contracts. Often, such events are classified as exceptional circumstances that may exclude the standard obligation to pay compensation, while maintaining the obligation to provide assistance, such as meals and hotel accommodation. A critical aspect concerns insurance coverage. Many travel policies taken out in Switzerland include specific clauses for the cancellation or interruption of travel caused by natural disasters. If the passenger has taken out comprehensive coverage before departure, it is necessary to contact the insurance operations center immediately to activate the procedures for reimbursement of additional expenses incurred. For those working in Switzerland, the management of a delayed return must be communicated promptly to the employer, documenting the inability to reach the workplace due to the airport closure. Although there are no specific legal instruments that require the employer to pay for days of forced absence due to force majeure, many Swiss companies apply flexibility or allow time recovery. It is advisable to keep all documentation attesting to the flight cancellation, as it will be essential to justify the absence or request refunds from the airlines, following the complaint procedures provided for in the general terms of the contract.

Key points

Operational procedures for return and passenger protection

To address the ongoing airport blockade in Indonesia and plan a safe return, each traveler residing in Switzerland should adopt a methodical approach. First, it is advisable to monitor the directives of the Federal Department of Foreign Affairs (FDFA) regarding safety in areas affected by the eruption of Anak Krakatoa. Constant communication with Swiss embassies or consulates on-site can provide support in case of urgent needs. Regarding financial aspects related to travel and possible loss of workdays, it is recommended to consult your employment contract to verify provisions regarding forced leave or unpaid leave. Those managing their personal accounting or needing to handle the family budget following unexpected expenses for the return can refer to online income and expense monitoring tools. In crisis situations, liquidity management becomes crucial: checking the availability of funds in Swiss bank accounts, accessible via internet banking, allows for handling unexpected costs such as hotel accommodation or the purchase of new airline tickets. For those wishing to assess the overall economic impact of a period of absence or extraordinary expenses on their annual income, the use of calculation tools is always a useful support to maintain control of their personal financial situation. It is also important to remember that any expense incurred for accommodation and meals during the wait for the reopening of airports must be documented through regular invoices. Such documents are not only useful for possible insurance reimbursements but are also necessary for the correct keeping of personal accounting records in view of the future income tax return, should the expenses be deductible according to the current cantonal regulations.
