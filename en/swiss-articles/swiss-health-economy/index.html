The Swiss healthcare sector is in the spotlight for its challenges and innovations. As communicated in June 2025, Swiss multinationals are facing problems related to the strong franc, rising commodity prices and stagnant demand in China. The CEO of Kägi stressed that the biggest problem is exports, which are suffering from these factors.

Research and innovation

Researchers in Geneva are reusing existing drugs to treat rare diseases, offering new hope where options are scarce. This research is critical to improving the quality of life of rare disease patients, who often do not have access to appropriate treatments.

Healthcare costs

Health care costs in Switzerland are rising faster than the population can afford them. Thirty years after the introduction of compulsory health insurance, an economic sustainability crisis is forcing Switzerland to make difficult decisions to guarantee its future. Practical implications

The current challenges in the Swiss healthcare sector have significant practical implications for citizens. Rising healthcare costs could lead to higher insurance premiums, putting a strain on Swiss families. In addition, research and innovation in the pharmaceutical sector could lead to new, more effective and less expensive treatments, improving patients' quality of life.

Technological innovations

The use of artificial intelligence in the search for new drugs could revolutionize the pharmaceutical industry. This technology could reduce the time and cost of clinical trials, leading to new treatments more quickly and at a lower cost to patients. Additionally, precision psychiatry could offer new hope for patients suffering from mental disorders by improving the accuracy of treatments and reducing side effects.

Psychedelic Treatments

The new US push towards psychedelic treatments could have a positive impact on Switzerland. This could lead to new opportunities for Swiss patients, offering innovative and more effective treatments for mental disorders.

Identification of hantavirus

The identification of the hantavirus strain by the University Hospital of Geneva is crucial to prevent future outbreaks and improve the response to epidemics. This is an important step for Swiss public health, which requires a rapid and effective response to epidemics.

Drug prices

Addressing the problem of patent misuse by pharmaceutical companies could help reduce drug prices. This is a crucial issue for Swiss patients, who often face high costs for treatments.

Key points

Concrete actions

To address the current challenges in the Swiss healthcare sector, concrete actions are necessary. Swiss authorities must work to reduce healthcare costs and ensure access to treatments for all citizens. Additionally, it is essential to invest in research and innovation to develop more effective and less costly new treatments.

Step-by-step procedure

1. Identify areas for improvement: Swiss authorities must identify areas where the healthcare sector needs improvement, such as healthcare costs and access to treatments. 2. Invest in research and innovation: It is essential to invest in research and innovation to develop more effective and less costly new treatments. 3. Address drug pricing issues: Swiss authorities must work to address the issue of pharmaceutical companies' misuse of patents to reduce drug prices. 4. Ensure availability of medicines: Switzerland must be prepared to respond to any drug shortages, ensuring the availability of medicines for its citizens. 5. Promote the right to be forgotten: The campaign for the right to be forgotten for cancer survivors is an important step to ensure that patients are not discriminated against by insurance companies.
