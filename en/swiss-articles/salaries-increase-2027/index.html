Context

Swiss companies expect wage increases by 2027. According to a KOF survey, wage negotiations are expected to lead to an average 1.2% increase in wages next year. Inflation is expected to be at 0.5% next year, which means that the real increase in wages is expected to be at 0.7%.

This increase could translate into an increase of 150-200 Franciscans per month for a worker with an average salary of 4,500 Franciscans. For a worker with a salary of 6,000 francs, the increase could be 360-400 francs per month.

The KOF conducted a survey of 250 Swiss companies to determine the salary increases planned for 2027. The results show that 60% of companies expect a 1-2% increase in wages, 20% expect a 0.5-1% increase, and 20% expect a more than 2% increase.

The expected wage increase may vary by sector and region. For example, companies in the finance and insurance sector could expect a higher wage increase than companies in the manufacturing sector.

The KOF also highlighted that inflation could be an important factor in determining wage increases. Current inflation is around 0.5%, but could increase during 2027 due to economic growth.

Key points

Wage increases are expected to be a major factor for the Swiss in 2027. It is important to note that inflation should remain low, which means that the real increase in wages should be 0.7%. Real wage growth was only 0.2% per year between 2014 and 2023.

According to data from the Swiss Confederation, the average annual income for a worker in Switzerland is currently around CHF 67,000. With a real wage increase of 0.7%, this would mean an average annual increase of about CHF 469 per worker.

But what does this increase mean for workers in Switzerland? Here are some concrete examples:

  • A worker with an annual income of CHF 50,000 could see an increase of about CHF 350 per year.
  • A worker with an annual income of CHF 80,000 could see an increase of about CHF 560 per year.
  • A worker with an annual income of CHF 100,000 could see an increase of about CHF 700 per year.
