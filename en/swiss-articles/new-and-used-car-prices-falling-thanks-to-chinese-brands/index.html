In the third quarter, in Switzerland, the average asking prices for new cars advertised on AutoScout24 fell by 2,6% compared with the previous quarter; year on year, the decline was 5,8%. The figure is reported in a market analysis published today by the portal, described as a leading player in the Confederation for vehicle sales. The news was reported by Keystone-ATS.

The decline also affected used cars: average prices were 0,8% lower than in the second quarter and 4,3% lower in the year-on-year comparison indicated by the analysis. The trend therefore concerns both main categories observed, with varying intensity.

Categories with the largest changes

Among new cars, petrol vehicles fell by 4,4% compared with the previous quarter and by 4,9% compared with the previous year. The most substantial reduction concerned new plug-in hybrids: their cost fell by 16% year on year.

The picture for used electric cars is different over the short term. Prices rose by 1,5% compared with the April-June period, but the year-on-year comparison remains negative, with a decline of 1,4%. The source thus points to a difference between quarterly and annual trends, with no uniform development across powertrains.

As for supply, vehicle listings on AutoScout24 increased by 0,7% compared with the previous quarter and by 2,6% over a year. For used cars, the increase was 0,9% compared with the previous quarter and 2,5% year on year; listings for new cars, on the other hand, recorded a change of -0,8% in the quarterly comparison and +3,9% in the annual comparison.

The decline in prices is part of a trend that has already been observed for some time. In spring, AutoScout24, a platform belonging to Swiss Marketplace Group, or SMG, had reported the growing presence of Chinese manufacturers in Switzerland. Their offering combines lower costs, technological advances and a strong pace of innovation. According to the source, competition from Chinese brands is among the factors that could contribute to the decline in prices.

AutoScout24's analysis places these elements within the same framework, but does not use them to describe the same dimension of the market.

Price is not the only indicator

The quarterly and annual comparisons serve to show two distinct movements. The first highlights the most recent change, because it uses the previous three months as its reference; the second shows the trend compared with the annual period used by the analysis. For anyone examining a listing in Switzerland, this distinction prevents a short-term change from being treated as if it alone described the entire trend.

The number of listings also requires a separate reading. The total supply advertised on the platform has grown, while among new cars the quarterly figure fell and the annual figure rose. For used cars, on the other hand, both comparisons of listings indicate an increase. The resulting picture is complex: falling average prices and changing numbers of advertised vehicles are not the same data point.
