Volkswagen announces 100,000 cuts and closure of 4 factories (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 June 2026

The Volkswagen Group plans to cut 100,000 jobs and close four factories in Germany by 2030.

Context

In short - Volkswagen plans 100,000 redundancies by 2030 - Closure of four factories in Germany - 11 billion euro plan for reorganization ## Key facts - What : plan for cuts and reorganization - When: plan by 2030, proposed in July - Where: Germany (Hannover, Zwickau, Emden, Neckarsulm) - Who: Volkswagen, Audi, trade unions - Amount: 11 billion euro Volkswagen is preparing for a profound reorganization that envisages the elimination of 100,000 jobs by 2030. The news, anticipated by the magazine Manager Magazin, reveals how the CEO Oliver Blume has submitted to the management board a proposal that doubles the plan for cuts agreed with the unions, going from 50,000 to 100,000 redundancies out of a total of 657,000 employees. The plan also includes the closure of four production sites in Germany: three Volkswagen plants in Hannover, Zwickau and Emden, and one Audi plant in Neckarsulm. The aim is to streamline the structure of the group, also through the spin-off of the production of components and spare parts. This reorganisation is part of an €11 billion package of planned costs. The unions, however, have already announced a battle and have said they are ready to prevent the implementation of the plan, asking management to guarantee competitive products, innovative technologies and job safety. Practical Implications for Switzerland The reorganization of Volkswagen, with the planned elimination of 100,000 jobs and the closure of four factories in Germany, will have indirect but significant repercussions in Switzerland as well. The Volkswagen Group, one of the largest European car manufacturers, has economic and productive ties with Swiss companies and suppliers, influencing the labour market and supply chain in our country. The loss of jobs in Germany may translate into a reduction in demand for components and services provided by Swiss companies, with potential effects on the manufacturing sector and employment, especially in industrialised cantons such as Zurich, Basel and Vaud. In addition, restructuring could accelerate the need to adapt workers' professional skills, with implications for vocational training and the Swiss labour market. ### Comparison with the previous situation Before this proposal, Volkswagen had already planned cuts for 50,000 jobs by 2030. The increase to 100,000 redundancies represents an acceleration of restructuring, which could also affect investment and innovation strategies in Switzerland, especially in the automotive and sustainable mobility sectors.
