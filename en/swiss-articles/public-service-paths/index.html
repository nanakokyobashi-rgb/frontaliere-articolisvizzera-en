Working in the Federal Administration: opportunities and training (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 29 September 2026

The Federal Administration offers 50 vocational training programs, internships for recent graduates, and seven weeks of vacation.

Context

In brief

  • Meaningful work and responsibility in the Federal Administration
  • Internships for recent graduates
  • 50 vocational training programs available
  • Seven weeks of vacation during vocational training

Key facts

  • Internship recipients → recent graduates
  • Vocational training programs → 50
  • Vacation during vocational training → 7 weeks
  • Employer offering → flexible work, performance-based remuneration and continuing education

The Federal Administration offers recent graduates a wide range of internships. The offer is presented as a way to gain valuable practical experience, expand one's network of contacts and use one's knowledge to help shape Switzerland's future. The message thus combines concrete experience and contribution to the community.

Work at the Federal Administration is described as meaningful. Those who work there can take on responsibility and work for the well-being of the population in Switzerland. The page also indicates the opportunity to develop innovative solutions within multidisciplinary teams. The stated aim is to make a concrete contribution to the high quality of life in the country.

Three options presented by the page

Alongside internships, the text presents the offer for those seeking vocational training that is meaningful, fosters growth and opens up new opportunities. There are 50 programs available. The practical side is described precisely: those who follow these paths contribute to real tasks and projects, learn from the trainers who support them and develop skills for their future.

Leisure time also appears among the elements of the offer. The seven weeks of vacation are indicated as time to devote to relaxation, hobbies, travel and friends. This figure accompanies the description of vocational training and completes the picture together with practical learning.

The picture is completed by what the Federal Administration declares it offers as an employer: flexible forms of work, performance-based remuneration, continuing education and other offers. These are the elements with which the page describes the Federal Administration as an interesting employer.

For those reading the offer, the distinction is clear: the internship is linked to practical experience and networking; vocational training to tasks, projects and skills; work in the Administration to responsibility, innovative solutions and multidisciplinary collaboration. Anyone who wants to compare this offer with other positions can start from annunci di lavoro.

Operational details

From the offer to the choice

Those considering these opportunities must distinguish the type of experience they want to build. The page does not present a single format: it places internships for students who have recently graduated alongside the 50 vocational training programs and the conditions offered by the Federal Administration as an employer. The practical approach consists in linking each element to the personal objective, without confusing hands-on experience, training and employment conditions.

For a student who has recently graduated, the point of comparison is the experience that the internship makes it possible to acquire in the field. The network of contacts and knowledge complete this pathway: they are not presented as separate elements, but as part of the contribution through which to build Switzerland's future.

For those seeking vocational training, the criterion is more closely linked to doing. Collaborating on real tasks and projects makes it possible to view the offer through the activities envisaged; the support provided by trainers, on the other hand, signals the accompanying component. Future skills and the seven weeks of vacation add, in the same description, a dimension of growth and personal time.

A third dimension concerns those who consider the Federal Administration as an employer. Here the page draws attention to forms of flexible work, performance-based remuneration, continuing education and other offers. These are criteria different from hands-on experience or choosing a training program, but they contribute to the overall description of the employment.

The comparison with other employers should be made while keeping these dimensions distinct: the content of the experience, learning methods and conditions offered. To broaden the search, one can consult aziende che assumono, without losing the concrete references indicated by the page.

Key points

How to navigate the opportunities

Four practical steps

The first step consists of defining which element one wants to look for. The page brings together meaningful work, responsibility, contribution to the well-being of the population and the opportunity to develop innovative solutions in multidisciplinary teams. If these are the main points of reference, the reader can focus on the description of work in the Federal Administration.

The second step is to check whether one's goal is related to an internship. For recent graduates, the option to consider is a wide range of internships, with three stated outcomes: hands-on experience, a network of contacts and the use of one's knowledge to help build Switzerland's future.

The third step concerns vocational training. Here, it is advisable to start from the 50 possibilities available and look for the path that combines real tasks and projects, support from trainers and the development of skills for the future. The seven weeks of vacation are part of the same assessment: the page links them to time for relaxation, hobbies, travel and friends.

The fourth step is to read the offer from the employer's point of view. Flexible forms of work, performance-based remuneration, continuing education and other offers are the elements to consider together when assessing the overall offering.

This method avoids conflating different paths. First, one identifies the area of interest, then compares the elements explicitly described and finally chooses the direction most consistent with what one is seeking: experience, training or working conditions. It also pairs the training with seven weeks of vacation dedicated to relaxation, hobbies, travel, and friends.
What do internships offer to recent graduates?
The Federal Administration offers recent graduates a wide range of internships. The page lists three outcomes: gaining valuable hands-on experience, expanding one's network of contacts, and contributing, with one's knowledge, to building Switzerland's tomorrow. The internship is therefore presented as a concrete experience linked to one's professional future.

As an employer, the Federal Administration offers flexible forms of work, performance-based remuneration, continuing education and other benefits. The description therefore includes the way of working and opportunities for further training, as well as the type of activity. These are the elements presented to describe the employer's attractiveness.
