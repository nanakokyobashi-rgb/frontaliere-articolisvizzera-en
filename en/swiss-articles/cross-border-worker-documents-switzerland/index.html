Updated checklist of documents for border workers: contract, identity, G permit, bank account, AVS, health insurance.

To begin working in Switzerland as a cross-border worker, it is essential to provide a series of mandatory documents. The checklist includes the Swiss employment contract, which forms the basis of the professional relationship. Upon hiring, a valid identification document, such as an identity card or passport, is required for all administrative procedures and access to public and private offices.

The G permit is another essential element when applicable. This specific permit for cross-border workers must be requested from the relevant cantonal or federal authorities. Obtaining the G permit is an indispensable step for those residing in Italy and working in Switzerland, as regular employment is not possible without this authorization.

Among the data requested by the employer, bank account details may also be required for salary payments. Opening a bank account (Swiss or foreign, according to company policies) may be requested during the hiring process, depending on the individual company's policies.

Another important document is the AVS number, essential for insurance and contribution procedures. Registration with AVS ensures pension coverage, which is fundamental in Switzerland.

Finally, health insurance is mandatory for all workers in Switzerland, including cross-border workers. Employers often require proof of insurance coverage at the time of hiring.

Operational details

The management of documents to work in Switzerland as a cross-border worker has practical implications in all Cantons, from Ticino to Zurich. The application for the G permit, for example, represents a mandatory procedure for those who maintain residency in Italy and return daily. Each Canton applies uniform administrative controls, but the timing of permit issuance and the presentation methods may vary.

On the social security front, the communication of the AHV number is essential to access insurance and pension services, regulated by the Swiss system. The correct attribution of the AHV number ensures the payment of contributions and future LPP/BVG coverage, according to the rules in force. Those who do not timely present this information risk delays in the activation of benefits and the crediting of services.

The presentation of bank details, required by the employer, allows for the regular receipt of salary. In some cases, companies may prefer Swiss accounts to simplify payment procedures, but the source does not specify obligations in this regard. The absence of a communicated account may lead to delays in the first payments.

Health insurance and coverage

Health insurance is mandatory for workers in Switzerland, including those residing in Italy and working across the border. The employer may request proof of enrollment in Swiss health coverage (LAMal/KVG) or, where applicable, evidence of coverage from the Italian health system. Failure to present the documentation may jeopardize the signing of the contract and access to health services.

Key points

To work regularly in Switzerland as a cross-border commuter, it is necessary to follow a precise procedure for collecting and presenting the required documents. Here is a useful step-by-step guide for every Canton:

1. Obtain and sign the employment contract with the Swiss employer. 2. Prepare a valid identity document (identity card or passport). 3. Apply for the G permit (when required) at the competent cantonal authority, attaching the contract and the identity document. 4. Communicate the bank details requested by the employer for the salary credit. 5. Provide your AHV number, or request it if you do not have it yet, through the designated compensation office. 6. Present valid health insurance certification.

Timing and checks

The processing times for the G permit and AHV activation may vary, but the lack of any of these documents can block hiring. It is advisable to start collecting documents at least a few weeks before the expected start date of work. Employers carefully check the completeness of the documents, in line with federal and cantonal regulations.

Regular access to the AHV system and LAMal/KVG coverage ensures full social and health protection, which are central elements of the Swiss labor market. Timely submission of documents also allows for proper management of taxation, payslips, and payroll credit procedures.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the mandatory documents to start working in Switzerland as a border worker?
According to the updated checklist, the mandatory documents are: Swiss employment contract, valid identity document, G permit when required, bank details requested by the employer, AVS number and health insurance certification.
Is the G permit always required for border crossers?
The G permit is required for those who reside in Italy and work in Switzerland as a border worker, but the need depends on the individual situation and the indications of the cantonal authorities. It should be presented at the beginning of the employment relationship.
When should bank details and AVS number be disclosed?
The bank details and the AVS number must be communicated to the employer already at the time of hiring, to ensure the correct crediting of the salary and the activation of social security coverage.
