The Finance Committee of the National Council, meeting in Bern, supported by a large majority the commitment appropriations related to the so-called Bilaterals III, which total approximately 3,48 billion francs for the period 2030-2036. The vote recorded 14 in favor, 8 against and 3 abstentions.

With this vote, the committee reaffirms the course taken by the Federal Council and the Council of States. According to the majority, from a financial standpoint, the agreements with Brussels offer benefits that far outweigh the costs. The package includes the cohesion contribution, a commitment credit earmarked for migration and an additional one-off financial contribution for the cohesion sector.

The next parliamentary step

The Finance Committee has set out its position in a supplementary report addressed to the Foreign Affairs Committee of the National Council, known by the acronym APK-N. The latter will examine the dossier for the first time the following week. The Parliamentary Services made the position public on Friday.

This step does not bring the debate to a close. The package is in fact described as complex and controversial, while the Finance Committee has already identified issues that require further review.

The Erasmus+ chapter

The credit for the European exchange and training program Erasmus+ received even broader support. The committee majority considers Switzerland’s participation essential to prevent the country’s isolation in the fields of education and research.

The outstanding reviews

The Finance Committee approved 13 requests for review and tasked the administration with drafting additional reports for the meeting scheduled for mid-November. The National Council is expected to examine the package of agreements with the EU during the December winter session.

The same source indicates that questions remain open, that the administration must produce further reports and that the National Council is expected to discuss the package only during the December winter session.
