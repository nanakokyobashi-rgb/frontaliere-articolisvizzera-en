Operational details

The timely identification of the hantavirus strain in Geneva in 2026 represents a concrete example of the effectiveness of Swiss health surveillance at the national level. This success demonstrates how collaboration between university hospitals and federal authorities can help contain epidemics and protect public health, a central theme for the Cantons and the Federal Council.

Implications for patient safety and the national health system

The surge in the use of health monitoring devices and the high number of MRI scanners raise questions of sustainability and appropriateness of care in Switzerland. The high number of devices does not always translate into more effective diagnoses or a better quality of life for patients, but it can increase costs for the health system, with repercussions on the cost of living and sick pay premiums (LAMal/KVG). From a fiscal point of view, an inefficient use of health resources can also affect direct federal tax and cantonal finances, which partly bear the costs of public health. Therefore, a balance between technological innovation, patient safety and economic sustainability is necessary, an issue that directly involves the SECO and the UST/BFS in the definition of health policies.
