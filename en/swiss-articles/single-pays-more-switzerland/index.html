Operational details

The absence of a partner also affects the portfolio. There is no precise figure that quantifies how much a single person spends more than a couple. However, the OECD equivalence scale offers a useful indication: to maintain the same standard of living, a couple needs resources equal to one and a half times those of a single person, not twice as much. Living together, therefore, allows you to split many fixed expenses and benefit from significant economies of scale.

A concrete example can be found in the city of Bern, where a single person living alone in an apartment of 4.5 square meters can expect a monthly rent of about 1,500 Swiss francs. If, on the other hand, you decide to share the apartment with a partner, the rent could fall to around 1,200 Swiss francs, a saving of 300 Swiss francs per month. 💡

But it's not just about the rent. Some fixed expenses, such as water and electricity, can be reduced by a third or even by half when sharing a home. For example, a single person using 300 kWh of electricity per month could pay around 120 Swiss francs per month, while a couple using the same amount of energy could pay around 80 Swiss francs per month.
