The bill for the summer of 2026

BERN - The scorching summer of 2026 caused 400 million francs in economic damages in Switzerland. This is the central figure from the Allianz Trade study, which quantifies the loss at approximately 0.05 percent of the gross domestic product.

Intense heat also impacted labor productivity. During the summer, longer breaks were necessary and sick leaves were recorded. According to the study, the economic effect of the heatwave therefore concerns both the estimated overall loss and daily work operations.

Allianz Trade, together with Allianz, analyzes the effects of extreme weather events on the economy. The results will henceforth be published annually in the Allianz Climate Risk Tracker study. The new series will therefore have an annual frequency.

Jan Möllmann, CEO of Allianz Trade in Switzerland, stated that climate change is becoming an economic factor.

Following the scorching summer, according to the study, the next climate phenomenon is already on the horizon, with a very strong El Niño. In 2027, this phenomenon could cost the global economy around 451 billion US dollars.

The estimate specifically concerns China, the United States, and India, which together would bear nearly 60 percent of the losses. For Europe, including Switzerland, the study forecasts a net loss of about 21 billion euros.

This comparison defines the scope of the news: damages already quantified for the summer of 2026 in Switzerland and a forecast for 2027 linked to El Niño on a global and European scale. For the reader, this data can be compared alongside the cost of living in Switzerland, keeping the two aspects distinct.

Operational details

From the Swiss account to prevention

The figure for 2026 must be read on two floors. On the one hand, there is an already quantified cost to the Swiss economy; on the other, there is a forecast linked to a climate phenomenon that, in 2027, is measured on an international scale. The difference avoids putting a national loss and a world estimate on the same plane.

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the closest channel is work. The source does not only describe a final economic damage: it cites longer breaks and absences due to illness as effects of heat on productivity. The working day thus becomes the point where the weather phenomenon enters the economic reading.

The reference to gross domestic product, equal to about 0.05 percent, offers a second reading key next to the absolute amount. The two measures make it possible to distinguish the sum of the damages from their relative weight on the economy. Also for this reason, the productivity mentioned must be kept separate from the individual readings of the busta paga svizzera: the report talks about breaks and absences as signs of the impact of heat.

The transition to 2027

With a very strong El Niño, the scale of the analysis changes. Allianz Trade estimates a cost of around $451 billion to the world economy in 2027. China, the United States and India together would bear almost 60 percent of the losses, while for Europe, including Switzerland, the expected net loss is about 21 billion euros.

Key points

From reading to action

The Allianz study proposes a precise starting point: adaptation and prevention must strengthen economic resilience. To turn this conclusion into a concrete check, it is useful to separate the data already observed in Switzerland from the estimates for 2027 and follow the elements indicated in the report.

A concrete sequence

1. Separate factual data from forecasts. The balance sheet for the summer of 2026 concerns Switzerland and quantifies economic damages at 400 million francs. The reference to 2027 instead concerns a possible global cost linked to El Niño. Keeping the year, area and phenomenon separate avoids improper comparisons.

2. Observe the labor channel. The source points to longer breaks and sick leaves as effects of heat on productivity. These are therefore the two signals to keep in mind when assessing how extreme weather enters the workday.

3. Verify the prevention structure. The report indicates three conditions associated with better preparedness: consolidated early warning systems, risk maps and clearly defined competencies. Verification must remain anchored to these elements, without adding measures not mentioned by the study.

4. Update the framework every year. Allianz Trade will from now on publish the results in the Allianz Climate Risk Tracker study. The annual frequency makes it possible to follow new data while maintaining the 2026 balance sheet and the 2027 forecast as references.

This sequence also helps to communicate risk correctly. The 400 million francs concern damages in Switzerland; the 451 billion dollars concern the global economy; the approximately 21 billion euros concern Europe, including Switzerland. Area, year and currency are part of the data and must not be separated.
