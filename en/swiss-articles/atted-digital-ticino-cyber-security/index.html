Ated asks for permanent bodies for digital in Ticino (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 17 September 2026

The Ated association asks the State Chancellery to establish an Advisory Group and a cantonal Commission for Cyber Security.

Context

In brief

  • Ated calls for permanent digital bodies in Ticino
  • A cantonal Cyber Security Commission has been proposed
  • Objective: unite institutional, academic, and private expertise

Key facts

  • Association: Ated
  • Professionals represented: 7,000
  • Request recipients: State Chancellery and Norman Gobbi
  • Reference model: Digital Switzerland Advisory Committee

The Ticino umbrella association Ated, which brings together around 7,000 professionals active in the Tech, ICT, and Digital sectors, has submitted an official request to the State Chancellery and State Councillor Norman Gobbi. The initiative aims to establish two permanent bodies: an Advisory Group for the Canton's digital transformation and a Cantonal Cyber Security Commission. The request was presented during a meeting at Castelgrande with a delegation from the Grand Council, with the intent of bringing the debate directly into parliamentary venues. According to Ated President Luca Mauriello, the current management of digital affairs, characterized by only sporadic discussions, is no longer adequate due to the extreme speed at which technology evolves.

Governance for digital transformation

The model proposed for the advisory group is directly inspired by the Digital Switzerland Advisory Committee, active within the Federal Chancellery. The idea is to create a regular discussion platform that brings together various components of the region: the Canton, municipalities, the economic world, the ICT sector, the USI and SUPSI universities, educational institutions, and civil society. The function of this body would be advisory and proactive, allowing digital strategies to be harmonized transversally. At the same time, the Cyber Security Commission, inspired by the work of the Federal Office for Cybersecurity, would focus on networking authorities, critical infrastructure, and businesses, concentrating in particular on prevention and the capacity for a coordinated response against cyberattacks, an issue of growing urgency for the security of the entire Swiss economic and institutional system.

Operational details

Ated's initiative raises significant issues regarding the institutional governance of digitalization at the cantonal level. The invitation launched by President Mauriello to the members of the Ticino Parliament is to overcome party affiliations, arguing that the digital strategy must be understood as a unified vision for Ticino, devoid of right, center, or left connotations. The request for institutional stability clashes with political cycles, given that standing committees are usually established by the end of the election year. However, the association urges not to wait until 2027, reiterating that technology does not follow the cycles of legislatures. This divergence between the timescales of politics and those of technological innovation is a challenge common to many Swiss cantons, where the need for long-term planning must be reconciled with democratic decision-making processes.

Skills distributed across the territory

A central point of the proposal is the enhancement of the skills already present in the Canton. Ated emphasizes that there is no need to create new oversized bodies or recruit external figures, as the know-how is already widely distributed among institutions, local businesses, universities, and industry professionals. The stated goal is therefore not to claim positions within such bodies, but to foster institutional openness that allows existing skills to be systematically brought to the same table. This resource integration strategy is essential to avoid the dispersion of energy and to ensure that decisions made in the digital sphere are supported by solid technical analysis. For those operating in the economic or administrative sector, the establishment of such bodies would represent a reliable reference point for consultation, facilitating dialogue between the public and private sectors. Key points

Ated's proposal for a new digital advisory structure in Ticino opens up operational scenarios that could directly affect those working in the fields of new technologies and public administration. Although the outcome of the request is still being defined, the debate is part of a national context where the digitization of bureaucratic processes is a priority. For industry professionals, the creation of a permanent table would represent an opportunity to actively contribute to the drafting of cantonal guidelines, ensuring greater regulatory and operational consistency across various institutional levels. Those wishing to monitor the evolution of this proposal will need to observe the upcoming parliamentary steps scheduled at Castelgrande, where the competent committees will evaluate the technical and political feasibility of the initiative presented by the association.

Recommended steps for industry professionals

For ICT workers and companies intending to stay updated on ongoing transformations in the Ticino and Swiss market, it is essential to act with awareness. First, it is advisable to monitor the official publications of the State Chancellery to verify the possible launch of public consultations or technical tables linked to digital transformation. Second, participating in trade association meetings allows professionals to make their voices heard and bring technical demands directly to the attention of policy makers. The networking between authorities and businesses, advocated by Ated, could also generate new collaboration opportunities in the field of Cyber Security, a sector that requires increasingly high standards of competence and professional updating. It is important to remember that, regardless of new advisory bodies, compliance with current regulations on data and security remains a primary obligation for every economic operator. For those who need to better manage their employment status while awaiting these systemic changes, it is recommended to regularly consult the resources available for managing personal finances, using tools such as the salary calculator to evaluate their economic situation in the Swiss national context.
