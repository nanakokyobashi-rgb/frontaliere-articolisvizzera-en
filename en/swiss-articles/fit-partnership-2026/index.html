Rules for Trade: Common Commitment

Switzerland confirmed its commitment to promoting the rules for fair and sustainable trade. The second ministerial meeting of the FIT Partnership brought together representatives from 19 States to discuss common strategies to foster international trade and protect consumer rights.

During the meeting, Switzerland stressed the importance of effective regulation to prevent counterfeiting and protect trademarks. State Secretary for the Economy Helene Budliger Artieda said: "Switzerland is committed to promoting fair and sustainable trade rules, which foster economic growth and the protection of consumer rights."

Operational details

Rules for Trade: Common Commitment

Switzerland has supported economic security and supply chain resilience through the FIT Partnership. The ministerial declaration on economic security is intended to strengthen the resilience and security of supply chains for critical goods and sectors.

This commitment is essential to ensure the economic stability of the country, especially in a context of growing global uncertainty. Switzerland is an important economic hub, with a network of supply chains involving different regions and sectors.

According to data from the Federal Office for Economic Affairs, AO, in 2022, 70% of Swiss imports came from European countries, with Germany, Italy and France among the main suppliers. 📊

To address the challenges of economic security, Switzerland has implemented several measures, including:

  • The Supply Chain Act (LCAP), passed in 2019, which requires suppliers to provide information about their supply chain and security.
  • The creation of the FIT Partnership, an organization that focuses on strengthening the resilience and security of supply chains.
  • The definition of an action plan for economic security, which provides for the creation of an alarm system for supply chains and the definition of response protocols for emergencies.

Rules of the trade: common commitment

The FIT Partnership represents an important step forward for small and medium-sized economies, which are looking to integrate more deeply into global value chains. Switzerland, along with New Zealand, Singapore and the UAE, initiated this partnership to promote cooperation and collaboration between the parties involved.

Secretary of State Helene Budliger Artieda held a meeting with New Zealand Trade Minister Todd McClay to discuss bilateral trade relations between the two countries. This meeting represents an important step forward in strengthening trade relations between Switzerland and New Zealand.

To learn more about the FIT Partnership, you can visit the official SECO website, which provides detailed information about the partnership and its objectives.
