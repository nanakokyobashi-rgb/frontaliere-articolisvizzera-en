In 2026, Quellensteuer (withholding tax) rates will be applied in major Swiss cantons, including Ticino, Grisons, Valais, and Bern. This regime applies to both cross-border commuters and residents working in Switzerland who do not submit an ordinary tax declaration unless they exceed the NOV threshold set at 120,000 CHF.

The calculation of Quellensteuer is based on cantonal rates, which may vary from region to region. The NOV threshold represents the annual income limit beyond which the worker is required to submit an ordinary tax declaration, transitioning from the withholding tax regime to the ordinary one. In the mentioned cantons, this threshold is set at 120,000 CHF.

Deductions and allowances provided by current regulations are directly applied to the Quellensteuer calculation. The rate structure takes into account various factors, such as income, family situation, and other specific cantonal allowances. For instance, Ticino applies its own differentiated rates compared to Grisons, Valais, and Bern.

The management of Quellensteuer is entrusted to the respective cantons, which determine the rates and calculation methods. The withholding tax system aims to simplify tax collection for workers who are not obligated to file ordinary declarations.

Operational details

Practical implications and comparison between cantons

The withholding tax, regulated through the Quellensteuer, is applied differently across Swiss cantons. In 2026, the NOV threshold set at 120,000 CHF becomes a crucial element for all workers subject to this regime, both cross-border commuters and residents.

Those who exceed the 120,000 CHF limit are required to opt for the ordinary tax declaration, while below this threshold the withholding tax regime remains in effect, with rates and allowances applied directly to the salary. This difference has practical implications for the amount of tax due and the possibility of benefiting from specific deductions.

Quellensteuer rates are defined by the cantons and take into account variables such as income, family composition, and possible allowances. For example, Ticino applies rates that differ from those in Graubünden, Valais, and Bern, resulting in differences in the final tax calculation.

The application of the NOV threshold of 120,000 CHF allows taxpayers to assess whether it is more advantageous to remain in the withholding tax regime or switch to the ordinary declaration. The choice can affect the possibility of obtaining deductions and tax allowances.

The comparison between cantons highlights that rates and allowances are variable. In Graubünden, Valais, and Bern, the structure of the rates may differ substantially from Ticino. For this reason, it is recommended to consult the official tables of cantonal rates.

Step-by-step procedure and useful tools

For workers subject to Quellensteuer in 2026, the operational procedure consists of several fundamental steps:

1. NOV threshold check: verify whether your annual income exceeds 120,000 CHF. 2. Consultation of cantonal rates: request information from the tax office of Ticino, Grisons, Valais, or Bern. 3. Application of allowances: check the allowances and deductions provided by the canton. 4. Tax calculation: use the salary calculator to simulate the tax withholding. 5. Declaration management: if you exceed the NOV threshold, prepare the documentation for the ordinary tax declaration.

The NOV threshold of 120,000 CHF is the main parameter for determining the applicable tax regime. Below this threshold, Quellensteuer is withheld directly by the employer, while above the limit the ordinary declaration is required.

It is possible to compare the rates applied in different cantons by consulting the official tables and simulating the tax calculation with online tools. The management of allowances and deductions varies according to the current cantonal regulations.

For cross-border workers and residents, it is essential to monitor your income and evaluate the benefits between withholding tax and ordinary declaration. In case of doubts about the procedures, it is recommended to contact the cantonal tax offices.

Frequently Asked Questions
How are Quellensteuer rates applied in the different cantons?
Quellensteuer rates vary between Ticino, Graubünden, Valais and Bern. Each canton applies its own rates and allowances according to local regulations, which can be consulted at the cantonal tax offices. The calculation takes into account income, family situation and expected allowances.
When do I need to file my ordinary tax return?
Ordinary tax return is required when the annual income exceeds the NOV threshold of CHF 120,000. Below this threshold, you remain in the tax system at source and the ordinary declaration is not mandatory.
What tools can I use to calculate the Quellensteuer?
To calculate the Quellensteuer you can use the online salary calculator, consult the official cantonal tariff tables and contact the tax offices for detailed information on deductions and allowances.
