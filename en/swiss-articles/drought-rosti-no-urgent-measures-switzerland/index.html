Drought in Switzerland: Rösti excludes urgent and federal measures (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 August 2026

The Federal Council monitors the drought and the 46% water deficit, excluding the extraordinary state for now and leaving the decisions to the Cantons.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Federal Council rules out urgent federal drought measures
  • A water deficit of 46% was recorded in Ticino during 2026
  • Hazard level 4 is active throughout the national territory
  • The ATS agency and the authorities constantly follow the evolution

Key facts

  • What: Drought monitoring and cantonal water management
  • When: August 2026 and recent developments reported by ATS
  • Where: Switzerland and Ticino in particular
  • Who: Federal Councillor Albert Rösti and Federal Council
  • Amount: 46% water deficit and hazard level 4

Despite the persistence of the heat and the lack of rainfall, the Federal Council believes that the drought situation in Switzerland can still be managed directly by the Cantons. This was stated by Federal Councillor Albert Rösti, head of the Federal Department of the Environment, explaining that the Government is closely following the evolution of the situation and its effects on the different economic and social sectors of the country. According to the ATS agency, the water shortage is due to lack of precipitation and the prolonged heat that is affecting the entire Swiss Confederation.

Operational details

The current situation analysis highlights the need for a tight coordination between the various Swiss institutional levels in managing the water resource. The division of competences assigns to the Cantons and Municipalities the task of adopting restrictive or supportive measures based on regional specifics. This decentralized organization allows for real-time interventions, responding to local emergencies without the need for a rigid and centralized intervention from Bern. However, the persistence of the hot season and the decrease in reserves in water basins and watercourses raise relevant questions about the sustainability of the national logistics and agricultural production.

Key points

To effectively cope with the current situation and prevent potential inconveniences related to resource management, citizens and economic operators are called to follow the instructions issued by the cantonal and municipal authorities. Each Swiss municipality has the option to introduce specific restrictions on the use of potable water for non-essential purposes, such as irrigation of gardens or washing of cars, based on local directives. It is essential to regularly check the official information channels of your place of residence or work to learn about any bans on water intake from water courses or temporary restrictive ordinances in a timely manner.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the current drought hazard level in Switzerland?
Currently the entire country is at hazard level 4, which represents the maximum of the scale provided by the national platform on drought managed by the Confederation.
Who decides the restrictions on water use?
Concrete measures and any restrictions on the use of water, limits on withdrawals from watercourses or interventions to support agriculture are mainly the responsibility of the cantons and municipalities.
Has the Federal Council declared a state of emergency?
No, the Federal Council has so far ruled out declaring an extraordinary situation at national level, considering that the situation can be managed by the Cantons, despite having prepared an intervention plan in case of deterioration.
