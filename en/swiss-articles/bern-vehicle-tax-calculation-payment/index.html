Motor vehicle tax in Bern: calculation and payment

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 5 October 2026

In the Canton of Bern, the criteria, deadlines, amounts and office responsible for motor vehicle tax depend on cantonal rules.

In the Canton of Bern, motor vehicle tax follows a cantonal framework: calculation, payment, deadlines, change of vehicle and address update are matters to be handled according to the rules and offices of the canton. For anyone who has to manage the procedure, the consequence is direct: requirements, deadlines and amounts must be checked against the official source of the Canton of Bern.

The road traffic office is the operational point of reference indicated for this matter. Verification of the calculation must be read together with the instructions on payment, deadlines and changes reported for the change of vehicle and the address update.

A jurisdiction not to be confused

The Swiss tax framework provides for three levels: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and municipal tax. Each canton has its own law and multiplier; for municipalities, the multiplier is applied to the cantonal tax. This distinction helps explain why the competent office matters, but on its own does not provide the criterion for motor vehicle tax in the Canton of Bern. For this service, the source expressly indicates cantonal rules and offices.

AFC/ESTV manages direct federal tax and VAT. Cantonal administrations manage cantonal and municipal tax. UFAS/BSV concerns social security, including AVS/AI/LPP, and does not set taxes; UST/BFS produces statistics and does not set tax rates. These are different areas of responsibility, which should not be conflated when looking for the point of contact for motor vehicle tax.

For this reason, a consultation on dichiarazione delle imposte must remain separate from verification of motor vehicle tax. The former belongs to the general tax framework; the latter must be traced back to the cantonal indications on calculation, payment, deadlines and updates.

The principle also applies to anyone who changes vehicle or address: it is not enough to retain the procedure used previously. The change is among the steps to be checked with the road traffic office, while requirements, deadlines and amounts remain anchored to the canton’s official source.

Operational details

What changes in practical handling

The most concrete consequence of cantonal jurisdiction concerns how the case is assessed. The starting point is not a figure taken from another territory, but the criterion applicable in the Canton of Bern. The source calls for verifying requirements, deadlines and amounts with the canton: first the situation is identified, then which official guidance describes it is checked. A figure from another canton cannot be treated as confirmation for Bern, because motor vehicle tax is governed by cantonal rules and handled by cantonal offices.

From the ordinary case to changes

In a hypothetical ordinary-payment scenario, the logical process is straightforward: the cantonal criterion is checked, the relevant deadline is identified, and the amount indicated for that case is checked. There is no need to turn the general tax system into a formula for the car: the three levels of the Swiss tax system remain an institutional distinction, while the motor vehicle levy should be interpreted according to the canton’s guidance.

Changing the vehicle calls for a different check. The source lists it alongside calculation, payment and deadlines; therefore, anyone who changes the vehicle should not automatically reuse a previous check. It is necessary to check how the road traffic office handles the change and which requirements, deadlines and amounts are associated with the new case.

Updating the address follows the same administrative logic, but should not be confused with changing the vehicle. In a hypothetical scenario in which the two changes coincide, it is prudent to carry out two separate checks against the official source and the office’s guidance, without assuming that one update replaces the other.

Key points

Practical procedure

Management becomes simpler if the verification is broken down into short steps. The source does not replace the instructions of the Canton of Bern: it serves to establish which office to consult and which information to check.

The operational sequence

1. Define the procedure. Determine whether you need to verify the calculation, proceed with payment, manage a vehicle change, or update your address. If there are multiple needs, note them down separately: these are variations mentioned by the source.

2. Start from the official source of the Canton. Look for instructions regarding the criteria, requirements, deadlines, and amounts of the motor vehicle tax. These are the elements that the source entrusts to cantonal rules; do not replace them with a deadline or an amount obtained from another Canton.

3. Use the motor vehicle office as an operational reference. When the procedure involves a vehicle change or an address update, check with this office which update is required and in what form it must be followed. The procedure to apply is the one indicated by the competent cantonal authority.

4. Keep jurisdictions separate. FTA/ESTV deals with direct federal tax and VAT; cantonal administrations manage cantonal and municipal taxes. FSIO/BSV deals with social security, including AHV/IV/BVG, while FSO/BFS deals with statistics. For the motor vehicle tax, the path to follow remains the cantonal one.

5. Complete the check before payment. Double-check that the deadline and amount are the ones published for the procedure in the Canton of Bern. If your vehicle or address has changed in the meantime, repeat the check on requirements and updates instead of assuming the previous consultation is still valid.

This sequence avoids mixing up a motor vehicle procedure with the general tax return, social security, or statistics. It also keeps the question on calculation separate from the one on payment: for both, the verification must be completed in the official cantonal source and at the competent office.

This rule is simple to apply even at the national level: when a service is administered by the Cantons, requirements, deadlines, and amounts must be sought from the canton concerned. In the case of Bern, the final check must therefore remain anchored to the official cantonal information and the motor vehicle office.
