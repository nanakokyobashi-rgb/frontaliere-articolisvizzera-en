Rural Broadband: The Federal 2030-2037 Program (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 9 September 2026

The Federal Council approves the broadband support programme on 9 September 2026. Investment of 365 million federal CHF to bring fast internet to rural areas.

Context

In Brief

  • The Federal Council approves on September 9, 2026 the law on broadband support
  • Total investment of CHF 730 million (CHF 365 million from the Confederation)
  • Objective: minimum speed of 1 Gbps download for residences and commercial activities
  • 8-year program (2030-2037) in sparsely populated areas

Key Facts

  • What: Federal law on support for broadband expansion (LSBL)
  • When: Approval on September 9, 2026; program start from 2030
  • Where: Sparsely populated areas of Switzerland
  • Who: Federal Council, Municipalities, Participating Cantons
  • Amount: CHF 365 million from the Confederation (2030-2037)
  • Duration: 8 years, renewable only once for a maximum of 3 years
  • Target speed: At least 1 gigabit per second download

The Federal Council approved on September 9, 2026 the message to Parliament on the broadband support law (LSBL), with the objective of bridging the digital divide between urban and rural areas of Switzerland. The decision responds to the urgent need to expand modern fiber optic and radio communication networks where expansion is not economically convenient for private providers.

According to official estimates, approximately ten percent of Swiss households and commercial activities remain unconnected to modern broadband networks. The gradual phase-out cycle of copper cables, scheduled from 2030 onwards, makes federal intervention even more urgent. Without the support of the Confederation, entire rural municipalities would risk remaining excluded from access to essential digital infrastructure.

Operational details

The implications for the economic and residential fabric

Modern broadband is not just a convenience; it represents a strategic infrastructure that changes the attractiveness of rural areas as places of residence and work. Today, the digital divide amplifies the economic divide between urban centers and peripheral areas. A family considering moving out of the city carefully weighs the local cost of living, available job postings, public services, and increasingly, access to a reliable internet connection.

At speeds of 1 gigabit per second, fiber optic broadband enables actual telework, not just theoretical. Professionals, consultants, developers, designers, and administrative employees can work from anywhere in Switzerland, provided they have a stable and fast connection. The federal program recognizes that without public intervention, the private market would not invest in less populated areas, thus perpetuating the economic isolation of these communities.

How the program reduces the territorial divide

The expansion of broadband to all Swiss municipalities, regardless of population density, aims directly to reduce the divide in cost of living and opportunities between regions. Municipalities that today struggle to attract residents and investments could become more competitive if they offer telecommunications infrastructure comparable to urban areas. The moment is critical: copper cables, currently still in service, will be gradually decommissioned from 2030 onwards. Without intervention, this natural cycle of technological replacement would have resulted in service interruption in rural areas deemed unprofitable by private providers. The program anticipates this process, ensuring that all municipalities have simultaneous access to modern technologies.

Key points

How to Participate: Next Steps

If you are a mayor of a rural municipality or a representative of a canton interested in accessing federal funding, it is essential to understand the procedure in detail. Participation is voluntary, but timing is important to maximize implementation within the program's timeframe (2030-2037, potentially extendable until 2040).

The procedure begins at the municipal level: the interested municipality must submit an application to its canton, documenting the area to be covered, the proposed technology (fiber optics or radio communication), and estimated costs. The canton evaluates the application and, if it decides to participate in the program, commits to co-financing 50% of the expenses. The Confederation, through its credit commitment of 365 million francs, covers the other 50%. Financial aid is limited to what is necessary: these are not unlimited subsidies, but contributions calibrated based on actual implementation costs. This mechanism encourages efficiency in project management and reduces waste of public money.

Official Timeline and Deadlines

The Federal Council approved its message to Parliament on September 9, 2026. The next crucial step is the discussion of the draft law in both chambers of the Federal Assembly (National Council and Council of States). No precise schedule has yet been communicated for parliamentary votes, so municipalities and cantons should prepare now for the operational phase.

Frequently Asked Questions
When will the broadband support program actually start?
The Federal Council approved the message on 9 September 2026. The next step is the discussion in Parliament (National Council and Council of States). Once approved, the program will start on January 1, 2030 and run until December 31, 2037. An extension of up to three years is possible if funds remain available. So, infrastructures will start to be built from 2030.
How can rural municipalities participate in the programme?
Participation is voluntary. An interested municipality submits an application to its canton, documenting the area to be covered, the technology (fibre optic or radio) and the estimated costs. The canton, if it decides to participate, undertakes to co-finance 50%. The Confederation covers the other 50% through the commitment credit of CHF 365 million. Aid is limited to the minimum necessary to build the infrastructure.
What is the minimum broadband speed guaranteed by the program?
The objective is a coverage with a minimum transmission speed of at least 1 gigabit per second (1 Gbps) in download. This speed enables teleworking, video streaming, video conferencing, and other data-intensive applications. The preferred technology is fibre optics; where costs are too high, the programme also funds terrestrial radio communications.
How much will the Confederation invest in total in the program?
The total budget is CHF 730 million, with the Confederation funding CHF 365 million for the period 2030-2037. The other half (365 million) must come from the participating cantons and municipalities. Federal funding comes from taxes on future mobile phone concessions.
Why is it urgent to expand broadband in 2030?
The copper cables, currently in service, will be gradually decommissioned from 2030 onwards. Without public intervention, about 10% of Swiss households and businesses would remain excluded from modern broadband, creating a permanent digital divide between urban and rural areas. The federal program proactively steps in to avoid this scenario.
