Compulsory school Basel Countryside: cycles and enrolment (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 September 2026

The Swiss compulsory school lasts 9 years, starts at 6 years and is divided into primary and secondary. Enrollment procedure and school transitions according to federal regulations.

Context

In brief

  • Compulsory school lasts 9 years, starting at the age of 6
  • Structure: primary (5-6 years) + secondary I (3 years)
  • Languages vary by canton; in Basel-Landschaft: Swiss German
  • Calendar and enrollment procedures managed at the cantonal level

Key facts

  • What: Swiss federal compulsory school
  • When: Starting at the age of 6
  • Where: Switzerland (HarmoS agreement in all cantons)
  • Who: All resident children with the right of residence
  • Duration: 9 years in total (unchanging federal standard)

Federal structure of compulsory school

Switzerland has decentralized competencies for education: each canton organizes its own compulsory school, but all follow the HarmoS agreement (Harmonization of Compulsory School), which sets common federal standards for mobility between cantons. This means that structure, duration, and cycles are nationally consistent, although administrative details and school calendar vary by canton.

At the federal level, compulsory school lasts 9 years and starts when the child turns 6 years old. All children resident in Switzerland are legally required to attend. The structure includes: primary school (5-6 years, approximately from 6 to 11-12 years) and secondary I (3 years, approximately from 12-13 to 14-15 years). After the ninth year, school becomes optional, but almost everyone continues in upper secondary schools (gymnasium, vocational school, full-time school).

Basel-Landschaft, like all Swiss cantons, implements this federal structure and adheres to the HarmoS agreement. However, organizational details — precise school calendar, local holidays, enrollment procedures, forms of differentiation in secondary I — are managed by the cantonal administration.

Operational details

School cycles and transitions

Primary school covers the first 5-6 years of education (from 6 to 11-12 years). During this phase, children study fundamental subjects: Italian/cantonal language, mathematics, sciences, physical education, arts, music. The class remains relatively stable; often the same teacher teaches multiple disciplines. The focus is on literacy and calculation, as well as socialization.

At the end of primary school, around the age of 12, children automatically transition to lower secondary school, which lasts 3 years (until 14-15 years). In this phase, the subjects become more specialized, and there are more teachers for each discipline. Here, the first differentiations begin to emerge: some cantons offer courses at level A (advanced) and B (basic) in subjects such as mathematics and languages, to better prepare students for higher education based on their abilities.

Teaching languages and educational offerings

In Switzerland, the teaching language is the official language of the canton. Basel-Landschaft has Swiss German as the main teaching language in public schools of compulsory education. Italian is taught as a foreign language starting from primary school (the method varies by canton), and English from lower secondary school as a mandatory modern language.

In multilingual cantons (such as Bern or Fribourg), regions with different languages coexist. Basel-Landschaft is a German-speaking canton, so public schools of compulsory education mainly teach in Swiss German. International private schools may offer programs in other languages, but they do not fall under federal compulsory education.

Key points

Practical procedure for enrolling children

If you are moving to Switzerland with children or have a child who reaches the age of 6, here are the concrete steps for enrollment.

Step 1: Confirm your residence in Switzerland. The obligation to attend public school applies to all official residents (those with a residence permit in Switzerland). Ensure you are registered in the population register of your municipality of residence. Registration is preliminary and essential for school enrollment.

Step 2: Identify the primary school in your district. Check which school is assigned to your address. Look on the municipality's website or the cantonal education page. Each school has an office with reception hours (often Monday-Friday 8:00-12:00).

Step 3: Gather the required documentation. Contact the school office and explicitly ask for the documents needed for enrollment. Generally: birth certificate (original or certified copy), proof of residence (lease agreement or property), and sometimes a vaccination certificate. Requirements vary if the child is a Swiss citizen, a resident with a residence permit, or a child of a cross-border commuter.

Step 4: Submit the enrollment application within the cantonal deadlines. Cantons communicate enrollment deadlines (usually March-April of the year before school starts). Complete the form (paper or online) and submit it to the school.

Step 5: Receive confirmation and information on the first day. Once enrolled, the school will communicate the first day, the schedule (usually 8:00-12:00 and 14:00-16:00 with an afternoon break), the list of necessary school supplies, and the names of the assigned teachers.

Frequently Asked Questions
At what age does compulsory schooling begin in Switzerland?
Compulsory schooling begins at 6 years of age. The child must be registered in the calendar year in which he/she turns 6 years old. Enrollment takes place at the primary school of the district of residence and does not require an entrance exam. It is free and compulsory for all residents of Switzerland.
How long is compulsory schooling in Switzerland?
The compulsory school lasts a total of 9 years, as established by the federal norm and the HarmoS Concordat, and is identical in all cantons. It is divided into: primary school (5-6 years, from 6 to 11-12 years) and secondary school I (3 years, from 12-13 to 14-15 years). After the ninth grade, school becomes optional, but almost all continue in high school.
What languages are taught in Swiss primary school?
The main language of instruction is the official language of the canton. In Basel Countryside it is the Swiss German. Italian is taught as a foreign language in primary school (modes vary by canton), and English becomes a compulsory modern language by secondary school I. Other languages depend on the cantonal programme and available resources.
How does the transition from primary to secondary I work?
At the end of primary school (end of the fifth or sixth year, around the age of 12), all children automatically move to secondary school I in their area of residence. There is no formal passing test. In secondary I, differentiations (levels A and B in some subjects) are introduced to customize the path according to the student's abilities.
What are the options after compulsory school at age 15-16?
After the ninth year (around 15-16 years old), children enter secondary schools with three main strands: gymnasium/gymnasium (3-4 years old, academic path to university), vocational school with dual apprenticeship (3-4 years old, practical training), or full-time school (alternative for those who do not access directly). The choice depends on the student's grades and abilities. Each canton offers free school guidance to support the decision.
