F-35A: Fixed Price Doubts and Parliamentary Reactions

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 September 2026

The report on the lack of a fixed price for the F-35A shocks Parliament. Green toward the ICC, while the center-right brakes.

Context

In brief

  • Critical report on the absence of a fixed price for the F-35A jets.
  • The center-right in Parliament opposes a parliamentary commission of inquiry.
  • Viola Amherd will not issue further statements on the dossier.
  • The Greens are evaluating a motion for a parliamentary commission of inquiry in the autumn session.

Key facts

  • What: Lack of a fixed price for the purchase of F-35A fighter jets.
  • Where: Bern, Swiss Parliament.
  • Who: Control Committee of the National Council, Viola Amherd, The Greens.
  • Amount: 400 million francs requested as a supplementary credit.
  • Status: No immediate consequences, political debate ongoing.

The report drawn up by the Control Committee of the National Council regarding the acquisition of the F-35A fighter jets has sparked strong controversy in Bern, revealing the absence of a contractual fixed price. This discovery had the effect of a political bombshell, although the regulatory and procedural framework will not undergo changes in the immediate future. The former Defense Minister, Viola Amherd, made it known through Keystone-ATS that she had already expressed her position on the matter, stating that she did not wish to make further comments on the charges contained in the document. The Swiss political class appears deeply divided: while the center-right, which holds the majority in Parliament, has already expressed itself negatively on the possibility of establishing a parliamentary commission of inquiry (CPI), the Greens are pushing decisively for this solution to shed light on what happened.

Party reactions and parliamentary debate. Despite the divergence of views on the need for a formal investigation, the contents of the report have caused transversal consternation. National Councilor Clarence Chollet, a member of the Greens, defined the impact on Switzerland's image as catastrophic. In parallel, the UDC group leader, Thomas Aeschi, raised doubts about the management of the dossier, asking to clarify who was responsible for supervision and whether the law firm involved operated in total autonomy or under control. The FDP, for its part, insists on the need to restore a relationship of trust between the executive and the Federal Chambers, underlining that parliamentarians must be able to access clear and precise information during acquisition processes of such magnitude. The climate of uncertainty remains high, waiting to see how parliamentary discussions will evolve in the coming sessions.

Operational details

The analysis of the potential legal consequences surrounding the F-35A fighter jet affair offers food for thought regarding administrative and political accountability within the Swiss context. Andreas Stöckli, professor of constitutional and administrative law at the University of Fribourg, explained that assessing the liability of federal administration employees is extremely complex. The requirements for claiming financial liability are, by law, very strict: it would be necessary to prove the existence of illicit conduct committed with intent or through gross negligence. In the absence of such prerequisites, it is unlikely that administrative personnel or the law firms involved in the acquisition will face legal proceedings.

Immunity and the role of politics. Regarding the issue of criminal liability, Vincent Martenet, professor of constitutional law at the University of Lausanne, also weighed in. He recalled that members of the Federal Council enjoy absolute immunity concerning statements made officially before the Federal Chambers. Therefore, the judicial path appears almost impracticable, leaving the political route as the only option, namely the establishment of a parliamentary commission of inquiry. However, the path to achieving such a commission remains uphill. Pierre-Alain Fridez, former Socialist National Councilor, expressed in an interview with the newspaper '24 heures' his fear that the parliamentary majority will limit itself to interpreting the event as a misreading of the contracts, avoiding a deeper investigation into political responsibilities. The Socialist Party has therefore requested that the Federal Council verify the possible presence of false statements that could constitute criminal offenses. This position is also reflected in budget management: the left seems inclined to deny its support for the request for a supplementary credit of nearly 400 million francs intended for the purchase of the American aircraft. The situation remains in a stalemate, with the cost of living continuing to be a central theme in the debate on national public spending.

Key points

For citizens and observers of Swiss politics, the F-35A fighter jet affair translates into a complex parliamentary procedure that will influence public spending decisions in the coming months. The crucial step, from a procedural standpoint, will take place during the autumn session. In this forum, the Greens may formalize the motion for the establishment of a parliamentary commission of inquiry. For taxpayers and those interested in budget dynamics, it is essential to monitor the official communications of the Swiss Parliament to understand whether the additional credit of 400 million francs will be approved or if the resistance from part of the left will lead to a blockage of resources.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main critical issues regarding the purchase of F-35As?
The report of the Management Commission of the National Council highlighted the absence of a fixed contract price for the acquisition of fighters. This gap has generated strong political consternation, raising doubts about the supervision of the dossier and transparency in the purchasing processes. Although the current regulatory framework is not undergoing immediate change, the issue has triggered a debate on political and administrative accountability, with requests for clarification on who oversaw the law firm involved and how the contracts were handled.
Why is there talk of an additional credit of CHF 400 million?
The amount of CHF 400 million represents the additional credit needed to proceed with the purchase of the aircraft. The parliamentary left, following the revelations about the management of the contract, has expressed its intention to deny its support for this appropriation. The final decision on the approval of these resources will be a crucial step for national finances and will be discussed at the next sessions of the Swiss Parliament, in particular during the autumn session.
Is it possible to set up a parliamentary committee of inquiry (CPI)?
The Greens are considering tabling a motion for the establishment of an ICC during the autumn session to shed light on what happened. However, the path appears to be uphill: the centre-right, which holds the majority in Parliament, has already expressed itself negatively. Moreover, constitutional law experts such as Vincent Martenet recall that members of the Federal Council enjoy immunity for official statements, making the judicial route impracticable and leaving the political solution of the ICC as the only option, albeit a complex one to implement.
