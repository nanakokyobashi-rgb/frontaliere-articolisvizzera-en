Operational details

The analysis of the potential legal consequences surrounding the F-35A fighter jet affair offers food for thought regarding administrative and political accountability within the Swiss context. Andreas Stöckli, professor of constitutional and administrative law at the University of Fribourg, explained that assessing the liability of federal administration employees is extremely complex. The requirements for claiming financial liability are, by law, very strict: it would be necessary to prove the existence of illicit conduct committed with intent or through gross negligence. In the absence of such prerequisites, it is unlikely that administrative personnel or the law firms involved in the acquisition will face legal proceedings. ### Immunity and the role of politics. Regarding the issue of criminal liability, Vincent Martenet, professor of constitutional law at the University of Lausanne, also weighed in. He recalled that members of the Federal Council enjoy absolute immunity concerning statements made officially before the Federal Chambers. Therefore, the judicial path appears almost impracticable, leaving the political route as the only option, namely the establishment of a parliamentary commission of inquiry. However, the path to achieving such a commission remains uphill. Pierre-Alain Fridez, former Socialist National Councilor, expressed in an interview with the newspaper '24 heures' his fear that the parliamentary majority will limit itself to interpreting the event as a misreading of the contracts, avoiding a deeper investigation into political responsibilities. The Socialist Party has therefore requested that the Federal Council verify the possible presence of false statements that could constitute criminal offenses. This position is also reflected in budget management: the left seems inclined to deny its support for the request for a supplementary credit of nearly 400 million francs intended for the purchase of the American aircraft. The situation remains in a stalemate, with the cost of living continuing to be a central theme in the debate on national public spending.

Key points

For citizens and observers of Swiss politics, the F-35A fighter jet affair translates into a complex parliamentary procedure that will influence public spending decisions in the coming months. The crucial step, from a procedural standpoint, will take place during the autumn session. In this forum, the Greens may formalize the motion for the establishment of a parliamentary commission of inquiry.
