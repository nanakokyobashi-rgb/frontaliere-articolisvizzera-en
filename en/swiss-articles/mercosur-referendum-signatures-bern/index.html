Tuesday afternoon, during a press conference in Bern, a broad front of opponents to the free trade agreement involving Switzerland and the Mercosur countries launched the collection of signatures for a referendum. The mobilization follows two steps mentioned in the report: the signing of the text by all EFTA countries and its approval by the Chambers in September.

The opponents presented the mobilization as a response to the consequences they attribute to the agreement. The themes identified are three: the Amazon rainforest, human rights and agriculture. The Bern conference therefore placed at the center the associated environmental and social repercussions of large-scale agricultural production, according to the alliance.

The objections presented in Bern

Deforestation is the first issue raised. For the alliance, among the main causes in the four countries mentioned are large-scale soybean cultivation and intensive cattle farming: Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay. The geographical reference therefore encompasses the entire list in the report and, in the opponents’ view, links the criticism to the organization of production.

The second argument concerns the communities that have settled in the affected territories for some time. The opponents argue that the expansion of industrial agriculture causes them to be displaced. The third concerns plantations, where, according to the criticism from those opposed to the treaty, cases of forced labor are also repeatedly recorded.

On this basis, the alliance urges rejection of the text, so as not to encourage practices it considers harmful.
