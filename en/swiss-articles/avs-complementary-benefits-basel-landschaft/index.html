AVS and Supplementary Benefits in the Canton of Basel-Landschaft (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 2 September 2026

How the Swiss First Pillar Works in the Cantonal Compensation Fund. AVS Pensions, Contribution Gaps, and Benefits to Ensure a Minimum Standard of Living.

Context

At a Glance

  • AVS: 5.3% employee contribution, 10.6% gross (including employer contribution). Forms the basis of the pension system.
  • Supplemental benefits: supplement insufficient AVS pensions up to the subsistence level.
  • Compensation fund: Manages contributions and calculates pensions at the cantonal level.
  • Contribution gaps: Periods without contributions reduce the pension; the system provides for top-up payments.

Key Facts

  • What: AVS (First Pillar), supplementary benefits, cantonal compensation fund
  • Where: Canton of Basel-Landschaft (uniform federal procedures across all cantons)
  • Who: Employees, employers, Swiss residents; administered by the Federal Social Insurance Office (BSV) at the federal level
  • Contribution: 5.3% employee, 10.6% total (including employer’s share)
  • Administration: Cantonal administration + cantonal compensation fund

The first pillar of the Swiss pension system, the AVS, forms the foundation of the social security system for those living and working in Switzerland. In the Canton of Basel-Landschaft, as in other cantons, the local compensation fund manages the contributions paid by employees and employers in accordance with uniform federal regulations. Every employed resident pays 5.3% of their gross salary into the AVS (to which the employer adds another 5.3%), accumulating pension rights year after year. Those with contribution gaps—periods without contributions—may be eligible for supplementary benefits to ensure a minimum standard of living in retirement.

Operational details

Supplementary benefits are not an optional additional pension, but a federal legal entitlement recognized by Swiss law. Anyone whose AVS pension falls below the legally defined subsistence minimum (an amount indexed annually, not yet specified here) may apply to the relevant cantonal office. In the canton of Basel-Landschaft, the application is submitted to the cantonal social services office, which assesses income, assets, and personal circumstances. The system takes into account both the AVS pension and other sources of income (rental income, LPP/BVG occupational pensions, third-pillar savings, and family allowances) and supplements them until the minimum cost of living recognized by federal law is covered.

Scenario: Contribution Gaps and Reduced Pension

A resident who has paid AVS contributions for 35 years (instead of 43) has an 8-year contribution gap. Their AVS pension will be calculated based on 35 years of average earnings, with a proportional reduction compared to a full 43-year career. If this pension is insufficient, supplementary benefits will top it up to the subsistence minimum (the amount varies by canton and family situation; check with your local compensation office). The application for supplementary benefits is submitted to the cantonal administration, not to the AVS fund. Supplementary benefits also take into account Disability Insurance (AI) if the applicant is entitled to it. In the canton of Basel-Landschaft, the same federal rules apply as in all other cantons: the federal agency UFAS/BSV defines the criteria, the cantonal administration manages the cases, and the compensation office provides data on the AVS pension.

Key points

How to Apply for an AVS Pension and Supplementary Benefits

The process consists of two parallel steps. Step 1: About 1–2 months before turning 65 (for women) or 65 (for men), the employee automatically receives an informational letter and an AVS pension application form from the compensation fund. If you do not receive this letter, contact the compensation fund for the canton of Basel-Landschaft directly. Fill out the form, attach a copy of your passport or ID card, and submit it by mail or in person. Step 2: At the same time, if you anticipate that your AVS pension will be insufficient (e.g., due to gaps in coverage or a fragmented work history), submit a separate application for supplementary benefits to the cantonal social assistance office in Basel-Landschaft. There is no strict deadline for this application, but it is advisable to submit it immediately after receiving the AVS pension calculation from the fund to avoid losing retroactive entitlements.

Frequently Asked Questions
What exactly are supplemental benefits?
They are a federal statutory right that supplements an AVS annuity that is insufficient to the bare minimum. The State, through the cantonal administration, covers the difference between the calculated rent and the recognized essential expenses (rent, food, medical care). It is not a loan, it is a permanent benefit for the entire pension, unless the financial situation changes.
Who is eligible for supplementary benefits in the canton of Basel-Landschaft?
Who receives AVS (or AI, or LAI) income below the minimum living threshold. The criteria are federal and identical in all cantons: assessment of total income, assets, and compliance with subsidy ceilings. In the canton of Basel-Country, eligibility is managed by the cantonal social welfare office, following the directives of UFAS/BSV.
How do I know how much my AVS pension will be?
Contact the cantonal compensation fund Basel-Country at least 6 months before retirement and request an estimate of the pension (based on the data in your file). The estimate is binding once the official application is submitted. For a personalized indicative assessment, [usa il nostro calcolatore →](nav:pension)
Can I still make up for any gaps in my social security contributions before I retire?
Yes, the AVS system allows voluntary retroactive payments up to 5 previous years. The clearinghouse will inform you about which years can be integrated. Voluntary payments increase the final income. Consult the cashier without delay if you think you have gaps.
What is the subsistence level, and how is it indexed?
The amount varies by canton and personal situation (single, couple, with children). It is indexed annually according to the regional price and cost index. In the canton of Basel-Country, the cantonal administration communicates the current minimum every year. It is not published centrally because it is subject to annual variation; check with your local cashier.
