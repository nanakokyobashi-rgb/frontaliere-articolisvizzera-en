Key facts

  • What: Company registration in the canton of Bern
  • Where: Commercial register office of the canton of Bern
  • Who: Entrepreneurs, companies, associations, professionals
  • Legal forms: Sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, associations
  • Main obligation: Registration with the commercial register
  • Costs: Cantonal registration fees (varies by canton)
  • Insurances: AVS/AI (self-employed), LAMal (all residents), LPP/BVG (optional)

Anyone who decides to start an activity in the canton of Bern must register with the local commercial register, an administrative procedure that varies depending on the chosen legal form. Registration entails specific obligations regarding minimum capital, registration fees and insurances, all factors that influence the structure of the new company.

Choosing the legal form

The choice of legal form is the first decisive step. In the canton of Bern (as in all of Switzerland) an entrepreneur can opt for a sole proprietorship, a partnership, a corporation or an association. Each choice has different implications: a sole proprietorship maintains unlimited liability on personal assets, while a corporation limits liability to contributions. The legal form also determines the bureaucratic complexity and necessary administrative requirements. Use our tax calculator to assess the tax impact of the choice of form.

Operational details

The choice of legal form directly impacts the taxation of the business. In the Swiss tax system, which is structured at three levels — federal, cantonal and municipal — each entity has specific competencies. The federal direct tax on individuals (for sole proprietorships) and on corporations is administered by the Confederation, while cantonal and municipal taxes are managed by the administration of the canton of Bern and its municipalities. The choice of legal form therefore determines not only where to register but also how and where to pay taxes.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the tax difference between a sole proprietorship and a limited company in the canton of Bern?
A sole proprietorship is taxed as a natural person: income is cumulated with personal income and is subject to federal, cantonal and municipal tax rates. A limited company, on the other hand, is taxed autonomously as an entity, and only dividends distributed to shareholders are subject to personal taxation. What are the insurance obligations for those who start a sole proprietorship in the canton of Bern?
A sole proprietor must enroll in the Swiss social security system (AVS/AI) as an independent, with contributions calculated on declared net income. Health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory for all Swiss residents and is not a tax but a private premium insurance per capita with deductibles from CHF 300 to CHF 2500. You can also choose to join the second pillar (LPP/BVG) for a complementary annuity. Accident insurance obligations (LAINF) depend on the industry.
How long does it take to register a business in the canton of Bern?
There is no single federal deadline because the administrative competence is cantonal. Each canton, including Bern, has its own timetable. It is advisable to complete the registration as soon as possible after the decision to start the activity, because obligations such as AVS/AI registration for independents and the subscription of the LAMal start from the effective date of the start of the activity. The Bernese trade register office provides detailed checklists on the procedures.
What documents are required to register a joint-stock company in Bern?
Generally you need: identity document of the shareholders, certificate of residence, deed of incorporation of the company, bank confirmation of the deposit of the minimum capital, and specific authorizations if the activity requires special licenses. The exact list depends on the legal form chosen. The limited liability company requires documents other than a limited company. The commercial register office of the canton of Bern provides complete checklists for each legal form.
