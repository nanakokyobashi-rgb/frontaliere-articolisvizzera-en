Key points

EU controls AI Act, transparency for chatbots and deepfakes

The European Union is taking steps to regulate artificial intelligence (AI) and ensure transparency of AI models, such as chatbots and deepfakes. These measures are part of the package of European laws called the AI Act.

Suppliers of Gpai models will be required to document a range of information to be made available to competent authorities and developers using such models. In addition, it will be necessary to adopt a policy on respect for copyright and publish a sufficiently detailed summary of the content used to train the models. These new rules will be applied by the AI Office set up within the Commission.

The new rules require GPAI model providers to provide detailed information on the data used to train the models, including data collection methods and data sources. In addition, suppliers must provide information on the techniques used to develop the models and how to use the data.

For example, if a Swiss company like the Lugano-based company Sygma, which operates in the finance and financial services sector, were to use an AI model to provide financial advisory services, it would have to provide detailed information on the data used to train the model, including data collection methods and data sources.
