Trump does not stop the export of Swiss watches: in July up 10% (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 20 August 2026

Exports of Swiss watches registered a sharp increase in July, confirming how Trump does not stop the export of Swiss watches: in July by 10%. Growth was 9.6% year-on-year, bringing the total to CHF 2.63 billion.

The Swiss watch industry has seen a significant increase in exports in July, with a 10% rise compared to the same period last year. This growth is largely driven by the strong demand in the United States, which remains the main market for Swiss watches. The total value of exports reached 2.63 billion francs, a 9.6% increase on an annual basis. The Swiss Watch Industry Federation (FH) has reported that the growth in exports is a positive sign for the industry, which has been affected by the global economic downturn. The FH has also noted that the growth in exports is a result of the strong demand in the United States, as well as in other key markets such as France and the United Kingdom. However, the industry is also facing challenges in other regions, such as Japan, where exports have declined.

Operational details

The analysis of disaggregated data by price range offers further insights into the Swiss watch industry's performance. The high-end segment, which includes watches priced at 3,000 francs or more, recorded a 12.0% increase in July. At the same time, the economic segment, characterized by watches priced between 200 and 500 francs, also registered a significant growth of 26.1%. According to the Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry (FH), it is precisely the more affordable watches that confirm the recovery of this year, after the same segment had recorded a consistent decline for four consecutive years. This polarization demonstrates how demand is moving simultaneously towards extreme luxury goods and more accessible products, partially excluding intermediate segments from such marked growth dynamics. The evolution of export volumes and values reflects different commercial strategies adopted by major groups and independent brands, engaged in positioning their catalogs based on the changing preferences of global consumers.
