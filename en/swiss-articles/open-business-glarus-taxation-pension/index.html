The first step: choose the legal form

Starting a business in the Canton of Glarus first requires choosing the legal form. An entrepreneur can operate as a sole proprietorship, if the business is simple and manageable by a single person, or set up a joint-stock company (anonymous or limited liability company) for more complex structures. The decision influences not only administrative management, but also tax and social security obligations.

Once the legal form has been defined, registration in the commercial register of the Canton of Glarus is mandatory for all practitioners of commercial, industrial or artisanal professions. This fulfillment is the prerequisite for operating legally and allows the company to have a corporate identification number (UID) necessary for all relations with the tax and social authorities.

Taxation: three levels of taxation

Switzerland applies a tax system on three levels: federal, cantonal and municipal. Anyone who opens a business in Glarus will have to pay direct federal tax (IFD) on profits, in addition to cantonal and municipal taxes, which vary according to the specific municipality. Each canton determines its own rates and the municipality applies a multiplier on the cantonal tax, creating a complexity that requires careful analysis.

Value added tax (VAT) comes into play if the turnover exceeds a certain limit. While not the subject of this guide in detail, it is a key element to evaluate from the initial stages. For businesses with employees, payroll taxation is a further crucial chapter: those who hire must pay social security contributions and comply with tax withholding obligations on payroll.

Social security: AVS and LPP are mandatory

Every entrepreneur is required to pay contributions to Old Age and Survivors Insurance (AVS) and Disability Insurance (AI). If the activity generates employees, the AVS/AI contribution rate is 5.3% paid by the employee and 5.3% paid by the employer (total 10.6%). Contributions for unemployment compensation (AD) and disaster compensation (AC) amount to approximately 1.1% up to a defined annual maximum.

For 2026, occupational pension provision (LPP, i.e. Provident Fund) is mandatory for employees with a coordinated salary (difference between total salary and coordination deduction). The contribution rate varies according to the age of the worker: from 25 to 34 years it is 7%, from 35 to 44 years it is 10%, from 45 to 54 years it is 15%, and from 55 years up to the reference age it is 18%.

Health insurance: immediate obligation

Anyone residing in Switzerland, including the business owner and his employees, must take out health insurance (KVG) within 3 months of arriving in the Canton of Glarus. Insurance is private, with per capita premiums varying by canton and region. Standard deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 to CHF 2500. The Canton of Glarus offers cantonal subsidies for reducing premiums based on income, an important aspect to consider during initial financial planning.

Operational details

Legal form and minimum capital

The legal form chosen directly influences the administrative complexity and initial costs. A sole proprietorship does not require minimum capital and represents the least expensive solution for small businesses, but exposes the entrepreneur to unlimited liability on his personal assets. A limited liability company (Sàrl/SARL) provides minimum capital and offers protection of personal assets, while a limited company (SA) is suitable for larger structures and attracts external investors.

In the Canton of Glarus, municipalities can request registration in the commercial register with documents that vary slightly depending on the form chosen. It does not matter which form you choose: registration is mandatory and must take place at the cantonal commercial register office, which issues the company identification number (UID) necessary for tax and social security purposes.

Cost deduction and tax planning

Once operational, the business generates operating costs (rent, utilities, salaries, supplies) which are deductible from the tax base for the calculation of direct federal tax (IFD). This feature reduces your tax burden, but requires accurate documentation and the filing of a detailed annual tax return.

The three-level Swiss tax system (federal, cantonal, municipal) creates very different scenarios depending on the municipality chosen, given that the municipality applies a multiplier on the cantonal tax. An entrepreneur who opens a business in Glarus (the capital) will face a different tax burden than someone who chooses a peripheral municipality in the same canton. It is advisable to consult the specific rates of the municipality of interest before defining the initial tax strategy.

The employee's role and contribution obligations

If the business involves hiring employees, the employer enters into significant contributory obligations. In addition to paying the tax withheld from your salary, you must pay AVS/AI contributions (5.3%), unemployment benefit (AD, approximately 1.1%) and accident insurance (LAINF/LAA, rate 0.7–1.5% depending on the sector).

A further obligation is the employee's enrollment in the occupational pension fund (LPP) with contributions that vary according to the worker's age as described above. Furthermore, the employer must ensure that each employee has active health insurance (LAMal) within 3 months and complies with the rules of Swiss labor law: maximum working hours of 45 hours per week (industry, offices, sales) or 50 hours in other sectors, with a minimum of 4 weeks of annual holidays (5 if under 20). The deadline for renewing the employee's registration in the personnel register is annual.

Accounting and reporting obligations

Anyone who runs a business is required to keep regular accounts and submit a tax return within the deadlines established by the Canton of Glarus. The cantonal and municipal authorities collect the taxes for which they are responsible according to defined deadlines, usually with installment payments if the amount is significant. Federal taxes are paid through the decentralized system of cantonal administrations, which act as collectors on behalf of the Confederation.

A crucial element: if the business generates profits, the owner must consider allocating part of the profits to personal pension provision (self-employed AVS) and, if not covered by a professional fund, opening a third pillar 3a account for additional tax savings. These aspects are part of medium-term financial planning.

Practical checklist: the steps to start a business

Phase 1: Legal and administrative foundations

The first step is to decide between a sole proprietorship, an Sàrl (GmbH), or an SA (AG). This decision determines tax and social security obligations throughout the company's entire life. Consulting an accountant or a cantonal business advisory office to assess the implications is strongly recommended, as mistakes in the early stages are costly to correct.

Immediately afterwards, contact the Commercial Register office of the Canton of Glarus with the required documents (articles of incorporation for companies, copy of identity document, certificate of good conduct). After approval, you will receive the business identification number (UID/IDI), which is essential to operate legally and to interact with tax and social authorities.

Opening a business bank account is the next step. Go to a local bank with the UID number and corporate documents. A dedicated account allows you to operate officially and to separate personal financial flows from business ones — an informal requirement but strongly recommended by tax authorities to avoid disputes.

Phase 2: Social security and health insurance

Contact the cantonal AVS compensation office (usually managed by a common or regional body in Glarus) to register as an employer or self-employed person. Obtain the affiliation number required for periodic contributions.

If you will be hiring employees, register the company with a professional pension fund (LPP/BVG) appropriate to the economic sector of the activity. Contributions start from the employment start date and vary according to the employee's age.

Ensure that the owner and every employee have an active LAMal/KVG health insurance policy with an insurance company operating in the Canton of Glarus. Check the deductible options (CHF 300–2,500) and apply for cantonal subsidies if income permits. Registration must be completed within 3 months of arrival or the start of the activity.

Phase 3: Tax and insurance obligations

Fill in the tax notification form provided by the financial administration of the Canton of Glarus for company registration. This activates the obligation to file the annual FTA (federal) and cantonal tax returns.

If the activity involves employees, take out an accident insurance policy (LAINF/UVG) with an approved insurer. Complete occupational safety courses if required by the specific sector of the activity.

Operational conclusion

Opening a business in the Canton of Glarus requires structured planning and in-depth knowledge of the Swiss tax, social security, and labour law system. The timings indicated in this checklist are indicative: administrative reality can speed up or delay processes depending on the municipality and the season. However, the overall framework remains valid for any entrepreneur intending to establish themselves legally in Glarus or in any other Swiss canton.

The most important advice is to consult an accountant specialized in corporate law at least when choosing the legal form: initial mistakes create annual administrative complications and lost tax optimization opportunities. Subsequently, delegating the preparation of the annual tax return and accounting management to professionals reduces the risks of non-compliance and maintains smooth relationships with the authorities.

Switzerland is a country with a transparent and well-structured bureaucracy: respecting the deadlines and obligations guarantees smooth operations and regulatory compliance throughout the company's entire life.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the first step to open a business in the Canton of Glarus?
The first step is to choose the legal form (sole proprietorship, Sàrl, SA), as this decision determines tax and social security obligations. Once chosen, it is mandatory to register the activity in the cantonal trade register, which assigns the company identification number (UID) necessary to operate legally and to interact with tax and social authorities.
What are the costs of registering with the trade register in Glarus?
The fees for registration in the trade register vary slightly depending on the specific municipality and the legal form chosen. It is advisable to contact directly the office of the Commercial Registry of the Canton of Glarus to obtain an accurate quotation of the administrative expenses and any municipal taxes applied.
Am I required to pay AVS and LPP if I open a business and have no employees?
Yes, as a self-employed person you are obliged to pay AVS/AI contributions even without employees. The AVS clearinghouse will generate an annual invoice based on your estimated net income. LPP (occupational pension) is mandatory only if you hire employees; as a self-employed person without employees, you can subscribe to the third pillar 3a as an additional form of social security savings and tax deduction.
How long do I have to take out LAMal health insurance?
Health insurance (LAMal) must be taken out within 3 months of arrival in the Canton of Glarus or from the date of commencement of activity if you are already a resident. Insurance is private with variable premiums per canton and region. Standard adult deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2500. The Canton of Glarus offers cantonal subsidies to reduce premiums based on income.
What labor law obligations must a company that hires employees comply with?
An employer in Switzerland must respect the maximum hours of 45 hours per week (industry, offices, sales) or 50 hours in other sectors, guarantee a minimum of 4 weeks of annual leave (5 if the employee is under 20), pay AVS/AI contributions (5.3%), unemployment benefits (AD, approximately 1.1%) and accident insurance (LAINF/LAA, 0.7-1.5% per sector), enroll the employee in an LPP fund with contributions variable by age (7–18%).
