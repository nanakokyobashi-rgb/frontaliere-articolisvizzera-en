Operational details

Another extremely sensitive point for financial markets and economic observers concerns the current situation in the bond markets. The recent rise in yields recorded on government bonds and fixed-income securities is probably due to higher-than-expected inflation and the expectation of further interest rate hikes by central banks. However, the UBS economist emphasizes that it is also possible investors are gradually losing confidence in the debt sustainability of several countries, a factor that could fuel new and marked turmoil in international financial markets. Debt management and the reaction of bond markets represent a fundamental thermometer to measure global financial health, indirectly influencing the cost of credit and financing conditions for businesses and households. Despite this overall complicated and pitfall-laden picture, Swiss bank UBS nevertheless bets on a clear recovery of the Swiss economy, highlighting how domestic fundamentals show encouraging signs of resilience and vitality. ### Economic indicators in Switzerland Although the international scenario is marked by multiple uncertainties, various indicators point to a decisive economic improvement within Swiss borders. First of all, the PMI purchasing managers' indices have remained almost constantly, starting in March, above the critical growth threshold set at 50 points: a particularly favorable signal for the industrial sector. The PMI relating to the service sector has also recorded a very significant improvement, confirming the solidity of domestic and tertiary demand. Following the economic slowdown recorded over the past year, the positive trend of the labor market and employment also contributes to instilling confidence in the UBS analyst, a fundamental pillar for supporting private consumption in Switzerland. Completing the favorable picture are international prospects, as experts see positive trends in the eurozone. To give a concrete example cited by the analysis, in Germany economic indicators in recent months have clearly turned upwards, offering a precious assist for Swiss exports. Good signs of recovery also come from the United States, leading the expert to conclude that overall the current picture indicates a progressive economic improvement for the Swiss and global economy.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the risks related to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz according to UBS?
According to analyses by Alessandro Bee, an economist at UBS, if the Strait of Hormuz were to remain closed for a long time, oil reserves would be depleted and the market would enter into total panic. In this extreme scenario, crude oil prices could skyrocket from the current $80-100 to $150-200 per barrel, triggering a severe economic collapse and a global recession with direct impacts on the cost of living and purchasing power.
What are the forecasts for the Swiss economy in the current framework?
Although the international scenario is complex and marked by various geopolitical and economic uncertainties, the Swiss bank UBS is betting on a clear recovery of the Swiss economy. Internal fundamentals show encouraging signs of resilience and vitality, supported by SME indicators from purchasing managers that remain above the 50-point growth threshold since March, an improvement in the services sector and a dynamic labour market.
What geopolitical and technological factors influence global markets?
In addition to tensions in the Middle East, the global economic equation considers Donald Trump's unpredictable politics and the actual economic impact of artificial intelligence. If the great productivity gains promised by artificial intelligence do not materialize, companies could significantly reduce investment, slowing down overall growth and generating new waves of caution on international financial markets.
