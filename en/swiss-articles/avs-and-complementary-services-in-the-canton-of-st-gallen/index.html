AVS and complementary services in the Canton of St. Gallen (cross-border guide)

Swiss social security is divided into three pillars: the first is the AVS/AHV, which is mandatory for anyone who carries out a gainful activity in Switzerland. In the Canton of St. Gallen, as in all the Cantons, the collection of contributions and the payment of AVS annuities are entrusted to the cantonal compensation fund, which operates under the supervision of the UFAS/BSV at the federal level. The compensation fund is the first point of contact for workers, employers and recipients of annuities.

When the AVS annuity, possibly added to other benefits of the first and second pillar, is not sufficient to cover the minimum living allowance recognized by law, the supplementary benefits (PC) intervene. It is a benefit subject to conditions of income and substance, financed in part by the Confederation and in part by the Cantons. The UFAS/BSV defines the regulatory framework, while the operational management is entrusted to the cantonal offices of the PCs — also in the Canton of St. Gallen.

Who is entitled to PCs and how they are calculated

Persons who receive an AVS or AI annuity and whose determining income, net of recognized expenses, does not exceed the recognized expenses for vital needs are potentially entitled to PCs. Obtaining the AVS pension and, if necessary, supplementary benefits is a path that may seem bureaucratic but follows clear steps. Here is how to proceed concretely in the Canton of St. Gallen.

Step 1: Verify your AVS account

The first step is to request the individual AVS account statement, which lists all recorded earnings and credits for childcare or care tasks. The statement can be obtained online via the federal portal or at the cantonal compensation fund. Any missing income must be reported with supporting documentation (salary certificates, employer attestations).

Step 2: Apply for the AVS pension

The AVS pension is not paid automatically: it must be formally requested from the compensation fund of the Canton of St. Gallen approximately three to six months before reaching reference age. The application can be submitted online or in paper form and requires personal and bank details as attachments.

Step 3: Consider redeeming contribution gaps

If the account statement shows contribution gaps in the last five years, a redemption can be considered. However, the redemption is only partly tax-deductible and must be assessed case by case: for people with high incomes, it is often better to leave the gap and compensate through PC; for people with substantial assets, redemption may be more efficient.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Canton of St. Gallen Compensation Fund?
It is the cantonal body that manages the collection of contributions and payment of AVS/AI/IPG annuities for residents and workers of the Canton of St. Gallen. It operates under the supervision of the UFAS/BSV, which defines the federal regulatory framework of social security.
When are you entitled to supplementary benefits?
The PCs belong to those who receive an AVS or AI annuity and whose decisive income, net of recognized expenses (vital needs, rent, LAMal premiums), does not cover the vital minimum. The calculation takes into account net substance and income from gainful activity, and is recalculated annually.
How does the calculation of the AVS annuity work?
It is based on the sum of income subject to AVS contributions earned during working life, supplemented with credits for educational and assistance tasks. Contribution gaps proportionally reduce income. The maximum and minimum annuities are defined at the federal level and indexed periodically.
Is it possible to redeem AVS contribution gaps?
Yes, it is possible to redeem the gaps of the last five years with a voluntary payment to the compensation fund. The redemption affects the calculation of the annuity and is partially deductible from taxes, but must be assessed according to the individual income and equity situation.
How do AVS, LPP and complementary performance interact?
The AVS is the first mandatory and universal pillar; the LPP/BVG is the second professional pillar, mandatory above a certain threshold of coordinated salary. When the sum of the first and second pillar performance does not cover the vital minimum, the PCs intervene as a safety net of the first pillar.
