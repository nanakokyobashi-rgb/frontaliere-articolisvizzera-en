Context

Swiss unemployment benefits for frontier workers: opposing positions The objective of the measure is to avoid delays and inconsistencies in the payment of benefits, linked to communications between unemployment funds of different States. The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OCST) expressed support for the measure, pointing out that border workers paid contributions in Switzerland for twenty years without actually being able to claim them through Swiss allowances. Switzerland is a country with a population of about 8.5 million inhabitants, of which about 250,000 are border workers, that is, people who work and pay taxes in Switzerland but reside in another neighboring country. Most of these frontiersmen work in agriculture, industry and services, and many of them have contributed to the Swiss economy for decades. However, Swiss law states that unemployment benefits can only be claimed by those who have paid contributions in Switzerland for at least one year. This means that frontier workers who have paid contributions in Switzerland for more than a year but less than a year may not be able to claim unemployment benefits. Opposing positions

The issue of unemployment benefits for Swiss border workers has been the subject of heated debate in recent months. The measure approved by the EU Permanent Representatives Committee aroused enthusiasm from some organisations, such as the OCST, which expressed its support for the measure. However, not everyone agrees with this decision.

According to some experts, the measure could create bureaucratic problems and could further delay the payment of compensation. In addition, some border workers may not be able to claim compensation due to lack of adequate documentation.

Practical analysis

The recent decision of the Swiss government to change the rules for unemployment benefits for border workers has provoked several reactions among professionals in the sector. The measure could have significant impacts on the lives of border workers, who may have to adapt to new rules in order to receive unemployment benefits.

It is important that border workers are informed about the new requirements and deadlines to avoid delays or inconsistencies in the payment of compensation. According to official data, in 2022, around 100,000 border workers benefited from unemployment benefit in Switzerland, with an average amount of CHF 2,500 per month.

The new legislation, which came into force on 1 January 2023, requires border workers to work at least 12 months abroad to be eligible for unemployment benefits. In addition, border workers must submit a claim for compensation within 6 months of arrival in Switzerland.
