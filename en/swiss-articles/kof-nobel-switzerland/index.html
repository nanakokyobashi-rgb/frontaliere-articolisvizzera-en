KOF's assessment

Robert Hall and Michael Woodford, two American economists, received the Nobel Prize in Economics today. Commenting on the award for Switzerland is Jan-Egbert Sturm, director of KOF, the business-cycle research center of the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Zurich.

The 57-year-old Dutch professor considers the contribution of the two scholars relevant for Switzerland as well. In his statement, he presents Hall and Woodford as economists who have had a decisive impact on contemporary understanding of business-cycle fluctuations and stabilization policies.

The role of expectations

The aspect Sturm considers particularly relevant concerns expectations about the future. According to his assessment, they influence the current behavior of households and companies. This also affects the effectiveness of economic policy: what households and companies expect can enter into present decisions and influence how economic measures work.

“In Switzerland, these considerations take on considerable importance, particularly as regards the monetary policy of the Swiss National Bank, the exchange rate and international business-cycle shocks”.

The reference to Switzerland is therefore precise. Sturm cites the monetary policy of the Swiss National Bank, the exchange rate and international business-cycle shocks. His position links these three topics to the scope of the award-winning research and to the way economic policy can respond to cyclical developments.

Sturm also places KOF's work at the same intersection: understanding economic developments, empirical analysis and the possibility of stabilization through economic policy. The director's comment thus interprets the Nobel as recognition of studies connected to the interpretation of fluctuations and economic policy responses, with an expressly stated significance for Switzerland.

The text does not present a numerical forecast for the exchange rate, a new decision by the Swiss National Bank or a measure intended for households and companies. Its subject is the significance of Hall and Woodford's research for understanding business-cycle mechanisms and the effectiveness of stabilization policies.
