Yes to a neutral and independent Switzerland

Neutrality is not a comfortable choice, but it presupposes a straight back. It means resisting the pressures of the belligerents to take sides. This is our commitment, not an accessory to put on and take off according to the fashions of the moment.

Switzerland has a long tradition of neutrality, dating back to the end of the First World War. In 1920, the Swiss people voted to join the League of Nations, the international organization that would later give birth to the United Nations. However, Switzerland has always maintained its neutrality, refusing to join World War II and maintaining diplomatic relations with both sides.

Neutrality is not only a matter of principle, but also a practical choice. Switzerland has a strong and stable economy, which is largely dependent on trade and exports. If Switzerland were to side with either side, it could lose access to both sides' markets, with negative consequences for the national economy.

In addition, neutrality allows Switzerland to maintain its independence. Switzerland is not a member of the European Union, but has a free trade agreement with the EU, which allows it to export goods and services without customs. Neutrality allows Switzerland to maintain this agreement, without having to give up its sovereignty.

Why is Switzerland so important to Europe?

Switzerland is a strategic country that plays a crucial role in maintaining stability and security in Europe. With its central geographical position and historical neutrality, Switzerland is an important meeting point for European nations and a guarantor of peace and stability in the region.

Neutrality as a guarantee of peace

Switzerland's neutrality is a key factor for peace and stability in Europe. Switzerland is not a member of any military alliance and does not participate in armed conflicts. This makes it an important arbiter in conflict resolution and a guarantor of peace in the region.

"Switzerland's neutrality is a fundamental value for peace and stability in Europe." - Alain Berset, President of the Swiss Confederation

Switzerland and the EU: a complex relationship
