The Fare Structure in Graubünden

In the Canton of Graubünden, public transit passes must be understood within the context of a fare zone system. The issue goes beyond simply choosing between an annual and a monthly pass: this guide covers zones, fares, combinations with Half-Price and AG passes, as well as discounts for students and apprentices.

Four Elements to Consider Together

The first consideration is the zone. This is the reference point for determining the fare associated with the route in question. The second consideration is the duration: annual and monthly are the two options highlighted in this article. The comparison becomes meaningful only when zone and duration are considered together, rather than treating one fare as independent of the other.

The third element is the combination with half-price or AG passes. The source lists these among the factors to consider alongside season tickets, so the analysis shouldn’t stop at the chosen duration. The fourth concerns the passenger category: students and apprentices are listed among those eligible for discounts. This information must be kept separate from the zone, as it describes another criterion for comparison.

The fare network thus provides the key to organizing the information: first, identify the area; then, consider the duration; and finally, check for combinations and discounts. Annual, monthly, half-price, AG, student, and apprentice are not synonyms, but rather different categories in the analysis proposed for Graubünden.

Anyone looking for a solution can start with the mezzi pubblici guide and ask four questions: which zone, which duration, which combination, and which discount, if any?

Key points

Procedure for Choosing a Subscription Plan

The verification process can be completed in five steps, keeping data related to the rate plan community separate from personal data.

1. Define the zone. Start with the route you want to examine and identify the zone to use in the comparison. Don’t move on to the duration just yet: first, establish the scope. 2. Choose the period to examine. Compare annual and monthly options based on the period you want to cover. The question isn’t which plan is better in theory, but which of the two corresponds to the period under review. 3. Check the combinations. Verify separately whether the plan you’re evaluating includes half-price or AG benefits. This step prevents you from focusing solely on the pass’s name without considering the overall structure. 4. Look for personal discounts. If the profile you’re comparing is for students or apprentices, check the section on discounts before finalizing your comparison. Keep this check separate from the zone: one pertains to the person, the other to the fare zone. 5. Double-check for consistency. List the zone, duration, combination, and any applicable discount on the same line. Only then should you compare the fares available for the alternatives structured in the same way.

Final Check Before Making a Choice

A well-organized comparison should leave a clear record: the zone under consideration, the selected duration, the combination with half-price or AG passes, and any student or apprentice status. If any of these elements is missing, the comparison risks pitting non-comparable options against each other. This procedure does not require you to substitute one fare formula for another in advance; its purpose is to determine which details need to be verified within the fare zone.

For those who include public transportation as part of their budget, the next step is to distinguish this recurring expense from other expenditures and maintain the same reference period. An annual comparison should be treated as annual, and a monthly comparison as monthly: changing both the zone and the duration at the same time makes the result difficult to interpret. Discounts for students and apprentices should also be added at the end, after establishing the basis for the comparison.

What terms are considered for season tickets in Graubünden?

Half-Fare travelcards and GA travelcards are indicated as elements to be combined with the analysis of travelcards. They must therefore be checked after the zone and duration have been identified, so that the comparison takes into account all the factors mentioned for the canton of Graubünden. Their role must be checked in the tariff solution considered.
Which categories are associated with discounts?
