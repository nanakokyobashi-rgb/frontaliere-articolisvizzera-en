A result without a net advantage

49% of eligible voters oppose the increase in VAT intended to finance an extra annual payment of the state pension. 46% support it, while 5% are undecided. The gap of three percentage points falls within the margin of error of the survey: the first available picture therefore describes a close competition, not an already defined outcome.

The survey is SRG's first representative survey on the project. gfs.bern conducted it from 5 to 18 October and the survey was published this week. The source presents the result on the total number of eligible voters.

What the proposal funds

Two years ago, Swiss voters approved a 13th annual payment under the AVS/AHV, the old-age and survivors' insurance scheme indicated in the source under the acronym AHV. Now the Federal Council and Parliament are proposing to increase VAT by 0.4 percentage points to finance that additional benefit. The extra payment is associated with the start expected in December 2026.

The sequence separates two steps: the approval cited by the source concerns the additional annual payment; the measure now assessed in the survey is instead its financing through VAT. The survey does not present this distinction as an acquired result on the tax increase: it records the positions detected in the indicated period. The data therefore concerns the support for the increase in VAT intended to finance the service, not a new approval of the payment already decided. The source keeps the benefit and its coverage distinct.

The statement adds a caution: since the gap falls within the margin of error, the numerical distance is not enough to clearly separate the two sides. The 5% share and the fact that the campaign has just begun complete the initial picture, without turning this first survey into a forecast.

Age, income and topics compared

The distribution of consensus

The national result is not distributed equally between age groups. Older voters are more likely to support the VAT increase, while younger voters overwhelmingly reject it; the middle age group is divided. Support is strongest among those who will benefit from the extra payment when it begins in December 2026. The source describes these guidelines, without providing a separate percentage for each band.

Income weighs less. Support and opposition are broadly balanced across income groups. Belonging to a party, however, clearly changes the picture, even if the quotas attributed to individual political forces are not indicated in the material provided.

When reasons count

The clearest part of the survey concerns the arguments against it. All three tested get clear majorities. The most persuasive argues that AHV funding gaps should be closed with structural reforms, not higher taxes and levies. The source indicates a later state pension age as a central element.

The favorable arguments are less strong. 54% consider the VAT increase fair because it would guarantee the contribution of all generations. The idea that the measure represents a reasonable compromise, however, does not reach the majority, despite being the one with the greatest influence on voting intentions.

The comparison is useful because it separates the judgment on the proposal from the reaction to the reasons given to support it. The source says that, taken together, the favorable arguments garner less support than the proposal itself. It is therefore incorrect to read the 54% on equity as a percentage of overall support for the increase: it measures a specific topic, while the survey also separates voting intentions and judgment on other topics.

Key points

A margin still open

Two different measures of uncertainty

The survey presents a useful distinction between declared indecision and unconsolidated choice. The 5% indicated in the initial framework includes voters classified as undecided. Later, however, the source states that 46% of those entitled have not yet reached a final decision. This second quota includes both those who lean towards one of the two sides and those who do not know how they will vote. The number coincides with the favourable percentage of the first figure, but the definition is different: 46% of the summary measures the declared support, while the broader 46% measures the absence of a firm choice.

This distinction reduces the risk of reading the result as an immutable block. The source points out that the campaign has just begun and that opinion may still change considerably. The data from the first survey should therefore be interpreted as an initial photograph, not as a prediction of the outcome.

A campaign that can change the picture

As the campaign progresses, voters usually move closer to the position taken by the Federal Council and Parliament on government proposals. For supporters of the increase, however, the race is starting on difficult terms: the contest is close, opponents’ arguments are strong and purchasing-power concerns remain.

Support from voters over the age of 65 introduces an element of compensation. Their above-average participation could prove to be important, according to the source. This data adds a variable to the comparison between orientations by age: the survey describes attitude, while attendance can affect the actual weight of that group.

Concerns about purchasing power, that is, the cost of living, are cited as an obstacle, but the source does not offer an individual calculation of the effect of the increase. In the material provided, the perimeter remains that of the AVS/AHV: there is no comparison with the LPP/BVG nor a breakdown of the results for Cantons. The available tax data remains the increase of 0.4 percentage points of VAT and its financing purpose; it is not translated into an expense per person or family.
