Bern: protest against the tightening of asylum law (cross-border guide)

Several thousand people in Federal Square calling for health, work and education regardless of residence status and origin.

On Saturday, several thousand people took part in a demonstration in Bern by the asylum and migration movement. The initiative was directed against the tightening of asylum law and against the marginalization of people without a Swiss passport.

The mobilization in Federal Square

The demonstrators converged on Federal Square. The banners displayed during the day conveyed a central demand: to recognize fundamental rights such as health, work and education for all people, regardless of their residence status or origin.

The wording chosen by the participants does not limit the demands to a single category. The reference to residence status and origin concerns two elements explicitly mentioned in the message of the demonstration. The demand is that neither of the two determine exclusion from the rights indicated. The point made in the square is that health, work and education be guaranteed to all people, without distinction based on residence status or origin.

The demonstration added two political demands. The participants called for unlimited freedom of movement and the possibility of participating in social and political decision-making. Health, work, education, movement and participation thus make up the set of demands brought to Bern.

The organizers and the disputed issues

The demonstration was called by the organization Solidarité sans frontières, alongside around a hundred other organizations, trade unions and civil society groups. The organizers used the press release to criticize three issues: the 2027 asylum strategy, the UDC initiative for border protection and the reform of the Common European Asylum System.

Bern's day thus combines the protest against the tightening of asylum law with opposition to these policies. The source reports the demonstrators' demands and the criticisms contained in the press release, without attributing any other decisions or measures to them. The message pairs opposition to the tightening of asylum law with the demand not to marginalize people without a Swiss passport. The source presents both positions as parts of the demonstration in Bern.

Operational details

The requests that emerged in Bern have a practical reflection for those living in Switzerland even without a Swiss passport, but the source does not present them as a new rule already in effect. The key point to remember is the distinction between what the protesters are asking for and what the organizers are contesting: on one hand, rights and participation; on the other, three dossiers on asylum, borders, and the Common European Asylum System.

Five levels of the demand

Health, work, and education are indicated as fundamental rights. According to the participants, the request applies to all people and does not depend on residency status or origin. Unlimited freedom of movement adds the dimension of mobility; participation in social and political decisions adds the dimension of having a voice in society.

A practical pathway

Then the promoting body is identified: Solidarité sans frontières, together with around a hundred organizations, trade unions and civil society groups. This step serves to distinguish who called the demonstration from the political content being contested.

The third step is to separate the demands addressed to all people from the issues criticized in the statement.
