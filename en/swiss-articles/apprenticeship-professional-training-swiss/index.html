Apprenticeship is a pillar of Swiss professional training, recognized at the federal level and managed by individual cantons with specific regulations. In the canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden, as in other cantons, those wishing to start a professional training path face three crucial practical issues: where to find a training position, how the apprentice contract works, and what salary is due.

The canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden, located in northeastern Switzerland, operates under the same federal framework as every other canton: companies that hire apprentices sign a contract governed by the Code of Obligations (arts. 344–346), valid nationally. The practical modalities of training position search, minimum wage amounts, and organizational details remain the responsibility of the cantonal administration and vary between cantons.

Operational details

# Apprentice Pay: How Calculation Works

The remuneration of an apprentice varies significantly depending on the sector and economic region. In the Inner Appenzell Canton, as in every canton, the employer is obliged to pay at least the minimum set by the cantonal rules or collective agreements of the sector where it is present. Some cantons have set specific minimum wages for apprentices; others refer to industry standards set by trade associations.

In the absence of a national federal minimum wage, each canton has autonomous fixing power. The Inner Appenzell Canton, like other cantons, uses this regulatory leverage. The exact amount of the cantonal minimum wage varies annually according to specific cantonal ordinances; it is advisable to check with the Vocational Training Office of the Inner Appenzell Canton for updated data and references to category agreements.

The same contributions of ordinary employees, proportionate to the duration of work and income, apply to the internship salary:

  • AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% to be paid by the apprentice (the employer pays the additional 5.3%, total 10.6%)
  • AD/AC: 1.1% up to the annual ceiling, paid equally

Key points

Step-by-step: how to start an apprenticeship in the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden

Phase 1: Orientation and research (12–18 months before compulsory schooling)

Contact the professional orientation office of the school or canton. In this phase, aptitudes, areas of interest, and job availability in companies are assessed. Many schools in the canton organize orientation days and company visits; participating facilitates knowledge of the local market.

Phase 2: Application and interview (6–12 months before the start)

Prepare an application dossier with CV, cover letter, school certificates. Contact training companies directly or through cantonal internship search portals. Interviews can take place at the company or via video call. It is advisable to apply to multiple positions simultaneously to increase chances.

Phase 3: Signing the contract (2–3 months before the start)

Once an agreement is reached with the employer, the apprenticeship contract is signed in duplicate. The federal model (Arts. 344–346 CO) is available from cantonal authorities or industry associations. The contract must contain all essential data: duration, sector, working hours, remuneration, school frequency.

Phase 4: School registration (weeks before the start)

Registration with the cantonal or inter-cantonal vocational school responsible for the sector. Swiss vocational schools operate on an inter-cantonal basis for many sectors; the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden is part of network agreements with other cantons (Appenzell Ausserrhoden, St. Gallen, Thurgau) to cover less common specializations.

Frequently Asked Questions
How many years does an apprenticeship last in the Inner Appenzell Canton?
Duration varies by industry. Most AFC (Certificate of Vocational Education and Training) courses last 3–4 years. Some specialised sectors, such as certain aspects of craftsmanship or industry, can provide courses of 2 years (for holders of high school certificates with good results) or up to 4 years. The exact duration is specified in the apprenticeship contract and in the cantonal legislation of the sector. Check with the Vocational Training Office of the Inner Appenzell Canton.
What is the minimum salary of an apprentice in the canton?
The Inner Appenzell Canton, like every canton, independently fixes the minimum wage for apprentices (absent a national federal minimum). The amount varies by sector and economic region; some sectors adopt category standards. We recommend checking the updated value at the Vocational Training Office of the Inner Appenzell Canton or at the trade association of the chosen sector. The amounts are reviewed annually according to cantonal ordinances.
Is an apprentice required to pay AVS/AI and health insurance contributions?
Yes. The same social contributions as employees apply to the internship salary: AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3% paid by the apprentice (the employer pays the additional 5.3%), AD/AC at 1.1% up to the annual maximum. Health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory for all residents and must be taken out within 3 months of the start of the traineeship; it is borne by the apprentice (private per capita, cantonal premiums). The canton may grant subsidies to low incomes.
What is the difference between AFC and Professional Maturity?
The AFC (Certificate of Vocational Education and Training) is the standard, nationally recognised qualification of completion of the professional traineeship. Professional Maturity (MP) is a complementary degree that allows access to university schools of applied sciences (sup, SUPSI, ZHAW). The AFC is obtained automatically at the end of the internship after passing the final exam; the MP is optional and requires attendance at additional, parallel or subsequent courses. What happens if a company denies an internship on discriminatory grounds?
Discrimination in the recruitment of apprentices is prohibited by federal (art. 8 Federal Constitution) and cantonal regulations. In the event of a suspected discriminatory refusal, the apprentice or parents may lodge a complaint with the relevant cantonal office (Vocational Training Office or Cantonal Human Rights Office). Specialized organizations (SOS Racism, Equality) offer free assistance.
