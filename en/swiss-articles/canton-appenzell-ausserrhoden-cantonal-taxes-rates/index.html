Cantonal taxes External Appenzell: rates and deductions (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 25 August 2026

Complete guide to cantonal and municipal taxes in Appenzello Esterno: 3-level structure, rates, allowable deductions, declaratory deadlines and online procedure.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Swiss taxes on 3 levels: federal, cantonal, municipal
  • Each canton has its own rates; municipalities apply multipliers on the cantonal value
  • Declaration deadline: 15 March or 30 April according to the canton
  • Equal federal deductions throughout Switzerland; cantonal deductions vary by canton

Key facts

  • What: Income and wealth tax system on 3 levels (federal, cantonal, municipal)
  • Where: Canton Appenzello Outer, north-eastern Switzerland
  • Who: Residents and domiciled in the canton (natural and self-employed persons)
  • Federal deadline: 15 March or 30 April according to cantonal provisions
  • Tax levels: 3 (Confederation + Canton + Municipality)
  • Multiplier: Each municipality applies a percentage on the cantonal value

Taxation in Switzerland is divided into three distinct levels: the federal direct tax (IFD), the cantonal tax and the municipal tax. Those who reside in the Outer Appenzell Canton, as in any other canton, are subject to this tripartition. While the DFI is set by the Confederation and applies equally throughout Switzerland, cantonal and municipal rates vary significantly based on the tax competence of the canton and municipality.

Operational details

Practical analysis: scenarios and contributions

Let's assume a hypothetical scenario: an employee residing in the Outer Appenzell Canton with gross income of CHF 80'000 per annum. Compulsory social security contributions, which reduce the net income on which the DFI is calculated, are deducted from the gross:

  • AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% to be paid by the employee (corresponding to 10.6% total between employee and employer)
  • Unemployment insurance (AD): 1.1% up to an annual maximum
  • Non-occupational accident insurance (LAINF): 0.7–1.5% according to the sector of activity

If the worker is subject to a pension fund (LPP/BVG), additional progressive contributions are added by age group: 7% for 25–34 years, 10% for 35–44 years, 15% for 45–54 years, 18% for 55+ years up to the reference age.

Federal and Cantonal Deductions

After calculating the net income from social security deductions, the allowable deductions are applied: LPP contributions (pension fund), interest payable on mortgage loans, LAMal premiums (health insurance), optional AVS contributions for self-employed, donations to public utilities, medical expenses not covered by insurance. How to Declare Taxes in the Outer Appenzellian Canton

The declaration procedure takes place entirely online through the portal of the Appenzellian cantonal tax administration. Here are the main steps:

Step 1: Gather Required Documents

Before proceeding, have the following documents at hand:

  • Employer income certificates (or balance sheet and accounts for self-employed individuals)
  • Bank and postal account statements (for interest, dividends, plus/minus valuations)
  • Certificate of housing tax (for property owners or tenants)
  • Statements of AVS/LPP contributions paid during the year
  • Receipts and documents for medical expenses not covered, donations, passive interest on mortgages

Step 2: Access the Online Portal

The Outer Appenzellian Canton provides an online portal dedicated to declaration. Access is via personal credentials (contributor number, AVS number, password). Consult the Appenzellian cantonal tax administration website for login instructions and to check the availability of biometric identification or digital certificate.

Step 3: Complete the Guided Declaration

The portal guides you step by step in entering data: gross income, deductions, assets, family burdens, children in care, significant medical expenses. Fields are usually pre-filled where possible based on data provided by third parties (employer, banks, pension funds). Verify pre-filled data and correct if necessary.

Step 4: Verify the Calculation and Send

Before sending, verify the accuracy of all entered data. The portal automatically calculates the estimated tax (federal + cantonal + communal), showing a preview of the tax due and the refund or balance to be paid. Once satisfied with the calculation, send the declaration by the deadline (March 15 or April 30, depending on the year).

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between federal, cantonal and municipal tax?
In Switzerland, income taxes are divided into three levels. The federal direct tax (IFD) is set by the Confederation and is the same in all cantons. Cantonal tax varies from canton to canton, and the Outer Appenzell Canton has its own rates. Finally, the municipal tax is calculated by applying a multiplier to the cantonal value, so that two different municipalities in the same canton have different final rates. All three contribute to the total tax burden.
How do municipal multipliers work in the Outer Appenzell Canton?
Each municipality of the Canton of Appenzello Outer applies a multiplier to the value of the cantonal tax. For example, if the cantonal base is 100 CHF and the multiplier of your municipality is 1.15, the municipal tax due is 115 CHF. This means that different municipalities have different tax burdens. The specific multiplier of your municipality can be consulted on the portal of the cantonal tax administration.
What are the main deductions from taxable income?
Common federal deductions across Switzerland include: mandatory and optional AVS/AI contributions, contributions to pension funds (LPPs), interest on mortgage debt, LAMal health insurance premiums, contributions to foundations, donations. The Outer Appenzell Canton may add its own cantonal deductions (e.g. medical expenses, dependent children, training expenses). For a complete list, please consult the cantonal tax administration.
When does the tax return in the Outer Appenzell Canton expire?
The federal and cantonal declaration must be filed by March 15 or April 30 according to the provisions of the canton of residence. In the Canton of Appenzello Esterno it is necessary to check on the portal of the cantonal tax administration what the exact deadline is for the current year. Most cantons apply on April 30.
I am a recent resident of the Outer Appenzell Canton: how do my taxes change?
If you have just established your residence in the Outer Appenzell Canton, the start date is decisive: if you arrived on January 1, you pay taxes for the entire year; if in June, you usually pay taxes pro rata (6 months). It is essential to check the entitlement to specific cantonal deductions (e.g. reduction of LAMal premiums for low incomes). Consult the cantonal administration for your specific case.
