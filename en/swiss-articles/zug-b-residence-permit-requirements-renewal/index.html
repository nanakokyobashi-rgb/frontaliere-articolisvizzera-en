Permit B to Zug: requirements, procedure and renewal (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 30 August 2026

How to obtain and renew the residence permit B in the Canton of Zug: documents, duration up to 5 years for EU/EFTA and bureaucratic steps.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Permit B issued by the Cantonal Office of Migration of Zug
  • Requires employment contract, adequate housing and means of subsistence
  • Typical duration 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens, renewable on request
  • Documents and forms available on the cantonal portal

Key facts

  • What: Residence permit B (Aufenthaltsbewilligung) in the Canton of Zug
  • When: Issuance and renewal according to the Federal Law on Foreigners (LStrI)
  • Where: Canton of Zug, German-speaking Switzerland
  • Who: Cantonal Office for Migration (Amt für Migration) in Zug, under the supervision of the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM)
  • Requirements: Profit-making activity, adequate accommodation, ascertained identity, sufficient financial means
  • Duration: Up to 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens; duration linked to the employment contract for third-country nationals
  • Renewal: Early application before the deadline, verification of conditions, cantonal decision
  • Documents: Passport, employment contract, accommodation certificate, photos, cantonal forms

Operational details

Obtaining the B permit in the Canton of Zug means entering an administrative system that combines federal legislation and cantonal practice. The framework conditions are defined by the LStrI and the ALC, but the methods of submitting the application, the required documentation and the processing times are established at the cantonal level. This creates concrete differences between an applicant who goes to the Zug office compared, for example, to those who go to the similar office of another Canton.

Practical implications for the applicant

The first practical decision is about timing. The B permit is never automatic: even for EU/EFTA citizens, renewal requires the submission of an application for an extension before the expiry of the licence, accompanied by verification of the original conditions (employment, accommodation, integration) and a new decision by the cantonal authority. Sending the application late may result in the interruption of the right of residence and the obligation to leave the country.

The documentation to be produced typically includes the valid passport, a passport format photo, the employment contract or an employer confirmation, the accommodation attestation (rental contract or owner's certificate), and the specific forms provided by the Cantonal Office of Migration. Reunited family members also need certificates of marital status and, if required, documents attesting to the family relationship.

Key points

Obtaining or renewing the B permit in the Canton of Zug requires a precise procedure, marked by bureaucratic steps that the applicant can manage independently or with the support of a consultant specialized in immigration law. Below is an operational procedure based on federal and cantonal practice.

Step-by-step procedure

Step 1 — Document collection. The applicant must prepare: a valid passport or identity document, an employment contract (or confirmation from the employer), proof of accommodation in the Canton of Zug, a passport-size photo, and the completed cantonal application forms. For family reunification: translated and, where applicable, legalized civil status certificates.

Step 2 — Submission of the application. The application is submitted to the Cantonal Migration Office of Zug, either directly or through the municipality of residence. Some municipalities act as a first-level desk and forward the documentation to the cantonal office. It is advisable to check on the Canton's website whether the municipality of residence offers this service.

Step 3 — Awaiting the decision. The Cantonal Office verifies compliance with the requirements (employment, accommodation, sufficient financial means, and any relevant absence of criminal record) and issues the B permit. For EU/EFTA citizens with an employment contract, the decision is generally quick; for more complex cases, processing times are longer.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who issues the B permit in the Canton of Zug?
The issuance and management of permit B is the responsibility of the Cantonal Office for Migration (Amt für Migration) in Zug, which operates on the basis of the Federal Law on Foreigners (LStrI) and in coordination with the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) in Bern. The methods of submitting the application and the forms are established at cantonal level.
What is the duration of permit B for EU/EFTA citizens?
For EU/EFTA citizens, federal practice provides for the issuance of a B permit for a period of five years, which can be extended. For third-country nationals, the duration is generally linked to the validity of the employment contract. The renewal requires a new application to the Cantonal Office before the expiry date.
What documents are needed for the application?
The documentation includes: valid passport, passport format photo, employment contract or employer confirmation, proof of accommodation (rental contract or owner's certificate) and specific forms provided by the Cantonal Office of Migration. For reunited family members, civil status certificates and documents attesting to the family bond are required.
When should you start the renewal process?
Practice recommends starting the extension procedure at least two to three months before the expiry of the permit B. Submitting the application late may result in the interruption of the right of residence and the obligation to leave the country. Permit B does not renew automatically.
What post-release obligations must the holder carry out?
After issue, the holder must register with the Municipality of residence, take out compulsory health insurance (LAMal) within three months of arrival and, if starting a lucrative activity, register for social contributions (AVS/AI/IPG, AD, LAINF, LPP). It is also subject to the Swiss tax system at three levels (federal, cantonal and municipal).
