The B residence permit is governed by the Federal Act on Foreign Nationals and Borders (SECO). It is a long‑term residence permit, renewable upon expiry, which allows the holder to settle permanently in Switzerland. In the Canton of Jura, the authority for issuing and renewing it lies with the Cantonal Migration Office, the body responsible for verifying compliance with the legal requirements.

How the B permit works

The B permit is distinct from the L permit (short‑term, up to one year) and the C permit (settlement, usually after ten years of residence). To obtain or renew the B, the regulations require proof of regular accommodation, LAMal insurance coverage within three months of arrival, and regular AVS/AHV contributions. LAMal insurance is mandatory for all residents, with deductibles ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 depending on income.

The Swiss tax system provides three levels of taxation: direct federal tax, cantonal tax and communal tax, each with its own multipliers. This affects the tax burden of the B permit holder, who must file tax returns at the federal and cantonal levels, taking into account the deductions provided for AVS/AHV contributions and LAMal insurance expenses. Managing these aspects is crucial for those who intend to stay long‑term in the canton.

Operational details

Practical analysis

The B permit directly affects different areas of daily life in Switzerland. From a tax point of view, the holder is subject to direct federal tax, VAT (which affects goods and services) and cantonal and municipal taxes, with multipliers varying from canton to canton. In the case of the Jura, the cantonal legislation establishes a specific multiplier that applies to the cantonal tax, while the municipalities apply an additional multiplier on the result.

Implications for Social Security and Health

AVS/AHV contributions, equal to 5.3% of the employee's salary, are mandatory for all B permit holders. These contributions finance old-age pension (AVS) and disability insurance (AI). In parallel, LAMal insurance must be taken out within three months; the premium is determined by the canton of residence and income, and deductibles can range from CHF 300 to CHF 2500. Compliance with these obligations guarantees access to health care and a future pension.

Comparison with other permits

Compared to the L permit, the B offers greater stability because it is renewable and not limited to one year. However, unlike the C permit, the B does not automatically confer the right to vote or the possibility of naturalization; for the latter it is necessary to have lived at least ten years (eight for EU/EFTA citizens) and meet cantonal and municipal requirements.

Key points

Step-by-step action

1. Gather the documents – residence certificate in Jura, LAMal coverage certificate (insurance policy), AVS/AHV contribution statements, employment contract or proof of income. 2. Verify the deadlines – the expiry date of the B permit is indicated on the residence document; the renewal application must be submitted at least 30 days before the expiry at the Cantonal Migration Office. 3. Fill out the renewal form – the form is available on the official website of the canton of Jura; you must provide personal data, employment situation and confirm compliance with AVS/AHV contributions and LAMal. 4. Pay any fees – the renewal may involve payment of an administrative fee; the amount varies and is not specified in the source, so it is advisable to check on the office portal. 5. Submit the application – deliver the complete file to the competent office, in person or via certified mail. 6. Wait for the outcome – the office acts within the established deadlines; in case of approval, the new B permit will be issued with the same duration as the previous one.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main requirements to obtain or renew the B in Jura permit?
Permit B requires proof of regular residence in the canton, LAMal insurance taken out within three months of arrival and compliance with AVS/AHV contributions (5.3% for the employee). The application for renewal must be submitted to the Cantonal Office of Migration at least 30 days before the expiry date.
How much should I contribute to the AVS/AHV with permit B?
The AVS/AHV contribution for the employee is 5.3% of the gross salary, equal to 10.6% overall with the employer's share. These payments finance old-age pension and disability insurance.
When should I take out LAMal insurance?
LAMal health insurance is mandatory for all residents and must be taken out within three months of arrival in Switzerland. Premium varies by canton and depends on income, with deductibles ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2500.
