E-bike accident: cyclist dies in hospital

A tragic event occurred in the Bernese Oberland region, where a man died from serious injuries sustained during an accident with an electric bicycle (e-bike). The man, engaged in an excursion to Lauenen bei Gstaad, fell and sustained serious injuries.

He was immediately rescued by the people present and then transported in critical condition to the hospital with a Rega helicopter. According to the first information, the man had reached a speed of about 30 km/h when he fell on the paved road. The accident occurred in the area of the old Bernina railway track, one of the oldest and most dangerous roads in the canton.

The accident occurred in an area that is very popular with tourists and cycling enthusiasts, who flock to the streets of Bernese Oberland every year. The region is known for its beautiful views and scenic streets, but also for its narrow and dangerous streets.

The police released a statement stating that the accident occurred due to a combination of factors, including excessive speed and inattention on the part of the cyclist. Police are still investigating the incident and have not released any further information.

This law was passed by the Swiss Parliament on 28 April 1996 and has undergone numerous changes over the years.

LAMal is mandatory for all Swiss and foreigners residing in Switzerland, regardless of their nationality. The policy must be taken out with a private insurance or with a group insurance, such as the health insurance of the canton of Bern. The minimum amount of the LAMal is 3,400 Swiss francs per year, but it may vary depending on the region and the type of insurance chosen.

In the event of an accident, the victim is entitled to receive compensation for medical expenses and loss of income. LAMal also covers the costs of rehabilitation and wound care. However, it is important to note that LAMal does not cover expenses for the treatment of pre-existing illnesses or for the treatment of illnesses that have not been declared to insurance.
