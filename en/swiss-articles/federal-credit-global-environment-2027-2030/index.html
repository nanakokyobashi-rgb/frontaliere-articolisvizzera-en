On 26 August 2026, the Federal Council adopted a message to Parliament concerning a total commitment appropriation of CHF 176.23 million to support the global environment in the four-year period 2027–2030. The decision represents Switzerland's commitment to financing international environmental projects, through four main channels of intervention.

The largest portion, equal to CHF 132.10 million, flows into the Global Environment Facility, known by the acronym GEF (Global Environment Facility). This instrument is one of the main sources of international funding for environmental protection projects in developing countries. Operational details

The strategic distribution of funds

The structure of the commitment credit reflects Switzerland's international environmental priorities, with a clear hierarchy between the different intervention instruments. The Global Environment Facility absorbs 75 percent of the total appropriation (132.10 million out of 176.23), confirming the central role of this mechanism in financing global environmental protection.

The GEF operates according to a co-financing logic: Switzerland does not finance projects on its own, but contributes to mobilising larger resources. With its 132.10 million, the country participates in the ninth reconstitution of the fund, a fundraising cycle among international donors aimed at renewing the operational capacity of the instrument for the four-year period 2027–2030.

# Impact multiplication strategies

With regard to the specialized climate funds (LDCF and SCCF), the Federal Council explains that Switzerland is committed to ensuring that these funds have the greatest possible impact and attract additional private funds for environmental protection. This means that the Swiss contribution is not purely disbursing, but aims to catalyze additional investments, thus expanding the effect of the allocated public money.

Key points

The parliamentary approval process

The commitment credit, although adopted by the Federal Council on 26 August 2026, does not automatically take effect. The message must be submitted to Parliament for formal approval. This legislative step is essential: no federal appropriation can be operational without the green light of the representatives elected by the Chambers.

Parliament, once it has received the message, will have to examine it in the relevant parliamentary committees, assess the financial and political implications of the commitment credit, and then proceed to the vote in both Houses. Only after this approval will the Federal Council be able to proceed with the payment of the funds according to the schedule established for the period 2027–2030.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the exact amount of the approved commitment credit?
The commitment appropriation adopted by the Federal Council on 26 August 2026 amounts to CHF 176.23 million. This appropriation is spread over four years (2027-2030) and is intended to finance international environmental projects through four channels: the Global Environment Facility (132.10 million), the Multilateral Ozone Fund (12 million), and the two specialized climate funds LDCF and SCCF (29.33 million).
How is the commitment credit divided between the different funds?
The total credit of 176.23 million is distributed as follows: 132.10 million francs to the Global Environment Facility (GEF), 12 million to the Multilateral Ozone Fund, and 29.33 million to the two Specialized Climate Funds (LDCF and SCCF). The largest share goes to the GEF, which finances projects in developing countries on climate, biodiversity, water resources, pollution and desertification.
What does the Global Environment Facility (GEF) fund?
The GEF is one of the main sources of international funding for environmental protection projects in developing countries. It funds projects focused on five critical areas: climate change, biodiversity, water conservation, pollution reduction and combating desertification. Switzerland participates in the ninth reconstitution of the fund with CHF 132.10 million.
When does the commitment credit go into effect?
The commitment credit was adopted by the Federal Council on 26 August 2026, but does not automatically take effect. It must be submitted to Parliament for formal approval. Once approved by Parliament, the appropriations will become operational for the period 2027-2030 according to the established schedule.
What has the Multilateral Ozone Fund achieved since 1991?
The Multilateral Ozone Fund, established in 1991 for the implementation of the Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer, has achieved a significant result: it has helped to reduce by about 90 percent the ozone-depleting substances used in developing countries. Switzerland contributes CHF 12 million to the fund.
