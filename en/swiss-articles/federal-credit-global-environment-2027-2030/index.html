Switzerland invests €176 million in global environment fund

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 August 2026

The Federal Council approved on 26 August 2026 a commitment appropriation of CHF 176.23 million for the global environment in the period 2027–2030.

Context

In brief

  • Federal Council approves allocation of 176,23 million CHF for the global environment 2027–2030
  • Funds allocated to the global environmental fund, ozone protection and adaptation to climate change
  • Request for Parliament's approval of the commitment credit

Key facts

  • What: Commitment credit for international environmental financing
  • When: Adopted August 26, 2026, valid from 2027 to 2030
  • Where: Bern (Swiss Federal Council)
  • Who: Federal Council of Switzerland
  • Total amount: 176,23 million francs
  • Main fund: Global Environment Facility (GEF)
  • Breakdown: 132,10 million to GEF, 12 million to the Ozone Fund, 29,33 million to specialized climate funds

The Federal Council adopted, on August 26, 2026, a message addressed to Parliament concerning a total commitment credit of 176,23 million francs allocated to supporting the global environment over the four-year period 2027–2030. The decision represents Switzerland's commitment to financing international environmental projects through four main channels of intervention.

The largest share, amounting to 132,10 million francs, goes to the Global Environment Facility, known by the acronym GEF (Global Environment Facility). This instrument is one of the main sources of international financing for environmental protection projects in developing countries. Switzerland's participation in the ninth replenishment of the GEF enables the country to finance projects focused on critical areas: climate, biodiversity, water resources, pollution and combating desertification. The other financing instruments

The commitment credit also provides for 12 million francs for the Multilateral Fund for the Ozone. Established in 1991 to implement the Montreal Protocol on substances that deplete the ozone layer, this fund has achieved significant results: it has helped reduce by approximately 90 percent the hazardous substances used in developing countries that are harmful to the ozone layer.

A third line of financing concerns the two specialized climate funds: the Least Developed Countries Fund (LDCF) and the Special Climate Change Fund (SCCF). A total of 29,33 million francs is allocated to these instruments. These funds have the specific task of financing climate change adaptation measures aimed at the poorest countries and small island states, categories particularly vulnerable to the impacts of global warming.

Operational details

The strategic allocation of funds

The structure of the commitment credit reflects Switzerland's international environmental priorities, with a clear hierarchy among the various instruments of intervention. The Global Environment Facility absorbs 75 percent of the total allocation (132,10 million out of 176,23), confirming this mechanism's central role in financing global environmental protection.

The GEF operates according to a co-financing logic: Switzerland does not finance the projects alone, but rather helps mobilize broader resources. With its 132,10 million, the country is participating in the fund's ninth replenishment, a fundraising cycle among international donors intended to renew the instrument's operational capacity for the four-year period 2027–2030.

Strategies for multiplying impact

With regard to the specialized climate funds (LDCF and SCCF), the Federal Council explains that Switzerland is committed to ensuring that these funds have the greatest possible impact and attract additional private funds for environmental protection. This means that the Swiss contribution is not purely disbursement-based, but aims to catalyze additional investments, thereby expanding the effect of the public money allocated.

The Multilateral Fund for the Ozone Layer, with its 12 million francs, represents a complement to a global policy for protecting the atmosphere. Its track record of success — a 90 percent reduction in harmful substances in developing countries — demonstrates how coordinated international financing can achieve concrete and verifiable environmental objectives.

These allocations fall within the broader framework of Swiss environmental policy, which aims to combine domestic action with the financing of projects in developing countries, in accordance with the principles of international cooperation and the fight against climate change on a global scale.

Key points

The parliamentary approval process

Although the commitment credit was adopted by the Federal Council on August 26, 2026, it does not enter into force automatically. The dispatch must be submitted to Parliament for formal approval. This legislative step is essential: no federal appropriation can become operational without the approval of the representatives elected to the Chambers.

Once Parliament has received the dispatch, it will have to examine it in the competent parliamentary committees, assess the financial and political implications of the commitment credit, and then proceed to a vote in both Chambers. Only after this approval will the Federal Council be able to disburse the funds according to the schedule established for the 2027–2030 period.

Implications for the federal budget

In terms of the federal budget, the commitment credit of 176,23 million francs represents a significant allocation of public resources to international environmental cooperation. This allocation represents Switzerland's commitment to international environmental financing, in line with global priorities for conservation and climate adaptation.

For Swiss taxpayers, these 176,23 million constitute a fraction of the overall federal budget, used to finance environmental projects in distant geographical contexts but with global impacts (protection of the atmosphere, biodiversity conservation, climate adaptation). International environmental financing, although it does not generate direct and immediate economic benefits in Switzerland, contributes to global environmental stability and the mitigation of long-term climate risks.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the exact amount of the approved commitment credit?
The commitment appropriation adopted by the Federal Council on 26 August 2026 amounts to CHF 176.23 million. This appropriation is spread over four years (2027-2030) and is intended to finance international environmental projects through four channels: the Global Environment Facility (132.10 million), the Multilateral Ozone Fund (12 million), and the two specialized climate funds LDCF and SCCF (29.33 million).
How is the commitment credit divided between the different funds?
The total credit of 176.23 million is distributed as follows: 132.10 million francs to the Global Environment Facility (GEF), 12 million to the Multilateral Ozone Fund, and 29.33 million to the two Specialized Climate Funds (LDCF and SCCF). The largest share goes to the GEF, which finances projects in developing countries on climate, biodiversity, water resources, pollution and desertification.
What does the Global Environment Facility (GEF) fund?
The GEF is one of the main sources of international funding for environmental protection projects in developing countries. It funds projects focused on five critical areas: climate change, biodiversity, water conservation, pollution reduction and combating desertification. Switzerland participates in the ninth reconstitution of the fund with CHF 132.10 million.
When does the commitment credit go into effect?
The commitment credit was adopted by the Federal Council on 26 August 2026, but does not automatically take effect. It must be submitted to Parliament for formal approval. Once approved by Parliament, the appropriations will become operational for the period 2027-2030 according to the established schedule.
What has the Multilateral Ozone Fund achieved since 1991?
