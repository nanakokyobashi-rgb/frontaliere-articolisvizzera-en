Calculation of AVS annuity and contribution gaps

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 23 August 2026

How the cantonal compensation fund works in the calculation of the AVS annuity, what contribution gaps reduce the amount and how the supplementary benefits fill the living minimum.

Context

In brief

  • The cantonal compensation office manages the calculation of the OASI pension in the Swiss first pillar
  • Contribution gaps reduce the pension amount in proportion to the missing months
  • Supplementary benefits ensure coverage of the minimum subsistence level when the pension is insufficient
  • The application process begins 3-4 months before reaching retirement age

Key facts

  • What: Cantonal compensation office responsible for calculating and paying the OASI pension
  • When: Pension application 3-4 months before the reference age (64 for men, 63 for women)
  • Where: At the compensation office of the canton of residence (Obwalden or another)
  • Who: UFAS/BSV coordinates at federal level; cantonal offices manage matters locally
  • Amount: Depends on contributions paid during the working career and contribution gaps
  • Duration: Minimum 1 year of contributions to qualify for the pension; a full contribution record is typically 44 years for men
  • Procedure: Documentation sent automatically; the form must be returned at least 2 months before the desired date

In the Swiss social security system, the first pillar (OASI) represents the foundation of old-age security. Each canton has its own cantonal compensation office responsible for managing the calculation of the OASI pension for its residents. The office collects and records contributions throughout the entire working career and, when the reference age is reached, calculates the pension amount based on the accumulated contributions.

This process is coordinated at federal level by UFAS (the Federal Social Insurance Office, BSV in German), which establishes uniform calculation criteria and ensures the fairness of the system throughout Switzerland. However, it is the cantonal office that actually manages the contribution records of individual insured persons, communicates deadlines and notifies them when entitlement to the benefit arises.

Role and function of the cantonal compensation office

The compensation office is not a bank, but rather a public administration responsible for paying first-pillar benefits (OASI). In the canton of Obwalden, as in the other Swiss cantons, the office keeps track of every month of contributions paid by the worker (whether employed or self-employed) or subsequently credited by way of a buyback. It maintains detailed records of each insured person's contribution history, verifies that the data is complete and, as retirement approaches, calculates the final pension amount according to the federal formula.

The pension calculation follows a scheme established at federal level: the contributions paid are converted into a "number of contribution years" and an "average coordinated income". On this basis, the office applies the federal calculation tables to determine the monthly amount of the old-age pension. This system ensures that every month of contributions paid carries a specific weight in the final result.

Impact of contribution gaps on the pension

If contribution gaps are recorded during the working career (periods in which contributions were not paid), these missing months reduce both the overall number of contribution years and the average income used in the calculation. The result is a lower pension than it would have been if the contribution record had been continuous. The cantonal compensation office identifies each gap and informs the insured person how many months of contributions are missing from the record.

Operational details

The calculation of the AVS pension follows well-defined steps at the federal level, even though they are actually carried out at the cantonal level. The pension amount depends on two main factors: the number of credited contribution years in the insurance account and the average coordinated income over the entire working career.

The system is progressive: it is not enough to have paid many contributions overall; continuity and length of the contribution career also matter. A worker who enters the labor market late will receive a lower pension than a colleague who started earlier, even if both paid the same total amount of contributions over the course of their employment. This federal mechanism encourages career continuity and favors those who enter the workforce at a young age. Similarly, secondo pilastro (LPP/BVG) completes pension coverage with mandatory employer contributions.

The concrete impact of contribution gaps

A common scenario concerns periods of job searching between one job and the next. Even just a few consecutive months of unemployment, if not covered by unemployment insurance, create a gap in the AVS contribution record. Although a single 6-month gap may appear to have a modest impact, the accumulation of multiple gaps over the course of a career can reduce the final benefit by a significant percentage.

For example, if an insured person completes 44 years of contributions (rather than the 45 years of an uninterrupted career), the calculated pension will be proportionally lower because approximately 2% of the career is missing. The compensation fund has detailed calculation tables that it applies automatically to determine the exact reduction in monthly CHF. In the case of more significant gaps (for example, 3-4 years of inactivity), the reduction in the pension can reach 6-8% of the potential amount.

Concrete opportunity to fill contribution gaps

An important option for those who have identified contribution gaps is the possibility of retroactively paying the missing contributions as "voluntary contributions". The cantonal compensation fund must inform the insured person, within specific deadlines and well before retirement, of the total amount of outstanding contributions they could pay to partially or completely fill the gaps. This is particularly relevant for workers who have spent periods outside Switzerland or who have experienced significant interruptions.

The cost of filling the gaps depends on the contribution rate in force when the payment is made (applied retroactively to the gap periods), not on the historical moment when the gap arose. For many insured persons approaching retirement, filling the gaps is economically worthwhile because it directly increases the amount of the pension they will receive each month for the rest of their lives. As an indication, filling 1-2 years of gaps typically requires a one-off payment of a few thousand francs and results in a monthly increase in the pension that is recouped within a few years of retirement.

Key points

How to apply for the AHV pension and check contribution gaps

The procedure for applying for the AHV pension begins approximately 3-4 months before reaching the reference age. The cantonal compensation office automatically sends the insured person a complete package containing the application form, a detailed extract from the contribution account (where all gaps identified in the system are highlighted), and operating instructions.

Step-by-step application procedure:

1. Carefully check the extract: Check every detail of the document received from the compensation office to identify any gaps, unrecorded contributions, or discrepancies in coordinated income. If you notice inconsistent data or missing contributions, contact the office immediately to request a correction. This phase is critical because errors can permanently reduce the pension.

2. Assess whether filling gaps is worthwhile: Ask the compensation office for a projection of the monthly pension increase that would result from paying voluntary contributions. Compare the total amount to be paid with the annual pension increase and the expected years of life in retirement to assess whether it is worthwhile.

3. Complete and sign the application form: Return the signed form to the cantonal office within the indicated timeframe (usually at least 2 months before the desired retirement start date). If you have decided to fill gaps, optionally attach the payment for the voluntary contributions.

4. Receive the notification and pension commencement: The office will formally communicate the final pension amount, the exact date on which payments begin (the first day of the month following the attainment of the reference age), and the payment method (usually via a current account).

Supplementary benefits: the vital minimum backstop

When the AHV pension, possibly supplemented by other income (occupational pension, third pillar, property income, widowhood pensions), does not reach the subsistence minimum established at cantonal level, the cantonal supplementary benefits automatically intervene to make up the difference. These are welfare benefits financed through general taxation (federal and cantonal), not pension contributions.

Supplementary benefits mainly cover: rent and housing expenses (up to a cantonal maximum), contributions to mandatory health insurance (LAMal) through cantonal premium subsidies, and home-care expenses for those who require ongoing medical support. Access is subject to an income and wealth assessment based on uniform federal criteria. This is not an automatic benefit: it must be explicitly requested from the competent cantonal office, including simultaneously with the AHV pension application.

Practical next steps:

Contact the Obwalden cantonal compensation office at least 3-4 months before your desired retirement date to receive the official contribution account extract and the application documentation. If you expect to have paid contributions in several Swiss cantons or if you have worked abroad and paid contributions into foreign systems, report this immediately: the office will coordinate with the other authorities to ensure that your full contribution history is taken into account in accordance with international treaties.

Use the calcolatore della rendita AVS available for a preliminary estimate based on your personal data. To better understand how contribution gaps affect your overall pension provision — also taking into account secondo pilastro LPP/BVG and terzo pilastro 3a — consult the detailed guides or contact the competent office in your canton of residence.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between the cantonal clearing house and the UFAS/BSV?
The cantonal compensation fund is a local administrative body that actually manages the tax registers and calculates the AVS annuities for the residents of the canton. UFAS (Federal Office of Social Insurance, in German BSV) is the federal body that defines the uniform calculation criteria, checks the correct application of the rules and ensures the uniformity of the system throughout Switzerland. Simply: UFAS/BSV sets the federal rules, the cantonal fund applies them in the territory.
How do contribution gaps affect my AVS income?
Each contribution gap (period in which AVS contributions have not been paid) reduces both the total number of years of contribution and the average income used in the calculation, resulting in a lower income. For example, 2 years of gap reduces the average rent by about 4%. The clearinghouse identifies all gaps in your record and communicates the specific impact on your annuity amount. It is possible to retroactively fill the gaps with voluntary payments before applying for retirement.
Can I claim supplementary benefits if my AVS annuity is insufficient?
Yes. If the AVS pension (possibly supplemented by LPP, third pillar, or other income) does not cover the minimum living allowance established by the canton, you are entitled to claim the supplementary benefits. They are welfare aid financed by general taxation, not social security benefits. They must be explicitly requested at the competent cantonal office, after verification of income and assets according to federal criteria. They are not automatic.
When and how should I submit the AVS annuity application?
The compensation fund automatically sends the documentation 3-4 months before reaching the reference age (64 years for men, 63 for women). You must return the completed and signed form at least 2 months before your desired retirement start date. The cashier will process the request and communicate the final amount of the annuity and the exact start date of disbursement (first of the following month).
Can I fill the contribution gaps before I retire?
Yes, it is a very common and often economically advantageous practice. You can retroactively pay voluntary contributions for gap periods before reaching the reference age, within the deadlines indicated by the cantonal fund (commonly communicated with the contribution account statement). The cashier provides an estimate of the monthly increase in income that would result from the full payment, allowing you to assess the cost-benefit ratio of the payment.
