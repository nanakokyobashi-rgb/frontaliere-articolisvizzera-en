Short-term permit L in Zug: validity, extensions & transfer to B (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 17 August 2026

Maximum duration of the L permit in the Canton of Zug, conditions for the extension, constraints with the employer and requirements to move to the B residence permit.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The short-term L permit in the Canton of Zug is valid for up to 12 months
  • Extension requires continuity of work and adequate accommodation
  • The transition to permit B is not automatic and requires 5 years of regular residence
  • The L permit is linked to the employer: change of job = new application

Key facts

  • What: Short-term leave (up to 1 year)
  • Where: Canton of Zug
  • Who: Non-EU/EFTA workers with a contract <12 months
  • Extension: Possible for similar periods, case-by-case evaluation
  • Constraint: Linked to the employer and the specific role

The short-term L permit in the Canton of Zug is a temporary authorisation issued to non-EU/EFTA workers for employment of up to 12 months. This canton, known for its advantageous tax regime (cantonal IFD rates among the lowest in Switzerland), enforces federal immigration rules with specific cantonal procedures. The initial validity of the L permit is closely linked to the duration of the employment contract, with a maximum of 1 year. Any extensions require a re-evaluation by the Cantonal Office of Migration.

Initial Duration and Renewal

The duration of the leave L exactly reflects the contractual period agreed with the employer. If the contract provides for 6 months, the permit will be valid for six months. To extend, the employer must demonstrate to the cantonal authority the need to keep the worker beyond the deadline

Operational details

Conditions for extension and work restrictions

The extension of the L permit is not automatic and requires:

  • Continuation of the employment relationship with the same employer
  • Maintenance of wage and contractual conditions compliant with Swiss standards (no dumping)
  • Adequate accommodation according to cantonal standards (minimum area 20 m²/person)
  • Absence of criminal convictions or ongoing proceedings

Switching to permit B

After 5 years of regular residence in Switzerland (with consecutive L or B permits), workers can apply for the B residence permit. This permit, renewable every 5 years, offers greater stability but requires:

  • Language proficiency (German B1 or equivalent)
  • Social integration and respect for public order
  • Economic independence (salary at least at the level of the cantonal median salary, in 2024 about CHF 6,500 per month)

⚠️ Important: the L permit is closely linked to the employer. In the event of dismissal or resignation, the worker has 30 days to find a new job in the same canton (with relative new L permit) or must leave Switzerland.

Key points

Practical procedure for extension and change of employer

To extend the L permit in the Canton of Zug: 1. The employer sends a written request to the Cantonal Office of Migration 2-3 months before the deadline 2. Attach: updated contract, proof of accommodation, criminal certificate 3. Pay cantonal tax (CHF 150-300, variable)

In the event of a change of employer: 1. New employer requires a new L permit for the worker 2. The worker must temporarily leave Switzerland (usually 1-2 days) to obtain the visa at a Swiss representation abroad 3. Return with new leave related to the new job

Frequently Asked Questions
How many times can the L permit be extended in Zug?
There is no predefined limit, but each extension requires a full re-evaluation. Usually 1-2 extensions are granted for the same employer, as long as there are economic reasons and the worker meets all the conditions.
Can I bring the family with a short-term L permit?
Only in exceptional cases, if the salary allows it (at least CHF 70,000 per year) and with adequate accommodation for families. The procedure requires a separate family reunification permit, with waiting times of up to 6 months.
What happens if I change jobs without applying for a new permit?
Irregular employment carries administrative penalties of up to CHF 10,000 for the employer and immediate expulsion for the worker, with a ban on re-entry for 5-10 years.
