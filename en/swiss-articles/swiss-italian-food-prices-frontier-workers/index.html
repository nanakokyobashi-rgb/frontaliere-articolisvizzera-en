Operational details

The New Frontier Agreement, signed on 23 December 2020 and entered into force on 1 January 2024, has made some substantial changes for frontier workers. One of these is the tax exemption in Italy, allowing them to benefit from lower prices for food expenditure. However, where should you shop for a border crossing?

Tax exemptions

With the border agreement, border workers not resident in Switzerland who have a residence in Italy are exempt from income and wealth taxes in Italy, up to a certain amount. This exemption applies only to expenses incurred in the exercise of the business, for example for the purchase of goods and materials necessary for their work.

Swiss and Italian prices

According to a study conducted by the Federal Statistical Office on Alcohol and Food Expenditure, Swiss prices are generally lower than Italian prices. For example, for a bottle of mineral water, the cost in Italy is about CHF 0.50 while in Switzerland it is CHF 0.40. For a liter of whole milk, the price in Italy is about 0.70 CHF while in Switzerland it is 0.50 CHF.

Cantons and cities

For border crossers interested in spending in Switzerland, the cantons of Geneva and Bern offer lower prices for food expenditure than the national average. Geneva is known for its many supermarket chains and pharmacies, while Bern offers a wide choice of local and organic products.

Key points

Frontier expenditure: Swiss and Italian prices, a detailed comparison

Comparing supermarket prices between Italy and Switzerland can be very interesting for commuters who often cross both borders. According to the available data, the prices of Italian supermarkets are on average 20-30% lower than those of Switzerland. This means that a commuter shopping in Italy can save up to 30% compared to shopping in Switzerland.

To dig deeper, here are some concrete examples with real numbers. For example, in Ticino, one of the richest cantons in Italy, the cost of fresh milk is 20% lower than in Switzerland. At the same time, the cost of bread in Ticino is 10% lower than in Switzerland.

In addition, the comparison between tax regulations can be interesting. For example, VAT in Switzerland is 7.7% against 22% in Italy for some goods such as cheese, bread and meat.
