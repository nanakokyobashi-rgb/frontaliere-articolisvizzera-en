Prerequisites to open a business in Switzerland

Before starting the process of opening a business in Switzerland, it is important to check if you meet the necessary prerequisites. These include:

  • Having obtained a residence permit (B) for foreigners
  • Having liability insurance
  • Having professional liability insurance
  • Have a business plan and a financial plan

Legal ways to open a business in Switzerland

The most common legal form to open a business in Switzerland is the limited liability company (SRL). However, there are other legal forms that can be used, such as:

  • Limited liability company (SRI)
  • Company in collective name (SNC)
  • Limited partnership (SAS)

Key points

To open a business in Switzerland, you need to register with the trade register of the canton where you want to open the business. The commercial register is the responsibility of the cantonal tax administrations. The opening procedure can take from 1 to 3 months.

Before you start, it's important to contact your cantonal tax administration to learn about the specific requirements for your canton or city. For example, in Zurich, the opening procedure may require the submission of a detailed business plan and proof of a bank guarantee of at least CHF 10,000.

To register with the trade register, you need to submit a number of documents, including:

  • a detailed business plan
  • a statement of intent to open a business
  • a copy of the company contract (if it is a company)
  • a copy of the driver's license (if it is an activity that requires driving vehicles)

The opening procedure may vary depending on the type of business and the canton in which you want to open the business. For example, to open a trading business, you need to present a detailed business plan and prove that you have a bank guarantee of at least 20,000 Swiss francs.
