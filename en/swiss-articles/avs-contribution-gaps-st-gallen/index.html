AVS and complementary services: St. Gallen guide

How the first pillar works in the Canton of St. Gallen. Learn about the role of the cantonal compensation fund, pension calculation, contribution gaps and supplementary benefits for the living minimum.

Context

TL;DR

  • The cantonal compensation office records the contributions and calculates the pension based on the years of contribution and the amounts paid.
  • Supplementary benefits cover the minimum subsistence level when the AHV pension is not sufficient: regulated by the canton of St. Gallen and subsidised by the Confederation.

Key facts

  • What: First pillar of Swiss social security (AVS/AHV), mandatory for all workers
  • When: Registration from the first day of work until retirement (currently 65 years for men, 64 for women, with ongoing reform)
  • Where: Managed by the compensation office of the Canton of St. Gallen and by FOAS/BSV at federal level
  • Who: Employers, employees, cantonal administration (supplementary services)
  • Amount: Contribution rate 5.3% employees, annuity calculation based on years and amounts credited to the insurance account

The AVS represents the fundamental pillar of Swiss social security. In the Canton of St. Gallen, as in every canton, operational management falls to the local compensation fund: the body that registers members, credits the contributions paid monthly by employers and employees, maintains the individual insurance account and, upon reaching retirement age, calculates and settles the annuity.

The system is mandatory for anyone working in Switzerland. The rate is set at 5.3% paid by the employee and 5.3% paid by the employer, for a total of 10.6%. These contributions do not represent a form of personal savings, but rather the joint financing of the collective pension system: those who work today finance today's pensioners, and in turn will be entitled to an annuity when they stop working.

The AVS income depends on two critical variables: the number of credited years of contributions and the average amount of contributions paid during the working career. Full coverage requires 44 years of contributions for men (with ongoing reform for women). If there are gaps - i.e. undeclared periods of work or periods of unemployment without coverage - the final pension is reduced proportionately. In these cases, complementary benefits intervene to guarantee a minimum level of subsistence.

How the cantonal compensation fund works

In St. Gallen, the compensation fund is the operational body that ensures the functioning of the first pillar. The main tasks include: registration of taxpayers on the first day of work, collection and crediting of contributions paid by employers, management of the personal insurance account (where each contribution is recorded in a traceable way), calculation of the AVS income based on the federal formula, and finally payment of the monthly benefit. The fund receives contributions from employers via annual wage declaration and operates according to the federal regulations of the LAVS (Federal Law on Old Age, Survivors' and Disability Insurance). Every resident of St. Gallen has the right to consult their AVS account at the compensation office and to receive a free statement to verify the accuracy of the credited data.

Operational details

Calculation of the AVS income: mechanics and determining factors

The AVS annuity is not a refund of contributions paid, but rather a benefit calculated according to a federal formula that takes into account two essential elements: the number of years of contributions from the first job until retirement age, and the average amount of annual contributions during the entire career. For a full and unreduced annuity, 44 years of contributions are required (for men). If years are missing, the annuity is reduced proportionally: each missing year involves a reduction calculated on the basis of the federal formula.

The compensation office of the Canton of St. Gallen carries out the calculation using the data stored in the personal insurance account. The process begins at least 3 months before your desired retirement age. The fund estimates the average coordinated income (i.e. the average salary during the career), applies the federal formula and communicates the result to the pensioner - usually 4 weeks before the pension start date. It is at this time that the taxpayer finds out if there are any gaps or if the coverage is complete.

An important element: the taxpayer can request the deferral of the annuity up to 5 years after the retirement age. This choice significantly increases the monthly benefit amount, as years of contributions are added and a federal enhancement factor is applied.

Contribution gaps: origin, impact and regularization

A contribution gap occurs when a period of work has not been declared to the AVS or when the taxpayer has gone through an absence from the labor market without unemployment benefit coverage. Concrete examples include periods of uninsured unemployment (when the unemployment fund has not paid AVS contributions), undeclared self-employment for a certain period of time, prolonged illness without insurance coverage, or simple absences from the labor market for any reason.

The impact of a gap on the final income is direct and significant: each missing year in the contribution history reduces the amount of the monthly benefit, because the calculation is based on the total number of years of contribution. A person with 43 years of contributions instead of 44 will receive a reduced pension; someone aged 40 will receive an even steeper reduction.

For this reason, it is essential to check your AVS account regularly at the compensation office of the Canton of St. Gallen (right of free consultation). If gaps emerge, the taxpayer has 5 years from discovery to regularize them by paying the missing contributions. This retroactive payment allows you to significantly increase your future pension — sometimes by hundreds of francs a month, depending on the number of years filled.

Complementary services: the safety mat for the vital minimum

When the AVS annuity is insufficient to guarantee a minimum subsistence income, complementary benefits come into play. These allowances are financed jointly by the Confederation and the Canton of St. Gallen and guarantee economic dignity to those with a low income and limited assets. In St. Gallen, the competent cantonal administration manages the receipt, evaluation and processing of applications.

The amount of the supplementary benefit is calculated as the difference between the AVS pension and the living minimum defined by the canton. This minimum includes covering essential expenses: rent or access to housing, food, clothing, energy, heating, basic medical care. The supplementary benefit aims to bridge the gap and ensure a dignified life. It is not a discretionary subsidy, but a right: anyone who meets the criteria of low income and low assets can access it.

Key points

How to apply for an AVS pension in the Canton of St. Gallen

The AVS pension application process must begin at least 3 months before your desired retirement date. The steps are as follows:

1. Contact the cantonal compensation office. The first step is to inform the compensation office of the Canton of St. Gallen of your intention to retire and the expected date. This contact can take place by telephone, email or personal visit. During this communication, request the official AVS pension application form and provide your personal details, AVS number and desired pension start date.

2. Fill out the official application. The fund provides a standardized form that must be filled out in every part. In the form you must declare whether you intend to continue working during retirement (this information influences the calculation of the annuity) and whether you wish to request the postponement of the annuity to obtain a higher monthly amount.

3. Submit your application within the correct deadlines. The completed form must be delivered to the compensation fund within the month in which the pension begins. The fund verifies the registration, the contributions credited over the years, any gaps and the correctness of the personal data.

4. Receive notification of the calculation. Approximately 4 weeks before the start date of the pension, the fund communicates the result of the calculation: the amount of the monthly income, the number of years credited, any gaps reported, and the date of the first payment. At this point, if there are gaps, the pensioner still has the possibility of reporting them for regularization.

5. Receive the income. The first payment of the AVS annuity is usually made the month following retirement, by bank transfer to the account indicated in the application. The annuity continues monthly and remains unchanged in amount, except for general adjustments to inflation decided by the Federal Parliament.

Request for additional benefits from the Canton of St. Gallen

If the AVS pension does not cover the minimum subsistence, the pensioner can access complementary benefits. The procedure is parallel but managed by the cantonal administration (specific office for complementary benefits).

1. Check your entitlement before retirement. It is recommended to contact the Supplementary Benefits Office of the Canton of St. Gallen before retirement to receive a preliminary assessment. By providing an estimate of the future AVS income, the office can indicate whether the right to supplementary benefits exists.

2. Apply after retirement. Once retirement begins, the pensioner fills out the application form for supplementary benefits at the competent cantonal office. The application must be accompanied by: certificate of AVS income (provided by the compensation fund), documentation of housing expenses (rental contract or ownership certificate), certificate of personal wealth (bank statement, assets documents).

3. Submission deadlines. The application should be made within 12 months of retirement, although there is no hard legal deadline. However, the payment of supplementary benefits starts from the date of submission of the application, not retroactively.

4. Evaluation and payment. The cantonal office examines the file (income, assets, housing expenses) and communicates the outcome. If the test is positive, the canton pays an additional monthly amount which covers the difference between the AVS pension and the subsistence minimum. The payment is automatic, every month, in parallel with the AVS income.

Tools and resources in the Canton of St. Gallen

For any need related to the first pillar in St. Gallen:

  • Compensation Fund of the Canton of St. Gallen: for annuity applications, verification of contributions, request for AVS account statements, information on contribution gaps.
  • Office of complementary benefits (cantonal administration): for applications for complementary benefits and preliminary assessments.

  • Online tools: many compensation funds provide online calculators to estimate future income based on already accredited data.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the mandatory AVS contributions for an employee?
An employee pays 5.3% of their salary as an AVS contribution; the employer in turn pays 5.3% of the employee's salary (total system: 10.6%). These payments are mandatory and determined by federal law. The cantonal compensation fund records all contributions paid to the personal insurance account, where they remain stored until retirement.
What are contribution gaps and how do they affect AVS income?
A contribution gap is a period of work not declared to the AVS or an absence from the labour market without coverage of unemployment benefits. Each missing year reduces the final annuity, as the calculation is based on the total number of years of contribution. It is possible to regularize a gap within 5 years of discovery by paying the missing contributions retroactively, which significantly increases the pension benefit.
Who is entitled to supplementary benefits in the Canton of St. Gallen?
Supplementary benefits are due to residents with an AVS income insufficient to cover the minimum living allowance and limited assets (canton-defined subdivisions). In St. Gallen, the competent Office assesses the application and settles an additional monthly amount that fills the difference between the AVH annuity and the essential expenses (accommodation, food, energy, medical care). Access is a right, not a discretionary subsidy.
When should I contact the compensation fund for the annuity application?
It is recommended that you contact the Canton of St. Gallen Compensation Fund at least 3 months before your desired retirement date. This allows you to fill in the official application calmly, check the accredited contributions, check any gaps and receive the calculation of the annuity in advance. The payment of the pension normally begins the month following retirement.
How can I verify the contributions made to my AVS account?
Each person has the right to request a statement of the AVS account from the cantonal clearing house. This document, provided free of charge, shows all contributions credited year by year and allows you to verify the accuracy of the data. It is important to check it periodically to promptly report any gaps or errors. Corrections must be requested within specific deadlines at the cash desk.
