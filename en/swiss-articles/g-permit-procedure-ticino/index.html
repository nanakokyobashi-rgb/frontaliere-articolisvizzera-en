G Permit for frontier workers: procedure and obligations in Ticino

Key facts

  • What: Application for a new G permit for cross-border commuters.
  • When: Before starting the activity or changing jobs.
  • Where: Migration Office of the Canton of Ticino.
  • Who: Foreign workers without residence in Switzerland.
  • Base cost: 15 CHF for biometric data and 10 CHF for residence card.

Starting gainful employment in Switzerland as a foreign citizen who does not intend to transfer their residence to Swiss territory requires obtaining a G permit. This authorization is strictly linked to carrying out dependent employment for an employer with an operational base in the Canton of Ticino. Current legislation requires that every new hire, as well as any change of workplace, be notified in advance to the Migration Office.

Notification and residence obligations

The cross-border worker is required to comply with the provisions concerning residence. If the worker does not return to their home daily, they are required to notify their presence to the Swiss municipality where they spend the night. Regardless of this possibility, the regulations stipulate unequivocally that the cross-border worker must return at least once a week to their main residence located abroad. Failure to comply with these procedures, including failure to submit the permit application or submitting it late, may result in fines. It is therefore essential that the bureaucratic process be completed accurately before the start date of the employment contract, thereby ensuring that the professional status in the canton is fully regular.

Operational details

The document verification procedure

A fundamental step in regularizing an employee's status concerns identity verification. Workers intending to operate in Ticino must personally go to one of the Gendarmerie stations designated to verify their identity document. This operation is a prerequisite for the issuance of the permit. Once verified, the document will be accompanied by an original certified copy issued by the Gendarmerie station itself. This certification must be strictly attached to the official application to be submitted to the Migration Office.

The handling of administrative procedures entails specific costs covering processing and secretarial expenses. In addition to the decision fees for the labor market assessment, there are costs for collecting biometric data, including a photograph and signature, amounting to 15 CHF. If the production of a residence card is necessary, an additional cost of 10 CHF applies. Postage costs, amounting to 5 CHF, are included in the fees for processing and returning the documents. It is advisable to pay attention to the opening hours of the competent offices, which operate from Monday to Friday, from 08:00 to 12:00 and from 13:30 to 17:00. For specific professional categories, such as those regulated in the healthcare sector, the procedures may require additional checks based on the applicable legal provisions. Document compliance is not merely a formal obligation, but represents the guarantee of carrying out gainful employment without interruption. Key points

Career-related obligations and management

Managing one's career as a cross-border worker in Ticino requires constant monitoring of administrative deadlines. In addition to the initial permit application stage, every change of employer triggers the obligation to submit a new notification. It is not permitted to start a new activity without first obtaining formal approval from the Migration Office. Timely planning is therefore the critical factor in avoiding financial penalties. In case of doubts about the documentation to attach or the specific requirements for particular professions, the legal bases and general information provided by the cantonal administration represent the primary reference guide.

For those entering the Ticino labour market, it is essential to understand that the system is based on cooperation between the worker and the cantonal authorities. Transparency in communicating personal and professional data facilitates the examination of the application and speeds up response times. It is also important to periodically check one's insurance situation, particularly with regard to LAMal/KVG, given that the cross-border worker has the right to choose their health insurance fund. Proper management of all contractual and pension-related aspects, including the correct LPP/BVG contributions where applicable, is an integral part of professional life in Switzerland. To examine one's income situation in greater depth and understand the impact of deductions, it is advisable to use the calcolatore stipendio available on the portal, which makes it possible to simulate the monthly net salary based on the agreed gross salary. Keeping one's status up to date, complying with notification deadlines and retaining a copy of all correspondence with the Migration Office are good practices that protect the worker throughout the entire period of employment in the Canton.

Frequently Asked Questions
What happens if the G permit application is submitted late?
Failure to submit the application within the established deadlines, or before the start of work, may result in the application of a fine. It is essential to notify the Migration Office of the commencement of each new activity or job change prior to recruitment to avoid penalties.
Does the border worker have to return home every day?
The border worker must return at least once a week to his main domicile abroad. If he does not return daily, he is obliged to notify his presence to the Swiss municipality where he stays during the working week.
What documents are required for the G permit application?
You must go to a Gendarmerie post to check your identity document. The copy of the document, certified in original by the Gendarmerie, must be attached to the application for the issue of the permit to be sent to the Migration Office before the start of the work.
