The director of the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (Seco), Helene Budliger Artieda, has issued a warning in the columns of the SonntagsZeitung, stating that Switzerland must prepare for a radically transformed global economy. We have entered a clearly multipolar era, where superpowers ruthlessly leverage their political and economic weight to impose the rules of the game.

"For us as Switzerland, this is a significant disadvantage," says Budliger Artieda, who believes this development began well before Donald Trump's second term as US president. "The rulebook of the World Trade Organization is weakened. What is remarkable about President Trump is that he says all this very clearly and implements it with his decisions."

Budliger Artieda sees not only the international order but the entire Western world in an identity crisis. Europe may not have made the most of the "30 years of peace dividend" following the fall of the Berlin Wall, relying too much on the assumption that everyone would respect the rules of the game. "Today we must admit to ourselves that we were probably too naive in many respects."

Regarding Switzerland's tariff policy with the United States, the Federal Council continues to aim for the conclusion of a legally binding agreement, says the Seco director without providing details. "Negotiations are underway; I cannot tell you more about it."

Practical Implications

Helene Budliger Artieda's words have important practical implications for Switzerland. The need for greater internal cohesion and a more active international role requires a significant change in the country's economic policy and trade relations.

Social Cohesion

Social cohesion is a central challenge for Switzerland. People need to better understand the benefits of a strong economy and the needs of the economy need to be communicated more effectively. This requires an open and continuous dialogue between government, business and citizens.

International Role

Internationally, Switzerland needs to be more proactive. This means actively participating in trade and diplomatic negotiations, and not waiting for other countries to take the initiative. Free trade agreements remain a crucial tool for ensuring access to foreign markets and protecting Switzerland's economic interests.

Duty Policy

Switzerland's tariff policy with the United States is another sticking point. The Federal Council is working to conclude a legally binding agreement, but details of the negotiations have not yet been made public. This agreement could have a significant impact on trade relations between the two countries and on Switzerland's economic stability.

Effective Communication: Improve the communication of economic needs and the benefits of a strong economy through information campaigns and educational initiatives.

Increasing International Role

1. Active Participation: Actively participate in trade and diplomatic negotiations to ensure that Switzerland's interests are represented and protected. 2. Free Trade Agreements: Continue to promote and conclude free trade agreements to ensure access to foreign markets and protect Switzerland's economic interests.

Tariff Policy

In conclusion, the concrete actions listed above are essential to address the challenges highlighted by Helene Budliger Artieda.
