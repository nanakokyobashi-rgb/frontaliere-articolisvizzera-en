UBS, SNB approves 90% capital requirements

The SNB approves the decision of the Council of States: UBS's foreign subsidiaries will have to be covered 90% with CET1 capital.

The Swiss National Bank (SNB) approves the Council of States' decision to increase capital requirements for UBS. The step concerns the coverage of the bank's foreign branches: 90% of their value must be covered with first class core capital, indicated with the acronym CET1.

The decision comes from Bern and puts the relationship between UBS and financial stability at the center. The proposal approved by the Council of States does not completely coincide with that advanced by the Federal Council. The executive had in fact proposed 100% coverage of the value of the foreign branches.

The vice-president of the general management of the SNB, Antoine Martin, commented on the choice as reported by Bloomberg. His assessment is in favor of the parliamentary compromise, while recognizing that the other solution would have offered greater protection from the point of view of financial stability.

«The 90% proposal is good» even if 100%, proposed by the Federal Council, «would have been even better from a financial stability perspective but we respect Parliament's decisions».

The difference between the two proposals

The comparison is therefore defined by two precise thresholds. On the one hand there is the 90% decided by the Council of States for the coverage of UBS's foreign branches with CET1 capital. On the other hand, there is the 100% supported by the Federal Council, which Antoine Martin considers even better if the objective is financial stability.

The SNB does not contest the parliamentary decision. He considers it a good proposal and declares that he respects Parliament's decisions. The central data of the news remains the new threshold referring to UBS and its foreign branches, not a general rule described for all Swiss banks. In fact, the source specifically talks about the capital requirements for UBS and the related decision of the Council of States. It does not present the measure as a cantonal choice or as a change to other requirements: the object is the coverage of foreign branches with CET1 capital.
