Swiss Oppose Taxes on Sugar, Salt, or Fats

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 30 September 2026

68% of those surveyed by Gfs.Bern reject the tax on food. The monitoring highlights calls for prevention and the protection of minors.

Context

At a Glance

  • 68% oppose taxes on sugar, salt, or fats
  • 76% consider measures against obesity insufficient
  • 89% support mandatory nutrition education in schools
  • 82% approve of a ban on advertising to children under 13

Key Facts

Sample → over 1,000 respondents Period → between March and May Research firm → Gfs.Bern Survey → 13th edition Source → Keystone-ATS Food tax → 68% opposed Tax to fund nutrition education → 45% in favor Minimum age for sugary foods → 19% in favor

68% of the more than 1,000 respondents surveyed between March and May by Gfs.Bern say they oppose a tax on foods containing sugar, salt, or fat. The data comes from the 13th edition of a survey sponsored by the Soft Drink Information Group, an organization founded in 2013 by industry producers that includes representatives from the political and business worlds.

Keystone-ATS reports that the Swiss public wants the government to play a more active role in combating obesity, but without new taxes or bans. The survey indicates a preference for individual responsibility, accompanied by a call for greater protection of children from advertising.

The findings therefore address both the role of the government and the choice of tools for intervention. The survey distinguishes between tax measures and measures focused on prevention, education, and advertising. Public opinion varies significantly among these options.

The Vote on the Proposals

Opposition to the tax is also driven by the fear that the levy would place a greater burden on the poorest people and would not change eating habits. The proposal to tax the same products to fund public information campaigns garners 45% support and thus fails to secure a majority. Support for a minimum age requirement to purchase sugary foods is even lower: 19% of respondents support this idea, making it the least popular option.

Prevention and Minors

76% of the sample believes that the measures taken so far to prevent obesity are insufficient. 90% supports prevention programs for at-risk groups and joint initiatives with the private sector. Eighty-nine percent support mandatory nutrition education in schools. Eighty-two percent also support a ban on advertising foods containing sugar, salt, or fat that is targeted at children under 13.

Food manufacturers are working with the federal government to develop a voluntary commitment plan. This monitoring effort thus complements support for more active government intervention with a focus on individual responsibility, prevention, education, child protection, and collaboration with the private sector.

Operational details

The survey offers a practical way to interpret the findings: not all proposals to combat obesity affect the same aspect of daily life. The tax on foods containing sugar, salt, or fat affects purchasing decisions; nutrition education focuses on schools; advertising targets children under 13; prevention programs target at-risk groups. Joint initiatives, on the other hand, involve the economy. This distinction clarifies a result that would otherwise seem contradictory: the government should do more, but the preferred approach does not necessarily involve a new tax or a restriction on purchases.

For those living in Switzerland, the difference is clear in practice: a tax proposal is judged based on its impact on the poorest people; school-based or advertising interventions focus on specific target groups; joint initiatives create a role for the private sector. Key points

To follow this issue without confusing opinions and policy measures, readers can create a summary in four steps. This reading method is geared toward tracking developments: it starts with the listed proposals, matches each one to the relevant audience, and distinguishes fiscal measures from educational or voluntary ones.

A Worksheet in Four Steps

  • Separate the fiscal proposals: a tax on foods containing sugar, salt, or fat; a tax on those same products to fund public education; and a minimum age requirement for purchasing sugary foods.
  • Associate each measure with its target audience: at-risk groups for prevention, schools for teaching nutritional principles, and children under 13 for advertising.
  • Keep the role of the economy distinct: joint projects and the voluntary commitment plan between food producers and the federal government are not the same as a tax or a ban.
  • Consider individual responsibility and public action together: the survey supports both dimensions, but with varying levels of consensus depending on the specific measure.

The first step avoids equating measures that serve different purposes. A tax to fund information is not presented as a prevention program; a minimum age requirement is not the same as protecting children from advertising. Distinguishing these proposals makes it possible to understand which part of the debate concerns purchasing, schools, communication, or at-risk groups.

The second step helps assess the concrete scope of the measures without including unintended audiences. Those who live or work in Switzerland can thus follow separately the issues concerning school meals, advertising aimed at minors, and prevention efforts for at-risk groups. The third step focuses attention on the voluntary plan currently being developed between food producers and the federal government, while the fourth step keeps the role of personal responsibility in view.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does the Gfs.Bern survey reveal about the food tax?
The survey conducted by the Gfs.Bern institute, based on more than 1000 respondents between March and May, shows that 68% of the population is opposed to a tax on foods containing sugar, salt or fat. This opposition is motivated by the fear that the levy would weigh more heavily on poorer people and would not change eating habits. Moreover, the proposal to tax the same products to finance information campaigns receives only 45% support, while the introduction of a minimum age for purchasing sugary foods stands at just 19%.
Which prevention and protection measures are supported by the population?
Monitoring data indicate strong support for measures alternative to taxation: 76% consider current initiatives against overweight insufficient, 90% are in favor of prevention programs for at-risk groups and joint projects with the economy, 89% support mandatory nutrition education at school and 82% approve the ban on advertising for foods containing sugar, salt or fat aimed at children under 13.
What is the role of the economy and food producers?
Food producers are drawing up a voluntary commitment plan in cooperation with the Confederation. This channel complements the population's demands for greater prevention and collaboration, clearly distinguishing itself from proposals for taxation or the introduction of purchase limits, while at the same time emphasizing the principle of individual responsibility.
