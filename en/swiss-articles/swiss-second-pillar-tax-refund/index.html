Since 2024, according to the account published by Fiscomania, refusals by the Swiss cantonal authorities have been reported in response to requests for reimbursement of withholding tax deducted from second-pillar capital. The issue concerns residents in Italy who have received the benefit in the form of capital and who may also be subject to the 5% Italian substitute tax.

This edition is dated October 2026; the time reference for the refusals remains 2024, while the federal guidance cited by the source dates back to August 12, 2022. The issue described is the coordination between the initial Swiss levy and the taxation that the Convention for the avoidance of double taxation assigns to the state of residence.

The initial levy in Switzerland

If second-pillar capital is paid to a person who is tax-domiciled abroad, Swiss rules provide for withholding at source, carried out at the time of payment. Such withholding, however, does not in itself demonstrate that final taxing authority belongs to Switzerland: it is necessary to verify the Convention applicable to the beneficiary’s state of residence.

In the Canton of Ticino, this mechanism has a specific application: pension capital paid to beneficiaries domiciled abroad is initially subject to tax even if an international agreement recognizes the other state’s taxing power. Where the required conditions are met, the beneficiary may subsequently request a refund of the Swiss levy.

For territorial purposes, the canton in which the taxpayer worked or lived during their professional activity does not necessarily matter. The refund application must be submitted with reference to the canton where the pension institution that paid the capital is based. The federal form mentioned by the source also asks for both the pension institution and the relevant canton to be specified.

Within the framework of the Italy-Switzerland Convention, the levy imposed initially does not coincide with the definitive allocation of taxing power. Under Art. 18, pensions and similar remuneration relating to a former private employment relationship are, as a general rule, taxed exclusively in the state of residence. Cases related to public functions, to which Art. 19 applies, remain separate.

Operational details

The comparison between withholding and final taxation

The case can be understood by separating two moments that may produce different effects. Switzerland may apply withholding when it pays LPP capital to a resident abroad. The Convention, however, may assign final taxation to Italy when the beneficiary is tax resident in Italy and the benefit derives from previous private employment.

The relationship between Article 18 and Italian tax

Article 18 of the Convention does not automatically eliminate the withholding applied at the time of payment. According to the source’s analysis, it establishes which State generally has taxing power over pensions and similar remuneration from former private employment. For an Italian tax resident, the convention rule therefore points to Italy. Article 19 maintains separate treatment for public functions and is not absorbed by the general rule of Article 18.

In Italy, the treatment of benefits from the Swiss occupational pension scheme LPP received by residents in Italy is governed by Article 76 of Law No. 413/1991. The introduction of paragraph 1-ter extends this regime also to payments made without the involvement of a resident financial intermediary required to apply the withholding. An LPP benefit paid as a lump sum was examined by the Italian Revenue Agency in the response to ruling request No. 125/2024.

The point linking the two aspects is therefore precise: the same capital may be subject in Italy to a 5% substitute tax after having already been subject to withholding at source in Switzerland. According to the source, the refund procedure serves to prevent both withholdings from remaining permanently borne by the beneficiary.

These elements also allow a numerical comparison, but only within its limits. If both taxes remained final, the burden described would comprise an Italian component equal to 5% of the capital and the amount actually withheld in Switzerland. The available text indicates neither the Swiss rate applied nor the amount of the benefit or withholding. It is therefore not possible to calculate an overall percentage or establish a specific sum.

The reporting of refusals since 2024 concerns precisely this coordination. The source does not provide the number of cases, does not indicate a uniform measure adopted by all Cantons and does not explain whether every refusal had the same reason. Accordingly, the documented fact is the existence of refusals even where Italian taxation applies, not a general conclusion about the outcome of every application.

The withholding does not in itself result in a refund: the beneficiary must submit a specific application and prove tax residence in the State to which the Convention assigns taxing power. For residents in Italy, the procedure was also explained by AFC in Circular Letter No. 199a of August 12, 2022.

Key points

Documents, jurisdiction and limits of reconstruction

The source makes it possible to identify precisely the territorial point from which the request originates, but it does not authorize equating it with the beneficiary’s employment history. The relevant Canton is the one where the pension institution that paid out the capital is based. It is not necessarily the Canton in which the person worked, nor is it necessarily the one in which they resided during their professional activity.

The form and missing information

The available text also refers to the federal Q-IS 2026 form. The excerpt, however, ends immediately after the beginning of the instructions concerning the form. On the basis of the material provided, it is therefore not possible to attribute to the document any additional mandatory fields, filing deadlines, specific attachments or procedures for challenging a denial.

This limitation also concerns the so-called new cantonal practice. The source documents that, since 2024, denials by cantonal authorities have been reported, including cases in which the capital had been subject to Italian taxation. However, it does not describe a complete cantonal decision, reproduce an administrative text or indicate whether an identical procedure exists for all pension institutions or for all Cantons.

The institutional framework remains divided between pensions and taxation. For the general framework of social security, the official reference indicated is the FSIO. The tax procedure described by the source, however, follows the specific elements of the refund application, proof of residence and the Canton of the institution that made the payment. The FTA provided the circular dedicated to residents of Italy, whereas the initial withholding and the denials mentioned concern the cantonal authorities.

It is also necessary to keep the situation of private-sector workers separate from that of public functions. The source attributes the conclusion regarding taxation in the state of residence to pensions and similar remuneration arising from a previous private-sector employment relationship, on the basis of Art. 18. Public functions, on the other hand, are addressed in Art. 19 and cannot automatically be brought under the same rule.

With the available data, the sequence can therefore be reconstructed: Swiss withholding upon payment, verification of the Convention, Italian taxation of 5% where applicable, and a non-automatic refund application. The excerpt cannot, however, be used to derive a Swiss percentage, a refundable amount, a deadline or the outcome of any appeal.
