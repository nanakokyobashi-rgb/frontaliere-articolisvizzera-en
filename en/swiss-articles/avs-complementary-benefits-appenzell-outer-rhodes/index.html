In brief

  • The Compensation Fund of the Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden administers the AVS, the cornerstone of the Swiss pension system. - Each missing year of contribution reduces the lifelong pension by approximately 1/44. - Supplementary benefits supplement the AVS pension to ensure the minimum living standard. - Pension planning requires constant verification of the individual account statement.

Key facts

  • What: Operational management and collection of AVS/AI/IPG contributions. - Where: Herisau, headquarters of the Compensation Fund of Appenzell Ausserrhoden. - Who: Mandatory insured persons and employers of the canton. - Legal basis: Federal Law on Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance (LAVS).

The AVS system in the Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden operates within a strict federal framework. Although the rules are uniform at the national level, interaction with the cantonal office is crucial for managing payments and regularizing gaps.

Pension calculation and impact of gaps

The AVS pension depends on the years of contribution and the average annual income. For 2024, the minimum pension is set at 1,225 francs per month, while the maximum reaches 2,450 francs. A single year of non-contribution, for example, for a period of study abroad not covered, results in a permanent reduction of the pension. Compared to cantons with a higher cost of living, such as Zurich or Geneva, management in Appenzell Ausserrhoden follows the same federal tables, but access to supplementary benefits is adjusted according to the spending limit recognized by the canton.

Operational details

The management of the first pillar AVS at the cantonal level has significant practical implications for the residents of the Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden. Before the introduction of the cantonal compensation fund, the calculation of the AVS pension was managed at the federal level. This change has led to greater customization of benefits but also to greater complexity in financial planning. A cross-border worker in a similar situation could benefit from personalized advice offered by the cantonal compensation fund. This advice can help better understand the specifics of the canton and plan the pension adequately. For example, in the Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden, the compensation fund has introduced a points system that takes into account the duration and amount of contributions, with a maximum of 300 points for each year of work. Each point corresponds to a certain amount in Swiss francs, which varies based on the average salaries in the canton. This system is more complex than the federal calculation but offers greater accuracy in the assessment of benefits. Supplementary benefits are essential to ensure that contributors can cover the minimum living standard. These benefits are managed at the cantonal level and can vary based on the specifics of the canton. For example, in the Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden, the minimum living standard is calculated based on a formula that takes into account the average amount of salaries in the canton and the cost of living. In 2023, the minimum living standard for a single person was 2,500 Swiss francs per month, while for a family of four it was 4,200 Swiss francs per month. Contributors must be aware of the specifics of their canton to plan their pension adequately. The practical implications of the management of the first pillar AVS at the cantonal level are significant. For example, in the Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden, contributors must declare their income and their contributions to the first pillar AVS each year, by March 31. This declaration period is shorter than the federal period, which runs from October 1 to April 30.
