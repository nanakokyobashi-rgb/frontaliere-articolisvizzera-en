Context

Management of the first pillar AVS in the Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden

In brief

  • The Compensation Fund of the Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden administers the AVS, the cornerstone of the Swiss pension system. - Each missing year of contribution reduces the lifelong pension by approximately 1/44. - Supplementary benefits supplement the AVS pension to ensure the minimum living standard. - Pension planning requires constant verification of the individual account statement.

Key facts

  • What: Operational management and collection of AVS/AI/IPG contributions. - Where: Herisau, headquarters of the Compensation Fund of Appenzell Ausserrhoden. - Who: Mandatory insured persons and employers of the canton. - Legal basis: Federal Law on Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance (LAVS).

The AVS system in the Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden operates within a strict federal framework. Although the rules are uniform at the national level, interaction with the cantonal office is crucial for managing payments and regularizing gaps.

Pension calculation and impact of gaps

The AVS pension depends on the years of contribution and the average annual income. For 2024, the minimum pension is set at 1,225 francs per month, while the maximum reaches 2,450 francs. A single year of non-contribution, for example, for a period of study abroad not covered, results in a permanent reduction of the pension. Compared to cantons with a higher cost of living, such as Zurich or Geneva, management in Appenzell Ausserrhoden follows the same federal tables, but access to supplementary benefits is adjusted according to the spending limit recognized by the canton.

Pension planning is not a static event but a dynamic process that requires continuous monitoring.

Operational checklist for the contributor

  • Periodically request the account statement from the Compensation Fund of Appenzell Ausserrhoden. - Check for any contribution gaps dating back to the age of 18. - Evaluate the buyback of missing years, possible only within 5 years of the deadline. - Consult the official website to download forms for supplementary benefits.

Comparison of scenarios: gaps vs. regularity

A contributor who has paid contributions without interruption for 44 years receives the maximum pension. Conversely, someone with 4 years of gaps receives a reduced pension of approximately 9%. In a context of inflation, this difference significantly impacts spending capacity, making it necessary to resort to supplementary benefits, managed at the cantonal level to bridge the gap between pension and the minimum living standard defined by law.
