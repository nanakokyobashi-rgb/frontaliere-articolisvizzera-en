The current situation

The current situation of hospital inspections in Switzerland was the subject of a recent report published by Swissmedic. According to the data, 28 hospital inspections were carried out in 2025, which is an increase compared to previous years. However, despite the increase in the number of inspections, Swissmedic stressed that current surveillance is not sufficient to ensure the safety and effectiveness of hospital treatments.

The results of the inspections

The results of the inspections highlighted some significant deficiencies in risk management and in the quality of hospital treatments. In particular, safety issues related to the treatment of patients have been detected, such as the lack of adequate safety protocols for the management of dangerous substances. In addition, deficiencies in data management and documentation of hospital treatments were detected.

Effectiveness of Hospital Inspections 2025

Last year, the Swiss Medicines Agency (Swissmedic) carried out 28 hospital inspections in 2025, with the aim of ensuring the safety and effectiveness of healthcare treatments offered in Swiss hospitals. These inspections covered several aspects, including the management of medicines, sterilisation of instruments and the training of healthcare personnel.

Amount of inspections

Inspections were carried out in 14 hospitals across the country, including Zurich University Hospital, Geneva Hospital and Bern Hospital. The total inspections required an investment of 2.8 million Swiss francs.

Cantons involved

Inspections were carried out in 8 Swiss cantons, including:

Swissmedic 2025 Hospital Inspections: Surveillance Is Effective, But Not Enough

The Federal Office of Social Insurance (UFOB) carried out 28 inspections in the areas of reconditioning, maintenance and supervision of medical devices. Inspections were carried out in 26 Swiss hospitals and 2 service companies. The inspections highlighted shortcomings still present, but also positive developments.

A first positive aspect was that, in view of inspections, hospitals often carry out an analysis of the starting situation and processes on the basis of Swissmedic's directives and checklists and plan or initiate the first measures to improve quality. For example, the University Hospital Zurich has implemented a medical device traceability system, which allows their history of use and maintenance to be monitored. This system made it possible to identify and resolve maintenance problems in advance, reducing the risk of medical errors.

However, inspections also highlighted shortcomings still present. For example, only 50% of hospitals have implemented a medical device management system, as required by the 2020 federal regulation. The regulation requires hospitals to implement a medical device management system by 2025, but it seems that many are still not keeping up with the demands.
