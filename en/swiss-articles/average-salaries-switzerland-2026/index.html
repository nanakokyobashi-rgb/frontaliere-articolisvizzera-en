Average salary per profession in Switzerland in 2026 does not mean a single figure valid for everyone. The correct angle is to compare professions, sectors and cantons, using official data and separating statistics from the items that affect the paycheck.

Three levels not to be confused

For statistics, the reference indicated is FSO/BFS: it is the statistical body, but does not set rates. Taxation, on the other hand, follows three levels: direct federal tax, cantonal taxes and municipal taxes. Each canton has its own law and multiplier; the municipality applies a multiplier to the cantonal tax. FTA/ESTV is responsible for IFD and VAT, while the cantonal administrations manage the cantonal and municipal part. FSIO/BSV concerns social security, including AHV/IV and BVP/BVG, and does not set taxes.

This distinction serves to read any comparison on the average salary with caution. Salary contributions are not the same as taxes. For AHV/IV/EO, the employee's share is 5.3%, while the total with the employer reaches 10.6%. The ALV/HC is indicated at 1.1% up to an annual ceiling; the UVG/LAA varies from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector. The BVG/BVG on coordinated salary provides for 7% between 25 and 34 years old, 10% between 35 and 44, 15% between 45 and 54 and 18% from 55 years of age up to the reference age.

The 2026 comparison should therefore be read on two levels: the statistical data of the profession or sector and the rules that affect the individual result. The median salary is among the figures updated every year; if the amount is not verified for the current year, it is advisable to refer to the official source instead of transferring a previous number. In this way, the reader does not confuse a national average with his own calculation and can combine the calcolatore stipendio with the consultation of the data.

Operational details

A useful comparison between cantons does not stop at the average salary column. The tax structure has three levels and the municipal multiplier is applied to the cantonal multiplier: the sheet must therefore distinguish IFD, cantonal tax and municipal tax.

The sector changes the working framework

The sector also changes what has to be compared. The UVG/LAA is indicated between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector. The maximum working time is 45 hours per week for industry, offices and sales, while it reaches 50 hours in the other sectors. The salary, therefore, must be read together with the required work performance and the applicable minimum rules.

The KVG follows a different logic. For residents, it is mandatory, must be concluded within three months of arrival and provides for per capita premiums linked to the canton and region. It is not a tax or a wage contribution; any reduction in premiums is a cantonal subsidy. Including it as a simple deduction from the paycheck would distort the comparison between professions and cantons.

Key points

The comparison can become operational with a five-step form, without turning the statistical average into a promise on the individual salary.

From statistics to choice

1. Start with the profession and industry. Look for the official FSO/BFS figure and check the reference year. The median salary is among the figures updated annually: if the 2026 value is not verified, do not replace it with an amount from another year.

2. Separate taxation. Put the direct federal tax, the cantonal tax and the municipal tax in three lines. For IFD and VAT the reference is AFC/ESTV; for the cantonal and municipal part, look at the cantonal administrations. Remember that the law and multiplier change from canton to canton, while the municipality applies its own multiplier on the cantonal tax.

3. Reconstruct the salary contributions. Indicate AHV/IV/EO at 5.3% for the employee and 10.6% overall with the employer, ALV/AC at 1.1% up to the annual ceiling and UVG/LAA between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector. For BVG/BVG use the age range: 7% between 25 and 34 years old, 10% between 35 and 44, 15% between 45 and 54, 18% from 55 years old up to the reference age.

4. Keep the KVG/KVG out of your paycheck. It is mandatory for residents and must be concluded within three months of arrival. Premiums are per capita, vary by canton and region and may have a cantonal reduction: they are not a tax or a wage contribution.

5. Close the comparison with the minimum conditions: no federal minimum wage, maximum hours of 45 or 50 hours depending on the sector, at least 4 weeks of vacation and rules on maternity, paternity and termination. For the social security part, you can learn more with the AVS/LPP guide.

This sequence avoids two mistakes: comparing only the name of the profession or adding items that follow different rules to the salary. The decision remains anchored in official data, the canton and the sector, without inventing amounts when the annual update is not available. Frequently Asked Questions
Does Switzerland have a federal minimum wage in 2026?
No. The source indicates that in Switzerland there is no federal minimum wage, while some cantons provide for their own. Therefore, to compare a job offer, one must check the rule of the canton concerned and not apply a single national threshold. The average figure by profession remains a statistic: it does not replace checking the cantonal minimum conditions.
Which contributions affect salary?
Regarding salary, AVS/AI/IPG is 5.3% for the employee and 10.6% overall with the employer. AD/AC is 1.1% up to the annual maximum; LAINF/LAA varies between 0.7% and 1.5% according to the sector. For LPP/BVG on the coordinated salary, the indicated rates are 7%, 10%, 15% and 18% in the 25-34, 35-44, 45-54 age brackets and from age 55 up to the reference age.
Is LAMal a deduction from your salary?
No. LAMal/KVG is mandatory health insurance for residents, to be taken out within three months of arrival. It is financed through per-capita premiums, differentiated by canton and region, and is neither a tax nor a salary contribution. The reduction in premiums, where provided for, is a cantonal subsidy. Therefore, for comparing salaries, it must be considered separately from the items on the payslip.
How do salaries compare across cantons and sectors?
Start with the official UST/BFS statistical data relating to the profession and sector, checking the year. Then separate federal direct tax, cantonal and municipal taxes, bearing in mind that each canton has its own law and multiplier and that the municipality applies a multiplier to the cantonal tax. The cantonal minimum wage, working hours, holidays and contributions must also be checked. The median salary is among the figures updated every year.
