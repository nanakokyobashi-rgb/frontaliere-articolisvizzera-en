Federal votes: neutrality and feeding at the polls

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 September 2026

The Swiss population votes today on neutrality and food. The key data, polls and the position of the federal institutions.

Context

In brief

  • Today, voters are voting on neutrality and food.
  • Neutrality has 30% support.
  • The no vote on neutrality is just below 70%.
  • Self-sufficiency: from 46 to 70% in 10 years.

Key facts

  • Votes → neutrality and «For safer food»
  • Neutrality → «Safeguarding Swiss neutrality»

The Swiss population is going to the polls today on two federal initiatives. After the votes on the broadcasting fee and on a Switzerland with 10 million inhabitants, neutrality is once again at the heart of the national debate. The other question concerns food: just hours before the polls close, the ballot thus compares the Confederation’s position with priorities for production, consumption and resources.

The neutrality proposal

The initiative «Safeguarding Swiss neutrality (Neutrality Initiative)» calls for a more restrictive approach and the inclusion in the Constitution of the concept of permanent and armed neutrality. The Federal Council and Parliament reject it: they argue that the practice followed in this area since 1848 has produced good results and that the current Constitution already provides the room for manoeuvre needed to protect the country’s interests in a changing international framework.

The pro-initiative committee brings together Pro Svizzera and UDC and, in Ticino, also forces from the more radical left, including the Partito Comunista. Supporters argue that international events and the geopolitical crises of recent years have watered down Swiss neutrality, reduced the Confederation’s role as a mediator and the scope of its good offices.

«The world does not need a Switzerland that does what everyone else does,» said UDC Council of States member Marco Chiesa.

The voting intentions cited by the source show support at around 30%, compared with an opposing camp steady at just under 70% and few undecided voters. The yes vote is concentrated almost exclusively among the UDC base; among the other historic parties, the no vote prevails.

The second question is the initiative «For safer food». Submitted in August two years ago with over 112'000 signatures, it proposes greater consumption and production of plant-based foods, reducing dependence on foreign countries by raising net self-sufficiency from 46 to 70% within 10 years, and stricter measures to protect drinking water, biodiversity and soil fertility. Franziska Herren, the «Acqua pulita per tutti» association and various public figures support the text. The initiative has, however, met with unanimous opposition in both federal chambers; the Federal Council is also opposed.

Operational details

Two votes, two possible directions

The most useful way to read the vote is to separate the two questions and their possible effects. The first concerns the Confederation's room for maneuver on neutrality; the second links consumption, food production and resource protection. These are not two overlapping dossiers: both the subject and the type of choice proposed to voters are different.

Neutrality: what constraint for the Confederation?

If the initiative were to pass, the point sought by its proponents would be permanent and armed neutrality enshrined in the Constitution. For voters, the choice can be understood as a comparison between a stricter rule and the room for maneuver that the Federal Council and Parliament already consider guaranteed by the constitutional text. The reference to practice since 1848 serves the institutional position in defending a path deemed effective; the committee in favor, instead, uses recent geopolitical crises to argue that this practice has been diluted. The vote therefore pits two interpretations of the same neutrality against each other: continuity of practice or its restriction.

The poll also shows a marked difference between the parties. The no vote reaches 76% in the PLR and 94% in the PS, while support in favor remains almost exclusively among the democentrist base. These figures explain why the proposal has been at the center of the national political debate, despite starting from support levels of around 30%.

Food: contested objective and costs

On the second question, the chain proposed by supporters is precise: reduce the areas devoted to animal feed, increase those for plant foods intended for human consumption, produce more calories and strengthen self-sufficiency. This direction is accompanied by a demand for greater protection of drinking water, biodiversity and soil fertility. Opponents, on the other hand, consider the 70% target unrealistic and fear state intervention in consumption, especially regarding meat and products of animal origin. They also point to the role of livestock farming in mountain regions and the risk of higher prices for Swiss products, resulting in shopping tourism.

The latest Tamedia poll indicates 29% support. Only the Greens' electorate is majority in favor, with 72% yes; the PS and PVL lean toward no, with 52% and 57%, respectively. Centre, UDC and PLR reject the initiative with 82%, 85% and 88% no votes. Among supporters, 30% cite the use of subsidies for sustainable rather than animal production as the main argument; among opponents, 39% fear a menu decided by the state. Key points

How to reach a decision

Four steps before the polls close

1. Separate the questions. The first concerns neutrality; the second concerns food supply. Evaluating the two initiatives independently avoids turning a choice on Confederation policy into an automatic response to the agricultural dossier.

2. For neutrality, identify the requested amendment. The text calls for a more restrictive practice and permanent, armed neutrality in the Constitution. The point to weigh is therefore the relationship between this greater rigidity and the margin of manoeuvre that the Federal Council and Parliament already consider available.

3. For food supply, check the three stated objectives: higher consumption and production of plant-based foods, a net self-sufficiency rate from 46 to 70% within 10 years, and stricter measures for drinking water, biodiversity and soil fertility.

4. Compare the possible costs evoked by the two sides. Proponents link the change to lower import dependency, the production of more calories and water protection. Opponents, on the other hand, point to intervention on consumption, the need for livestock farming in mountain regions, rising prices and shopping tourism.

The source places the decision a few hours before the polls close. At this stage, the Tamedia poll can help understand the political climate, but the choice must be brought back to the concrete contents of the two texts: a more restrictive rule on neutrality on one hand, production and resource protection objectives on the other.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Swiss Neutrality Initiative?
The initiative called 'Safeguarding Swiss Neutrality (Neutrality Initiative)' calls for the inclusion in the Constitution of the concept of permanent and armed neutrality and a more restrictive practice. The Federal Council and Parliament reject the proposal, arguing that the practice followed since 1848 has yielded good results and that the Constitution already offers the necessary room for manoeuvre to protect the country's interests.
What are the main contents of the food initiative?
The 'Safer Food' initiative proposes to increase the consumption and production of plant foods, reduce dependence from abroad by bringing the degree of net self-sufficiency from 46 to 70% within 10 years, and introduce stricter measures to protect drinking water, biodiversity and soil fertility. Deposited with over 112,000 signatures, the initiative is unanimously opposed by the Federal Council and both Chambers.
What are the voting intentions that emerged from the polls for the two questions?
Polls indicate that the initiative on neutrality gathers about 30% of the consensus, with the opposite front just under 70% concentrated in the historical parties, while the yes is supported above all in the democentric base. As for the initiative on food, the latest Tamedia poll indicates 29% support, with support prevailing among the Greens electorate and clear opposition from the other parties.
