Almost four kilometers between Hemishofen and Ramsen, in the canton of Schaffhausen, host the new test phase of Stadler Rail. In the spring the company built a dedicated route here for rolling stock destined for the Italian narrow-gauge networks. The line runs inside an existing standard gauge track and has a third rail.

The convoy is the first hydrogen train built on narrow gauge. It is intended for use in Sardinia and is tested in Switzerland before arriving on the network of the Sardinian Regional Transport Company, ARST. The news is attributed to ATS, indicated in the source as Keystone-ATS.

The regional lines in southern Italy partly use a gauge of 950 millimetres. Trains designed for this distance between the rails are running, according to the press release, in Puglia, Sicily, the Naples area and Sardinia.

From the Californian test to the Italian fleet

The technical reference used by Stadler is the FLIRT H2 made for California. That train carried out its tests on the standard gauge line; the success of the project led to the expansion of the route with the third rail. The systems of the vehicle produced for ARST are now being checked, before moving on to the final tests on the Sardinian network.

Carlo Poledrini, general director of the Sardinian railway company, defined the imminent delivery of the new trains as a decisive step. In the statement reported in the note, he links the testing phase to the possibility of quickly starting the final tests and continuing towards zero-emission mobility and greater energy self-sufficiency.

Over the next three years, the Schaffhausen route will play a role in the commissioning of the fleet that Stadler Rail is building for Sardinia, Calabria and Sicily. The planned checks concern a total of 21 vehicles.

Stadler Rail originates from the engineering firm founded by Ernst Stadler in Zurich in 1942. The company, now based in Bussnang, in the canton of Thurgau, builds high-speed and regional trains, trams and metro vehicles, also used on the Swiss and Ticino railways. The company employs 18,000 people, 6,000 of them in Switzerland, and recorded a turnover of 3.7 billion francs in 2025.

The path from testing to deployment

The third rail is the technical detail to bear in mind. The route built between Hemishofen and Ramsen is part of a standard-gauge line, while the rolling stock intended for the Italian networks is designed for a gauge of 950 millimetres. The source therefore describes a Swiss platform set up to test a train intended for a different type of network.
