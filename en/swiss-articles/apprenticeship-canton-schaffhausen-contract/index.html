The Swiss apprenticeship system in the Canton of Schaffhausen

Apprenticeships in Switzerland are regulated by the Federal Vocational Education and Training Act (LFPr 2002) and protected by the Code of Obligations (CO Art. 344-346). In the Canton of Schaffhausen, as in other cantons, the system follows uniform federal regulations, with cantonal management of training provision and available placements.

A valid apprenticeship requires a written contract between the apprentice and the employer, signed at least 12 months before the actual start. The contract must include: a description of the professional activity, total duration (usually 3-4 years), working hours and vocational school days, remuneration for each year, start and end date.

Federal law protects the apprentice through clear obligations for the employer: a maximum of 45 hours/week (industry, offices, retail) or 50 in other sectors; a minimum of 4 weeks of annual vacation (5 if under 20); protection of health and safety at work; access to theoretical training at the cantonal vocational school (usually 1-2 days/week, paid for by the company).

The initial probationary period is 3 months: during this period, both parties may terminate without giving reasons. After the probationary period, termination requires a valid reason (the employer's insolvency, seriously inadequate performance, health reasons).

Operational details

How to find an apprenticeship placement in the Canton of Schaffhausen

It is essential to start the search at least 18-24 months before the end of compulsory schooling. In the Canton of Schaffhausen, the Cantonal Office for Vocational Education and Training maintains a public database of authorized companies and regularly publishes lists of available positions, also accessible through national platforms such as Annunci di lavoro.

The main steps in the search:

1. Check the available sectors: metalworking, construction, sales, crafts and logistics have traditionally been strong in the canton 2. Contact the cantonal office: free advice and referrals to companies aligned with interests and skills 3. Consult the company database: identify active employers and their training profiles 4. Apply directly: send a CV, cover letter and school results (there is no federal minimum grade requirement, but many companies assess mathematics, languages and conduct) 5. Participate in cantonal orientation days: the canton organizes annual meetings between candidates and companies

Required skills and application timeline

Companies assess not only school grades, but also practical aptitude, concentration, precision, motivation expressed in the letter, personal hygiene and willingness to attend vocational school 1-2 days/week.

The ideal timeline: 7th year of school (informal research and visits), 8th year (formal applications and interviews), by the end of the 8th year (signing the contract), September of the following year (start of the apprenticeship).

If the initial search does not produce results, the cantonal office offers bridge courses and remedial programs to refine skills or fill academic gaps, postponing the apprenticeship by one year if necessary.

Key points

Pay, apprentice rights and vocational baccalaureate

Salary: how it is determined and variability

In Switzerland, there is no federal minimum wage for apprentices; remuneration is negotiated between the employer and the apprentice, often by consulting union rates or local sector standards. In the Canton of Schaffhausen, the level varies significantly by sector, company size and applicable collective agreements.

First year: modest remuneration, linked to the initial learning phase. Second and third years: gradual increases according to the skills acquired. Fourth year (if applicable): approaching the salary of a qualified worker.

Remuneration is paid monthly by the employer; the apprentice pays social security contributions (AVS/AI 5.3%, unemployment insurance 1.1%) on their gross salary, like every worker. The canton monitors that levels do not fall below reasonable thresholds, but does not impose uniform minimums.

Apprentice rights and duties

In addition to maximum working hours and minimum holidays, the apprentice is entitled to:

  • Protection of health and safety at work (equipment, training on risks)
  • Theoretical instruction at vocational school without loss of salary for attendance days
  • Teaching materials provided by the school (books, handouts)
  • Regular assessments by the school and the employer to monitor progress

Duties include: regular school attendance, loyalty at work, compliance with company procedures, and confidentiality regarding company matters.

Vocational Baccalaureate: added value for a career

During or immediately after the apprenticeship, the apprentice may pursue the Vocational Baccalaureate (MP). In the Canton of Schaffhausen, two options are offered: integrated MP (1-2 additional afternoons/week during the 3-4 years of the apprenticeship) or subsequent MP (1-2 years after obtaining the AFC).

The MP is recognized as the federal equivalent of an academic baccalaureate and grants direct access to Universities of Applied Sciences (SUP) in Switzerland without additional examinations. This significantly broadens career prospects.

Step-by-step procedure: from application to the start of the apprenticeship

Months 1-6 of the previous year: Active search through Annunci di lavoro svizzeri, the cantonal database, and direct contacts with companies. Preparation of a clear CV and cover letter.

Months 6-9: Interviews with companies. Possible requests for aptitude tests, school references, and behavior assessments.

Months 9-12: Signing of the contract (federal requirement: 12 months before the start). Verify that it contains all required clauses.

Three months before the start: Enrollment in the cantonal vocational school. The employer and canton coordinate the administrative procedure.

Start date (usually September): Apprenticeship begins. The canton records the contract in a public archive.

During the apprenticeship: School attendance (1-2 days/week), practical work at the company, monthly remuneration payments, and regular assessments by the school.

Last 6 months: Preparation for the AFC examinations (theoretical and practical parts). If the apprentice has started the MP, they continue it in parallel.

End of the apprenticeship: Obtaining the AFC (Federal Certificate of Proficiency). If the apprentice has attended the MP, the federal MP certificate is issued.

Next steps: how to get started concretely

Contact the Cantonal Office for Vocational Education and Training (Department of Education) to check specific deadlines, obtain the complete list of companies, and receive personalized advice.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum wage for an apprentice in Schaffhausen Canton?
There is no federal minimum wage for apprentices in Switzerland. Remuneration is negotiated between employer and apprentice, often following collective agreements or industry standards. In the Canton of Schaffhausen, it varies by sector and year of internship. The canton monitors that the levels are reasonable, but does not impose uniform minimums. Consult the Cantonal Vocational Training Office or trade associations for indicative rates in your industry.
How long does an apprenticeship last and when does it start?
The average duration is 3-4 years, according to the professional profile. The start is usually in September. The contract must be signed at least 12 months before the effective start date. After the first month (trial period), the contract is binding. The exact duration is specified in the contract itself and depends on the chosen professional sector.
Can I attend the professional maturity during the internship?
Yes. In the Canton of Schaffhausen, integrated professional maturity (1-2 additional afternoons/week during the internship) or subsequent professional maturity (1-2 years after the AFC) is available. The MP is federally recognized and allows direct access to University Professional Schools (SUPs) without additional exams. Speak to the Cantonal Office and your employer to assess which modality is most suitable.
What are the main rights and duties in an internship contract?
The apprentice is entitled to: maximum 45 hours/week (industry/offices) or 50 hours in other sectors, at least 4 weeks of annual holidays (5 if under 20), theoretical training in vocational school without loss of salary, health and safety protection. Duties include: regular school attendance, job loyalty, adherence to company procedures. Cancellation before the end of the trial period (3 months) is free; afterwards, it requires valid reason.
How do I actually find an internship in Canton Schaffhausen?
Consult Annunci di lavoro in Svizzera and the public database of the Cantonal Office of Vocational Training. Contact the canton to receive the list of authorized companies. Prepare a CV and clear motivation letter. Apply directly to the companies that interest you. Participate in cantonal orientation days. If initial research doesn't yield results, the canton offers bridging courses and support programs to hone your skills.
