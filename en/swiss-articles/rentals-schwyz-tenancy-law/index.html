The Canton of Schwyz, like all Swiss cantons, applies federal law on tenancy (Art. 253 et seq. CO). This means that the basic rules on security deposit, form of termination and dispute of the initial fee are the same in Schwyz as in Zurich, Geneva or Basel. There is no special cantonal discipline: what changes from canton to canton are procedural and organizational aspects, such as the official cancellation form and the competent conciliation authority.

How the security deposit works

The deposit serves to protect the lessor from any damage or unpaid fees. The Code of Obligations sets a maximum ceiling: it cannot exceed three months of rent (gross rent). The payment takes place in a tied bank account in the name of the tenant: this means that the lessor can not dispose of them freely, but only in the cases and in the manner prescribed by law. At the end of the rental relationship, the deposit is returned — with the accrued interest — after the landlord and tenant have checked the status of the property and settled any slopes.

In practice, those who rent an apartment in Schwyz should put on the balance sheet a sum equal to three months of gross rent as an advance, although in practice many landlords accept lower deposits, especially to keep their property competitive on the local market. It is understood that the ceiling of three months is a maximum, not a minimum.

Form of termination

Since 2014, federal law has provided that the renter's cancellation of a housing contract must be communicated via an official form made available by each canton. The form must be completed indicating the date of receipt, the term of termination and the reasons for the decision, so that the tenant can assess whether to resort to the conciliation authority. For the cancellation given by the tenant, the ordinary written form applies instead.

Terms and conciliation authority

If the tenant receives a notice that he considers abusive — for example, because the initial fee is too high compared to that charged in the area, or the reason indicated on the form appears pretentious — he can appeal it within 30 days to the conciliation authority in the rental matter of his canton. The authority attempts a mediation; in the event of a negative outcome, the tenant may refer the matter to the court. The same 30-day period applies to the challenge of the initial rent, a key tool against excessive rents.

Operational details

The rental market in the Canton of Schwyz is affected by a particular dynamic: it is a central canton, with local economies diversified between the Zurich agglomeration, tourist regions and more peripheral residential municipalities. Average rents therefore vary significantly from area to area — higher in the areas near Zurich and along the lake axis, lower in rural and mountain municipalities. Federal law does not establish maximum rents: it is up to landlords to define the price and to tenants to contest it if deemed abusive.

Average rents and differences by area

In Switzerland, the Bundesamt für Statistik (UST/BFS) regularly publishes data on average rents by canton and municipality, broken down by number of rooms and year of construction. These surveys represent the main reference for evaluating whether a rent is in line with the local market. In the canton of Schwyz the values ​​vary significantly: the suburban areas bordering the canton of Zurich record higher rents, while the more internal municipalities offer lower rents. When it is legitimate to dispute the initial fee

Disputing the initial rent is one of the most effective tools available to the tenant. The deadline is 30 days from receipt of the fee notification. The conciliation authority checks whether the requested rent is abusive, considering the previously charged rent, the characteristics of the property, the equipment and comparable rents in the area. If the authority accepts the request, it can order a reduction in the fee. The procedure is free for the tenant and takes place before a joint body made up of representatives of landlords and tenants.

Difference between contesting the fee and contesting the cancellation

The two institutions must be kept distinct. The dispute about the initial rent (or subsequent increases) concerns the price: the tenant claims that the rent is too high compared to the market. The challenge to the cancellation concerns the landlord's decision to end the contract: the tenant contests the validity of the cancellation or the reason given. In both cases the deadline is 30 days, but the effects are different. In the first case, a reduction in the fee is requested; in the second, the cancellation of the cancellation is requested or, alternatively, an extension of the contract.

Hypothetical application scenario

A tenant who receives a new rent that is significantly higher than the previous one can, within 30 days, contact the conciliation authority. If the rent is found to be abusive - for example because it is not justified by improvements or the performance of the local market - the authority can order a reduction. At the same time, if the landlord sends a cancellation using an official form, the tenant has the same 30-day deadline to contest it with the same authority, requesting its cancellation if the reason given does not appear legitimate.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure to contest a rent increase or a termination notice

Step 1 — Receipt of the notification. The landlord communicates the new rent or the termination notice via the official cantonal form. Note the date of receipt: the 30-day period starts from that moment.

Step 2 — Assessment of the grounds. Check whether the rent is in line with the average rents in the area, by consulting the latest FSO/UST data. For the termination notice, verify that the form is properly filled in and that the reasons are stated.

Step 3 — Filing the objection. Submit the written objection to the conciliation authority of the Canton of Schwyz within 30 days. The procedure is free of charge for the tenant.

Step 4 — Conciliation attempt. The authority summons the parties. If an agreement is reached, the dispute is closed; otherwise, the tenant can bring the case before the court within the prescribed deadlines.

Step 5 — Enforcement. If the authority upholds the request for a rent reduction, the landlord must apply the new rent; if it cancels the termination notice, the contract continues under the previous conditions.

When to consult a professional

For disputes of limited value, the process before the conciliation authority is often sufficient. For more complex disputes — for example, when challenging both the rent and the termination notice at the same time, or when the value of the case is high — it may be advisable to consult a lawyer specialized in tenancy law or a tenants' association active in the canton.
