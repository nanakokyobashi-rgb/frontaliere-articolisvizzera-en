Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 August 2026

In 2025, coal covers only 9.2% of EU production. A drastic drop compared to 1990, when it weighed more than a third of the electricity total.

Key facts

  • What: Gross energy production from coal and lignite
  • When: Data for 2025
  • Where: European Union
  • Who: Eurostat
  • Percentage 2025: 9.2% overall
  • Percentage 1990: More than a third of the total

In 2025, electricity production in the European Union underwent a structural change, with coal hitting a new all-time low. According to Eurostat data, the share of hard coal stood at 3.7% of total gross production, corresponding to 105,601 gigawatt hours (GWh). At the same time, lignite covered 5.5% of production, equal to 154,186 GWh. The combination of these two sources brought the overall share to 9.2%. This result continues a consolidated trend, given that already in 2024 coal had fallen for the first time below the critical threshold of 10% of total European electricity production.

Operational details

The European energy transition directly affects the Swiss energy market, which is closely connected to that of the EU. Although Switzerland has an energy mix characterized by a strong hydroelectric and nuclear component, import and export price dynamics are influenced by the sources used by neighboring countries. The drastic reduction of coal, down to 3.7% for hard coal and 5.5% for lignite, reflects pressure towards decarbonization that indirectly impacts the cost of living in Switzerland. When European electricity supply changes, tariff dynamics can also oscillate, reflecting on the final bill of residents and businesses.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the current share of coal in EU electricity production?
In 2025, hard coal accounted for 3.7% of total gross production, while lignite reached 5.5%. The overall share of the two sources stood at 9.2%.
How has coal consumption changed since 1990?
In 1990, hard coal and lignite together accounted for more than a third of total production, with shares of 19.8% and 15.3% respectively. This is a drastic drop from the 9.2% recorded in 2025.
What sources have surpassed coal in electricity production?
Over time, coal has been surpassed by nuclear energy, natural gas, hydropower, wind and, starting in 2024, also by solar energy.
