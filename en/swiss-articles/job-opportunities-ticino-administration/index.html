The plan, in fact, primarily leverages normal staff turnover: employees who leave the Administration would not be systematically replaced. Operational details

The challenge of creating new jobs in the private economy

The central question that emerges from the initiative to reduce the cantonal workforce is the following: if these 580 positions in the public sector are missing, what concrete policy do the promoters of the initiative – Marquis and the political and economic forces that support them – intend to implement to ensure the creation of at least as many opportunities in the private economy of Ticino? Above all, these new positions will have to offer residents decent wage and contractual conditions, capable of competing with those currently offered by the public sector.

This is not an a priori defence of the workplace in public administration. The real challenge is to define which job market you want to build for the future of Ticino and what prospects to offer to young people who are currently training in the Canton. The reduction of 580 units in the cantonal workforce can be justified as a measure of financial policy. However, if this reduction leads to the loss of 580 professional opportunities, a clear assumption of political and economic responsibility by promoters is necessary to actively encourage the creation of new equivalent opportunities for residents.

Key points

What Citizens Are Asking Before Voting

Before casting their vote on the initiative proposing the reduction of 580 positions in the Cantonal Administration, citizens have the right to receive clear and concrete answers. The fundamental question to ask concerns the future vision of Ticino's labor market and the commitment to creating opportunities for residents. While rationalizing public spending and administrative efficiency are shared goals, the potential impact on job availability for those who live and contribute to the Canton's economy cannot be ignored.

The political and economic responsibility of those promoting the reduction of public administration jobs materializes in their ability to generate, through targeted policies, an equivalent number of new opportunities in the private sector. These opportunities should be characterized by salary and contractual conditions that not only guarantee a dignified life but are also competitive compared to public sector offerings and, ideally, even to those found beyond the Gotthard. This implies an active commitment to supporting local businesses, attracting new qualified investments, and promoting high-value-added economic sectors.

Frequently Asked Questions
How will the reduction of the 580 posts in the Cantonal Administration take place?
The reduction will take place mainly through the normal replacement of personnel. Those who leave the Administration will not necessarily be replaced, leading to a net decrease in the workforce of about 580 units over time.
What are the main concerns about this initiative?
The main concern concerns the impact on the Ticino labour market for residents. The loss of 580 career opportunities in the public sector could reduce the prospects for young people and job seekers in the Canton.
What do citizens ask for before voting?
Citizens demand a concrete plan on how at least 580 new job opportunities will be created in the private economy, with decent and competitive wage and contractual conditions, to compensate for the reduction of jobs in the administration.
Who supports the cantonal workforce reduction initiative?
The initiative is supported by "Avanti con Ticino&Lavoro", by the politician Piero Marchesi, by the economic associations Cc-Ti and AITI, and by the deputy Cristina Maderni.
