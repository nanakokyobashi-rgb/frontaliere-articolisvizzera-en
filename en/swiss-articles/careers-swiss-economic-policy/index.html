Over 900 employees and managers, three locations in Switzerland and around 30 seconded staff in various countries: these are the figures with which SECO presents its role as an employer. The organization does not target just one profile. Alongside apprenticeships and internships, the organization describes opportunities for those who want to work on concrete projects or provide language services as external collaborators.

A diverse range of opportunities

SECO offers AFC apprenticeships for commercial employees, digital business developers and in the IT field. It also welcomes business school interns, completing the range of training opportunities with the SMC pathway.

For those attending university, the internship is presented as an entry point where they can put their knowledge into practice. Interns take on various responsibilities and work on a series of concrete projects. The description therefore links training to the work carried out, without separating learning from responsibilities.

The work environment is described as stimulating. SECO states that it values responsibility, open-mindedness and personal initiative, offering new perspectives to employees who wish to help shape Swiss economic policy. This is the framework accompanying the professional opportunities presented.

The page also includes translation assignments for external collaborators. The language services provide specialized services and have an extensive network of freelance translators, who are called upon to translate into the official languages or other languages. For those looking for job listings, SECO therefore presents itself through several entry points: vocational training, university experience and language collaboration.

The overall message of the page is that working at SECO can take different forms: apprenticeship, traineeship, internship or external assignment. In all the cases mentioned, the presentation emphasizes the possibility of taking on responsibilities and becoming part of work connected to Swiss economic policy, with working conditions described as excellent.

Operational details

Four pathways, one shared framework

The SECO page presents its opportunities by type of entry. It does not present a single standard role, but sets side by side pathways for different target groups and activities. To find one's way around, the most practical approach is to start with one's educational or professional situation and compare it with what is described, without conflating apprenticeships, traineeships, university internships and language collaboration.

The AFC apprenticeship is the pathway with the most precise list of fields: commerce, digital business and IT. The distinction helps prevent the offer from being read as a generic search for personnel, but rather as an offering that includes three explicitly named professional areas.

The reference to commercial school trainees broadens the interpretation of the SMC pathway. Here, the central point is not an activity described in detail, but rather SECO's openness to people coming from that educational background. For the reader, this distinguishes the SMC traineeship from the AFC apprenticeship, which the page presents through specific fields.

The university internship, on the other hand, has a more clearly operational profile: SECO speaks of knowledge to put into practice, various responsibilities and a series of concrete projects. The distinguishing element is therefore the relationship between preparation and the work carried out, not merely the presence of a training position.

Responsibilities and initiative

The common framework is the environment described as stimulating, where responsibility, open-mindedness and personal initiative are valued. This indication also makes it possible to evaluate the offering in terms of how work is done: not only which pathway to choose, but also what attitude SECO says it wants to support.

The fourth option concerns translation assignments. The language services work with a network of freelance translators, and the services concern official languages or other languages. Anyone looking at SECO as a possible employer can therefore distinguish between a training placement and an external collaboration. Key points

How to navigate opportunities

The available information makes it possible to find your way through four steps. The aim is not to add requirements that the page does not state, but to accurately interpret the type of opportunity associated with your path.

Four steps for finding your way

1. Identify the educational path. If the reference is an AFC apprenticeship, the areas indicated are commercial employees, digital business developers and the IT sector. If, on the other hand, the path is the school of commerce, the page refers to SMC internships.

2. Distinguish university education from the other paths. The university internship is described as an experience in which to put your knowledge into practice, take on various responsibilities and work on a series of concrete projects. These are the elements to look for in the opportunity description.

3. Focus on the form of collaboration. The offer includes apprenticeship positions, traineeships and internships; the language services, on the other hand, offer translation assignments for external collaborators. This distinction helps you understand whether you are looking at a training path or a freelance collaboration.

4. Compare your way of working with the framework stated by SECO. Responsibility, open-mindedness and personal initiative are the values indicated for the work environment. The page also links these perspectives to the possibility of contributing to shaping Swiss economic policy.

The final step consists of keeping the different channels separate: vocational training, school of commerce, university and translation. SECO's description does not present them as variants of the same position, but as different possibilities gathered in the same work environment. For those reading the page, this distinction makes it easier to choose which offer to explore further without confusing the characteristics of the various paths.

Frequently Asked Questions
How many employees work at SECO?
SECO has more than 900 employees and managers distributed across three locations in Switzerland. The source adds approximately 30 secondees in various countries. These figures accompany SECO's presentation as an employer and describe the overall size indicated on the page, alongside opportunities for apprenticeships, traineeships and internships.
Which EFZ apprenticeships does SECO offer?
SECO offers VET apprenticeships for commercial employees, digital business developers and in the IT sector. The page also notes that commercial school interns are welcome. SMC apprenticeships and traineeships are therefore presented as distinct pathways within SECO's training opportunities.
What does the university internship at SECO involve?
University internships allow interns to put their knowledge into practice. The source also indicates that they undertake various responsibilities and work on a series of concrete projects. SECO therefore presents the internship as an experience connected to activities and responsibilities, not merely as a period of presence within the organization.
Does SECO offer translation assignments?
Yes. SECO's language services provide specialized services and have an extensive network of freelance translators. External collaborators translate into the official languages or other languages. This possibility is indicated separately from apprenticeships, SMC traineeships and university internships.
