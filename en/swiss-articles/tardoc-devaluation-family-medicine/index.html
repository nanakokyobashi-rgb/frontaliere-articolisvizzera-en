TARDOC under the scrutiny of family doctors

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 September 2026

mfe criticizes the new tariff: rising outpatient costs, family medicine without revaluation. The risk to access to basic care.

Context

In a nutshell

  • mfe criticizes the TARDOC for not re-evaluating family medicine
  • Increasing outpatient costs, family medicine devalued in some cantons
  • Risk of removing young doctors from primary care in Switzerland

Key facts

  • What: Criticism of the TARDOC medical tariff by Mfe
  • When: Communication issued by mfe (date not specified in the source)
  • Where: Zurich (MFE headquarters), Swiss health system
  • Who: Family and children's doctors Switzerland (MFE), Monika Reber and Sébastien Jotterand, co-chairs
  • Problem: Increased outpatient costs without re-evaluation of family medicine

The Swiss Family and Children's Medical Association (Mfe) has issued a statement critical of the TARDOC, the new medical tariff. According to the association, the introduction of this tool has not materialized the revaluation of family medicine, a goal required by every political and health party.

On the contrary, the observed picture reveals a worrying trend. The TARDOC system has led to a considerable increase in overall costs in the outpatient sector, while family medicine has even suffered a devaluation in some cantons. The only notable exception: pediatricians and general practitioners register a slight improvement in their rates.

Operational details

The impact on young doctors and the healthcare system

The problem goes beyond the simple economic issue. According to mfe, the tariff evolution sends a decidedly negative message to young doctors who are considering which specialty to choose. If family medicine remains economically less attractive than other specialties, with equal training and clinical responsibility, the concrete risk is that an entire generation of professionals will move away from it.

"A tariff system that disadvantages primary care so blatantly worsens the shortage of family doctors instead of combating it," says Sébastien Jotterand, co-president of the association. The shortage of family doctors is already a critical reality in many Swiss regions. To better understand how your access to primary care is ensured by the healthcare system, you can consult the LAMal system guide. Further professional disincentives could drastically worsen access to services for resident citizens.

mfe's proposal: targeted, not linear corrections

mfe recognizes that, due to the general increase in costs, some tariff adjustments are inevitable to meet the cost-neutrality requirements set by the Federal Council. However, the association firmly opposes a linear devaluation that affects all medical specialties equally.

The association instead proposes differentiated and targeted tariff corrections in sectors where tariffs are truly inadequate. Specifically:

  • Areas with excessive time allocations
  • Specialties where infrastructure and personnel costs have been underestimated

This calibrated strategy should have protected family medicine from further reductions, concentrating adjustments only where effectively necessary and justified by cost data.

Key points

How TARDOC influences the Swiss healthcare system

TARDOC is the reference tariff for outpatient medical services in Switzerland. The established rates directly impact the costs managed by health insurance funds, which in turn influence the premiums of mandatory health insurance (LAMal/KVG) that every resident is required to subscribe to. When overall outpatient costs increase, health insurance funds commonly pass on these increases to citizens through premium hikes.

The current situation therefore represents a multi-level challenge: on one hand, the overall costs of outpatient care have risen, which could impact premiums for all insured individuals; on the other hand, family medicine — which serves as the gateway to the system for most patients — loses economic appeal, risking a shortage of professionals that would reduce supply and lengthen waiting times.

Next developments and monitoring

mfe's criticisms open a broader discussion on how the Federal Council intends to address the cost-neutrality constraints without compromising the quality and accessibility of primary care. The association will continue to urge the government to review linear tariff adjustments and opt for a more differentiated and sustainable strategy.

Residents of Switzerland can follow the evolution of the debate by monitoring the official communications of the Federal Council and professional medical associations. In the event of further tariff adjustments, communications will primarily occur through the official channels of the federal administration.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the TARDOC and how does it affect my healthcare costs?
The TARDOC is the medical tariff for outpatient services in Switzerland. It sets the rates that doctors and specialists can charge for their services. Since these costs fall on the health insurance funds, they directly affect your compulsory health insurance premiums (LAMal/KVG). An increase in TARDOC costs tends to reflect on premiums over time, although the translation may vary per canton and per sick fund.
Why is Mfe critical of the new TARDOC?
According to Mfe, the TARDOC did not allow the economic re-evaluation of family medicine, as required by many actors in the health system. At the same time, it has led to a considerable increase in overall outpatient costs. In some cantons, family medicine has even undergone a tariff reduction, creating an imbalance that penalizes primary care compared to other specialties.
How does this criticism affect the availability of family doctors?
If family medicine remains less economically attractive than other specialties, young doctors may choose to specialize in different fields. This risks aggravating the already critical shortage of family doctors in many Swiss regions, lengthening waiting times and reducing access to care for resident patients.
What corrections does Mfe propose to the TARDOC?
mfe opposes a linear devaluation that affects all specialties. The association, on the other hand, proposes tariff corrections aimed at sectors where tariffs are really inadequate, in particular where the details are excessive or where the costs of infrastructure and personnel have been underestimated. This targeted strategy should protect family medicine from further reductions.
When will any tariff adjustments take effect?
The article does not specify definitive timelines. However, the Federal Council, which has established the expense neutrality requirements, will be responsible for deciding whether and how to make corrections. Significant tariff changes are communicated through the official channels of the federal administration and may require transition periods.
