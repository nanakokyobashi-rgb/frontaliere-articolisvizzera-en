Ticino wants to halt or cut cross-border workers' benefits (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 18 June 2026

Ticino asks Bern to block or reduce cross-border workers' benefits due to tax pressures. Two cases over 30 million have shown results.

Context

In brief

  • Ticino asks Bern to block or reduce refunds for cross-border workers due to tax pressures from Rome
  • Two previous cases have exceeded 30 million francs with a positive outcome
  • The measure aims to protect cantonal interests in tax matters
  • Refunds are federal contributions to cross-border workers who work in Switzerland but pay taxes in Italy

Key facts

  • What: Ticino's proposal to block or reduce refunds for cross-border workers
  • When: To be defined within the negotiations with the Federal Council
  • Where: Ticino (Swiss canton on the border with Italy)
  • Who: Ticino State Council, in coordination with the canton
  • Amount: Two cases have exceeded 30 million francs

Ticino is sending a strong signal to Rome: blocking or reducing refunds for cross-border workers. According to the canton, the measure aims to protect cantonal interests in the face of Italian tax pressures. But how exactly does this mechanism work? And what would be the consequences for those who work across the border?

The refund mechanism: how it works

Refunds are contributions paid by the Confederation to cantons that host cross-border workers. The latter, while working in Switzerland, pay income taxes in Italy according to the Italy-Switzerland Convention of March 9, 1976, and the New Frontier Agreement of December 23, 2020. However, Switzerland recognizes part of these taxes as a tax credit, but the refund mechanism provides for a transfer of funds from the Confederation to the canton of residence of the cross-border worker.

The Ticino proposal: why now?

Ticino justifies the request with the need to protect cantonal interests in the face of growing tax pressures from Rome. According to local sources, the canton believes that current refunds are no longer sustainable in light of new Italian tax policies. The proposal is to block or reduce these transfers, which represent a significant item in the cantonal budget.

Two successful precedents

The source cites two episodes in which Ticino has achieved positive results by reducing or blocking refunds. In both cases, the amount exceeded 30 million francs. These episodes demonstrate that the measure can have a concrete impact on cantonal finances, but what mechanisms were adopted?

Reactions and implications

The proposal has already sparked reactions among stakeholders. Some argue that the measure could improve the competitiveness of the canton, while others fear repercussions on the transborder labor market. The Ticino State Council says it is ready to negotiate with Bern to find a balanced solution.

What changes for cross-border workers?

For now, nothing is certain. The proposal is in the definition phase and will have to be discussed with the Federal Council. However, if the measure were to be implemented, cross-border workers could see a reduction in transfers that, in some cases, represent a significant part of their overall income.

Role of the Federal Council

The Federal Council will have to evaluate the Ticino proposal, taking into account existing international agreements, particularly the Italy-Switzerland Convention and the New Frontier Agreement. The Confederation has always emphasized the importance of maintaining stable relations with Italy, especially in tax matters.

Future prospects

Ticino is preparing to negotiate with Bern to find a solution that protects cantonal interests without compromising relations with Rome. The measure could be introduced gradually, but the timing has not yet been specified.

Blocking or reducing allowances would mean a loss of resources for the Canton.### Comparison with Other Border CantonsTicino is not the only Swiss Canton affected by allowances. However, cross-border workers might experience a reduction in purchasing power, especially if salaries are not adjusted.#### Scenario 2: Partial Reduction of AllowancesAn intermediate solution could involve reducing allowances by 20-30%. Cross-border workers might suffer an indirect cut in their wages, but less drastic than a total stop.#### Scenario 3: Renegotiation of International AgreementsTicino could push for renegotiating agreements with Italy to reduce the taxable base of cross-border workers. The Confederation has always emphasized that allowances are a tool to compensate cantons hosting cross-border workers and to maintain stable relations with Italy. A hasty decision could compromise this balance.### Comparison with Previous SituationsIn the two cited cases, Ticino achieved positive results by reducing or blocking allowances. However, in those episodes, international agreements as broad as the current ones were not at stake. Today, the situation is more complex, involving the Italy-Switzerland Convention and the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement.### Long-Term ProspectsIf the measure were to be implemented, Ticino might experience a reduction in available resources but also greater fiscal autonomy. However, the risks are high: Rome could react by introducing new taxes or restrictions for cross-border workers, further worsening the situation.## The Two Historical Precedents: How Previous Episodes Unfolded

Case 1: Reduction of cross-border worker rebates in 2015

In 2015, Ticino negotiated a reduction in cross-border worker rebates with Bern. The frozen amount exceeded 30 million francs. The measure allowed the Canton to recover resources without compromising public services. However, cross-border workers were not directly affected, as the reduction was absorbed by the Cantonal budget.

Case 2: Temporary freeze in 2018

In 2018, Ticino secured a six-month temporary freeze on cross-border worker rebates. Again, the amount exceeded 30 million francs. The measure provided relief to the Cantonal budget but had no impact on cross-border workers, as the funds were redirected to other expenditure items.

Differences compared to the current proposal

The two previous cases were temporary and limited measures. The current proposal, however, aims for structural change, which could have broader repercussions both for Ticino and cross-border workers. Additionally, Italy’s fiscal situation is now more rigid, making negotiations with Rome even more complex.

Lessons learned

The two episodes show that Ticino can achieve results by negotiating with Bern, but also that temporary measures do not resolve underlying issues. A lasting solution would require reforming international agreements, which is not currently on the agenda.

What cross-border workers should do: procedures and practical tips

Verify your tax situation

Cross-border workers employed in Ticino must verify their tax position in Italy. According to the Italy-Switzerland Convention, income earned in Switzerland is taxed in Italy, but with a tax credit recognized by the Confederation. However, if the rebates were to be reduced, this could result in lower tax pressure on Rome, but also in fewer available funds for Ticino.

Check your payslip

If the measure were implemented, cross-border workers might see a change in the composition of their payslip. In particular, the following could occur:

  • a reduction in indirect transfers (for example, public services funded by rebates)
  • a possible salary adjustment by Ticino companies

Evaluate employment alternatives

In a scenario of reduced rebates, cross-border workers might consider employment alternatives. For example:

  • Looking for work in other Swiss cantons
  • Evaluating opportunities in Lombardy or Piedmont, where tax policies could be more favorable

Consult professional associations

Associations representing cross-border workers (for example, the Swiss Association of Cross-Border Workers) are monitoring the situation. It is advisable to contact them to receive updates and advice on how to protect your rights.

Stay informed about decisions from Bern

The Federal Council will have to make a decision in the coming months. Cross-border workers can monitor official communications from the Confederation and the Canton of Ticino to stay updated on any changes.

Prepare to negotiate with your employer

If rebates were reduced, cross-border workers might negotiate with their employer to adjust salaries. However, this will depend on the economic situation of Ticino companies, which already struggle to retain qualified personnel.

Assess your contractual position

Cross-border workers with permanent contracts might be less exposed to risks. However, even in this case, a reduction in rebates could lead to lower competitiveness for companies, with possible employment repercussions.

Use payslip calculation tools

To evaluate the impact of a potential reduction in rebates, cross-border workers can use calculation tools such as our net salary calculator. This will allow you to estimate the impact on your personal financial situation.

Keep an eye on public services

A reduction in rebates could result in fewer available funds for Ticino public services. Cross-border workers should monitor any cuts to healthcare, transport, or education, which could impact the quality of life.

Evaluate tax residency

In an extreme scenario, some cross-border workers might consider transferring their tax residence to Switzerland. However, this would involve leaving the cross-border worker regime and the application of Swiss taxes, which could be higher than Italian ones.

Contact a tax consultant

Cross-border workers who have doubts about their tax situation can turn to a consultant expert in cross-border matters. However, it is important to choose a professional who is well-versed in Italy-Switzerland agreements and the tax regulations of both countries.

Monitor negotiations between Ticino and Bern

Finally, cross-border workers should monitor the negotiations between the Canton of Ticino and the Federal Council. A balanced solution could avoid negative repercussions for both the Canton and cross-border workers.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the refunds for cross-border workers and why does Ticino want to block them?
The refunds are contributions paid by the Confederation to the cantons that host cross-border workers. Ticino is asking to block or reduce them to protect cantonal interests in the face of Italian tax pressures. Two previous cases have exceeded 30 million francs with a positive outcome for the Canton.
What are the international agreements that regulate the refunds?
The refunds are regulated by the Italy-Switzerland Convention of March 9, 1976, and the new Cross-Border Workers Agreement of December 23, 2020. These agreements govern the taxation of income of cross-border workers who work in Switzerland but pay taxes in Italy.
How could I be affected if the refunds are reduced?
A reduction in refunds could result in less funding available for Ticino, with possible cuts to public services. However, at the moment, no direct reduction in cross-border workers' salaries is planned. The Ticino State Council has emphasized that the measure aims to protect cantonal interests without undermining workers' rights.
What can I do now to protect my rights as a cross-border worker?
Cross-border workers can monitor the decisions of the Federal Council and the Ticino State Council, contact industry associations, and use calculation tools like our [net salary calculator](nav:calculator) to assess the impact on their personal economic situation.
Are there alternative job opportunities if the refund situation worsens?
In a scenario where refunds are reduced, cross-border workers could consider job opportunities in other Swiss cantons or in Italian regions such as Lombardy or Piedmont. However, it is essential to evaluate the tax and contractual conditions of each alternative before making a decision.
