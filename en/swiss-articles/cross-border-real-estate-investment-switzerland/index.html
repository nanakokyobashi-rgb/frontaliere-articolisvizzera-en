The purchase of real estate in Switzerland by a cross-border worker represents a choice that requires an in-depth analysis of the national regulatory framework. Switzerland, not being a member of the EU or the EEA, maintains distinctive legislation regarding real estate ownership for those who do not permanently reside in Swiss territory. For Italian citizens who work in Switzerland, the distinction between purchasing a first home, intended as a primary residence, and a pure real estate investment is clear-cut and governed by rules that vary depending on the canton concerned. The applicable framework is affected by the Convention against double taxation, signed on March 9, 1976, which governs tax relations between the two countries. It is essential to understand that, as of January 1, 2024, the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement, ratified in Italy by Law 83 of June 13, 2023, is fully in force and changes the taxation arrangements for employment income. Withholding tax is deducted in Switzerland, while Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit entered in section CE of Form 730. Old cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (Article 9 of the 2020 Agreement). The transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euro under Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for the income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, not a general tax-free allowance. Legislative restrictions for foreigners

Restrictions on the purchase of real estate are administered by the federal and cantonal authorities. Those who do not reside in Switzerland often have to follow specific authorization procedures to acquire property. It is necessary to check with the competent cantonal offices whether the property under consideration is located in areas subject to restrictions. The nature of the investment, whether for rental purposes or personal use, often determines the feasibility of the transaction. It is essential to consult local regulations because each canton has autonomy to regulate access to the real estate market for non-residents, in compliance with federal laws aimed at preventing land speculation.

Operational details

The practical analysis of real estate investment requires separating the management of employee income from the asset management of real estate. Although the cross-border worker pays income taxes in Switzerland, the purchase of a property entails tax obligations linked to the ownership of the property. In Switzerland, real estate is subject to rental value tax, which is a theoretical value that the owner would have to pay to live in their property. This value is added to the owner's taxable income. For a border investor, this aspect represents a recurring cost to be carefully considered. Unlike salary taxation, which is managed by the Federal Tax Administration (AFC) and cantonal administrations, real estate taxation follows specific cantonal logic.

Differences between first home and investment

The distinction between the purchase of a primary residence and a pure investment also affects the deductibility of mortgage interest. In Switzerland, mortgage interest is generally deductible from taxable income. However, the conditions for obtaining mortgage financing for a non-resident are often more stringent than those applied to residents with a residence permit. Swiss banks, which operate according to SNB guidelines, usually require a minimum equity capital which can vary between 20% and 40% of the value of the property. Pure real estate investment, on the other hand, does not usually allow the use of professional pension capital. Frequently Asked Questions
Can the frontier worker deduct the interests of the Swiss mortgage?
Yes, in Switzerland interest payable on mortgage debt is generally deductible from taxable income. However, it is necessary to assess the overall tax situation, since the property also generates a theoretical taxable income, known as the rental value, which must be added to its income. The deductibility is subject to the tax rules of the Canton in which the property is located.
What are the requirements for a non-resident mortgage?
Swiss banks usually require equity capital that varies between 20% and 40% of the value of the property. The applicant must demonstrate a solid earning capacity and, in the case of frontier workers, submit documentation relating to the net salary after withholding of the tax at source. Each institution assesses creditworthiness based on internal criteria compliant with SNB directives.
Does the New Frontier Agreement change the taxation on the property?
No, the New Frontier Agreement, applied since 1 January 2024, exclusively governs the taxation of employee income. Property taxation, including rental value tax and substance taxes, continues to be regulated by Swiss cantonal and federal laws, regardless of the border status of the owner.
