Starting a business in the Canton of Solothurn requires a series of bureaucratic and financial steps that every entrepreneur must know. The choice of legal form is the first crucial step. Each legal form has different tax and insurance implications, so it is important to make an informed choice. Registration in the commercial register is mandatory for all commercial activities, regardless of their size. This process involves registration costs that vary depending on the legal form chosen.

Choice of Legal Form

The choice of legal form depends on various factors, including the size of the business, the number of shareholders and financial responsibilities. The most common options include sole proprietorship, general partnership, limited partnership and limited liability company. Each shape has its advantages and disadvantages, so it is advisable to consult an expert to make the right choice.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

Starting a business in the Canton of Solothurn involves a series of practical implications that every entrepreneur must consider. Taxation is one of the most complex aspects, with three levels of tax: federal, cantonal, and municipal. Each canton has its own laws and tax multipliers, so it is important to know the specifics of the Canton of Solothurn.

Registration Costs

The costs of registering with the commercial register vary depending on the legal form chosen. For example, a sole proprietorship may have lower costs compared to a limited liability company. It is essential to have accurate financial planning to cover these initial costs. In the Canton of Solothurn, the costs for registering a sole proprietorship can range between CHF 100 and CHF 200, while for a limited liability company (LLC), the costs can go up to CHF 1,000. It is important to note that these costs may also include the expenses for publication in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SOGC).

Insurance Obligations

Insurance obligations are another crucial aspect. Every business must comply with social security regulations, which include contributions to AHV/AIV/IPG, AD/AC, and LPP. These contributions vary depending on the sector and the coordinated salary. For example, for an employee with a coordinated salary of CHF 6,000 per month, the AHV/AIV/IPG contributions amount to approximately CHF 360, while the AD/AC contributions are approximately CHF 120. For professional pension provision (LPP), the contributions can vary but generally amount to around 10% of the coordinated salary, or CHF 600 in this case.

Key points

Action

To start a business in the Canton of Solothurn, you need to follow a series of concrete steps. First, it is important to choose the legal form that best suits your needs. Then, you need to register with the commercial register, paying the relevant registration costs. Finally, it is essential to comply with all insurance obligations provided by law.

Step-by-Step Procedure

1. Choose the most suitable legal form.

  • Sole proprietorship: Ideal for those who want to maintain full control of the business. It does not require a minimum capital, but the owner is personally liable for the company's debts.
  • Limited Liability Company (LLC): Requires a minimum capital of CHF 20,000. Partners have limited liability to their capital contribution.
  • Joint-stock company (JSC): Requires a minimum capital of CHF 100,000. Partners have limited liability to their capital contribution.

2. Register with the commercial register.

  • The registration cost varies depending on the canton. In the Canton of Solothurn, the cost is approximately CHF 200 for the initial registration and CHF 100 for each subsequent modification.

3. Pay the registration costs.

  • In addition to the commercial register registration costs, there are also costs for publishing the constitution announcement in the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce (SOGC), amounting to approximately CHF 150.

4. Comply with insurance obligations.

  • It is mandatory to register with the Disability Insurance (DI) and the Maternity Insurance (MI). Contributions vary depending on income, but on average they amount to around 10% of the salary.
