Operational details

The change in the international security context also requires an acceleration on the passive protection front. In addition to weapon systems, the Federal Council has planned an investment of 100 million francs specifically dedicated to strengthening the self-protection of more than 60 military locations distributed throughout the national territory. The planned interventions include the upgrading of video surveillance systems, the implementation of electronic access controls, the installation of improved fences and the creation of dedicated security rooms. These measures, deemed urgent to ensure the resilience of infrastructure, must be completed by the beginning of 2029. The urgency of the situation has prompted the executive to request an additional credit under the 2025 real estate program, integrated in the second addition to the 2026 estimate, avoiding postponing decisions to the 2027 army message.

Financial analysis and procedures

The financing of these interventions will not lead to an increase in the direct tax burden for taxpayers, since the Confederation has planned to cover the expenditure thanks to the increase in income generated by the income tax. Key points

The procedure for approving these credits follows the institutional paths provided by the Swiss democratic system. The Federal Council has authorized the Federal Department of Defence, Civil Protection and Sport (DDPS) to submit the request to Parliament. The process involves two key steps: the decision on the 2026 Army Dispatch, scheduled during the Parliament's autumn session, and the decision on the second addition to the 2026 budget, scheduled for the winter session. Only after parliamentary approval will the Federal Council be able to formally proceed with the payment of advances to international suppliers.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the amount of investments decided by the Federal Council?
The Federal Council has decided to ask Parliament for an additional appropriation of CHF 970 million for air defence and an additional investment of CHF 100 million aimed at strengthening the passive protection of over 60 military locations on the national territory. The overall sum aims to ensure the resilience of infrastructures and the effectiveness of weapon systems in the face of a constantly evolving international threat context and prolonged delivery times in the arms market.
How will the 970 million francs be distributed?
The funds are intended to cover the necessary advances for different ground-to-air defense systems: 250 million francs for short-range defense, 650 million francs for medium-range defense, 60 million francs for a partially mobile medium-range radar and 10 million francs for defense systems against mini-drones. These investments are necessary to avoid multi-year delays in supplies and to equip the military with the minimum means to protect the population and critical infrastructure.
What is the expected parliamentary procedure for these credits?
The procedure involves two fundamental institutional steps: the decision on the 2026 army message, scheduled during the autumn session of Parliament, and the vote on the second addition to the 2026 budget, scheduled for the winter session. Only after the parliamentary green light can the Federal Council formally proceed with the payment of advances to international suppliers. The funding will be covered by federal income tax revenues, without burdening taxpayers.
