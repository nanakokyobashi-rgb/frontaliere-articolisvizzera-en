The Swiss National Bank (SNB) plays a crucial role in Switzerland's monetary policy, directly influencing the economy and wages. The strong franc, one of the country's main economic concerns, has a significant impact on various sectors, including wages and export competitiveness. The SNB uses tools such as interest rates to maintain price stability and support economic growth.

Tax Rates and Contributions

Switzerland has a complex tax system that includes federal and cantonal rates. Swiss rates/contributions include AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3% for employees, AD/AC at 1.1% with a ceiling of CHF 148'200, LAINF between 0.7% and 1.5%, and LPP between 7% and 18% for age groups from 25 years. In Italy, the IRPEF varies from 23% for income up to €28,000, to 35% for income between €28,001 and €50,000, and to 43% for income over €50,000.

Comparison between Old and New Frontiersmen

The old frontier workers, those who were already working before 17 July 2023, benefit from an exemption of €7,500, while the new frontier workers have a deductible of €10,000. This transitional regime, which runs from 2024 to 2033, aims to ease the transition for frontier workers.
