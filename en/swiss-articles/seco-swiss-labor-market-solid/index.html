Labour market in Switzerland: Seco confirms its solidity (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 September 2026

The Swiss labour market remains solid despite uncertainties. Unemployment in August stands at 3.0%, with a seasonal increase among young people.

Context

In brief

  • Unemployment rate at 3% in August
  • Increase among young people (15-24 years) to 3.4%
  • Confirmation of solidity by Jérôme Cosandey (Seco)

Key facts

  • What: Unemployment data in Switzerland
  • When: August
  • Where: Switzerland
  • Who: State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (Seco)
  • Amount: 3.0% total unemployment

The Swiss labor market as a whole continues to show resilience in a context still characterized by numerous geopolitical and economic uncertainties. This was stated by Jérôme Cosandey, head of the labor Directorate at the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (Seco), commenting in a teleconference on the unemployment data released in the morning by his officials.

Official August data

Official data released in August record an overall unemployment rate of 3.0%. The only significant increase concerns young people between 15 and 24 years of age, with the share of the unemployed rising to 3.4% in the eighth month of the year. This represents an increase of 0.6 percentage points compared to July, recording 2,400 more people for a total of 14,700 young people without a job. This dynamic is part of the general framework constantly monitored by the competent federal authorities for economy and employment throughout the national territory.

Operational details

Seasonal dynamics for young people

According to Jérôme Cosandey, the increase recorded among young people is an entirely normal seasonal phenomenon. Every summer, thousands of young people who have finished school, training or university enter the job market and have not yet found employment. The peak in unemployment in this segment is usually reached in August. The number of unemployed people among the under-20s then drops rapidly towards autumn, while for 20-25 year-olds the situation improves markedly with the start of the following year. The manager expressed confidence that this pattern will repeat itself on this occasion as well.

Structural differences across age groups

The data also confirms a significant structural difference within the Swiss labor market: young people are more exposed to the risk of being unemployed, but they find new employment much faster than older age groups. On average, for those under 25, it takes just over three months to leave the unemployment register, while for the over-55s it exceeds nine months. This difference in reintegration times highlights the different challenges that various generations face when looking for a new professional opportunity in the national economic context.

The trend of short-time work

Positive signals also come from short-time work. This tool, which allows companies to temporarily reduce working hours without proceeding with layoffs, is being used less and less. This trend confirms the overall resilience of the Swiss productive fabric, as concluded by Jérôme Cosandey during his economic analysis.
