Starting a business in the Canton of Bern requires a structured planning that starts from the identification of the legal form best suited to the needs of the business project. The Swiss system provides for several options, each with different implications in terms of responsibility, necessary share capital and registration requirements. The choice of the corporate model affects not only the day-to-day management, but also the administrative procedures that the owner must carry out with the cantonal authorities. The national legislation establishes the general guidelines, which are then declined at the cantonal level through the specific provisions of the Commercial Register of the Canton of Bern.

Operational details

The practical analysis of starting a business in the Canton of Bern must carefully consider aspects related to social security and employee protection, which represent an integral part of labor costs in Switzerland. Every employer is required to pay mandatory social contributions, which include AHV/IV/EO, unemployment insurance, and accident insurance (UVG/LAA). The correct management of these payments is not only a legal obligation but also ensures the necessary protection for workers in times of need. In addition to social contributions, it is important to remember that the Swiss tax system operates at three levels: federal, cantonal, and municipal. This means that the location of the business not only affects proximity to customers but also determines the applicable tax multiplier, a factor that can significantly impact the company's net profit.

Key points

To initiate a new business, the owner must follow a defined operational path that begins with verifying the availability of the company name at the competent office. Once the name is confirmed, the articles of association must be drafted and authenticated by a notary. This document is essential for proceeding with the registration in the commercial register. In parallel, it is necessary to open a Swiss corporate bank account to deposit the share capital, if required by the legal form. The banking documentation will subsequently be needed to complete the registration. After registration, it is necessary to register with the Federal Tax Administration for VAT, if the projected turnover exceeds the legal thresholds, and with the relevant AVS compensation fund for the management of social security contributions. Tax and social security compliance is an ongoing process that requires periodic updates, especially in relation to changes in Swiss economic indices.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main legal forms in Switzerland?
The main legal forms include sole proprietorship, limited liability company (Sagl) and public limited company (SA). Each type has substantial differences in terms of property liability, share capital requirements and registration procedures in the commercial register. It is advisable to carefully evaluate the most suitable structure for your business plan.
Is a bank account required for enrollment?
Yes, for joint-stock companies it is necessary to pay the share capital into a escrow account in the name of the company being incorporated. This bank documentation is essential to formalise the registration with the commercial register of the Canton of Bern.
What social insurance is mandatory for the employer?
The employer must register with an AVS compensation fund for the payment of social contributions (AVS/AI/IPG/AD). In addition, accident insurance (LAINF/LAA) is mandatory for all employees and the payment of LPP/BVG contributions for occupational insurance, according to the thresholds established by federal law.
