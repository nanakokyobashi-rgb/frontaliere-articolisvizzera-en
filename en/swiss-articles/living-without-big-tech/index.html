Living without Big Tech: Is It Possible?

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 21 June 2026

Two Swissinfo journalists tried to cut ties with US tech giants. Here's what happened.

Context

In brief

  • Two Swissinfo journalists have decided to distance themselves from big tech companies.
  • They started from scratch: computer, email, phones, cloud services, and artificial intelligence tools.
  • The process was slow and complicated, with difficulties in accessing services and payments.
  • The dependence on Big Tech is deep and requires time, money, and patience to overcome.
  • What: Two Swissinfo journalists tried to live and work without Big Tech.
  • Where: Switzerland.
  • Who: Kristian Foss Brandt and Sara Ibrahim, Swissinfo journalists.
  • Amount: Not specified.

The digital life is dominated by Big Tech. Almost every interaction with the outside world goes through a handful of US companies. Our personal data, from photos to passwords, are stored in the cloud infrastructure of these companies.

Two Swissinfo journalists, Kristian Foss Brandt and Sara Ibrahim, have decided to cut ties with these big tech companies. They embarked on a journey towards digital independence, starting from the basics: computer, email, phones, cloud services, and artificial intelligence tools.

Initially, it seemed like simple changes: abandoning one service to adopt another. However, they soon realized that the problem is not a single app or platform, but the ecosystem built around our digital lives by big tech companies.

Getting rid of it requires time, money, and a lot of patience. The two journalists experienced firsthand the difficulties of this process, from work apps crashing to mobile payments stopping to function.

Facts and figures

The dependence on Big Tech is a complex issue that involves many aspects of daily life. The two journalists had to face numerous challenges, from managing email to replacing cloud services.

Switzerland, with its advanced economy and strong presence of tech companies, is an interesting case to examine the dependence on Big Tech. The country has one of the highest internet usage rates and digital technology penetration in Europe.

However, Switzerland could also be a fertile ground for experimenting with a different approach, more independent from big tech companies. The growing concern for data privacy and security could push users to look for alternatives.

In this context, the experience of the two Swissinfo journalists offers a unique perspective on the challenges and opportunities of digital independence.
