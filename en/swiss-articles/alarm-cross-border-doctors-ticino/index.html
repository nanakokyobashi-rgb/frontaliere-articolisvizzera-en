Ticino, medical alarm: Teeth ask border crossers for limits (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 September 2026

The president of the Order of Doctors of Ticino Franco Denti warns: Ticino has one doctor for every 900 inhabitants, at Como one for every 2,000. Ethical reflection is needed.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Ticino has one family doctor for every 900 inhabitants.
  • In the province of Como, the ratio is one doctor for every 2,000 people.
  • Franco Denti calls for an ethical reflection on the recruitment of health workers.
  • A strategy is needed to ensure attractiveness to the medical profession.

Key facts

  • What: Alarm shortage of doctors and border nurses.
  • Where: Ticino and the province of Como.
  • Who: Franco Denti, president of the Order of Doctors of Ticino.
  • Doctors' ratio Ticino: 1 per 900 inhabitants.
  • Como doctors' report: 1 per 2,000 inhabitants.
  • Number of Ticino family doctors: Approximately 390.

The president of the Order of Doctors of Ticino, Franco Denti, raised an important issue regarding the maintenance of the regional health system and its relationship with the neighboring Italian territory. The situation in Ticino, characterized by the presence of about 390 family doctors for 351 thousand inhabitants, offers a framework of health coverage that appears significantly more solid than what has been observed across borders. In the province of Como, in fact, the number of active family doctors is less than 300 units for a population of about 598 thousand people, resulting in a significant disparity in the relationship between professionals and citizens. This gap, reconstructed through the comparison data between the two sides of the border, highlights how Switzerland represents an attractive destination for many health professionals trained in Italy.

The Ethical Question

Operational details

The stability of the Ticino healthcare system is inextricably linked to the demographic challenges affecting the entire country. According to Franco Denti, generational turnover represents the next major test for local family medicine. Professionals from the baby boomer generation are nearing retirement age, and although there are no mandatory age limits for ending professional activity in Switzerland, turnover appears complex due to the smaller number of new medical graduates. The Canton is attempting to address this transition through targeted investments in academic training, particularly through the Faculty of Biomedical Sciences at the Università della Svizzera italiana. The master's degree dedicated to family medicine, which reports full classes, is a positive sign of how the attractiveness of the study path is an essential pillar for ensuring continuity of care.

Key points

For healthcare professionals working in Switzerland or intending to pursue a career in the Canton, understanding market dynamics is essential. The Swiss system, based on a solid salary structure that reflects the high cost of living and the high level of specialization required, necessitates careful planning, especially for those evaluating a life path between Italy and Switzerland. Doctors and nurses wishing to work in Swiss territory must deal with requirements for the recognition of academic qualifications and with cantonal regulations governing the practice of the profession. It is essential to constantly check open positions at healthcare companies and hiring companies for qualified personnel, monitoring professional growth opportunities offered by the hospital network and private practices. Career management in Switzerland also involves proper pension planning, which includes the second pillar LPP/BVG and potential tax optimization, aspects that every professional should delve into to protect their long-term position.

What is the difference in the ratio between doctors and inhabitants between Ticino and Como?
Ticino has stronger health coverage than the province of Como. In Ticino, there is one family doctor for every 900 inhabitants, for a total of about 390 professionals compared to 351 thousand residents. On the contrary, in the province of Como the ratio is one doctor for every 2,000 people, with less than 300 active doctors for a population of about 598 thousand inhabitants.
Why does Franco Denti ask for an ethical reflection on recruitment?
Franco Denti, president of the Order of Ticino Doctors, points out that the massive recruitment of doctors and nurses from Italy raises ethical questions. Although the choice of professionals is understandable for economic reasons, the risk is to take essential human resources away from regions that have invested in their training, structurally weakening their health systems.
