Permit L in Vaud: validity 1 year and renewal up to 24 months (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 19 September 2026

The L permit lasts a maximum of 1 year, renewable for a second year. After 24 months it passes to permit B. Complete guide for the Canton of Vaud.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Permit L: maximum validity of 1 year
  • Renewable for a second year
  • Maximum total: 24 months
  • Switch to permit B after 2 years

Key facts

  • Permit type: L (short duration)
  • Initial duration: up to 1 year
  • Renewal: possible for 1 year
  • Total duration max: 2 years
  • Next step: Permit B (residence)
  • Authority: SEM (Secretariat of State for Migration)

The short-term permit, known as the L permit, is the main instrument for third-country nationals who intend to work in Switzerland for a limited period. The State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) defines the federal guidelines, which are applied by the various Cantons, including the specific procedure in the Canton of Vaud. The distinctive feature of this permit is its temporary nature, designed for defined work projects or for probationary periods before a professional stabilization.

The standard validity of the L permit is set at one year. This term is not fixed but represents the maximum limit for the first issue. The employer must be the applicant for the procedure, ensuring that the workplace is real and compliant with labour market regulations. Once the first year has expired, the holder may request a renewal for a second year, provided that the employment relationship continues and that there are no substantial changes in the contractual conditions or in the personal situation of the worker.

The structure

Operational details

The distinction between the L permit and the B permit is not merely formal but has direct practical implications for the daily life and financial planning of those living in Switzerland. While the L permit is strictly tied to a specific employment relationship, the B permit offers greater autonomy. However, the transition is not automatic: it requires a conversion procedure that must be initiated before the expiration of the second year of the L permit.

Implications for social security and taxation

During the period of the L permit, the worker is subject to the same social security regulations as any other resident. AVS/AI/IPG contributions are mandatory, with a rate of 5.3% paid by the employee and the same amount paid by the employer, for a total of 10.6%. Unemployment insurance (AD/AC) and mandatory daily allowance insurance for sickness and maternity (LAINF/LAA) also apply, with rates varying between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector.

Regarding professional pension schemes, the LPP/BVG comes into play for the portion of the salary that exceeds the coordination threshold. Contribution rates vary based on age: from 7% for those aged 25-34 up to 18% for those aged 55 and over. It is important to note that, despite being a short-term permit, work performed under an L permit generates social security contributions which, in the event of moving abroad, can be refunded or retained based on bilateral agreements.

On the fiscal level, a worker residing in Vaud is subject to the three-tier tax system: federal, cantonal, and communal. The Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV) manages direct federal tax (IFD) and VAT, while cantonal and communal administrations apply their own multipliers. Residence in Switzerland, even temporary, triggers the obligation to file a tax return. The cost of living and budget management must take these withholdings into account, as they significantly impact the monthly net income.

Transition scenarios

If the employer decides not to renew the contract at the end of the first year, the worker finds themselves in a delicate position. Without a new contract to justify the renewal of the L permit, residency is at risk. In such cases, the search for a new job must take place within the timeframes established by law to avoid losing the right of residence. The flexibility of the Swiss labor market, while high, does not automatically guarantee permit continuity if the employment link is broken. Advance planning, including verifying the requirements for a B permit, is recommended to avoid interruptions in social security and health coverage.

Key points

To properly manage the transition from an L permit to a B permit, it is essential to adhere to precise timing. The conversion application must be submitted before the expiration of the second year of the L permit. Delaying this procedure may result in the loss of insurance coverage and the need to restart the process from the beginning.

Step-by-step procedure

1. Document verification: ensure you have an updated employment contract, salary certificates, and proof of residence in Switzerland. 2. Application submission: contact the migration office of the Canton of Vaud with complete documentation. The employer must co-sign the request. 3. Health insurance: verify that your LAMal coverage is active and that the chosen deductible is adequate for your budget. Coverage is mandatory within 3 months of arrival and must remain valid throughout the entire period of residence. 4. Bank account: if you do not already have one, open an account in Switzerland to manage tax and social security withholdings. Current accounts are managed by national banks and must comply with anti-money laundering regulations.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the maximum duration of the L permit in the Canton of Vaud?
The initial validity of the L permit is a maximum of one year. It is possible to request a renewal for a second year, bringing the maximum total duration to 24 months. After this period, the permit cannot be further renewed as an L permit, but it is necessary to proceed with the conversion into a residence permit (B), provided that the employment relationship continues and meets the legal requirements in force.
What happens if the employment contract ends before 2 years?
If the contract ends before the two years, the L permit ceases to be valid. The worker must leave the country or find a new job that justifies a new permit, if conditions permit. Occupational stability is the critical factor that determines the possibility of proceeding towards permit B. Without a new contract justifying the renewal, the residence is at risk.
How do I switch from permit L to permit B in Vaud?
The switch is not automatic. The application for conversion to permit B must be submitted before the expiry of the second year of permit L. Delaying this procedure may result in the loss of insurance coverage and the need to restart the process from the beginning. It is essential to respect a precise timeframe to guarantee the continuity of residence and health and social security coverage.
