The Swiss inheritance tax system

In Switzerland, the taxation of successions and donations is the exclusive responsibility of the cantons. There is no federal tax on these transfers of assets: each canton legislates independently, setting rates, exemptions and procedures. Switzerland has 26 cantons, each with full legislative autonomy in this area.

Operational details

How rates vary between Swiss cantons

Although each canton has its own legislation, it is useful to understand how the Swiss inheritance tax system works as a whole. The general structure is based on some recurring elements:

The degree of kinship of the beneficiary directly determines the applicable rate. The relationship between the deceased and the heir is the first parameter of differentiation: usually, spouse and direct children (first line of descent) receive significantly facilitated tax treatment compared to collaterals and unrelated people.

The amount of the inheritance or donation affects the calculation. Some cantons apply a fixed tax modulated by degree of kinship, others a progressive percentage calculated on the total value transferred. The tax base also varies depending on whether there are deductions, reductions in value or specific exemptions according to cantonal legislation.

The territory of residence is decisive: if the deceased resided in the canton at the time of death, taxes are due to the canton of residence. If you own real estate in multiple cantons, obligations may arise towards multiple tax administrations.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure for fulfilling succession obligations

When a person with assets passes away, family members must fulfill tax and reporting obligations. Here are the general steps that apply at the Swiss level and are specifically applied according to the legislation of each canton:

Phase 1: Notification to authorities Within the timeframes provided by the legislation of the canton where the deceased resided (in the case of Appenzell Innerrhoden, the timeframes are established by cantonal tax law), heirs must report the death to the competent tax office. This requirement is sometimes managed jointly with the notary or the cantonal bankruptcy office, depending on the local procedure.

Phase 2: Documentation gathering It is necessary to gather the death certificate, the will (if present), a detailed inventory of inherited assets, bank documents, securities, real estate, and any evidence of ownership. This documentation is essential for the correct assessment of the taxable base.

Phase 3: Completion of the succession declaration The declaration form is specific to each canton and must be completed according to the instructions of the local tax administration. Appenzell Innerrhoden provides its own standardized forms at its tax office, with submission methods (paper or digital) established by local legislation.

Phase 4: Valuation of inherited assets Movable and immovable assets must be valued at market value or cadastral value according to cantonal regulations. Some assets may benefit from value reductions if subject to liens, usufruct, or other limitations.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who has to pay inheritance taxes in Appenzello Interno?
Heirs and legatees must pay inheritance taxes according to the rates established by Appenzello Interno according to the degree of kinship. The Swiss system does not provide for a federal inheritance tax: taxation is the exclusive competence of the canton. The surviving spouse and children benefit from significantly reduced exemptions or rates, while distant relatives and non-relatives face higher progressive rates.
What are the terms for the declaration of succession?
The declaration of succession is mandatory in Switzerland. The deadlines vary per canton: generally it must be presented to the tax office within 30-90 days of death, according to the provisions of Appenzello Interno. Failure to comply with the deadlines will result in default interest and possible administrative sanctions. After the tax assessment, the taxpayer generally has a few months to settle the debt.
How is inheritance tax calculated?
Inheritance tax is calculated by considering the degree of kinship, the amount of the inheritance and the territory of residence of the deceased. If the deceased resided in Inner Appenzell, taxes are due to the canton. If you own real estate in multiple cantons, obligations may arise towards multiple tax administrations. The tax base varies according to the specific deductions and exemptions of the cantonal legislation.
Does the spouse pay inheritance taxes?
In most Swiss cantons, including the general system of Inner Appenzell, the surviving spouse is not subject to any tax on his or her share of the estate, or benefits from a substantial partial exemption. This is a significant tax protection that reflects a choice of tax policy aimed at reducing the tax burden on transfers within the nuclear family.
Where do I get specific information for Appenzello Interno?
To obtain specific rates, official forms, precise deadlines and clarifications on the tax legislation of Appenzello Interno, consult directly the tax office (Steueramt) of the canton, which has all the official documentation and updated forms. You can also contact a competent notary office in the canton or a tax advisor specialized in inheritance law and cantonal taxes.
