Neutrality initiative: how the Grisons voted

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 29 September 2026

Fifteen municipalities in Graubünden approved the neutrality initiative. Contrary to the national 70,2% no vote, the yes vote reached 67% in Santa Maria.

Fifteen municipalities in Graubünden out of 100 approved the initiative on neutrality, promoted by Pro Svizzera and some UDC figures. The figure is in stark contrast to the national vote, in which no votes reached 70,2%. Most of the favorable municipalities are located in Italian-speaking Graubünden. The Keystone-ATS news agency gathered explanations from some mayors.

Santa Maria and the valley’s vote

In Santa Maria in Val Calanca, the yes vote reached 67%, the clearest result among the municipalities mentioned. Independent mayor Francesco Bogana, who has led the locality of 115 inhabitants for 18 years, ruled out an interpretation tied solely to the parties. In his view, what weighed were the fear of being weak in the face of problems affecting Europe and the world and the awareness, developed in a small valley, of the difficulties Switzerland could encounter if it were no longer neutral.

Bogana said he was shocked by the national 70,2% no vote. His interpretation is that neutrality «is not divisible, it is total», and that a more neutral Switzerland represents a guarantee. He also linked the result to proximity to Italy: Santa Maria is closer to the neighboring country than other Swiss municipalities and, according to the mayor, closely observes problems that could also become its own.

Moesano and Valposchiavo

In Grono, the yes vote exceeded 58%. Samuele Censi, PLR mayor of the municipality of nearly 1’600 inhabitants, attributed the result to fears about international developments, proximity to Italy and the issue of organized crime, which in his view also contributed to the rift between the region and Chur. In Brusio, the yes vote was 53,2%, or 216 people. UDC mayor Davide Migliacci pointed to the strength of his party in the municipality and the border with Italy as keys to understanding the result.

In addition to Santa Maria in Val Calanca, Grono and Brusio, Furna, Rossa, San Vittore, Samnaun, Mesocco, Roveredo, Lostallo, Cama, Soazza, Castaneda, Buseno and Luzein approved the initiative. At the cantonal level, however, the initiative was rejected with 66,6% of the vote.

How to read the gap between voting levels

The most useful data for understanding the outcome is the comparison between three levels. In Switzerland, 70.2% voted no; in Graubünden, the no vote was 66.6%, while 15 municipalities out of 100 said yes. The municipal result does not replace the national one: it shows where the proposal found local support.

The mayors’ perspectives

The statements collected by Keystone-ATS do not offer a single explanation. Bogana referred to the fear of being weak and the idea of total neutrality. Censi mentioned Moesano’s attention to Ticino in federal votes, international concerns and organized crime. Migliacci pointed to the weight of the UDC in Brusio, as well as the direct perception of Italian problems, including crime and security.

The comparison distinguishes measured data from interpretations. Bogana does not attribute the yes vote solely to the parties; Censi links it to international concerns, not to a party; Migliacci, on the other hand, considers the strength of the UDC in the municipality decisive. Geographic location recurs in all three readings, but with different emphases: a small valley, proximity to Italy and a direct border.

Frequently Asked Questions
What was the outcome of the vote on the neutrality initiative in Graubünden?
At the cantonal level, the initiative was rejected with 66.6% of votes against. However, 15 out of 100 Grison municipalities approved the proposal in contrast to the national figure, which saw 70.2% no. Among the favourable municipalities, Santa Maria in Val Calanca stands out with 67%, Grono with over 58% and Brusio with 53.2%.
What motivations prompted the Gray Commons to vote yes?
The reasons vary among the statutory auditors. The mayor of Santa Maria, Francesco Bogana, cites the fear of being weak in the face of international problems and the idea of total neutrality. Samuele Censi, mayor of Grono, pointed out the fears on the international scene, the proximity to Italy and the concerns related to organized crime. In Brusio, Mayor Davide Migliacci underlined the political weight of the local UDC and the proximity to the Italian border.
What other Grison municipalities have approved the initiative?
In addition to Santa Maria in Val Calanca, Grono and Brusio, the neutrality initiative was also approved in the municipalities of Furna, Rossa, San Vittore, Samnaun, Mesocco, Roveredo, Lostallo, Cama, Soazza, Castaneda, Buseno and Luzein. Overall, 15 out of 100 Grison municipalities voted in favour of the proposal promoted by Pro Switzerland and by some representatives of the UDC.
