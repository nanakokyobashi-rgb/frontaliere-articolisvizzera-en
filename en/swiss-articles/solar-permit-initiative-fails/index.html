The collection stops in Bern

More than 117 thousand signatures were not enough. The popular initiative For solar installations exempt from authorization failed because the 18-month deadline set for collecting the 100 thousand required signatures expired without the target being reached. This is reported in the Federal Gazette, with the signature of the Federal Chancellery.

The initial figure seemed to exceed the threshold. The sponsoring committee's website stated that 117'500 people had signed the constitutional amendment text. Verification, however, reduced that number: checks carried out in July and August revealed that almost 20 thousand signatures were invalid.

Contacted by Keystone-ATS, Roland Weiss, one of the initiative's promoters, confirmed the anomaly. «Our lawyer is currently clarifying the circumstances,» he explained. Weiss added that the committee was trying to understand how such a high number of invalid signatures could have been reached, despite the involvement of entities specialized in collecting signatures.

The proposed constitutional amendment

The text aimed to introduce a new constitutional article on solar installations installed on buildings and structures. The proposed rule would have excluded the need for a building permit both inside and outside historic centers and protected landscapes.

The exemption would not have been absolute: the text provided for exceptions for installations placed on cultural monuments of national or cantonal importance and on historic sites, where a building permit could have been issued under certain conditions.

The failure of the collection therefore closes the procedure without the constitutional amendment requested by the committee. The source does not link the outcome to a decision on the installations' technical content: it reports that the threshold was not reached after verification of the signatures and the promoters' request for legal clarification. Operational details

The difference between signatures and text

The practical point for anyone following Swiss politics is to distinguish between the number announced by the committee and the outcome of the verification. The website indicated 117'500 signatures, while the check in July and August excluded almost 20 thousand signatures. The threshold was 100 thousand signatures within 18 months. The failure therefore shows that the gross collection figure did not match the result needed to proceed with the initiative.

The second element is the scope of the exemption. The text called for a new constitutional provision for solar installations on buildings and structures, without a building permit inside and outside historic centres and protected landscapes. The issue therefore concerned not only the collection of signatures: it also defined where the exemption would apply and which exceptions would remain.

How to follow the dossier without confusing it with a rule

The news makes it possible to follow the dossier through a simple sequence, without attributing to the initiative an effect it did not produce. The starting reference is the Federal Gazette, where the Federal Chancellery recorded that the target had not been met. From there, one can distinguish the formal outcome from the content of the proposal.

Four useful checks

1. Check the deadline. The period was 18 months and the required threshold was 100 thousand signatures. The deadline expired without the target being met.

2. Separate the figures. The committee's website indicated 117'500 signatures; the July and August verification found nearly 20 thousand invalid signatures. The reported number and the verified result should not be treated as equivalent.

3. Read the scope. The proposal concerned installations on buildings and structures, inside or outside historic centers and protected landscapes. 4. Follow the clarifications. This is the only development following the verification reported by the source.
