The wage gap between women and men in Switzerland continues to narrow: in 2024 it stood at 15.7%, down from 16.2% in 2022. This is what emerges from the latest report published by the Federal Statistical Office (FSO), taken from the Keystone-ATS news agency. The photograph taken at the end of 2024 confirms a dynamic of progressive compression of the pay differences between the sexes, which nevertheless remain significant in both the private and public sectors.

Operational details

Unexplained Component: What Remains 'Without Justification'

The FSO report examines the portion of the pay gap that cannot be traced back to observable structural factors. In 2024, this component stood at 6.5% of salary, equivalent to 640 gross francs per month. Two years earlier it was 7.0%, amounting to 657 francs. The reduction cuts across both major sectors: in the private sector the unexplained part was 6.7%, i.e. 644 francs per month, compared with 7.2% and 653 francs in 2022; in the public sector it was 5.9%, or 574 francs per month on average, versus 6.7% and 623 francs in 2022. This is the share that, according to the FSO definition, cannot be statistically attributed to differences in education, seniority, or hierarchical position.

Where the Gap Is Widest

The differences are particularly evident in specific sectors. The unexplained component amounted to 191 francs in accommodation and food services, 606 francs in retail, 829 francs in the manufacture of machinery and equipment, and 1,506 francs in financial and insurance activities. The latter figure is roughly 2.4 times higher than that of the manufacturing sector and almost eight times higher than that of accommodation and food services. For workers in the financial and insurance sector, the residual gap is therefore the largest in absolute value.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the wage gap between women and men in Switzerland in 2024?
According to the Federal Statistical Office (FSO), the overall gross wage gap in 2024 was 15.7%, down from 16.2% in 2022. In the private sector the figure is 17.2% (from 17.5% in 2022), while in the public sector — Confederation, Cantons and Municipalities — it is 12.8% (from 13.8% in 2022).
What is the 'unexplained' component of the wage gap?
It is the part of the gap that the UST fails to statistically trace back to structural factors such as training, years of service or hierarchical position. In 2024 it was 6.5%, equivalent to CHF 640 gross per month, compared to 7.0% (CHF 657) in 2022. In the private sector it was 6.7% (CHF 644), in the public sector it was 5.9% (CHF 574).
In which sectors is the wage gap wider?
Financial and insurance assets remain the most penalizing sector, with a gross gap of 30.6% and an unexplained component of CHF 1,506 gross per month. Machinery manufacturing (17.2%, CHF 829), retail (16.9%, CHF 606) and accommodation and catering (7.9%, CHF 191) follow.
How do differences with hierarchy, age and company size change?
The UST points out that the unexplained component is more marked in small businesses, tends to increase with the hierarchical level and accentuates with age. Among married staff, the unexplained share was 8.1% in 2024 (compared to 2.7% among the unmarried), down from 8.8% in 2022.
Is the gap really narrowing?
Yes, on all fronts: between 2022 and 2024 the overall figure fell by 0.5 percentage points, by 0.3 points in the private sector and by 1.0 point in the public sector. Compared to 2018, the reduction is even more marked: from 19.6% to 17.2% in the private sector and from 18.1% to 12.8% in the public sector.
