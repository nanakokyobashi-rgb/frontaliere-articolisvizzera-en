What you need to open a business in the Canton of Schwyz: legal form, trade register and costs.

Context

The Canton of Schwyz requires that business activities be entered in the commercial register. The legal form chosen for the opening of a business in the Canton of Schwyz is a fundamental aspect. Registration in the commercial register is a requirement for the opening of a business in the Canton of Schwyz. The costs for registering and registering an activity in the Canton of Schwyz are determined by cantonal law.

According to Article 1 of the Implementing Regulations of the Commercial Code of the Canton of Schwyz (in force since 1 January 2020), registration in the commercial register is mandatory for all commercial activities operating in the Canton of Schwyz. This includes partnerships (SAP), joint-stock companies (SA), limited liability companies (SRL) and other business legal forms.

The choice of legal form depends on the specific needs of the business and the characteristics of the shareholders. For example, a partnership (SAP) is best suited for small businesses with few partners, while a joint-stock company (SA) is best suited for larger businesses with many partners.

Key points

To open a business in the Canton of Schwyz, you must submit an application for registration in the trade register. The application must contain the information required by cantonal law, including the name and address of the business, the legal form chosen and the description of the business. Once the application has been submitted, it will be examined and registered in the trade register. The costs for registering and registering an activity in the Canton of Schwyz are determined by cantonal law.

Legal forms provided for by law

The Canton of Schwyz provides for different legal forms for commercial activities, including:

  • Limited liability company (SRL)
  • Limited Liability Company (SRI)
  • Joint stock company (SA)
  • Limited Liability Company in Collective Name (SLNC)
  • Cooperative society

The choice of legal form depends on the needs of the business and the characteristics of the shareholders. For example, a limited liability company is suitable for small and medium-sized enterprises, while a joint-stock company is more suitable for larger and more complex enterprises.
