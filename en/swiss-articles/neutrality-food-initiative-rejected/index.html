September 27 vote: a resounding no to neutrality and food

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 28 September 2026

On 27 September, over 70% rejected the neutrality initiative and 72.5% rejected the food initiative in all cantons.

On Sunday, September 27, the double ballot ended with two clear rejections. The neutrality initiative was rejected by the people and the cantons: no canton voted yes, and over 70% of voters chose no. In the data associated with the vote, 3% were still undecided about the neutrality text.

The food initiative was also rejected in all cantons. The no vote reached 72.5%; the associated documentation also indicates 2% undecided and, in some cantons, rejection exceeding 80%. The national result is therefore clear on both issues.

The Federal Council's interpretation

Ignazio Cassis, federal councillor and head of the Federal Department of Foreign Affairs (FDFA), ruled out the possibility that the no vote on the neutrality proposal represented an abandonment of the principle.

«This result is not a vote against neutrality. Switzerland was neutral yesterday, is neutral today and will remain so in the future».

For the Federal Council, the practice of the past 180 years has proven valid because it has not treated neutrality rigidly. Cassis described periods that were more restrictive and others that were more open to cooperation, always with the same objective: ensuring the country's security and preventing Switzerland from becoming involved in a war.

The federal councillor pointed to 2022, after Russia's attack on Ukraine, as the case that makes this approach concrete. According to his explanation, the Federal Council reacted because the attack by a larger country against a smaller one constituted a decisive violation of international law. The political rule invoked is therefore to assess the situation as it is.

Following Cassis, Guy Parmelin commented on the no vote on the food initiative. The President of the Confederation acknowledged the importance of agricultural issues and self-sufficiency, arguing that Switzerland must be able to guarantee it for future generations as well. He added that the discussion on agriculture and food will continue and that the Federal Council intends to seek balanced solutions. The stated objective is safe, sustainable and high-quality food, together with solid prospects for Swiss agriculture.

Operational details

What it means for Switzerland

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the double no should be read as a choice about policy direction, not as the announcement of a new measure, amount or deadline to apply. The source does not indicate immediate effects for residents. Instead, it offers two lines of interpretation: neutrality remains a stated principle, while its application must be able to change according to the situation; food remains an open issue, even after the initiative was rejected.

Neutrality: room for assessment remains central

The practical difference compared with a rigid rule lies in the decision-making method. The Federal Council maintains the possibility of examining each crisis according to its characteristics, with the country's security as its objective. The example of 2022, cited by Cassis, helps clarify the scenario: when a larger country attacks a smaller one, the Federal Council considered the episode a decisive violation of international law. The account does not say what the next decisions will be, but clarifies the stated criterion for addressing them.

For a resident, this means not turning the result into an absolute formula. The no is not presented as a vote against neutrality, nor as giving up on security. The position expressed by the DFAE is that of a neutrality capable of adapting, with more restrictive periods and others more open to cooperation. It is a national political indication, not a new instruction to apply to a private matter.

Food: the vote does not close the debate

Parmelin acknowledged that the initiative touched on agriculture and self-sufficiency. His statement therefore shifts attention from the rejected text to future discussions: the Federal Council wants to contribute responsibly and seek balanced solutions. How to follow the dossier without confusing the issues

The report does not indicate any forms to fill out or requests to submit after the vote. The useful action for a resident is to create a simple monitoring sheet and update it when the Federal Council returns to the two topics.

Four practical steps

1. Mark the temporal reference. Keep the date Sunday 27 September and associate with it the two distinct items: the neutrality initiative and the food initiative. This way, you avoid reading the 72.5% for one as if it concerned the other.

2. Separate outcome and policy direction. For neutrality, record that the vote was rejected, but that Cassis described neutrality as still valid and adaptable. For food, record the no vote in all Cantons, then note Parmelin's declared commitment to continue the discussion on agriculture and self-sufficiency.

3. Use percentages with their label. The 70% concerns the no vote on the neutrality initiative; the 72.5% the no vote on the food initiative. The 3% and 2% are shares of undecided voters in the data linked to the two dossiers, while the 80% indicates the level the no vote exceeded in some Cantons regarding food. The figure without its subject generates incorrect comparisons.

4. Wait for subsequent acts before attributing consequences. The source speaks of security, international law, agriculture and self-sufficiency. It does not indicate a change to salaries, taxes, prices or obligations for residents; therefore, any concrete effect must be linked to a subsequent decision by the Federal Council, not inferred from the result alone.
