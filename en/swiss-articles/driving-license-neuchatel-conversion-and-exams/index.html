Driver's license in the canton of Neuchâtel: conversion and exams (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 September 2026

Learn how to convert your foreign license and take exams in the Canton of Neuchâtel.

Context

In brief

  • The driving license in Switzerland is issued by the Cantons under the supervision of the Confederation.
  • The Canton of Neuchâtel manages licenses through the Road Service (SCRN).
  • Holders of a foreign license must convert it within the deadlines set by federal law.
  • The theoretical and practical exams are mandatory to obtain the Swiss license.

Key facts

  • What: Conversion of foreign license, theoretical and practical exams, mandatory courses
  • When: Deadlines defined by federal road traffic legislation
  • Where: Canton of Neuchâtel, competent office for traffic
  • Who: Residents and new residents with a foreign license
  • Amount: Variable cantonal fees, not yet specified in the source

In Switzerland, road traffic is regulated at the federal level by the Road Traffic Act (LCStr), but the issuance of licenses is the responsibility of the cantons. Each Canton, including Neuchâtel, defines its own administrative procedures, the amounts of fees, and the organization of exams, while respecting federal prescriptions. For residents who hold a license issued abroad, the law provides specific deadlines for the conversion of the license.

Cantonal competence

The Road Service of the Canton of Neuchâtel (Service cantonal des routes et de la navigation, SCRN) is the competent office to receive applications for the conversion of foreign licenses, organize theoretical and practical exams, and issue the Swiss license. The procedure involves the presentation of identity documents, a valid residence permit, and the original license with a sworn translation.

Operational details

Analysis of practical implications

Obtaining a Swiss licence in the Canton of Neuchâtel means being part of an articulated federal system, where the Confederation dictates the framework rules and the Canton manages the execution. For a newly transferred resident, this translates into concrete steps to be taken within precise deadlines, with costs that vary depending on the category of license required and the outcome of the exams.

Differences between EU/EFTA licences and non-European licences

Licences issued by European Union or EFTA countries enjoy a facilitated recognition regime. In many cases, the holder can avoid the practical examination and obtain the conversion by presenting the original driving licence, a recent medical certificate and proof of residence in Switzerland. The theoretical examination is generally mandatory. For licences from third States, the procedure is stricter: both the theoretical and practical exams are required, and in some cases it is necessary to follow training courses at authorised driving schools.

Compulsory courses and training

Swiss legislation provides for compulsory courses for certain categories of vehicles or for newly patented vehicles. In the canton of Neuchâtel, as in the rest of Switzerland, first aid courses are a prerequisite for issuing a licence. Licensed driving schools offer both theoretical and practical training, with fees depending on the number of hours and license category.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure for obtaining a driver's license in the Canton of Neuchâtel

Obtaining a Swiss driver's license in Neuchâtel requires following a sequence of administrative steps. Below is the standard procedure based on available information.

Phase 1: Gathering Documents

The first step is to gather the necessary documentation: a valid original driver's license, a sworn translation if the license is not in an official Swiss language, a valid ID document, a residence or stay permit, a recent medical certificate, and a passport-sized photograph. For non-EU/EFTA citizens, an apostille or legalization of the document may be required.

Phase 2: Submitting the Application

The application must be submitted to the Service cantonal des routes et de la navigation (SCRN) of the Canton of Neuchâtel. The office verifies the documentation, assesses whether the foreign license can be directly converted or if exams are required, and communicates the next steps to the applicant.

Phase 3: Exams

If required, the theoretical exam covers Swiss road legislation, signage, and specific regulations. The practical exam takes place on the road with an authorized examiner and assesses the candidate's driving skills. The source does not specify the exact fees for the exams but indicates that they are the responsibility of the applicant.

Phase 4: Issuance of the License

Once the exams are passed and the documentation is verified, the cantonal office issues the Swiss driver's license. The document has the duration specified by federal law and must be renewed according to the established deadlines.

Frequently Asked Questions
What documents are required for the conversion of the foreign license?
To convert the foreign driver's license in the Canton of Neuchâtel, you need identity documents, a valid residence permit, the original driver's license with sworn translation, a recent medical certificate and a passport-sized photograph.
What exams do I need to take to get a Swiss driver's license?
Residents with a foreign license must take a theoretical exam and, in some cases, also a practical exam. EU/EFTA licences may avoid practical examination, while non-European licences require both examinations.
What are the costs for converting the license?
Costs vary depending on the category of license and the outcome of the exams. They include the cantonal issuance fee, exam fees and driving school fees.
