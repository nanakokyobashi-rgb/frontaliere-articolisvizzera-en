Switzerland entrusts the cantons with the management of childcare services, allowing parents to reconcile employment and family. In the Inner Appenzell canton, as in the other Swiss cantons, the facilities operate according to national quality standards but with locally governed organization and pricing. The system responds to a principle of equity: high incomes contribute more, while families with limited resources have access to substantial cantonal reductions and subsidies.

Operational details

How income pricing works

The tariff system is the pillar of the Swiss custody system. In the Inner Appenzell canton, tariffs are proportionate to the net household income declared in the previous fiscal year, ensuring fair access to families with fewer resources. Income is divided into progressive tiers: a household with income below CHF 30,000 per year could pay 5-15% of the maximum daily rate, while one with income above CHF 80,000 could pay 100% of the base rate. Gross costs vary by canton and structure: public kindergartens are typically more accessible than the private sector; cooperatives and nonprofits occupy an intermediate position.

Daily costs may vary for part-time services (half-day, a few days a week) versus full-time (full-day, five days). After-school are often charged per time slot or monthly flat rate. Many facilities have reduced rates for siblings or long-term memberships. It is essential to contact the cantonal office or facility directly to find out the current figures, as they are revalued annually according to the price index.

Key points

How to access services: step by step procedure

Access to custodial services in the Inner Appenzell canton follows an orderly procedure that is easily managed by most families.

Step 1: Identify available facilities

Contact the relevant cantonal office (usually the Office of Social Services, the Children's Office or the Family Division) or directly the municipality of residence. Most cantons maintain detailed online lists of public and affiliated kindergartens, day families and afterschools, with information on capacity, hours, accepted age group, indicative rates and contact details. Assess proximity to home and work to facilitate daily transfers.

Step 2: Check eligibility and prepare documents

Most public facilities require residence in the canton; private facilities can also accommodate non-residents. Prepare in advance: certificate of residence (which can be requested from the municipality free of charge), extract from the previous year's tax return, identity document of both parents, extract from the family booklet if married or in a registered partnership. If the child has allergies, food intolerances or special educational needs, promptly communicate to the facility during the interview.

Frequently Asked Questions
At what age can I enroll my child in kindergarten?
Most kindergartens accept children as young as 3 months old. Some offer places for infants as young as 2 months old if both parents are busy. After-school education starts around the age of 5-6 (beginning of primary school) and continues until the age of 12-13. Contact the specific facility to check the minimum and maximum age accepted and the availability of seats.
How is my exact monthly rate calculated?
The rate is based on the previous fiscal year's household net income, broken down into progressive tiers. For example, income below CHF 30,000 could correspond to 5-15% of the maximum rate, while income over CHF 80,000 pays 100%. The cantonal subsidy further reduces the net amount. Ask the cantonal office for the specific spreadsheet and a custom simulation with your income.
What documents do I need to submit to access the cantonal subsidy?
The extract of the previous year's tax return, certificate of residence in the canton, identity document of both parents and, sometimes, a statement certifying the absence of alternative care (e.g. available grandparents, parental leave). The cantonal office specifies the complete list in the documentation of the grant application.
If my income changes during the year, does the benefit adjust?
The subsidy is recalculated annually based on the declared income. Significant changes during the year (loss or new job) may warrant an early review. Promptly notify the cantonal office of changes to avoid surprises when renewing or over-reimbursement requests.
Can I combine daycare and kindergarten to increase flexibility?
Yes, many families combine facilities to cover different hourly needs: e.g. kindergarten from 6:30 to 11:00 and daytime family from 11:00 to 18:00. Check with the cantonal office if the subsidy applies to both facilities or if there are cumulation limits to avoid unexpected reductions.
