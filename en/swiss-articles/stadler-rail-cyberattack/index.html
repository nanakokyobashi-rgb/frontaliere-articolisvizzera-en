Context

The trial against the alleged hacker opened today before the Zurich District Court. The man, accused of hacking companies, including Stadler Rail, denies the allegations.

According to investigations, the alleged hacker allegedly compromised the security of several computer systems of Swiss companies, including Stadler Rail, one of the country's leading train construction companies. The basic engineering studio in Bussnang in the Canton of St. Gallen, a manufacturer of trains and locomotives, suffered a cyber attack in 2022, which compromised the security of sensitive data.

The case was brought to light thanks to investigations conducted by the Swiss Federal Police, which found that the alleged hacker had used a number of attack methods, including phishing and ransomware, to gain access to companies' computer systems.

The trial is taking place in a tense climate, as investigations have revealed that the alleged hacker allegedly compromised the security of more than 100 Swiss companies, including banks and insurance companies. According to estimates, the economic damage caused by the cyberattack could amount to more than 10 million Swiss francs.

The alleged hacker, accused of hacking companies, denies the allegations and claims to be innocent. His lawyer stated that his clients were not involved in any kind of criminal activity and that the allegations are “baseless.”

The trial against the alleged Ukrainian hacker opens in Zurich, Switzerland, after an investigation that lasted several years. The investigation was conducted by the Swiss Federal Police (NFI) and also involved the Federal Prosecutor's Office. The federal prosecutor accused the man of orchestrating a cyberattack against the railway technology company Stadler Rail, based in Bussnang, in the canton of St. Gallen.

The cyberattack has been described as “one of the most serious cyberattacks in the history of Switzerland”. According to the Federal Prosecutor's Office, the man allegedly used malware to access Stadler Rail's computer systems and steal sensitive data. The investigation also revealed that the man allegedly used a Swiss hosting service, based in Lugano, in the canton of Ticino, to hide his tracks.

The investigation was conducted in collaboration with cybersecurity authorities of several nations, including the United States, Germany, and Russia. The federal prosecutor accused the man of violating the Electronic Communications Act (LCE), which prohibits the spread of malware and the collection of sensitive data without authorization.
