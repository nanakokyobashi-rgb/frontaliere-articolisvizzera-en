Cantonal taxes in the canton of Vaud: rates and deductions

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 4 September 2026

3-tier tax system, progressive rates for income brackets, allowable deductions and official portal of the canton of Vaud for declarations.

Context

In short

  • 3-level system: federal + cantonal + municipal multiplier
  • Progressive rates according to income and wealth brackets
  • Authorized deductions (3a, professional expenses, mortgage interest)
  • Declaration by 31 May of the year following the tax period

Key facts

  • What: Cantonal and municipal taxes on income and wealth in the canton of Vaud
  • Where: Canton of Vaud, Romandy, Switzerland
  • System: 3 tax levels (federal, cantonal, municipal)
  • Structure: Progressive brackets defined by the canton + variable municipal multiplier
  • Portal: Cantonal tax administration publishes rates, multipliers, and forms

The canton of Vaud applies a system of cantonal and municipal taxes structured according to Swiss federal legislation: three levels of taxation (federal, cantonal, municipal). Unlike countries with a centralized tax system, Switzerland grants each canton the autonomy to define rates, brackets, and authorized deductions. The municipalities of Vaud subsequently add a percentage multiplier to the cantonal taxes, which results in variations even between neighboring municipalities.

Vaud cantonal taxes apply to personal income, wealth, and, for business owners, business profits. Gross income is reduced by authorized deductions (pension contributions, professional expenses, mortgage interest, donations); the resulting net income is taxed at progressive rates defined by the canton. Two residents with the same income in two Vaud municipalities may pay slightly different amounts, as the municipal multiplier varies from one municipality to another.

The progressive bracket system

The rates of the canton of Vaud increase by income brackets: lower incomes pay reduced percentages, higher incomes pay increasing rates. A resident can consult the updated brackets annually on the portal of the cantonal tax administration. In addition to income, there is a separate wealth tax (real estate, accounts, investments) also divided into value brackets. The municipal multiplier — a percentage coefficient set by each municipality — applies to both income tax and wealth tax. Two Vaud municipalities can have different multipliers (for example, one at 120%, the other at 135%), so the taxpayer's residence significantly affects the final tax. The cantonal administration publishes an official table annually with brackets, rates, and municipal multipliers. The income tax return must be filed by the end of May of the year following the tax period, unless there are official extensions.

Operational details

The canton of Vaud recognises an extensive catalogue of authorised deductions, in line with Swiss federal law and with specific cantonal additions. The main deductions common to all Swiss cantons, including Vaud, include payments to the third pension pillar (3a), up to the annual ceiling set federally and indexed each year.

Those who practice a self-employed or freelance profession can deduct documented professional expenses and costs of continuous training. Property owners deduct interest expense on mortgages related to the primary residence. Compulsory contributions to unemployment (AD) and accident (LAINF/AC) insurance, already withheld in the paycheck, are recognised as deductions. Donations to recognised non-profit organisations are deductible up to a cantonal ceiling.

Check deductions in Vaud

As the federal and cantonal regulatory framework is frequently updated, it is crucial that every resident consults the official canton Vaud portal before filing taxes. The site of the cantonal tax administration provides an exhaustive list of authorized deductions, the maximum amounts applicable, the conditions of application and the documents to be attached. Many deductions require supporting documentation (social security contribution certificates, mortgage statements, donation receipts); without this documentation the deduction is not accepted.

Key points

Tax declaration in the canton of Vaud follows a standardized procedure, made easier by the online services of the cantonal tax administration. The general deadline for submitting the declaration is May 31 of the year following the tax period (May 31, 2025 for the 2024 income, and so on). Late declaration incurs an administrative fine; it is therefore important to respect the deadline, unless official extensions are granted by the canton. Step-by-step: how to declare in Vaud Before declaring, the resident must gather income certificates (pay slips, self-employment certificates), bank account statements, receipts for deductible expenses (donations, mortgage interest, pension contributions), and asset documents (deposit statements, property value declarations). The cantonal administration provides paper forms and fillable online versions. The form requires personal data, income by source, itemized deductions, declared assets, and estimated tax calculation based on cantonal brackets and the municipal multiplier. The declaration can be submitted online via the cantonal portal, by mail, or in person at the cantonal tax office; online submission reduces processing times. The cantonal administration reviews the declaration and, if correct, issues an official assessment with the amount due. In case of discrepancies or lack of documentation, the taxpayer receives a request for clarification. The official website of the Vaud cantonal tax administration (www.vd.ch — dedicated tax section) hosts all necessary information: updated brackets, rates, and municipal multipliers, list of authorized deductions, declaration forms, payment deadline guide, FAQ, and local office contacts. It is advisable to consult the official portal before each declaration to verify any regulatory or administrative changes. Many residents also use private tax consulting services or seek help from unions and professional associations, which often offer free assistance in filling out the forms. For a preliminary estimate of your tax burden in the canton of Vaud, you can use the online tax calculator, which applies the municipal multipliers and updated rates for a quick assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between federal, cantonal and municipal tax in Vaud?
Switzerland applies three-tier taxation. The federal tax is the same for the whole of Switzerland and is calculated by the federal authorities. The cantonal tax is determined by the canton of Vaud with its own rates and brackets for income and assets. The municipal tax is an additional one: each municipality of Vaud applies a percentage multiplier on cantonal taxes. The result is that two residents with the same income in two different municipalities of Vaud can pay slightly different amounts due to the different municipal multiplier.
When does the tax return in the canton of Vaud expire?
The general deadline for filing in the canton of Vaud is 31 May of the year following the tax period (e.g. 31 May 2025 for 2024 income). The cantonal administration may grant official extensions in case of exceptional circumstances. Declaring after the deadline will result in an administrative fine. It is advisable to consult the official portal of the canton of Vaud (www.vd.ch) before the deadline to check for specific extensions or variations.
What are the main allowable deductions in the canton of Vaud?
The deductions allowed in the Vaud include: payments to the third pillar (3a) up to the federal ceiling, professional expenses for self-employed, interest payable on mortgages for main residence, mandatory contributions to unemployment and accident insurance, and donations to recognized benefit institutions. Each deduction is capped and requires supporting documentation. It is mandatory to consult the updated list on the official portal of the canton of Vaud before filing the return, as deductions may change annually.
Where do I find the updated rates and multiplier for my municipality?
Cantonal rates, income brackets and municipal multipliers are published annually on the portal of the cantonal tax administration Vaud (www.vd.ch — tax section). There you can download official forms, check deadlines and access preliminary calculation tools. If your municipality is not immediately visible online, you can contact the municipal counter or cantonal office directly to request the specific multiplier.
Can I file taxes online in the canton of Vaud?
Yes, the canton of Vaud offers an online reporting service through the official portal of the tax administration. Online submission reduces processing time compared to paper or face-to-face. You can also submit your return by post or directly to a cantonal counter. Before filing, please check the specifics on the official portal, as online features may vary by tax year.
