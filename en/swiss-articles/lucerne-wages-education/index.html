In the canton of Lucerne, as in the whole of Switzerland, the salary depends mainly on three factors: the degree of training, the economic sector and professional experience. These elements determine not only the initial salary level, but also the salary progression over the course of the career.

Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage, but over 100 collective bargaining agreements (CCCs) set binding wage minimums in different sectors. In the canton of Lucerne, the labour market follows precise rules on pay, working hours, holidays and employment protection.

Operational details

Salaries by level of training

The UST/BFS data show that the median annual salary varies significantly by degree of training. Those who have only completed middle school have access to positions with lower salaries than those who have an apprenticeship. With a professional maturity or a high school maturity, the salary grows again.

Those with a university degree (bachelor's, master's, doctorate) receive significantly higher salaries, often 30-50% higher than middle school graduates. However, this difference varies by sector: in some ICT or finance sectors, the differential can be even more pronounced.

Effect of experience and the economic sector

In addition to the level of training, the salary grows with experience in the role. An apprentice completes the training with an initial salary, but after 5-10 years in the industry, the salary can increase by 20-40% with the same qualification. This progression is often codified in collective agreements, which set salary scales based on seniority.

The sector of employment has an even greater impact than the training itself. A graduate working in ICT can earn significantly more than a colleague with the same degree in the hotel industry. Similarly, a technician specializing in manufacturing has higher salaries than a technician in retail.

Key points

## How to evaluate a job offer in Lucerne When you receive a job offer in the canton of Lucerne, the proposed salary must meet at least the following conditions: If a CCC applies : The salary cannot be less than the minimum established in the collective agreement of the sector. If your role is covered by a CCC, the proposed figure must be checked against the salary bracket corresponding to your level of training and seniority. If a CCC is not applicable: Remuneration must be consistent with market conditions for your level of education and industry. In this case, there is no federal legal minimum, but market standards provide a reference. Working hours: Check that the weekly hours do not exceed 45 hours (industry, offices, trade) or 50 hours (other sectors). If the company proposes overtime, it must be specified how it will be paid (percentage increase or compensation in free time). Holidays: Make sure the offer guarantees at least 4 weeks of annual leave (5 if you are under 20). Pension: If the coordinated salary exceeds CHF 21'510 per year, the employer must offer a LPP. Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a legal minimum wage in the canton of Lucerne?
Lucerne, like many Swiss cantons, does not have a legally binding cantonal minimum wage. However, collective bargaining agreements (CCCs) applicable to certain sectors (industry, health, construction) set minimum wages that the employer must comply with. If a CCC is not applicable, the salary is defined by negotiation between employer and employee, following market standards. Switzerland doesn't even have a federal minimum wage.
How is the net salary calculated from the paycheck?
The net salary is calculated from the gross by subtracting: 5.3% AVS/AI/IPG, 1.1% AD/AC (up to the annual ceiling), LPP contribution if applicable (7-18% of the coordinated salary), direct federal tax and cantonal/municipal taxes. Each canton has different rates, so the net varies by place of residence and specific municipality. For an accurate calculation, use a salary calculator that considers your personal tax situation.
What is the maximum weekly working time in Switzerland?
In industry, offices and commerce, the maximum is 45 hours per week. In other industries, it's 50 hours. Hours beyond the limit must be compensated with a salary increase or compensation in free time. This rule applies in all cantons, including Lucerne, and is established by the Federal Labour Law.
What is a collective bargaining agreement (CCC) and how does it affect me?
A CCC is an agreement between trade unions and employers' associations that establishes minimum working conditions (salary, hours, holidays, social security) for a sector or category of workers. There are over 100 CCCs in Switzerland. If your employment is covered by a CCC, the employer must comply with the established minimums. You can check whether your industry is covered by CCCs at SECO or at industry unions.
How many weeks of holiday am I legally entitled to in Switzerland?
In Switzerland, the legal minimum is 4 weeks of vacation per year (5 if you are under 20). If you work under a CCC, the contract may provide for longer vacation. Also, if you are under the age of 20, you are entitled to an additional day of vacation. This rule applies in all cantons, including Lucerne.
