Phytosanitary approval: backlog cleared by 2032 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 25 September 2026

Federal Council sets 2032 to process 525 pending applications; fees increasing from 2028 to cover 50% of costs.

  • The plan provides for around 18 million francs by 2032

Federal decision

In Bern, on 25 September 2026, the Federal Council decided that all pending authorisation applications for plant protection products should be processed within six years. The deadline indicated in the communication is 2032. The measure concerns a federal procedure that precedes market placement: every product must be authorised by the Confederation, which verifies its efficacy and the risks to humans and the environment.

The dossier directly concerns farmers, who need effective and safe products to protect crops from pests or fungal infestations. The assessments are scientific but complex. With limited staffing resources, the number of pending applications increased for several years, and waiting times particularly delayed the market placement of modern plant protection products.

The authorities involved

At the Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office, the Federal Office for the Environment FOEN, the Federal Office for Agriculture FOAG and the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs SECO, several hundred applications are pending. The Federal Administration has reduced the backlog from more than 700 to around 525 applications, but the Federal Council considers that the pace is still insufficient.

The decision provides for more staff resources, further digitisation of the procedure, better information for applicants on the status of their cases and fewer interfaces between offices. The aim is to make the process more efficient and customer-oriented, while also closing the gaps created when products are no longer available on the market for certain harmful organisms.

The plan provides for around 18 million francs by 2032 for the FSVO and FOAG: 16 million for the FSVO and 2 million for the FOAG. Funding is guaranteed through the ordinary budgeting process. For 2027, the two offices will submit a request for a supplementary credit of 2,8 million to Parliament. From 2028, the necessary resources will have to be offset by an increase in authorisation fees. The FDHA will have to submit an amendment to the ordinance on fees to the Federal Council by the end of 2027.

Operational details

Practical analysis: what changes

The practical value of the measure can be seen on two levels. For farmers, the problem described is not only the wait: emergency approvals are currently being granted for older products, while modern substitutes are still in the approval process. For some harmful organisms, no products are available anymore. Clearing the backlog is therefore linked to availability gaps, without guaranteeing the approval of a specific product.

For anyone submitting an application, the clearest difference concerns funding. Current coverage is only approximately 15 per cent of costs; the Federal Council wants to bring it closer to approximately 50 per cent of the level for EU authorizations. The increase in fees is announced from 2028. The source does not indicate a fee per individual application: it is therefore not possible to calculate the amount payable by an applicant.

The financial schedule must be separated from the individual case. The approximately 18 million francs by 2032 concern investments at USAV and UFAG, while the 2,8 million for 2027 are a supplementary credit request that the two offices will submit to Parliament. Key points

How to follow the process

The source makes it possible to construct an operational sequence based on the deadlines indicated, but it does not describe forms, documents or a portal for applications. Therefore, the reader can follow the decision calendar without adding unannounced administrative steps.

Five steps for applicants and operators

1. If an approval application is still pending, consider it part of the backlog that the Federal Council wants to clear by 2032. The communication does not indicate a ranking or an individual deadline.

2. For 2027, distinguish the request for supplementary credit from the approval procedure: USAV and UFAG will submit a total request of 2.8 million francs to Parliament.

3. By the end of 2027, follow the step planned for the DFI, which will have to submit the amendment to the ordinance on fees to the Federal Council. The proposal concerns the degree of cost coverage, currently around 15 per cent.

4. From 2028, take into account the increase in approval fees announced to offset the resources needed. The source does not specify the amount of the individual fee, so it does not allow for a more precise estimate.

5. Use 2032 as the horizon for the overall measure. As part of the process, the Federal Administration will have to strengthen staffing, further digitize the procedure, better inform applicants about the status of their applications and reduce interfaces between offices.

For farmers, the operational interpretation remains linked to product availability: the communication points out that for some harmful organisms there are no longer any products on the market and that emergency authorizations concern older products. It does not indicate which product will be approved, nor when a specific application will receive a decision. This distinction avoids presenting 2032 as a guarantee regarding every individual outcome.

For applicants, the most concrete information announced is the future improvement of progress status and the reduction of steps between offices. The text instead lacks indications on forms, digital channels, required documents or the office responsible for an individual application. The usable reference is therefore the federal timetable: 2027 for the credit and the ordinance on fees, 2028 for financing through fees, 2032 for the clearance target.

Frequently Asked Questions
By when will all pending approval applications be processed?
The Federal Council has set 2032 as the deadline to process all the approximately 525 applications for approval for plant protection products still pending, reduced by over 700 thanks to recent measures.
How will the arrears disposal plan be financed?
18 million expected by 2032 (16 USAV, 2 FOAG). For 2027, 2.8 million was requested as a supplementary credit. From 2028, approval fees will increase to cover costs from 15% to 50%.
What organisational measures are in place to speed up procedures?
More staff, digitalization, better information on the practical status and reduction of interfaces between USAV, UFAM, UFAG and SECO to make the process more efficient.
What changes for farmers waiting for new products?
Today, emergency authorizations are used for old products; disposing of the backlog serves to fill availability gaps for harmful organisms without products on the market, but does not guarantee the approval of a specific product.
