Zurich sees the publication of a new study by AXA that analyses the trend of staff absences within Swiss small and medium-sized enterprises. Staff absences are increasingly costing small and medium-sized enterprises money, a phenomenon that has affected four out of ten companies in the last ten years. The situation varies according to the size of the company, reaching particularly high shares among medium-sized companies. Among companies with 50-250 employees, the share reaches 62%, highlighting how human resource management and business continuity represent a complex challenge for the Swiss economic fabric. Operational details

A deeper analysis of the results of the research conducted by AXA reveals direct correlations between the principles adopted in personnel management and the spread of absences for psychological reasons within companies. Company culture and the degree of autonomy granted to employees seem to play a decisive role in the prevention of emotional and psychological discomfort in the workplace. The differences detected between the various organisational models offer a concrete perspective on the internal dynamics of Swiss business realities, allowing us to understand which management approaches are most effective in safeguarding the psychological well-being of workers.

The impact of management principles and accountability

The statistical data published in the study show significant differences depending on the management philosophy adopted by the company's top management. Among companies that consider freedom and personal responsibility important, 27% report absences for psychological reasons. On the contrary, the share rises to 43% among companies that do not follow this principle, demonstrating how rigid control or lack of autonomy can exacerbate the psychological burden on employees. In addition, 66% of companies surveyed believe that responsibility for the mental well-being of staff rests with managers, confirming that the role of leadership is central in the perception of the work climate. Key points

The response of Swiss small and medium-sized enterprises to the rise in absences due to psychological reasons highlights the need to carefully evaluate prevention practices adopted in the workplace. Preventive measures are not increasing compared to 2023, and the proportion of companies focusing on an open feedback culture has dropped from 35 to 28 percent. Other initiatives aimed at overall employee well-being remain at modest levels: the promotion of physical health through diet and exercise stands at 14 percent, while management training stops at 11 percent. A particularly relevant finding is that one in four SMEs reports adopting no specific measure to prevent absences related to psychological reasons, highlighting considerable room for improvement in internal organization and the preventive management of work-related risks.

Frequently Asked Questions
How much do psychological absences affect Swiss SMEs?
Over a third of the SMEs surveyed, equal to 35%, declare that they are on average or strongly affected by absences due to the mental state of their employees. This percentage shows a significant growth compared to 2023, the year in which the survey stopped at 25%, highlighting a growing attention to a problem that also involves leaner business structures and not only large industrial groups.

In the last ten years, the phenomenon of absences has affected four out of ten companies. The situation varies according to company size, reaching particularly high shares among medium-sized companies: among companies with a number of employees between 50 and 250, the share reaches 62%.
