The national figure

In the third quarter, the unemployment rate calculated according to the definition of the International Labour Organization (ILO) rose in Switzerland to 5,6%, from 5,1% in the previous three months. On Thursday, the Federal Statistical Office (FSO) reported 228'000 people without employment, 36'000 more than the level recorded in the April–June period.

The increase is not distributed equally among the age groups. The share in the 15–24 age group is 12,6%, after increasing by 4,7 percentage points over twelve months. This is the highest figure among the groups mentioned in the source, which identifies young people as the most affected by unemployment.

The comparison by nationality also highlights a gap: the rate is 8,8% among foreigners and 4,2% among Swiss people. The reported difference is therefore 4,6 percentage points. By gender, the share reaches 5,8% among women and 5,3% among men, with a gap of 0,5 points.

The picture thus brings together a rising national figure and clear differences among the groups considered: the overall rate is higher than in the previous three months, while the youth figure records the increase indicated over twelve months. The overall figure is expressed on a quarterly basis: 5,6% is compared with the previous three months, while the 12,6% for young people is accompanied by a comparison over twelve months. The source therefore uses two time windows to describe the phenomenon and does not indicate, in the available text, a specific sector or cause of the increase.

The method and the employment data

The ILO statistic is based on surveys. A person without a job who has looked for work in the previous four weeks and is available to work is considered unemployed. The definition therefore brings together three conditions stated in the source: lack of employment, recent job search and availability.

The monthly statistic of the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs, on the other hand, considers people registered with the regional employment offices; between July and September, its value remained at 3.0%. In the text, 3.0% is the value from the monthly survey based on registrations, while 5.6% is the ILO rate for the third quarter. The source presents the two figures separately, associating them with their respective survey criteria.

Employment increasing year on year

There were 5.4 million employed people. Their number was 0.3% lower than in the previous quarter and 0.5% higher year on year. The two references produce different readings of the same figure: in the quarter-to-quarter comparison, the change is negative, while compared with the previous year it is positive. The summary provided by the source is that employment is growing in the Confederation and, at the same time, unemployment is also increasing.
