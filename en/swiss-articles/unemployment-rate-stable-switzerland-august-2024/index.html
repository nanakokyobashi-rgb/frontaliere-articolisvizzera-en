Switzerland's Unemployment Rate Stable in August

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 7 September 2026

The unemployment rate in Switzerland remained stable at 3% in August, with a slight increase of 0.2% year-on-year.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The unemployment rate in Switzerland is stable at 3% in August.
  • On a year-over-year basis, there is a slight increase of 0.2%.
  • In Ticino, the rate is 2.9% with an increase of 0.1 points on a monthly basis.

Key facts

  • What: Unemployment rate in Switzerland
  • When: August 2024
  • Where: Switzerland
  • Who: State Secretariat for the Economy (Seco)
  • Amount: 3% (stable compared to July)

The unemployment rate in Switzerland remained stable at 3% in August, as reported by the State Secretariat for the Economy (Seco). This figure is in line with the previous month, but on an annual basis there is a slight increase of 0.2%. Regionally, the rate was 2.9% in Ticino, with an increase of 0.1 points on a monthly basis and 0.3 points in a year. In Graubünden, the rate is 1.1%, stable compared to July and with an increase of 0.1 points on an annual basis.

The published data does not take into account people who have exhausted the right to receive benefits and who, for example, live on savings or are beneficiaries of assistance. In addition, it is based on the number of people actually registered with the Regional Employment Offices (URC), a criterion different from that of the International Labour Office (ILO), which for the 2nd quarter of the year calculated a rate of 4.9% for Switzerland.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

The unemployment rate stable at 3% in August 2024 indicates a certain stability in the Swiss labor market. However, the 0.2% increase on an annual basis suggests a slight upward trend that may require attention from the authorities. In Ticino, the 0.1-point increase on a monthly basis and the 0.3-point increase on an annual basis may reflect specific regional dynamics that deserve a more in-depth analysis. In the Graubünden, the unemployment rate of 1.1% remained stable compared to July, but with a 0.1-point increase on an annual basis. This data may be influenced by local factors that could vary from the national situation. It is important to note that the Seco data does not take into account people who have exhausted their right to receive benefits, which could influence the perception of the labor market.

Comparison with the ILO

The International Labour Office (ILO) calculated an unemployment rate of 4.9% for the 2nd quarter of the year, using a different criterion than the Seco. This gap in the data may be due to different calculation methodologies and the reference base used. It is essential for policymakers and companies to understand these differences to make informed decisions.

Implications for Workers

For Swiss workers, an unemployment rate stable at 3% can be seen as a positive sign of stability in the labor market. However, the annual increase may indicate a slight tension that could influence job opportunities and employment conditions. It is important for workers to stay informed about the dynamics of the labor market and the employment policies adopted by the authorities.

Regional Comparison

The comparison between the different regions of Switzerland reveals different dynamics in the labor market. While Ticino and the Graubünden show slight increases, other regions may have different situations. This underscores the importance of targeted employment policies that take into account the specific regional needs.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the unemployment rate stable at 3% in August 2024 indicates a certain stability in the Swiss labor market. However, the annual increase and regional differences require particular attention. It is essential for the authorities and workers to understand these dynamics to make informed decisions and adopt effective policies.
