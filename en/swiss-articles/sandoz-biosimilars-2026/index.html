Sandoz accelerates with biosimilars and exceeds first-half 2026 estimates (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 11 August 2026

Turnover at $5.8 billion, biosimilar +20% and contribute to 33% of revenues

Context

Sandoz, the pharmaceutical division of the Novartis Group, closed the first half of 2026 with net growth results, far exceeding analysts' forecasts. Turnover amounted to 5.8 billion dollars (4.7 billion francs), with an annual progression of 10%; in local currencies the increase was 5%. This result was made possible thanks to the strong momentum of the biosimilars segment, which recorded a 20% jump in local currencies, reaching 1.9 billion dollars and bringing its contribution to total revenues to 33%.

The biosimilars sector, which includes biotech drugs equivalent to traditional pharmaceuticals, has played a key role in Sandoz's growth. The Group has invested heavily in this area, opening new production lines in Switzerland and other countries. In July 2025, Sandoz inaugurated a new biosimilar factory in Bern, canton Bern, with an investment of CHF 500 million. This new facility has increased Sandoz's production capacity and reduced production costs.

The strong push for biosimilars was also made possible by Swiss legislation, which introduced measures to facilitate the market access of biotechnological medicines. Operational details

Adjusted EBITDA of Sandoz, the Swiss leader in biotech drugs, marked a 15% increase over the same period in 2025, reaching $1.2 billion. Operating margin increased to 20.9%, up 0.9 percentage points compared to the same period last year. These results were announced by CEO Richard Saynor, who said that Sandoz made excellent progress in the first half of the year, further consolidating momentum across all business sectors and strengthening the growth path. Sandoz's growth was driven by its biosimilars division, which saw a 25% increase in sales compared to the same period in 2025. This sector is particularly important for the company, as it represents one of the main growth areas for biotechnological drugs. According to market data, biosimilars already account for 30% of biotech drug sales in the Swiss market. This agreement provides for Sandoz to pay a contribution of 5 million Swiss francs to ASP for the marketing of the drug.
