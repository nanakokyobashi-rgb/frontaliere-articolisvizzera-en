Drones and robotics: DDPS sets strategic priority (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 August 2026

DDPS announces a center for unmanned systems, a drone battalion from 2028, and investments to counter hybrid threats on August 27, 2026.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Center for unmanned systems with tech hub and military command from 2026
  • Drone battalion operational since 2028 with its own recruit school
  • Investments in drone defense to protect critical infrastructure

Key facts

  • What: Center for unmanned systems (tech hub + military command)
  • When: 27 August 2026 (announcement); operational battalion from 2028
  • Where: Switzerland at federal level, in collaboration with cantons and private individuals
  • Who: DDPS (Federal Department of Defence, Population Protection and Sport)
  • References: Defence guidelines 19 June 2026; army message 2026; armament programme 2027

On 27 August 2026, the Federal Department of Defence, Population Protection and Sport (DDPS) announced a strategic breakthrough: drones, drone defence and robotics become national priorities to strengthen Switzerland's military capabilities.

The decision responds to a changed threat situation. Drones, powered by artificial intelligence, have radically transformed the way wars are conducted. In Switzerland, there is an increase in drone flights over military and critical infrastructure. At the same time, drones assert themselves as a tool of hybrid warfare, a mode of conflict that goes beyond the traditional military confrontation.

Operational details

How the drone systems center works

The new center integrates three crucial elements. The tech hub brings together the operational needs of the armed forces, the technological expertise of armasuisse (the federal armament agency), and knowledge from universities, startups and the private sector. The objective is to collaborate to develop, test and quickly deploy innovative solutions for military use.

The military command, on the other hand, manages an organization for experimentation and training: it tests new systems in realistic conditions and is responsible for training the drone battalion. A virtuous circle: the knowledge acquired by troops in the field is transmitted to the scientific and business world through the tech hub.

Implications for the national defense system

The choice of shorter acquisition cycles and smaller quantities reflects the accelerated pace of technological innovation. The armed forces intend to acquire systems in a targeted manner, in short cycles, rapidly introducing what proves effective and developing it further based on acquired experience. This agile approach reduces the risks of technological obsolescence, a serious problem in the military sector due to the exponential progress of artificial intelligence and robotics.

For the system to function, seamless collaboration is required between institutional levels: federal, cantonal and private. The DDPS has provided that the center be accessible to other departments, cantons and emergency response organizations, expanding the public benefit of research and experimentation.

Key points

Timeline and Deadlines

The schedule is defined and timely. The center for unmanned systems was established in 2026, with the formal announcement on August 27. The drone battalion becomes operational starting in 2028, with the concurrent opening of the recruit school for drones. In the meantime, the 2027 armament program already provides specific funding to equip the new unit and strengthen drone defense systems.

Investments will not stop in 2028. The military will continue the systematic integration of drone expertise throughout the military structure, adopting a continuous learning approach based on operational data and experiences acquired in the field.

The role of cantons and civil collaboration

Swiss cantons have a crucial role in defending against drone attacks, particularly those targeting critical regional infrastructure such as water and energy networks. The interdepartmental working group led by the DDPS is precisely defining who assumes which responsibility. This regulatory clarification is urgent: current ambiguities over competencies could create operational gaps in case of real crisis.

For those operating in the tech sector, in startups or in the armament and defense industry, the center represents a concrete opportunity for collaboration with the Swiss Army. Public contracts for technological supplies, software development and military hardware will follow the ordinary calls from the Federal Confederation.

Those operating in the military, cybersecurity or robotics sector should follow the developments of motion 25.4410 and updates from the interdepartmental working group. Switzerland is investing significant resources in this area, and the regulatory landscape will be subject to important changes in the coming months.

Frequently Asked Questions
When does the Swiss drone battalion become operational?
The drone battalion of the Swiss army becomes operational from 2028. In the same year, it will open its own recruit school dedicated to drones, with the aim of systematically developing the necessary skills and guaranteeing a constant contribution of specialized personnel to the new unit for reconnaissance, effectiveness and defense.
What is the DDPS Unmanned Systems Center?
It is an institutional structure created by the Federal Department of Defense, consisting of a tech hub and a military command. The tech hub brings together the needs of the troop, the skills of armasuisse and the knowledge of universities, startups and private industry to quickly develop and test new solutions. The military command runs the experimentation and education organization and the drone recruit school.
What are the planned investments in drone defense?
DDPS invests in both the deployment of drones and their defense. The first drone defense systems have already been introduced. With the message on the army 2026 and the armament program 2027, the Federal Council initiated a further capacity building, with means aimed at protecting critical infrastructure and equipping the new drone battalion.
Who decides on drone defense responsibilities in Switzerland?
Currently, defense against drones is the responsibility of various public authorities and private infrastructure managers. An interdepartmental working group led by DDPS is examining current competencies and legal bases, assessing what regulatory adjustments are needed in light of the changed threat situation. The work also proceeds on the mandate of parliamentary motion 25.4410.
What job opportunities does the new drone strategy offer?
The establishment of the drone battalion and the recruit school opens up employment prospects in the Swiss army for personnel qualified in technology, robotics and artificial intelligence. The DDPS also relies on the experience of militia members already active in the economic and scientific sector, creating concrete collaboration between the civilian and Swiss military worlds.
