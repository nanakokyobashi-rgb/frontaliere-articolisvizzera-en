Implications of Hans Meyer's death The death of Hans Meyer marks the end of an era for the Swiss National Bank (SNB) and leaves an indelible legacy in the country's economic history. Meyer, who led the institution between 1996 and 2000, played a crucial role in a period of economic and monetary transition for Switzerland. During its presidency, the Confederation faced significant challenges, including the end of the recession of the early 1990s. Meyer found himself managing a period of fluctuating interest rates: the SNB's benchmark rate fell from 3.5% in 1996 to 2.5% in 1999, demonstrating his ability to adapt monetary policy to a changing economic environment. The introduction of the euro in 1999 was a watershed event. Although Switzerland did not adopt the single currency, Meyer focused on the stability of the Swiss franc, maintaining an advantageous exchange rate with the euro to support exports, especially in the heavily industry-oriented cantons, such as Zurich and Vaud. Another important milestone was the reorganization of the cash circuit. Under his leadership, the SNB optimised the cash distribution centres, reducing them from 18 to 10, with an estimated annual operating saving of CHF 12 million. Its most lasting contribution, however, remains the definition of the concept of monetary policy based on a medium-term inflation target, set between 0% and 2%.
