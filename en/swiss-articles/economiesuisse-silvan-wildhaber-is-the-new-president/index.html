Key facts

  • What: Change of leadership at the presidency of Economiesuisse
  • When: Today
  • Where: Bern
  • Who: Silvan Wildhaber and Christoph Mäder
  • Amount: 100,000 companies represented

(Keystone-ATS) The handover took place in Bern during the economy day organized by the association representing Swiss businesses. Economiesuisse has a new leader: after two three-year terms, Christoph Mäder today handed over the presidency to his successor Silvan Wildhaber. During the body's annual general meeting, according to communications from Economiesuisse itself, the new president highlighted the international context. A trade and customs war is raging around us, Wildhaber stated in his speech to the annual assembly, emphasizing that for a small open economy like Switzerland this represents a massive challenge. The 48-year-old added that every opportunity must be seized to improve trade relations, confirming a full commitment to the new free trade agreements with Malaysia and Mercosur, as well as the bilateral III agreements. The entrepreneur from St. Gallen then highlighted the importance of convincing the population of the added value of a strong economy. Companies and their employees create the country's prosperity day after day, offering an indispensable contribution. The outgoing Mäder instead pointed the finger at the stalemate in framework conditions, expressing the hope that Switzerland will once again demonstrate the will to be among the most prosperous countries, with the best educational opportunities, the most sustainable welfare systems, a stable energy supply, and the greatest number of free trade agreements.

Operational details

Economic priorities and the comparison between organizations

The statements that emerged during the Economiesuisse Economy Day highlight the divergences and complexities that characterize the Swiss entrepreneurial fabric. On the one hand, there is the position of Economiesuisse, focused on defending the interests of large multinationals and global integration through unconditional support for free trade agreements with Malaysia and Mercosur, as well as the bilateral III agreements. On the other hand, the Swiss Arts and Crafts Union operates, led by Council of States member Fabio Regazzi, which represents Swiss small and medium-sized enterprises. This structural distinction means that the positions of the two bodies do not always coincide on crucial issues of economic and federal policy.
