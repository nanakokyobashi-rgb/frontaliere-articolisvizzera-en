Operational details

LAMal Awards: regional factors and cantonal variability

Optimal deductible is a fundamental concept for determining health insurance premiums (LAMal) in Switzerland. The subsidies available can also affect premiums, making the choice of border workers who are entitled to LAMal insurance even more complex. The latter may opt for another crate, but it is important to consider the optimal deductible to avoid unwelcome surprises.

The optimal deductible is determined by the frontier worker's income. According to the federal law of 23 March 2006, the maximum deductible for employees is CHF 2,500 per year (Article 23, paragraph 1). However, this amount may vary depending on the frontier worker's income. For example, a frontier worker with an income of CHF 80,000 per year could have an optimal allowance of CHF 1,500, while a frontier worker with an income of CHF 120,000 per year could have an optimal allowance of CHF 3,000.

A concrete example is the case of a border worker who works in Zurich and has an income of CHF 100,000 per year. The optimal allowance for this border crossing could be CHF 2,500, but if you decide to opt for another cashier, you could have an optimal allowance of CHF 3,000. In this case, the frontier worker could save CHF 500 per year.

The subsidies available can also affect the prizes. For example, if a worker receives a subsidy of CHF 1,000, the cashier could increase the premiums by CHF 100. It is important to check the rules of your canton before changing cashier, as the rules may vary from canton to canton.
