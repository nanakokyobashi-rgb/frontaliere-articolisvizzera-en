On 02 Sep 2026, an analysis dedicated to Switzerland's fight against dirty money and its Achilles' heels was published. Procedural aspects of restitution

An examination of the source highlights the intrinsic difficulties that characterize the recovery of capital that has been diverted or embezzled through international corruption. Switzerland has long faced this crucial knot linked to transparency and international cooperation. Fund repatriation procedures require in-depth checks, diplomatic and judicial steps that considerably lengthen the time required to close proceedings. Within this scenario, the question regarding the role of banks represents a pivotal point for understanding the effectiveness of the preventive and repressive measures adopted in the country. The analysis delves into the mechanisms that regulate the freezing and subsequent destination of these financial resources, placing emphasis on the structural criticalities that emerge when trying to accelerate the restitution of assets stolen from their rightful state owners.

Operational details

Analysis of the system and Achilles heels

The Swiss financial system, despite being equipped with strict standards of control and prevention of money laundering, still has several Achilles heels in the management and return of illicit funds. The published article analyses the flaws and complexities that slow down the process of restitution to States that have been damaged by acts of political or administrative corruption. The bureaucratic complexity and the need to respect the legal guarantees of the various parties involved transform each individual case into an obstacle course that can last for years. Banks operating in the Swiss territory are often at the centre of these dynamics, having to balance diligence obligations with requests from national and foreign judicial authorities. This framework highlights the gap between expectations of swift justice and the operational reality of the complex financial and legal channels needed to track and return dirty money.
