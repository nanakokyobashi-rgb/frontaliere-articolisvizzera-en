FFS to close ticket counters at 13 stations from January 1

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 October 2026

## TL;DR - From January 1, ticket offices will close at 13 stations. - Staffed points will decrease from 115 to 102. - 97% of tickets pass through channels

Context

TL;DR

  • Starting January 1, ticket offices at 13 stations will close.
  • Staffed points will decrease from 115 to 102.
  • 97% of tickets are purchased through self-service channels.
  • 3% of tickets are still sold at the ticket counter.

Key facts

  • When → from January 1st
  • Closures → 13 stations
  • Points with staff → 115 today, 102 in three months
  • Over-the-counter purchases → 3%; in 2019 just under 10%
  • Self-service purchases → 97%; digital 85%

Starting from January 1st, thirteen other small FFS stations will no longer offer personalized advice: in the last decade the service had already disappeared in around fifty locations. The information was confirmed in writing to Keystone-ATS by Moritz Weisskopf, spokesperson for the SBB, following the news published by the Zurich newspaper Blick.

With the exception of Geneva Eaux-Vives, in the district on the left bank of Lake Geneva, the stations affected from next year are all located in German-speaking Switzerland. Travel Centres, a name used by SBB, will remain available in 102 stations.

The reason given for the reduction in supply is the sharp decline in demand for tickets purchased over the counter. The share dropped to 3%, while in 2019 it was still just under 10%. Today 97% of transport tickets are purchased through self-service channels; the majority, 85%, are purchased digitally through the company's app or website. For SBB, operating manned sales outlets in stations with few customers is not economically sustainable.

The number of staffed sales points was 169 at the end of 2016, it is 115 today and in three months it will be 102. In the worst case, reaching the nearest consultancy point could mean up to 30 minutes of extra travel on mezzi pubblici for customers.

Personal consultation remains available for those who need a special subscription, want to exchange cash or need a certification of authenticity for an important document. The closure of travel centers will not lead to layoffs, according to Weisskopf.

«There will be no layoffs; the affected employees will be able to continue to carry out their activities in other travel centers".

The distinction is practical. Anyone who needs to purchase a security through a self-service channel can do so without necessarily looking for a consultancy point. However, anyone who needs to carry out one of the operations listed by the SBB must continue to go to a counter. The availability of the service therefore depends less on the individual departure station and more on the type of request.

For customers at the affected stations, the change may result in a longer journey to the nearest travel centre. The source indicates, in the worst case scenario, up to 30 additional minutes by public transport. This makes it important to distinguish between an ordinary purchase and a practice that requires personal assistance, especially when you have to organize travel to another location.

The plan passes through several cantons of German-speaking Switzerland, with the exception of Geneva Eaux-Vives. It therefore does not concern a single area of ​​the network, but stations distributed across several cantonal territories. SBB communicated the closures via regional press releases, following usual practice.

The long-term perspective also remains open. Weisskopf did not indicate how many service points will be present in 2036 nor what their concrete configuration will be. Operational details

Key points

Practical procedure

1. Check the relevant location. FFS announced the closures through regional press releases. For an affected station, the first step is therefore to check whether the staffed location is included in the published list. 2. Determine the type of transaction. Purchasing transport tickets falls under the use of self-service channels; a special pass, cash exchange or authenticity certification, on the other hand, requires assistance at the counter. 3. Use the digital channel when it is sufficient. The FFS app and website are identified by the source as channels through which tickets are purchased in digital format. Alternatively, ticket machines are available. 4. Plan the journey for matters requiring personal assistance. If the usual station no longer offers assistance, another travel centre must be reached. In the worst case, the journey by public transport may take up to 30 minutes longer.

For those who need to go to a counter, distinguishing between the different requests avoids starting from the assumption that every service is available online. The source expressly indicates that some needs will continue to require a staffed location. The digital channel is therefore a solution for purchasing tickets, not a general substitute for assistance.

For staff too, the situation described by FFS is handled differently from that of customers: closing a travel centre does not entail layoffs, and the affected employees can continue their work at other travel centres. For users, however, the operational point of reference remains the location where the necessary assistance is available.

There is no fixed configuration of the network through 2036. Weisskopf explained that it is not currently possible to predict with certainty how many service points will be present then. It is therefore advisable to check regional communications before handling a matter that requires staff, rather than considering access to the counter stable.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which stations will lose the staffed counter?
The closures concern Geneva Eaux-Vives, Arbon (TG), Brunnen (SZ), Döttingen (AG), Frick (AG), Laufen (BL), Oensingen (SO), Rotkreuz (ZG), Schönbühl Shoppyland (BE), St. Margrethen (SG), Stein am Rhein (SH), Uznach (SG) and Ziegelbrücke, on the border between Glarus and St. Gallen.
How much has the way of buying SBB tickets changed?
Currently only 3% of transport tickets are still sold at the counter, compared to just under 10% in 2019. 97% of purchases are made through self-service channels, of which 85% in digital format through the SBB app or website.
What should passengers who need a special pass, change cash or get a certificate of authenticity do?
For these operations, it is necessary to contact a counter with staff who are still active. In the worst case, the nearest advice point can take up to an additional 30 minutes to travel by public transport.
