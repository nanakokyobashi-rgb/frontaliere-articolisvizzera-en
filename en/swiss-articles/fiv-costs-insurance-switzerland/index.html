IVF in Switzerland: Costs are not covered (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 September 2026

In vitro fertilization costs up to 8,000 francs and is not reimbursed by KVG. One expert calls it "an injustice" because the WHO recognizes infertility as a disease.

In vitro fertilization is often the last option for those who are unable to have children naturally. In Switzerland, although infertility is recognized internationally as a disease, resorting to IVF involves an initial cost of around 8,000 francs. A cost that falls entirely on the patient's shoulders, without any guarantee of pregnancy and without reimbursement from basic insurance.

«An injustice», according to Fabien Murisier, director of the Center for Medically Assisted Procreation (CPMA) in Lausanne. "Reimbursing also means recognizing illness and suffering," underlined the expert.

The path is partially covered

Every year in Switzerland around 6,500 couples turn to reproductive medicine. Yet not everyone necessarily needs to resort to in vitro fertilization. «Almost 50% of our patients manage to have a child without going through IVF», explains Murisier.

Operational details

Financial injustice and cultural taboo

It is precisely the variability of costs that makes the situation financially unfair. As Murisier points out, it is impossible to establish an average price for the complete journey up to birth. The mother's age has a huge impact on the outcome: the older she gets, the fewer embryos are obtained and the greater the risk of chromosomal anomalies. Some patients will get pregnant on the first transfer, others will need three or four attempts. This unpredictability makes the situation financially unfair and creates unacceptable inequalities in the right to access parenting.

In addition to the economic issue, the reimbursement would have an important symbolic value. Infertility remains surrounded by a certain cultural taboo in Switzerland, with the idea sometimes implied that the people affected are responsible for their own situation: too stressed or that it is enough to "leave it to nature". “The same would not be said for other diseases, such as cancer, for example,” underlines Murisier. Recognition through reimbursement would also mean dispelling this taboo and openly addressing the issue of infertility.

The unequal treatment between heterosexual couples and female couples

There is a further difference in treatment in the Swiss healthcare system. If for a heterosexual couple with infertility problems some treatments are partially covered, for a couple made up of two women the situation is different. Health insurance companies generally believe that it is not infertility caused by illness and refuse to cover insemination with donor sperm.

Key points

Where and how to contact

For those who live in Switzerland and find themselves in a situation of infertility, the first step is to consult your GP or a gynecologist for an evaluation. Diagnostic tests to identify the causes are generally covered by basic insurance, as are the first year of ovarian stimulation or up to three attempts at intrauterine insemination.

If these treatments do not bring results and the doctor believes that IVF is necessary, it is essential to be aware that the costs will not be covered. It is therefore necessary:

  • Contact specialized facilities such as medically assisted procreation centers (CPMA) to obtain a realistic estimate of costs for your specific case
  • Contact your health insurance company to see which items may be partially covered
  • Check whether your KVG policy provides additional coverage which could include assisted reproduction

Financial planning and options

According to Murisier and other experts, IVF reimbursement falls within the logic of inclusive healthcare that recognizes infertility as a disease and not as a personal choice. Although it is not currently covered by the Swiss basic insurance system, it is important to monitor political debates that could change this situation.

In the meantime, it is advisable to check whether some health insurance companies offer coverage for reproductive medicine. Each canton may also have specific guidelines for couples facing infertility issues. 💡 Disparities in costs and coverage make a personalized consultation with your insurance essential before embarking on an IVF process.

To better plan the financial implications and understand the various options available in your canton, compare the different Swiss health insurance companies and discover their coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions
How much does IVF cost in Switzerland?
A first IVF costs around 8,000 francs, plus between 500 and 1,500 francs for medication. If the procedure is unsuccessful and the patient has frozen embryos, each new transfer costs around 2,000 francs. If there are no frozen embryos left, the entire procedure must be repeated with all the associated costs.
Is IVF covered by KVG in Switzerland?
No, IVF is not covered by Swiss basic health insurance. However, diagnostic tests for infertility and some preliminary treatments are covered: ovarian stimulation for up to one year and up to three attempts at intrauterine insemination. Only IVF remains completely the responsibility of the patient.
What percentage of patients do not need IVF?
According to the director of the CPMA in Lausanne, almost 50% of patients manage to have a child without resorting to IVF, using other reproductive medicine treatments that are at least partially covered by insurance.
Is there a difference in treatment between heterosexual couples and female couples?
Yes. For heterosexual couples with infertility problems, some treatments are partially covered by insurance. For female couples, insurance companies generally do not consider insemination with donor sperm as medical coverage, as they do not recognize infertility caused by disease. This creates a disparity in access rights.
Does the WHO recognize infertility as a disease?
Yes, the World Health Organization (WHO) officially recognizes infertility as a disease. However, in Switzerland the health system does not cover IVF, although it is a recognized and very effective medical treatment to deal with this medical condition.
