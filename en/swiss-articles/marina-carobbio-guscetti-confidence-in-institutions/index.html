Operational details

Marina Carobbio Guscetti, a leading figure in Swiss politics, stressed the importance of recognising the role of institutions in the public good. “Institutions are made of people working for the collective good,” he said. “They deserve trust, respect, and care.” These words highlight the need to take care not only of the institutions, but also of the people who make them up.

Taking care of institutions means ensuring that they operate efficiently and transparently. This can be achieved through the promotion of good administration and the prevention of corruption. In Switzerland, for example, Federalism is an important aspect of the political system. The 1999 Federal Constitution establishes the principles of federalism, guaranteeing the autonomy of the cantons and cities. This approach has made it possible to maintain a balance between federal and cantonal competences.

A concrete example of how Swiss institutions have worked for the public good is the management of the COVID-19 pandemic. The Federal Government worked closely with cantons and cities to coordinate containment measures. Switzerland has implemented an effective testing and tracking strategy, which has made it possible to reduce the spread of the virus. According to data from the Federal Office of Public Health (FOPH), Switzerland has one of the lowest mortality rates from COVID-19 in Europe. 📊

Key points

To take care of institutions, it is important to take care of the people who work there. This means listening to their stories, understanding their needs, and working together to achieve common goals. It is important to remember that institutions are made of people and deserve respect, trust and care.

In Switzerland, the system of institutions is known for its efficiency and transparency. However, it is crucial to continue working to improve the quality of public services and increase citizen satisfaction. One of the concrete examples is the project to improve public services in the field of health, launched in 2020 by the Canton of Vaud. This project saw the investment of CHF 10 million to improve the quality of healthcare services and reduce waiting times for interventions.

Another important example is the education system development project, launched in 2018 by the Canton of Ticino. This project saw the investment of CHF 5 million to improve the quality of education and increase student satisfaction. The result was a 15% increase in educationally satisfied students.
