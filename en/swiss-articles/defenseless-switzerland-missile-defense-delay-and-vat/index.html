Defenseless Switzerland: missile defense delay and VAT

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 5 October 2026

The delivery of the Bodluv GR2 anti-missile system is delayed; the Patriot already ordered will be delayed by years. Funding could come from an increase in VAT, still awaiting approval by Parliament. Suppliers: France and Israel.

The delay in missile defense

BERN - The source describes a situation in which, if Switzerland were attacked with ballistic missiles, it would currently have no way to defend itself. Blick's research shows that delivery of the Bodluv GR2 defense system is delayed.

The system was supposed to provide defense capability, while the Patriot already ordered will be delayed by years. According to Blick, the problem does not come from the manufacturer. The delay is attributed to the Confederation, because the financing of the delivery has not yet been clarified.

Among the measures indicated to cover the expense is a VAT increase, which has not yet been approved by Parliament. The text therefore links the financial coverage of the purchase to an unresolved step in federal politics. In the meantime, some federal parliamentarians criticize the fact that Armasuisse apparently favors the Israeli system.

Jean-Luc Addor, an SVP National Councillor from Valais, argued that Israel is at war and that Swiss neutrality would be at risk. The politician called for a quick solution, citing international competition for these systems.

«Many countries currently want to purchase such systems. If Switzerland waits, it could be too late to receive a system in time».

Security politicians have also referred to the Federal Council's strategy, which calls for purchases from Europe: according to this view, France should be given priority. The DDPS told Blick that, for now, excluding non-European manufacturers would not be justified. In the future, part of the value chain could be relocated to Switzerland or Europe.

There is still no schedule for missile defense. The Department wants to know from the two suppliers, France and Israel, whether their offers will remain valid in a few months. The decision thus remains tied to timing, financing, and the choice of supplier.

The scenarios indicated by the text

The first scenario is parliamentary approval of the VAT increase: it would follow through on the funding channel mentioned, but the source establishes neither the amount nor the timing. The second is a prolonged wait: the financing would remain unclear, and DDPS would continue to have to verify the validity of the offers from France and Israel. The third concerns the industrial choice. On the one hand, security politicians invoke a European strategy and call for priority to be given to France; on the other, DDPS says that excluding non-European manufacturers at this point is not justifiable.

The dossier also has an organizational dimension. Christian Fichter explained to 20 Minuten that, in a large organization such as DDPS, complex decision-making processes can create communication gaps and unclear responsibilities. In his view, a pronounced hierarchy can make it more difficult to report risks or propose improvements. For anyone assessing the matter, this means distinguishing between political, departmental and industrial responsibilities instead of attributing every delay to the manufacturer.
