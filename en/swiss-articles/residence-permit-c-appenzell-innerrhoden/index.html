The residence permit C represents the recognition of your permanent integration in Switzerland. In the Inner Appenzell Canton, as in the other Swiss cantons, this permit follows the federal procedure established by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM), with the addition of specific cantonal requirements.

Federal legislation sets the minimum residence period: 10 years for non-EU citizens, reduced to 5 years for citizens of the European Union and the European Free Trade Association (EFTA). The C permit is not automatic after the period: it depends on assessment of integration, language skills and the absence of grounds for refusal.

Operational details

Difference between Permit B (residence) and Permit C (domicile)

Many applicants confuse the two permits. Both are permitted in the long term, but with different legal implications.

Permit B (Residence):

  • Renewable annually (not automatic, but usual renewal)
  • Linked to occupation or initial reason for stay
  • Revocable if you lose your job or change your circumstances
  • Average duration: 5 years of consecutive renewals

Permit C (Address):

  • Indefinite (renewable on request every 10 years, formally)
  • Independent of employment (you do not lose your permit if you are unemployed)
  • Right to stay almost acquired
  • Path to Swiss naturalisation

For those who want to move to a permanent residence in Switzerland (not just temporary employment), the C permit is the crucial step.

In the Inner Appenzell Canton, the transition from B to C takes place upon explicit request. It is not automatic: you must prove 10 years of residence and meet the integration criteria.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between 10 years of residence for non-EU nationals and 5 years for EU/EFTA nationals?
The Federal Migration Act (LEMI) sets different terms for nationality. EU and European Free Trade Association (EFTA) citizens enjoy free movement agreements, so the requirement is reduced to 5 years of residence. Non-EU citizens must respect 10 years. The Inner Appenzell Canton applies these federal standards without exception.
Can I apply for the C permit before the expected 10 years or 5 years?
In exceptional cases (marriage to a Swiss citizen, extraordinary public service, naturalization on merit), the canton may grant early release. However, it is not guaranteed — it depends on Appenzello Interno's discretionary assessment and the specific circumstance. Tip: Contact the cantonal immigration office to evaluate your case.
What level of German do I need for the C permit in Inner Appenzell?
There is no minimum prescribed federal standard. Appenzello Interno normally requires functional capacity (A2-B1 according to the European CEFR framework) — sufficient to maintain work and converse locally. You can prove it with a recognized certificate (GOETHE, Telc), self-declaration + interview, or direct evaluation by the cantonal office.
If I am unemployed, can I apply for C leave?
Short-term unemployment does not automatically exclude C. However, if you are dependent on cantonal social aid, it is very difficult to obtain permission (almost impossible). You must demonstrate your own economic capacity or family benefits. Tip: Wait for economic stability, or consult the immigration office about your specific situation for a preliminary assessment.
How much does it cost to apply for a C permit in the Inner Appenzello?
The cost is not fixed by federal law. Each canton sets its own administrative fees (usually CHF 50-300 depending on complexity and presentation channel). Contact the Immigration Office of Appenzello Interno directly to find out the current exact quotas and payment methods.
