Bilateral Agreements III: Marathon begins in the Council of States

Proceedings resume today in the Federal Chambers, and the Council of States opens the marathon debate on the Switzerland-EU stabilization package, referred to as Bilateral Agreements III. Three days of discussions are planned to dispose of the dossier, at the heart of the final week of the session.

The schedule in the chamber

The debate on whether to consider the bill will begin in the afternoon. The session could continue into the early hours, even past midnight. Proceedings will resume on Tuesday morning until 13:00 and continue all day Wednesday.

The discussions promise to be lively because the package brings together several sensitive dossiers. At least five federal councillors are expected in the chamber. Ignazio Cassis, responsible for Swiss diplomacy, will have to be present for a large part of the proceedings, in what the source describes as a real tour de force.

The first part of the package concerns the stabilization and updating of existing agreements in the fields of air and land transport, trade in food products, mutual recognition of conformity assessments and the free movement of persons. The second deepens relations through new agreements on healthcare, food security and electricity. The electricity agreement will not be discussed, however: the commission’s analysis of the text has not yet been completed.

At the domestic level, accompanying measures are planned to protect wages and combat wage dumping. The package also includes four credits: two for the cohesion contribution, which should increase and become permanent, one for migration and one for Switzerland’s participation in the Erasmus+ programme in 2027.

The Swiss People’s Party is the only party fundamentally opposed to the entire package and will call for the bill not to be considered. The other political groups, the social partners and the business associations support the outcome of the negotiations in principle, but impose conditions. The issues also include wage protection, adjustments to the free movement of persons and the resolution of disputes between Bern and Brussels.

Operational details

Implications to monitor

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the first concrete implication concerns the political process that will accompany the package. The source describes a discussion in the Council of States, not the application of new measures. The practical point to observe, for now, is therefore the content of the conditions set by the various actors.

The referendum as the first crossroads

The choice of referendum changes the type of majority indicated in the dossier. The Federal Council, the left, the PLR and the Green Liberals support the optional referendum, linked to the majority of the people. The UDC instead calls for the mandatory referendum, with a double majority of the people and the Cantons. The Centre will wait until the end of the debates before taking a position.

For a resident, the difference is not a technical detail: it indicates which political step would have to be followed for the agreements. Until the discussion is concluded, it is not advisable to treat as definitive the conditions that are still under negotiation.

Wages, mobility and economic sectors

The most sensitive chapter for employment concerns national measures to protect wages and combat dumping. The left and trade unions consider the dossier delicate and could reconsider their support for the entire package if they deemed worker protection insufficient. For those active in the labor market, the issue to monitor is therefore the link between agreements and wage guarantees, not an amount that has already been set.

The free movement of people also involves several levels of discussion. The adjustments, including the adoption of the EU directive on citizenship, are not supported by everyone; the Federal Council has provided for a safeguard clause in the event of high immigration, and a new immigration tax will be discussed in the chamber. For those following federal politics, the practical point is to distinguish between the clause provided for by the Federal Council and the tax still under discussion.

The implications concern more than just employment. Transport, food trade, conformity assessments, healthcare, food safety and electricity are the sectors listed in the package, while state aid and funds for research and education are among the other controversial points. Key points

How to follow the marathon

The most useful way to read the dossier is to turn the parliamentary calendar into a checklist. The first step is to distinguish the timing of the discussion from the content of the measures: today the debate on entering into the matter opens in the Council of States.

A checklist for workers and residents

1. Mark the first window: the Council of States begins in the afternoon and the discussion may continue past midnight. 2. Follow the resumption on Tuesday morning until 13:00. At this stage, it is advisable to note every point devoted to wage protection, dumping and the free movement of persons. 3. Also set aside Wednesday, when the discussion will continue throughout the day. If the debate touches on immigration, separate the safeguard clause from the new immigration tax: in the source, these are distinct issues. 4. Divide the package into four reading folders: updating existing agreements, new agreements, national accompanying measures and credits. This is the simplest way not to confuse transport and food trade with healthcare, food safety and electricity. 5. Keep the electricity agreement separate, then compare the positions on the referendum and the conditions set by parties, social partners and business associations. Il Centro will decide after the debates, while the left and the trade unions link their support to worker protection.

For a personal reading, the criterion is simple: if you work, look first at wages, dumping and free movement; if you operate in an economic sector, compare your field with those listed in the package; if you are interested in education or research, consider the reference to the funds allocated and the Erasmus+ programme in 2027. The phase described remains parliamentary, so the useful action is to follow the steps without turning the debate into a rule that is already applicable.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the timetable for discussions on Bilateral III?
The entry debate on the matter at the Council of States begins this afternoon and could last beyond midnight. Work will resume on Tuesday morning until 1:00 p.m. and will continue for the entire day on Wednesday. In total, there are three days of discussions to settle the dossier in the middle of the last week of the session.
Which sectors are included in the stabilisation package?
The package is divided into two parts. The first concerns the updating of agreements on air and land transport, food trade, conformity assessments and free movement of people. The second introduces new agreements on health and food safety. The electricity agreement will not be dealt with as the analysis of the text in committee is not yet concluded.
What are the most critical points for workers and wages?
The dossier provides for accompanying national measures for the protection of wages and the fight against dumping. The Left and trade unions closely monitor these guarantees, being able to review their support for the package if deemed insufficient. In addition, adjustments to free movement will be discussed, including the adoption of the EU Citizenship Directive and a new "immigration tax".
