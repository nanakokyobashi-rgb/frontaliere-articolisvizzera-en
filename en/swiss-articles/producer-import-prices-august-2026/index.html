Neuchâtel, September 14, 2026. The Federal Statistical Office (FSO) has released updated data on producer and import price trends in Switzerland. The official press release, dated September 14, 2026, reveals that in August 2026, the total producer and import price index recorded an increase of 0.7% compared to the previous month. This increase brings the index level to 100.4 points, using December 2025 as the reference base, set at 100 points.

Market dynamics

The monthly rise was primarily driven by specific industrial and energy sectors. FSO data show a particular increase in the prices of petroleum products, chemical products, as well as those related to crude oil and natural gas. Specifically, the increase in the producer price index compared to the previous month is mainly due to higher prices for petroleum products and pharmaceutical preparations. Price increases were also observed for slaughter pigs, pork, raw milk, rubber and plastic products, as well as plastics in primary forms.

However, the picture is not uniformly positive for costs. The FSO reports a decrease in the prices of basic pharmaceutical products, other chemical products, and electricity for large consumers. On the import side, compared to July 2026, the import price index recorded higher prices especially for petroleum products, organic products of the chemical industry, crude oil, and natural gas. Increases were also recorded for plastics in primary forms, vegetables, melons and potatoes, rubber and plastic products, inorganic products of the chemical industry, other chemical products, paper and paper products, and green coffee. Conversely, a drop in computer prices was observed.

On a trend level, the situation shows stabilization. Compared to August 2025, the price level of the total supply of domestic and imported products fell by 0.7%. This figure indicates that, despite the monthly inflationary push, national price pressure remains below the level recorded a year ago. Full results and further information on the subject can be found on the FSO website, which is the official source for these crucial economic indicators for the Swiss economy.
