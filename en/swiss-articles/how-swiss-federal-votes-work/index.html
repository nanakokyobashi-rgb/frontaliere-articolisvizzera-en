Federal Voting: Guiding the Functioning of Direct Democracy (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 August 2026

Find out how popular initiatives and referendums, pillars of Swiss direct democracy involving the people and cantons, work.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Switzerland applies direct democracy through popular votes.
  • Decisions often require a double majority of the people and Cantons.
  • The system guarantees constant control over the work of the government.

Key facts

  • What: Federal Voting in Switzerland
  • When: Periodically throughout the year
  • Where: Swiss Confederation
  • Who: People and Cantons
  • Rule: Double majority

The Swiss political system is based on the active participation of citizens, who can directly influence the legislative process. Unlike many other countries, Switzerland is not an EU/EEA member, maintaining a unique direct democracy structure. The heart of the system is the right of referendum and popular initiative. Citizens can request to put laws passed by Parliament to a popular vote or propose amendments to the Federal Constitution. This mechanism requires the Federal Council and the legislature to constantly seek consensus between the different political and regional forces.

Federal Decision-Making Mechanisms

The voting process meets strict constitutional criteria. For amendments to the Constitution, it is essential to obtain a double majority: the proposal must be accepted by the majority of voters at the national level and by the majority of the Cantons. Participation in Swiss democratic life requires an understanding of the dynamics between the branches of government. The Italy-Switzerland Double Taxation Convention, signed on DECEMBER 9, 1976, is an example of an international agreement that can be a subject of political debate, as are the recent regulations concerning cross-border commuters. The new Cross-Border Commuter Agreement, signed on 23/12/2020 and in effect since JANUARY 1, 2024, illustrates how political choices have direct impacts on daily life. These decisions influence tax rates and social contributions, such as the AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3% borne by the employee, or the AD/AC contribution at 1.1% up to the cap of CHF 148,200.

Economic and fiscal impacts

Decisions made at the polls are directly reflected in payroll management. Workers in Switzerland are subject to specific deductions, such as the LAINF which fluctuates between 0.7% and 1.5%, and the LPP contribution, which varies from 7% to 18% based on the age bracket starting from age 25. For those residing abroad, taxation follows precise rules: Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit in the CE section of the 730 tax return. For new cross-border commuters, there is a tax-free allowance of €10,000, while for old cross-border commuters, active before 17/7/2023, an exemption of €7,500 is provided with a transitional regime from 2024 to 2033. The Italian tax system applies IRPEF rates of 23% up to €28,000, 35% between €28,001 and €50,000, and 43% over €50,000. Frequently Asked Questions
How do you calculate withholdings for a border crossing?
Deductions include AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3% and AD/AC at 1.1% up to a cap of CHF 148'200. LAINF ranges from 0.7% to 1.5%, while LPP ranges from 7% to 18% based on age. These quotas are established by federal laws and administered by cantonal administrations.
What changes with the new Frontier Agreement?
In force since 1 JANUARY 2024, the agreement provides for a deductible of €10,000 for new frontier workers. For old border workers, already employed before 17/7/2023, an exemption of €7,500 is provided for with a transitional regime that will last until 2033.
How does the double majority in Switzerland work?
To approve constitutional changes, a double majority is required: the consent of the majority of voters at the national level and the majority of the Cantons. This mechanism ensures that federal decisions reflect broad territorial consensus.
