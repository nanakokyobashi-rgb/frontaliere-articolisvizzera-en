Two issues still unresolved

Six weeks before the 29 November vote, the first poll conducted by the gfs.bern institute on behalf of SSR paints a divided picture. The survey was conducted between 5 and 18 October and published today. The news item, marked Keystone-ATS, summarizes voting intentions on the four items on the ballot.

Additional OASI financing through an increase in VAT remains in the balance. 46% of respondents are in favor, 49% opposed and 5% have not yet decided. Among SP supporters, the yes vote reaches 60%, while it stands at 58% among Greens and Centre supporters and at 56% among GLP supporters. The SVP, by contrast, records 68% no votes and the FDP 54%.

The demographic profile further differentiates voting intentions. Among those aged 18 to 39, support for the financing is 35%; among those over 65, it rises to 55%. By sex, men reject the proposal more strongly, with 52% opposed. Among women, the figures are balanced: 47% are in favor and 47% opposed. The difference between the linguistic regions is smaller: approval reaches 45% in German-speaking Switzerland and 47% in both Romandy and Italian-speaking Switzerland.

The revision of the Federal Act on War Material also has no clear majority. 49% say they are opposed, 41% are in favor and 10% remain undecided. Those aged 18 to 39 are more opposed to the proposal, with 54% voting no, while among those over 65, the yes vote reaches 60%. By gender, 57% of men support easing the rules on exports of war materiel, compared with 41% of women. By language, support is 53% in German-speaking Switzerland, 37% in Romandy and 45% in Italian-speaking Switzerland.

Operational details

Comparison between the two uncertain proposals

Different margins in the same poll

The arithmetic comparison between the two proposals shows two close, but not identical, situations. For AVS financing, no votes exceed yes votes by 3 percentage points: 49% versus 46%, with 5% undecided. For war materiel, the gap is 8 points: 49% opposed versus 41% in favor, while 10% are undecided. These are differences calculated based on the values published by the poll, not definitive voting results.

The distribution by age follows a similar direction in both cases, but with different indicators. For AVS financing, support rises from 35% among 18-39-year-olds to 55% among over-65s: a difference of 20 points. For war materiel, by contrast, no prevails among younger people at 54%, while yes prevails among over-65s at 60%. The figure does not measure the same response in the two proposals, but indicates how age separates voting intentions in both issues.

Key points

The two initiatives leaning toward yes

Fireworks: the broadest support

The proposal that appears relatively less contested is the «For a restriction on fireworks» Initiative. 69% of respondents say they are in favor, 29% against, and only 2% undecided. The difference between yes and no is therefore 40 percentage points, calculated based on the survey figures.

Support remains in the majority in the political breakdown reported by the poll. Among the various party electorates cited, the shares in favor are 59%, 67%, 80%, 83% and 91%. The source therefore shows a range of 59% to 91% for this initiative, in addition to the overall figure of 69%.

The difference is also visible by gender: women support the proposal with 76% yes, compared with 60% among men. In the language regions, the initiative receives 69% in German-speaking Switzerland, 67% in French-speaking Switzerland and 61% in Italian-speaking Switzerland. Frequently Asked Questions
What are the results of the survey on AVS funding?
According to the gfs.bern survey conducted between October 5 and 18, supplementary funding for the AVS through an increase in VAT is in the balance: 46% of respondents are in favor, 49% are opposed and 5% are undecided. Support varies significantly by age, ranging from 35% among 18–39-year-olds to 55% among those over 65, and by political orientation, with peaks among PS sympathizers (60%) and strong opposition within the UDC (68% voting no).
What emerges regarding the revision of the War Materials Act?
The poll shows the absence of a clear majority: 49% of respondents say they are opposed, 41% are in favor and 10% remain undecided. There are strong gender and linguistic differences: 57% of men support easing the export rules compared with 41% of women. What are the voting intentions for the other two initiatives?
The initiative to limit fireworks garners the broadest support, with 69% in favor, 29% opposed and 2% undecided. The initiative for fair taxation of spouses is supported by 55% of respondents, with 34% opposed and 11% undecided.
