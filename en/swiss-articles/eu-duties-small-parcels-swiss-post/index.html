EU duties on parcels: impact on Posta svizzera

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 October 2026

Since July, the EU has charged 3 euro for each item from third countries. The Posta svizzera reports a double-digit decline in parcels to the EU.

Since July, the European Union has imposed a flat-rate duty of 3 euro for each item coming from a third country. The measure concerns small parcels and has also had repercussions for Posta svizzera: the number of parcels sent to the EU has fallen by a double-digit percentage.

The figure was reported by Pascal Grieder, director of Posta svizzera, in an interview with Schweiz am Wochenende. The executive adds that the situation should gradually return to normal. This forecast accompanies a critical assessment of the measure's objective: Grieder doubts that protecting the market from Chinese imports will actually be achieved.

The rule and the consequences indicated

The duty is flat-rate: the source indicates the same unit amount, 3 euro, for each item coming from a third country. In principle, payment is the responsibility of the seller or importer. Some merchants, however, pass this amount on to consumers. The rule therefore identifies a main responsible party, while the cost may reach whoever makes the purchase.

Grieder's point concerns the organization of shipments. The executive cites Temu, a low-cost Chinese online sales site, and reports that companies like this are opening large warehouses in Europe. Parcels are shipped from those warehouses and, according to his assessment, thus avoid paying the fee. For Grieder, this step calls into question the practical effectiveness of the duty in relation to the stated objective of protecting the market from Chinese imports.

The connection with Switzerland emerges from the data on Posta svizzera's volumes: the European measure is described not only as a rule for sellers and importers, but also as a change that has already had repercussions on Posta svizzera shipments to the EU. The director indicates a decline expressed as a double-digit percentage and expects a gradual recovery. The news thus brings together a new European duty, a different organization of shipments, and a response observed in Swiss postal activity.

Operational details

How to read the impact

To understand the practical effect of the duty, it is necessary to keep distinct the origin of the item, the party required to pay, and the price ultimately borne by the consumer. The source does not present these three elements as equivalent: the measure indicates the amount per item coming from a third country; the seller or importer is responsible in principle; some merchants may pass on the cost. For anyone observing a final price, this distinction avoids confusing the party responsible for payment with the one who ultimately bears the amount.

Those who use the Posta svizzera to send parcels to the EU can read the source's figure as an indicator of the trend: the volume of shipments has fallen by a double-digit percentage. There is no need to turn this wording into a more precise figure. Grieder expects a gradual return to normality, so the forecast describes a recovery phase without setting an immediate transition.
