The CRS initiates legal action against Souveraineté suisse for the improper use of its name in the neutrality campaign on the ballot on 27 September.

The Swiss Red Cross has decided to appeal to the judiciary against the misuse of its name. The humanitarian organisation has formally launched legal action following the improper use of its name by Souveraineté suisse, a Geneva-based association that launched a campaign in favour of the neutrality initiative, which was subject to a federal vote on 27 September. The CRS was directly mentioned in the social media posts of the promotional campaign, a circumstance that led the organization to react legally to protect its identity and reputation.

A spokeswoman for the Swiss Red Cross told the Keystone-ATS agency that the legal steps could lead to a criminal complaint, but the procedure will first follow the path of a warning

Operational details

Political neutrality as an indispensable principle

The Swiss Red Cross reiterates a fundamental rule that guides its operation: as a matter of principle, the organization does not take a position on popular votes whose outcome does not directly affect the exercise of its institutional mandate. This is a line that is applied systematically, without exception, and also applies in the case of the neutrality initiative put to the vote on 27 September. Although the Souveraineté suisse campaign tried to associate the CRS with its cause through the unauthorized use of its name and emblem, the organization maintained a position of absolute distance from the political issues at stake.

The legal issue raised by the CRS does not concern the merits of the neutrality initiative, but rather the constitutive right of an organization to protect its identity and preserve its independence from political disputes. When an association such as Souveraineté suisse uses the name or emblem of a recognized body without explicit authorization, it goes beyond simple advertising communication: it is making an attempt at appropriation that aims to exploit the prestige, reputation and neutrality of the organization to give false credibility to its campaign.

Key points

How organizations protect their rights

Swiss organisations, whether public, private, for-profit or non-profit, have established legal instruments to protect themselves from misuse of their name, logos and distinctive symbols. The first step of the procedure is usually a warning, a formal document drawn up by a lawyer or an organization itself that calls for the immediate cessation of abusive use within a specific period, usually ranging from 7 to 14 days. If the recipient of the notice does not comply with the notice within the established period, the applicant organization may initiate further legal actions, including formal complaints for infringement of intellectual property rights, misappropriation of protected trademarks or emblems, or even criminal proceedings in case of violation of specific rules.

In the particular case of the Swiss Red Cross, the importance of legal protection is even higher and more motivated. The Red Cross emblem is not only protected by Swiss national law - through trademark and intellectual property law - but is also protected by international conventions that recognize the symbolic, humanitarian and protective value of this emblem worldwide. Frequently Asked Questions
Does the Red Cross always have the right to prevent the use of its name in political campaigns?
Yes, any organization has the right to protect the use of its name and symbols. The CRS, in particular, has an obligation to preserve its neutrality and to prevent its name from being associated with specific political positions. Misuse without explicit authorization is a violation that can lead to serious legal consequences, including formal warnings and criminal complaints under Swiss law.
What precisely does' misuse 'of the Red Cross name mean?
It means using the name, emblem or symbol of the CRS in public contexts, official communications, campaigns or social media posts without having received explicit written permission from the organisation. In the case of the neutrality campaign, Souveraineté suisse cited the CRS in its social posts to promote the initiative, which was considered by the CRS as an indebted and unauthorized association in its own name.
What are the real legal risks for unauthorized use of an organization's name?
The risks range from a formal warning (legal invitation to cease use within a set period) to criminal complaints, especially if the emblem or trademark is protected by specific laws. In the case of international symbols such as the Red Cross, protected by international conventions, the consequences can be even more serious than with ordinary trademarks.
Will the CRS legal action affect the outcome of the neutrality vote?
No. The Swiss Red Cross has explicitly made it clear that it does not take a position on votes whose outcome does not directly affect its mandate. Its legal action is motivated solely by issues of protection of the reputation and protective function of the organization, not by political considerations concerning the merits of the neutrality initiative.
What happened to the Red Cross logo after the CRS report?
According to the weekly Blick, following a direct confrontation between the CRS and Souveraineté suisse, the Red Cross logo has been removed from the campaign materials and posts. However, the name of the organization remained mentioned in a promotional slogan of the campaign, which led the CRS to proceed with legal action for a complete protection of its identity.
