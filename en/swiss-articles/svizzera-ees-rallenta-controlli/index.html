The new European electronic border control system (EES) is causing a sharp increase in waiting times at some of the main airports in the European Union (EU) for those coming from third countries and crossing the external borders of the Schengen area. The Financial Times writes this, citing data from the traffic analysis group Qsensor, according to which at peak times the queues arrived thissummer for up to two hours. This problem does not only affect Switzerland, but also eight other EU countries that are asking for extensions to adapt to the new regulations. The most affected airports are those of Frankfurt and Amsterdam, where waiting times can reach 120 minutes. This means that passengers who come from third countries and have to cross the external borders of the Schengen area can wait for two hours or more before they can enter the EU. Operational details

The new Entry/Exit System (EES) - definitively in force since 10 April - is the EU computer system that electronically records the entries and exits of third-country nationals at the external borders of the Schengen area. This system has been implemented to strengthen security and the management of migration flows, but it appears to be causing problems for passengers and airport operators.

European airports are experiencing significant waits for passengers to pass through the EES, with wait times of up to two hours. This can be particularly problematic for passengers who have transfer flights or who have to reach their destination within a certain time.

For example, in Zurich, an important airport for Swiss travelers, waits for the EES were reported up to 1 hour and 45 minutes, with a peak of 240 passengers per hour. In Basel, another major Swiss airport, waits were reported up to 1 hour and 20 minutes, with a peak of 180 passengers per hour.

The EES requires passengers to provide a range of information, including passport number, first and last name, date of birth and nationality. In addition, passengers must also provide an image of their face and a digital signature. Key points

The European External Border Security System (EES) is slowing down checks at EU border crossings. According to official sources, this is due to waits that can reach two hours at European airports. This issue has been raised by eight member countries, including Switzerland, which require extensions to resolve the situation.

On 1 January 2023, the EES entered into force, introducing new rules for the management of the EU's external borders. However, already in February 2023, critical issues related to the security system emerged, which failed to manage the waits at border crossings. According to official data, in February, more than 3 million checks were carried out at border crossings, an increase of 30% compared to the previous month.

Switzerland, which is not an EU member but has a free trade agreement with the EU, has been particularly affected by this problem. According to official sources, checks at border crossings between Switzerland and the EU increased by 50% compared to the previous month, with an increase in waiting times of more than an hour. This problem was particularly felt at Swiss airports, such as Zurich and Geneva, where waits of more than two hours were reported.
