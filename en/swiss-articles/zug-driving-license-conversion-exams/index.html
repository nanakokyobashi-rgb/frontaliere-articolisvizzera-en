Driving licence Zug: foreign conversion, exams & procedures (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 22 September 2026

Complete guide to converting a foreign driver's license in the canton of Zug: required documents, theoretical and practical exams, mandatory courses and deadlines.

Context

In short&#10;

  • Canton of Zug requires residents to convert foreign driving licences within 1 year&#10;
  • Theoretical (multilingual) and practical exam required, with some exceptions&#10;
  • Mandatory courses: first aid (10 hours) and ecological training (4 hours)&#10;
  • The Zug Road Traffic Office is the competent body&#10;&#10;

Key Facts&#10; Conversion deadline: 1 year from the B/C permit&#10; Required exams: Theoretical + Practical&#10; Mandatory courses: First aid (10h) + Ecology (4h)&#10; Responsible office: Strassenverkehrsamt Zug&#10; Basic documents: Passport, permit, criminal record, photo&#10;&#10; For residents of the Canton of Zug with a foreign driving license, converting to a Swiss license is mandatory within one year of obtaining a B or C permit. The process involves the Strassenverkehrsamt Zug (Road Traffic Office), which verifies documents and administers exams. Candidates must pass both the theory and practical exams, except in specific cases. The theory exam can be taken in German, French, Italian, or English, reflecting Switzerland’s multilingual nature. The practical test evaluates driving skills in real-world conditions, with particular attention to safety and local regulations.&#10;&#10;

Required documents&#10;

The application requires a valid passport, B/C residence permit, criminal record from the country of origin (no older than 6 months), passport photo, and original driver’s license. The office verifies the authenticity and validity of these documents before admitting applicants to the exams.&#10;&#10;

Operational details

Practical implications for residents&#10; Driving license conversion in the Canton of Zug is unique compared to other Swiss cantons. While some cantons recognize EU driving licenses without an exam, Zug maintains stringent requirements to ensure high road safety standards. This choice reflects the canton’s cautious approach, known for its low accident statistics (according to FSO/BFS data).&#10;&#10;

Intercantonal comparison&#10; Unlike cantons like Vaud or Geneva, where EU citizens can exchange their driving license without an exam, Zug requires theory and practical tests for all non-Swiss drivers. This uniform policy simplifies administration but requires greater preparation from applicants. Mandatory courses, aligned with federal regulations on road safety, prepare drivers for the specific conditions of Switzerland, such as mountain roads and winter climates.&#10;&#10;

Key points

Step-by-step procedure&#10; 1. Document collection: Obtain a criminal record and translate it into German (the official language of the canton).&#10; 2. Course registration: Attend first aid (10h) and ecology (4h) at authorized centers.&#10; 3. Official application: Send the form to the Strassenverkehrsamt Zug with all the documentation.&#10; 4. Exam booking: Theoretical (at approved centres) and practical (with local instructor).&#10; 5. Withdrawal of driving licence: After passing the exams, collect your Swiss driving licence from the office.&#10;&#10;

Critical deadlines&#10;

  • Start the process at least 4 months before the expiration of your residency year to avoid delays.&#10;
  • Criminal record certificates are valid for a maximum of 6 months: request them promptly.&#10;
  • Waiting lists for practical exams can reach 8 weeks during peak season.&#10;&#10;

Frequently Asked Questions
What documents do I need to convert my driving licence in Zug?
You need: valid passport, B/C permit, apostilled and translated criminal record, photo, original driver's license and certificates of compulsory courses.
Can I take the exams in Italian?
Yes, the theory exam is available in Italian. The practical exam is generally held in German, but instructors can use Italian if necessary.
How much does the conversion of the license cost?
The total costs typically amount to CHF 300-500, including administrative fees (approx. CHF 150), courses (CHF 200) and exams (CHF 100-200).
