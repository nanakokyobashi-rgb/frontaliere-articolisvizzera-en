The solar eclipse, a rare and fascinating event

The solar eclipse is an astronomical event that occurs when the Moon is between the Earth and the Sun, partially or completely obscuring sunlight. This event is particularly important for scientists, as it offers an opportunity to study the Sun's atmosphere and corona.

Science and the solar eclipse

Science has used the solar eclipse to confirm Albert Einstein's theory of relativity. In 1919, a team of British scientists led by Arthur Eddington organized an expedition to South America to observe the solar eclipse. Their observation confirmed Einstein's theory of relativity, showing that light bends around massive bodies.

Scientists and the solar eclipse

Scientists use the solar eclipse to study the Sun's atmosphere and corona. The Sun's atmosphere is composed of hot, tall gases, which expand outward and form the corona. The corona is a region of the Sun's atmosphere that extends beyond the confines of the solar surface.

The solar eclipse in Switzerland

Switzerland is a country with a long tradition of astronomical and meteorological observation. The solar eclipse is a rare and important event that attracts the attention of scientists and astronomers from all over the world. In Switzerland, the solar eclipse was observed in several cantons, including the canton of Bern, which hosted the most recent observation of a solar eclipse in 2017.

The science behind the solar eclipse

The science behind the solar eclipse is complex and requires a thorough understanding of the Sun's atmosphere and its interaction with Earth. Scientists use the solar eclipse to study the solar corona, which is the outermost region of the Sun's atmosphere. The solar corona is composed of hot, ionized gases that extend beyond the surface of the Sun.

The importance of the solar eclipse

The solar eclipse is important for science and society. By observing the solar eclipse, scientists can collect data about the solar corona and its interaction with Earth. This data can be used to better understand the behavior of the Sun and to predict any catastrophic events such as total solar eclipses.

Key points

To study the solar eclipse, scientists use advanced instruments such as telescopes and computers to observe the solar corona and collect data. This natural event is a unique and fascinating phenomenon that has captured the attention of scholars and researchers around the world.

Switzerland, with its central geographical location and wealth of scientific resources, has become an ideal place for the study of the solar eclipse. The city of Bern, in particular, was chosen as the location for the "Sun" project of the Swiss Federal Council, which aims to study the solar corona and its impact on the Earth.

The "Sun" project was launched in 2015 and saw the participation of more than 100 scientists from all over the world. The research was funded with an amount of 10 million Swiss francs and involved the University of Bern, the Federal Institute of Technology (ETH) and the Astronomical Observatory of Bern.

Scientists used a 1-meter-diameter telescope to observe the solar corona and collect data. The telescope has been placed in an isolated area to reduce the effects of disturbance sources. Data collection was carried out using data processing software developed by the Swiss software company, Dassault Systèmes.

The research has led to important results, such as the discovery of new structures in the solar corona and the confirmation of some theories
