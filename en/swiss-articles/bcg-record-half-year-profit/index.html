Operational details

Strength of the Swiss cantonal banking system

Cantonal banks are a backbone of the Swiss financial system. BCG, operating in the Canton of Graubünden, is among the players in this category. Record results such as those of the first half of 2026 signal that the cantonal banking model maintains significant economic vitality, despite global macroeconomic challenges: fluctuating interest rates, volatile inflation, and pressure from digital competition and online banking supply.

The increase in the Cost/Income Ratio to 46.8% does not represent a deterioration in management quality. On the contrary, for every franc of revenues, BCG spends 46.8 cents in operating costs: a ratio that for a regional and cantonal bank indicates managerial efficiency and control of operating costs, even during an investment phase in infrastructure and skills.

Implications for savers and customers

For BCG's private and corporate clients, capital strength (equity of CHF 3.0 billion) is a reassuring indicator. In the Swiss banking system, governed by stringent federal solvency rules, a bank with growing results and robust capital offers favourable conditions for savers and borrowers. Deposits with the BCG enjoy the protection provided by the Federal Deposit Guarantee Act of up to CHF 100,000 per depositor.

Deposit Protection and Guarantees in the Swiss Banking System

All deposits with regularly authorized Swiss banks (including BCG) benefit from a protection of up to 100,000 Swiss francs per person at each institution. This protection is provided by the deposit guarantee system regulated at the federal level and implemented by individual banks or banking consortia. If you deposit 150,000 CHF with BCG, the first 100,000 CHF are guaranteed by the federal protection; the remaining 50,000 CHF do not fall under the coverage.

If you want to protect amounts exceeding 100,000 CHF, you have several options:

  • Distribute your savings: open accounts with different banks (each bank offers protection up to 100,000 CHF per depositor).
  • Investment management instruments: deposit with BCG through investment funds or portfolio management mandates, which offer internal diversification and additional protection based on the underlying securities.
  • Certificates of deposit and bonds: instruments that transform the deposit into a bearer credit, with different risk and return profiles.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does the Cost/Income Ratio at 46.8% of BCG mean?
The Cost/Income Ratio at 46.8% means that BCG spends 46.8 francs per franc of operating revenue. For a cantonal regional bank, it is a favourable ratio and indicates efficient cost management even during an investment phase. Larger banks may have similar or higher ratios.
Does the growth in interest income (+9.3%) lead to better rates for my savings?
Not automatically. BCG's increased interest income reflects a general context of higher interest rates as determined by the Swiss National Bank. If you intend to benefit from these rates, you need to check the current conditions of the BCG and compare them with other banks. Rates vary by savings account, term deposits, and investment products.
Are my BCG deposits protected?
Yes, up to CHF 100,000 per depositor. The Swiss Deposit Guarantee Scheme automatically protects funds deposited with the BCG (and any other authorised bank) up to this limit. If you deposit higher amounts, only the first CHF 100,000 is guaranteed; the remainder has no protection. You can protect larger amounts by distributing funds between different banks.
What does the 2026 profit forecast above 2025 mean for BCG's customers?
A bank that makes increasing profits is generally able to invest more in infrastructure, technology, and personnel. This can translate into improved services, more advanced banking apps, and stronger customer support. In addition, a robust bank is a reliable partner for those who have financing needs (mortgages, trade credits, asset management).
How can I compare BCG's offers with other Swiss banks?
Collect current data from at least 2-3 cantonal banks and Swiss commercial banks: contact the branches directly, visit the websites, or use online tools to compare economic conditions.
