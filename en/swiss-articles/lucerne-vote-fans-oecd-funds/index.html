Lucerne: vote on fans, shops and OECD funds

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 28 September 2026

In Lucerne, both the initiative and the counterproposal on fan violence pass. 58.8% choose the counterproposal; the go-ahead is also given to shops, schools and economic promotion.

In Lucerne, voters today decided on two proposals created to reduce violence linked to FC Lucerne matches.

The double yes on fan violence

The first was the popular initiative of the Centre, entitled «Against fan violence», calling for stricter measures. The second was the counter-proposal drafted by the Grand Council.

According to Keystone-ATS, both texts were approved. The initiative obtained 52.8%, while the counter-proposal reached 74.8%. The result did not stop at the double yes: in the subsidiary question, which asked which of the two proposals to prioritise, Lucerne voters chose the counter-proposal with 58.8% of the votes. Turnout was 48.7%.

The difference between the two approaches was at the heart of the debate. The legislative text focuses more on prevention, dialogue, greater club responsibility and video surveillance. It instead forgoes identity checks, which would require national coordination.

The Centre had criticised this approach. In its view, it would have enshrined the current practice in law without introducing real change. The government and the other parties replied that the initiative only addressed some aspects, was not coordinated with national efforts and therefore would not have been effective.

The vote also covered three decisions on different local matters. Voters approved with 55.2% the extension of opening hours for unstaffed shops, with 67.6% the expansion of the cantonal school of Reussbühl, and with 65.1% the economic promotion financed by 250 million annually from the OECD minimum tax. Operational details

An approach based on prevention and coordination

The practical significance of the vote on fan violence emerges from the comparison between the two paths submitted to the voters. The Centre's initiative called for tougher measures. The counter-project of the Grand Council, chosen in the subsidiary application, instead focuses action on prevention, dialogue, greater club responsibility and video surveillance. This is the concrete difference described by the source: not just a numerical result, but a different way of intervening.
