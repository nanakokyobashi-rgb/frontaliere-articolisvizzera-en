Tax incentive for local purchases in Ticino: proposal

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 25 September 2026

The Centre offers a tax credit of up to 25% for those who allocate up to 5% of their annual salary or income to participating Ticino merchants.

On Friday 25 September, in Giubiasco, the Center and the Youth of Centro Ticino presented a generic parliamentary initiative to strengthen Ticino trade. The proposal would allow citizens to voluntarily allocate a portion of their salary to a scheme that can only be used by participating Ticino merchants, in exchange for a possible cantonal tax credit.

The project was illustrated by MP Sabrina Gendotti, Giovanna Pedroni and Luca Taglialatela, respectively president and vice-president of the Presidential Youth Office of Centro Ticino. The declared objective is to support trade and businesses in the canton, retaining a greater share of purchasing power in Ticino.

The hypothesized operation passes through an electronic card or a digital wallet. An employee may allocate a limited portion of their salary to it; an independent person, a share of their income. The money could only be spent at Ticino merchants participating in the system. The refundable cantonal tax credit would be recognized on the amounts actually used, in compatibility with the financial sustainability of the measure.

Why the Center proposes the circuit

Giovanna Pedroni recalled the difficulties of local trade and numerous small and medium-sized businesses. In his reading, the digitalisation of consumption, cross-border purchases and international online platforms are changing habits. The proposal therefore seeks a voluntary instrument, without obligations or limitations on the freedom of choice of citizens and businesses.

The Center also cites the economic data for May 2026: the Ticino retail trade has recorded a worsening of the business and sales situation. Among the possible factors, the press release recalls competition from cross-border purchases and online commerce, attributing the reference to the cantonal statistics office.

Taglialatela presented the quantitative hypotheses: employees could allocate up to 5% of their gross annual salary, while self-employed workers could allocate up to 5% of their annual income for AVS purposes, with a maximum of 5,000 francs per year per person. As an indication, the credit could reach up to 25% of the sums used through the circuit. The scenarios remain theoretical: their impact would depend on the number of accessions and the amounts actually allocated to the system.

The initiative asks the Council of State to develop a cantonal legislative proposal and to define operational methods, financial sustainability, membership criteria, controls and legal compatibility. To follow the tax topic, the reader can consult the tax return.

Operational details

The effect will depend on actual use

The practical point of the proposal is the link between the allocated share, the expenditure actually made and any tax benefit. The text links the credit to the amounts actually used at participating Ticino merchants. For those evaluating membership, this distinction separates the sum loaded onto the card or wallet from that which can contribute to possible recognition.

For an employee, the reference base is the gross annual salary; for a self-employed person it is the annual income that is decisive for AVS purposes. The choice remains voluntary and does not change the freedom to purchase elsewhere. The proposal does not impose a place of shopping: it encourages the use of the circuit without turning it into an obligation.

The role of employers adds a second possibility. The initiative imagines that they can contribute to the loading of the card or wallet, or offer it as an optional company benefit. Therefore, any participation of the company is not described as automatic and remains distinct from the individual decision of the worker.

On the commercial side, the result depends on the presence of participating merchants and the frequency with which the sums are spent. The circuit is designed to support local trade, SMEs, catering and the Ticino economy. The promoters themselves define purely theoretical scenarios and postpone the actual impact to the number of participants and the amounts used.

However, the practical analysis stops in front of three conditions still to be regulated: financial sustainability of the credit, traceability of operations and prevention of abuse. The proposal also refers to compatibility with federal law, economic freedom and the principle of proportionality. These are criteria that must be included in the legislative proposal requested from the Council of State, together with the operating methods, controls and membership criteria.
