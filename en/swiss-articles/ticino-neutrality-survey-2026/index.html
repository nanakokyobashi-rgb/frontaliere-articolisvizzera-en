The first 20-minute survey/Tamedia on the two initiatives on the ballot on 27 September 2026 reveals a clear opposition of the Swiss population to both proposals. For the neutrality initiative, 62% are against the requested change, while only 36% support it. The remaining 2% remains undecided. The initiative on food has even less consensus: 31% in favour against 65% against, with 4% undecided.

But the most surprising fact concerns the regional differences. While German Switzerland and Romanian Switzerland clearly reject both initiatives, Ticino stands out from the rest of the country on the issue of neutrality: in our canton, the initiative would pass with 52% approval, against 45% against. Operational details

Ticino against the rest of Switzerland

The Ticino result on the neutrality initiative represents a resounding exception in the Swiss landscape. This gap highlights a Röstigraben that is not only linguistic but also political on a central issue for Switzerland. Proponents of the neutrality initiative argue that enshrining neutrality in the Constitution would prevent the Federal Council and politicians from weakening or interpreting it at their own discretion. They see a stricter constitutional wording as a guarantee against gradual changes in Swiss foreign policy.

Those who oppose the initiative, on the other hand, put forward arguments based on the Swiss tradition of openness and dialogue. According to the opponents, the initiative would severely limit Switzerland's ability to act in the face of global foreign policy challenges. Renouncing sanctions in the event of violations of international law could be seen as taking a stand in favor of aggressor states and damage Switzerland's reputation in the world. Finally, a rigid conception of neutrality would entail the sacrifice of cooperation in matters of security and defense, weakening the country's defensive capacity.

Food supply: less divided, but still rejected

The food supply initiative shows a radically different situation. 65% of respondents oppose it, while 31% support the proposal and 4% remain undecided. Unlike the neutrality issue, no significant Röstigraben emerges: the entire national electorate moves with relative uniformity in its rejection. German-speaking Switzerland expresses 70% 'no' (27% 'yes'), French-speaking Switzerland 58% 'no' (37% 'yes'), and Ticino 50% 'no' (45% 'yes').

Key points

What it means to vote on September 27

The federal vote on 27 September 2026 represents a crucial appointment to define the future course of Switzerland on two fronts: foreign policy and food sovereignty. On neutrality, the central question is whether to maintain neutrality in its current form — already enshrined in the Federal Constitution since 1848 — or to insert in the Federal Constitution an article imposing a more restrictive practice. It would be a clear change of course.

The referendum does not call into question the future neutrality of Switzerland, but how to apply it in the future. The vote on neutrality therefore takes place in a context where, according to the survey data, a clear national majority intends to remain on the current line, except for Ticino, which positions itself differently.

How to prepare and decide

Before you go to the polls on 27 September, make sure you have consulted the official materials of the Federal Chancellery and reflected on what values and principles you want Switzerland to uphold in the coming period. You can learn more about the official details of the initiatives by visiting the website of the Swiss Federal Chancellery.

Frequently Asked Questions
When do we vote on the initiative on neutrality and nutrition?
The vote is scheduled for 27 September 2026. The first 20 minutes/Tamedia poll, conducted between 12 and 13 August 2026, shows that 62% of Swiss are against the neutrality initiative, while 36% support it. For food, 65% are opposed (31% favourable). In Ticino, the result on neutrality is the opposite: 52% vote yes, the only canton where the initiative would have a majority.
What exactly does the neutrality initiative ask for?

Why does Ticino vote differently from the rest of Switzerland on neutrality?
The survey does not provide an in-depth analysis of the reasons for the Ticino gap, but a political and linguistic Röstigraben on this issue clearly emerges. While German Switzerland (64% no) and French Switzerland (61% no) reject the initiative, Ticino positions itself in the opposite way with 52% in favour. The differences could relate to different perceptions of Switzerland's safety, tradition and international role between linguistic regions.
How many people took part in the survey?
The 20 minutes/Tamedia survey involved 13,154 participants between 12 and 13 August 2026, with processing between 13 and 17 August. The data were weighted according to demographic, geographical and political variables. The sample included 9,961 respondents from German Switzerland, 2,936 from French Switzerland, and 257 from Ticino. The margin of error is ±1.5 percentage points.
Does the nutrition initiative have the same divergent results on a regional basis?
No. The initiative on food does not show a significant Röstigraben: the entire national electorate moves with relative uniformity in the rejection. 65% of Swiss oppose (31% in favour, 4% undecided). German Switzerland expresses 70% no (27% yes), French Switzerland 58% no (37% yes), and Ticino 50% no (45% yes). The only group that mostly supports the initiative is the Green electorate with 69% in favour.
