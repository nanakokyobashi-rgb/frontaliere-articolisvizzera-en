Demonstration in Bern for migrants' rights

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 September 2026

Several thousand people demonstrated in Bern against the tightening of asylum law, calling for fundamental rights and freedom of movement.

Several thousand people took part today in Bern in a demonstration against the tightening of asylum law and the exclusion of people without Swiss passports. The demonstrators called for fundamental rights to be respected for everyone, without distinctions based on residence status or origin.

The demands also included full freedom of movement and the possibility of participating in social and political life. The march thus linked the asylum debate to the everyday situation of people living in Switzerland without Swiss passports.

A mobilization supported by many organizations

The demonstration was called by Solidarité sans frontières, an association based in Bern that advocates for asylum and migrants, together with around one hundred other organizations, trade unions, and civil society groups. The march took place under the slogan «No borders between us».

The participants crossed the city center before reaching Federal Square. Along the way, they chanted slogans such as «Long live solidarity without borders», as observed by a reporter from the Keystone-Ats news agency.

In a press release, Solidarité sans frontières spoke of profound changes ahead in migration policy. The association criticized Asylum Strategy 2027, the UDC initiative for border protection, and the reform of the Common European Asylum System.

The flyer distributed during the demonstration read: «We defend our fundamental rights against authoritarian tendencies». The organizers described migration as a normal phenomenon that existed in the past and is set to continue. They also called for an open and diverse society, urging people not to let themselves be divided.

The final part of the demands concerns deportations, regularization, and the immediate closure of detention centers for deportation. According to the demonstrators, the fundamental rights to be guaranteed to everyone include access to healthcare, education, and work, as well as legal protection regardless of residence status.

The second concerns freedom of movement, presented as an alternative to borders. The third touches on participation in social and political life. Taken together, these demands broaden the issue beyond the asylum procedure alone.
