Swiss fleeing overseas: the cost of the house weighs (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 August 2026

Over 154,000 Swiss live in foreign border areas: a 14% increase driven by unsustainable real estate prices and steadily growing rents.

Context

In a nutshell

  • 154,700 Swiss citizens now reside in foreign border areas
  • There has been a 14% increase in the phenomenon in the last seven years
  • France is the main destination, followed by Germany and Italy

Key facts

  • What: Emigration of Swiss citizens to border regions
  • When: Data collected between 2017 and 2025
  • Where: Haute-Savoie, Ain, Freiburg, Como, Varese, Liechtenstein
  • Who: Federal Statistical Office (FSO)
  • Amount: 14% increase in Swiss residents abroad

The abandonment of the Confederation by its citizens is assuming the contours of a structural phenomenon. According to data processed by the Federal Statistical Office (FSO), the number of Swiss who chose to settle just across the border reached 154,700. This is a population of "reverse border workers" who, while often maintaining their centre of work interests in Switzerland, prefer to reside in neighbouring countries such as France, Germany, Italy and Liechtenstein.

The routes of Swiss emigration

The territorial analysis shows a significant concentration in the Grand Genève region. More than 50,000 Swiss now live in the department of Haute-Savoie alone, with an increase of 5,189 people between 2017 and 2025. The Ain department follows with over 18'000 residents (+1'603). Moving to German Switzerland, the Freiburg district in Germany has 18,017 residents

Operational details

The main driver of this shift is not sporadic events, but persistent economic pressure on the domestic real estate market. Stéphanie Périllard, Consul General of Switzerland in Lyon, points out that the progression of the phenomenon is slow but continuous. At the heart of the problem is costo della vita and, specifically, the inaccessibility of property and rental prices in Switzerland.

The pressure of the Roman real estate market

A recent study conducted by Comparis confirms the seriousness of the situation, especially in French-speaking Switzerland. Data indicate that around 70% of respondents believe that the price of renting or buying in their region of residence is excessively expensive. This perception translates into concrete intentions: 30% of Romani residents seriously consider moving to France to find more sustainable housing solutions.

Becoming a homeowner in many Swiss regions is now considered out of reach for a significant share of the population. As rents continue to rise, the option of crossing the border becomes a financial survival strategy for many families. Diplomat Périllard reiterates that this is not a "sudden explosion", but a constant trend that reflects the difficulty of reconciling salaries, albeit high, with the claims of the Swiss real estate market. Key points

Anyone who decides to take this path must consider various bureaucratic and fiscal aspects. Although one becomes a resident abroad, the link to Switzerland often remains strong through work. It is essential to manage one's position correctly to avoid sanctions or administrative complications. The first step is to notify the transfer to the Swiss municipality of residence and register with the Swiss Abroad Register at the competent consulate.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the main cause of the transfer of Swiss abroad?
The main cause is economic and specifically concerns the real estate market. According to Stéphanie Périllard, Consul General in Lyon, rental and property prices in Switzerland have a considerable impact. A study by Comparis reveals that 70% of Romanians find prices in their region too expensive, prompting 30% to consider a move to France to find more affordable accommodation.
How many Swiss citizens live in foreign border areas today?
Currently there are 154,700 Swiss citizens residing in a border region abroad, acting as a "reverse border". This figure, provided by the Federal Statistical Office (FSO), shows an increase of 14% over just seven years, demonstrating a constant and unstoppable phenomenon linked to the search for lower housing costs.
What are the Italian provinces with the most Swiss residents?
The province of Como tops the Italian ranking with 4,430 Swiss residents, registering an increase of 729 units. Varese is followed by 3,106 residents (+683). Other affected areas include Verbano Cusio-Ossola (1,205 residents), Bolzano (1,067), Sondrio (951) and the Liechtenstein region which has 5,600 Swiss residents.
