The Swiss system: no federal minimum wage

Switzerland is one of the few European countries without a mandatory federal minimum wage. This means that the Canton of Schaffhausen, like every other canton, determines autonomously whether and what minimum wage to set. Some Swiss cantons have introduced their own cantonal minimum wage; others do not. Salary determination follows criteria related to professional training, experience, and collective labor agreements (CCL), which regulate salaries for sector and profession.

In Switzerland, the labor market recognizes three main levels of education: vocational training with a federal certificate (EFA), high school diploma (which allows access to university), and university education (bachelor and master). Each level corresponds to different salary scales. A professional with EFA earns on average less than someone with a high school diploma, who in turn earns less than a degree holder. The differences are not fixed but vary by sector.

Operational details

Salary differences by level of education and sector

The Swiss job market rewards education, but the starting salary of a new hire also depends on the sector. Manufacturing, finance, technology, and healthcare tend to offer higher salaries. Retail, hospitality, and agriculture generally pay less. In Schaffhausen, as in the rest of Switzerland, traditional economic sectors (industry, craftsmanship) remain important, and wages follow national levels for their branch.

A generic scenario illustrates the differences: a new hire with a Federal Vocational Baccalaureate (FVB) in the manufacturing sector receives a base salary linked to the collective labor agreement (CLA) of their branch, possibly increased by performance bonuses. Those who have completed a high school diploma can access administrative or technical positions with higher salaries. A university graduate generally starts with an even higher salary, although this depends on the specialization and sector. However, these differences are not automatic: a graduate in a poorly paid sector (e.g., cultural animation) might earn less than a specialized FVB technician in a high-demand sector (e.g., construction).

Key points

Informarsi sui salari: dove cercare

Il primo passo per chi entra nel mercato del lavoro svizzero è comprendere la gamma salariale del proprio settore e della propria regione. Esistono diverse risorse pubbliche gratuite:

1. Portali statistici federali (UST/BFS): L'Ufficio federale di statistica pubblica dati annuali su salari medi per branca economica, cantone e livello di formazione. Questi dati sono liberi e riportano la situazione reale del mercato.

2. Contratti collettivi: I CCL sono pubblicati da associazioni sindacali e datoriali. Consultarli prima di accettare un'offerta di lavoro permette di verificare se lo stipendio proposto è conforme ai standard negoziali della branca.

3. Uffici cantonali del lavoro: Nel Canton Sciaffusa, come in ogni cantone, esiste un ufficio cantonale competente per il mercato del lavoro. Questi uffici forniscono informazioni su vigenza di contratti collettivi, salari minimi cantonali (se esistono) e diritti dei lavoratori.

4. Portale nazionale SECO: La Segreteria di Stato dell'economia (SECO) pubblica una banca dati di contratti collettivi vigenti. È possibile cercare per cantone e settore per verificare i termini in vigore.

Procedura per valutare un'offerta di lavoro

Quando ricevi un'offerta di lavoro in Svizzera, segui questi passaggi:

  • Controlla lo stipendio lordo proposto contro i dati UST/BFS e il CCL vigente per il settore.
  • Chiedi una simulazione della busta paga netta usando un calcolatore (disponibile sul sito) per capire realmente quanto percepirai considerando tutti i contributi.
  • Verifica i termini di disdetta: il contratto deve rispettare i termini legali (1, 2 o 3 mesi secondo il periodo di impiego già completato).
  • Leggi con attenzione il contratto per verificare benefit aggiuntivi (orari, flessibilità, bonus, giorni di congedo extra).

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum wage in Schaffhausen?
Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage. The Canton of Schaffhausen determines independently whether to establish its own cantonal minimum wage. The salaries are mainly regulated by collective agreements (CCL) negotiated between trade unions and employers' associations for each sector. How does salary vary based on training?
The Swiss labour market recognizes three levels of training: federal certificate (AFC), high school graduation, and university education. On average, those with an AFC earn less than those who have completed a degree, which in turn earns less than a college graduate. However, industry and experience greatly influence pay scales. An AFC specialist technician in a required sector (e.g. construction) could earn more than a graduate in a less lucrative sector.
What are the main contributions deducted from the paycheck?
The main contributions are: AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3% of gross salary (total payment with employer: 10.6%), unemployment insurance (AD) 1.1% up to annual ceiling, accident insurance (LAA) 0.7-1.5% by sector. Occupational pension (LPP) varies with age: 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54), 18% (55+ up to the reference age). Health insurance is private, not deducted in the paycheck. All these amounts are indexed annually.
How do I check if a salary is compliant?
Compare the offer against three sources: UST/BFS (Federal Statistical Office) data for the sector and canton, the collective agreement in force for your branch (available in the SECO database), and union consultants or cantonal labour offices. These tools allow you to understand if the offer is in the market range and complies with the negotiating terms of your sector.
What is the maximum working time in Switzerland?
The Federal Labor Law sets the maximum hours at 45 hours per week for the industrial sector, offices and sales, and at 50 hours for other sectors. The minimum holiday is four weeks a year for adults, rising to five weeks until the age of twenty. These limits apply in all cantons, including Schaffhausen.
