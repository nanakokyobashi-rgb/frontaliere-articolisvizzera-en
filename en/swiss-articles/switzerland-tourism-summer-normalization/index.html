Switzerland Tourism, summer demand is normalizing (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 16 September 2026

The August data confirm a slow normalization of tourism demand in the Confederation, with declines from the Gulf and Asia and an internal boom.

Context

In brief

  • August survey by the Federal Statistical Office
  • 2.1% decrease in overnight stays compared to last year
  • Increase in demand from Swiss, European, and American guests
  • Sharp drop in visitors from Gulf countries and Asia

Key facts

Organization: Switzerland Tourism Reference period: August and summer (June-August) Overnight stays variation: 2.1% decrease compared to last year Growing markets: Switzerland, Europe, America Declining markets: Gulf countries, Asia

Data on overnight stays recorded in August indicate a slow normalization of tourist demand in the country. This is Switzerland Tourism's official reaction to the information published in the afternoon by the Federal Statistical Office, which specifically certifies a 2.1% decrease in overnight stays compared to the same period of the previous year. The tourism promotion organization summarizes that the need for security and stability, together with repeated heatwaves, led to an increase in demand for summer holidays—between the months of June and August—in the Confederation by Swiss, European, and American guests. This flow largely compensated for the sharp drop in visitors coming from Gulf countries and Asia. The overall picture emerging from these surveys is therefore decidedly varied.

Analysis of tourist flows

Switzerland Tourism experts highlight how the situation differs according to the various geographical regions of the national territory. While Graubünden or Zermatt clearly benefited from the combination of a scorching summer and a particular focus on the domestic market, destinations like Geneva or Interlaken were heavily affected by the absence of guests belonging to the Arab and Asian middle class. Supporting the summer season was the phenomenon of Nahweh, meaning the trend of preferring nearby destinations, which was clearly observable in these months although not uniformly across the entire country. Operational details

The analysis of data released by the Federal Statistical Office allows for a detailed examination of the performance of the hotel industry and tourist reception during the past summer season. The 2.1% drop in overnight stays in August compared to the previous year is not an isolated figure, but rather part of a broader context of adjustment following the dynamics recorded in past years. Domestic and European tourism has shown remarkable resilience, driven by the search for stable and safe destinations in the face of the heatwaves that characterized the summer months from June to August.

The impact on the accommodation sector

Th disparity among various Swiss destinations highlights the vulnerability of certain areas heavily dependent on intercontinental flows, particularly tourists from the Gulf countries and Asia. Locations historically attractive to this specific middle-class clientele have recorded significant contractions, which were not entirely offset by proximity customers. Conversely, mountain resorts such as Zermatt and the Canton of Grisons benefited from the preference given by Swiss travelers and neighboring European countries. This dynamic underscores the importance of diversifying the tourism offering to cope with fluctuations in foreign markets and to best leverage local territorial resources. Key points

The summer season therefore closes with conflicting signals that require operators in the national tourism sector to carefully plan strategically for the future. Promotional bodies and individual accommodation facilities are called upon to constantly monitor the evolution of the source markets, balancing promotion on the internal and neighbourhood market with the recovery of long-haul markets. The tendency to privilege nearby destinations offers concrete opportunities for alpine and extra-urban destinations, while large cities must rethink their strategies of attractiveness to intercept new international flows.

Outlook and economic planning

To cope with changes in demand, economic operators in the Swiss tourism and non-hotel sector must carefully consider the trend in operating costs and personnel management. The general economic context of the Confederation, characterized by specific wage and tax balances, requires constant attention to the competitiveness of prices and services offered.
