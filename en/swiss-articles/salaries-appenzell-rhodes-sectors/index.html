Wages and the labour market in Appenzello Interna (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 August 2026

Guide to salary levels by level of training, employment sectors, AVS/LPP/LAMal contributions, legal hours and tools to calculate net salary in the Inner Appenzell canton.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Inner Appenzello: mixed economy (agriculture, textile and machinery industry, services, health)
  • Salary levels vary from unqualified (CHF 2800–3000/month gross) to graduates (CHF 8000+/month)
  • Mandatory federal contributions: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3%, AD/AC 1.1%, private LAMal, LPP 7–18%
  • Max hours 45 h/sept (industry, offices) or 50 h (other sectors); minimum 4 weeks annual holiday

Key facts

  • What: Cantonal labour market with three economic sectors and four degrees of training
  • Where: Inner Appenzell Canton, north-eastern Switzerland
  • Training: Compulsory school, AFC (apprenticeship 3–4 years), specialized school, university
  • Contributions: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% (employee), LPP 7–18% according to age group (25–34 years: 7%; 55+: 18%)
  • Hours: Maximum 45 hours/week (industry, offices, sales) or 50 hours (other sectors)
  • Holidays: Minimum 4 weeks per year (5 for those under 20)
  • Minimum wage: Non-existent at the federal level; canton has not yet established a general

The Inner Appenzell canton, like the other 25 cantons of the Swiss Confederation, structures its labour market around three complementary economic sectors. Operational details

Training and impact on net wages: practical scenarios The leverage of training broadly determines the net income received. A worker who completes only compulsory schooling accesses unskilled positions (general auxiliary, unskilled worker, basic clerk): the gross salary is the lowest. However, any Swiss salary — regardless of level — is subject to the same federal contributions in percentage (AVS/AI/IPG 5.3%, AD/AC 1.1%, mandatory LAMal, LPP if contracted) and federal/cantonal/municipal taxes. It follows that the net is approx. 55–65% of the gross for low incomes, because the fixed percentages affect more heavily. An apprentice who completes the AFC (16–19 years old) enters the market as a technician, skilled worker, craftsman, experienced salesman. The gross salary is higher than the unqualified because the qualification is federally recognized and is transportable between companies and cantons. The same amount of federal contributions (5.3% AVS, 1.1% AD/AC) continues to apply, but the basis of calculation is higher; consequently the absolute net increases, although the deduction percentage remains the same. In addition, the employer has an incentive to keep the employee qualified. Those who attend a specialized school (technicum, business school, nursing school) reach an intermediate level: advanced technical skills without a full academic degree. Key points

How to navigate the market: search, salary calculation, procedure

Job search in Switzerland follows standardized procedures, applicable also in Appenzell Innerrhoden. The main job listing platforms are: job.ch, indeed.ch, linkedin.com (geographic filter "Switzerland"), swisstalents.ch for academic profiles. Many employers publish directly on their corporate websites. In the public sector (cantonal administration, municipalities, public hospitals), announcements are on the official public administration portal.

### Step-by-step: from search to first paycheck

Step 1: Define the sought profile Choose the sector (primary, secondary, tertiary) and the level of education required (compulsory schooling, apprenticeship, specialized school, university). Consult the job announcement databases in your sector and with the desired qualification level.

Step 2: Assess the gross salary offered Many Swiss job listings indicate the salary range (e.g., "CHF 4500–5500/month gross"). If not specified, consult official sources:

  • UST/BFS (Federal Statistical Office, bfs.admin.ch): Official statistics of average salaries by economic sector, education level, sector, age group. These are the national reference data.
  • Salary Calculator: Enter the offered gross salary and instantly obtain the net amount, deductions, taxes, LAMal estimate, LPP.
  • SECO: The federal labor agency maintains guidelines on working conditions and wages by sector.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between gross salary and net salary in Switzerland?
The gross salary is the figure in the contract. The net salary is what you receive after deductions: AHV/IV/EO 5.3%, ALV/AC 1.1%, federal/cantonal/municipal tax, KVG (private premiums), and BVG if the contract provides for it. Typically net is 55–70% of the gross, varying by income and canton. Use a [calcolatore stipendio](nav:calculator) to simulate your case.
Is there a minimum wage in Switzerland?
No, there is no mandatory federal minimum wage. Some cantons (Geneva, Ticino specific sectors, Valais) have regional minimums; Appenzell Innerrhoden does not yet have a general one. The salary is determined by the individual agreement or collective agreement (CEA) of the sector. Consult SECO to check if your sector has an applicable CEA.
How much do health insurance premiums (LAMal) cost in Switzerland?
KVG premiums vary by canton, age, gender, deductible. In 2026, the national average is approx. CHF 200–350/month adults, but Appenzell Innerrhoden as an Alpine canton generally has low to medium premiums compared to urban cantons. Compare insurers on Comparis or myCoverage for your exact profile.
What are the AVS/AI/IPG and AD/AC contributions on salary?
These are mandatory federal deductions. AHV/IV/EO = 5.3% of salary (first pillar: national pension + disability). ALV/AC = 1.1% up to a maximum of approx. CHF 148,000 gross per year (unemployment). They are automatically deducted from your payroll every month. The employer pays an equal or higher amount.
How does the salary change for different degrees of training?
In Switzerland, education is the main discriminating factor. Unqualified: approx. CHF 2500–3000/month gross. EFT (apprenticeship): CHF 3500–4500. Specialist school: CHF 4500–5500. University (bachelor's/master's): CHF 6000–10000+. Federal contributions remain constant as a percentage, but the salary base increases significantly, so the absolute net increases significantly.
