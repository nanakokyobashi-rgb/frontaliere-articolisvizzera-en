Opening a business in Neuchâtel: costs and trade register (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 September 2026

Legal forms, registration in the trade register, social security contributions and taxes. Practical guide to starting a business in the canton.

Context

In brief

  • Federal register; mandatory registration for sole proprietorships and companies
  • Legal forms: sole proprietorship, simple company, SA (CHF 100,000), Sagl (CHF 20,000)
  • Mandatory contributions: AVS/AI/LPP; LAMal within 3 months from start of activity

Key facts

  • What: Choice of legal form (sole proprietorship, simple company, SA, Sagl/SARL) and registration with the federal commercial register
  • Where: Canton of Neuchâtel; procedure managed by cantonal offices according to federal standards
  • Who: Every entrepreneur, business owner, commercial company
  • Amount: SA requires a minimum capital of CHF 100,000; Sagl/SARL CHF 20,000; sole proprietorship without federal minimum
  • When: Before starting the activity; mandatory social security (AVS/AI) from the first day
  • Main obligations: Social security (AVS/AI/LPP), LAMal, taxes 3 levels (federal + cantonal + municipal)

Starting a business in the Canton of Neuchâtel requires following Swiss federal rules on commercial law, social security, and taxation. The procedure begins with choosing the legal form most suitable for the type of business.

The sole proprietorship is the simplest form: it only requires registration with the commercial register, no federal minimum capital, and the owner assumes unlimited liability for business debts. It is ideal for freelancers, consultants, and small entrepreneurs. A simple company (Soc. sem.) requires at least two partners, does not impose a minimum capital, but all partners are jointly and severally liable for the company's obligations.

For traders and craftsmen, a general partnership (SNC) works similarly, while a limited partnership combines general partners (unlimited liability) and limited partners (liability limited to their capital).

Capital companies offer a separation between personal and business liability. A public limited company (SA) requires a minimum capital of CHF 100,000, at least one shareholder, and deposited statutes. Ideal for medium to large-sized businesses with a more formal structure. A limited liability company (Sagl) or Société à responsabilité limitée (SARL) has a minimum capital of CHF 20,000, less rigid management than an SA, and is the popular choice among Swiss SMEs.

Registration with the federal commercial register

The commercial register is managed at the cantonal level but follows uniform federal standards. Registration is mandatory for sole proprietorships and all companies. The application requires: valid ID, residence declaration, articles of association (for companies), list of partners/managers, and any certificates of good conduct according to the canton. Processing times vary from 5-7 days to 2-3 weeks. Registration and administration fees specific to Neuchâtel are not standardized at the federal level; the Canton of Neuchâtel applies its own rates. We recommend contacting the Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Neuchâtel directly for current amounts and exact cantonal procedures.

Operational details

Anyone starting a business in Switzerland immediately faces social security and tax obligations, regardless of the canton. These fixed costs are standardized at the federal level, with cantonal variations for taxes and insurance premiums.

Frequently Asked Questions
What legal form is appropriate for a small business in Neuchâtel?
A **Sagl (Limited Warranty Company)** offers the best balance for SMEs: minimum capital CHF 20,000, limited liability to members, less bureaucratic management of the SA, lower registration fee. A **sole proprietorship** is even easier if you are self-employed with no liability to third parties. The SA (CHF 100,000) is only suitable for larger structures with significant investments.
How much does it cost to register in Neuchâtel?
Tuition fees vary by canton - there is no federal standard. The Canton of Neuchâtel applies its own tariffs (different from other cantons). Contact the Neuchâtel Cantonal Trade Register Office directly or visit the official canton Neuchâtel website for current quotes and exact amounts.
What social security contributions do I pay if I have the first employees?
For each employee, you pay 5.3% of AVS/AI/IPG as an employer (the employee pays another 5.3%), plus 1.1% of unemployment insurance (up to the annual ceiling), and 7–18% of LPP according to age group (7% for 25–34 years, 10% for 35–44, 15% for 45–54, 18% for 55+). Accident insurance (0.7-1.5%) depends on the sector.
Am I obliged to insure myself at LAMal immediately when I open the business?
Yes. Every resident starting a business must enroll in mandatory health insurance within 3 months of starting. Neuchâtel has regional coffers with premiums ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 + per year of excess, plus monthly premiums according to case, age and coverage model.
To whom do I pay AVS/AI payments if I am self-employed without employees?
You register with the competent Territorial Compensation Fund (according to your canton of residence). You pay 5.3% of AVS/AI on your declared net income, with payments usually quarterly or annual according to the instructions of the Fund. You will receive expiration notifications after signing up.
