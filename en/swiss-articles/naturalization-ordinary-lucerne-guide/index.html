Lucerne naturalisation: 10 years and double counting (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 August 2026

Permit C and 10 years of residency are the federal requirement. Periods between 8–18 years count double. Discover procedures, times and costs for naturalisation in Lucerne.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Ordinary naturalization requires a C permit and at least 10 years of residence in Switzerland
  • Periods between 8–18 years count double in the calculation of total residence
  • Each canton and municipality sets additional language, integration and duration requirements
  • Procedures and timing vary significantly between cantons and municipalities

Key facts

  • What: Access to Swiss citizenship through ordinary naturalization
  • Who: Foreign holders of permit C (domicile)
  • Where: Procedures managed by municipal and cantonal authorities (including Lucerne)
  • Basic federal requirement: Permit C + 10 years of residence (with the possibility of double counting)
  • Double counting: Periods between 8–18 years of age multiplied by two for calculation purposes
  • Variable conditions: Language, integration, duration of cantonal/municipal residence (specific for canton Lucerne in the definition phase)

The federal regulatory framework states that anyone who holds a C permit (residence permit) and has resided in Switzerland for at least 10 years can apply for ordinary naturalization. However, this federal minimum requirement does not exhaust the process: each canton has the right to introduce stricter rules, and the same applies to the municipalities that administer the process at the territorial level.

Operational details

# Residence years accumulation mechanism

The residence counting system for the purposes of ordinary naturalization is structured according to the age of the resident: periods between 8 and 18 years old count twice, while all other periods (from birth to 8 years old and from 18 years old onwards) count once. The total required remains fixed at the federal level: 10 years equivalent.

A hypothetical scenario clarifies the mechanism: a person residing in Switzerland from the age of 15 to the age of 30 (15 linear years) accumulates residence calculated as follows: from 15 to 18 years = 3 years × 2 = 6 equivalent years; from 18 to 30 years = 12 years × 1 = 12 equivalent years; total = 18 equivalent years. This person reaches the threshold of 10 equivalent years before turning 30 thanks to the double counting bonus, despite having resided linearly for 15 years in Switzerland.

This mechanism has an important practical implication: it allows those who arrived in Switzerland during adolescence or early youth to accumulate the necessary years in less time than those who arrive later. A forty-year-old who moves to Switzerland will have all the years counted only once and will have to wait for the tenth year of actual residence; on the contrary, those who move at 15 will have an advantage in the calculation.

Key points

Step-by-Step Procedure for Ordinary Naturalization

The path towards ordinary naturalization follows standardized phases at the cantonal level, although with administrative variations from municipality to municipality.

Phase 1: Verification of Status and Prerequisites Before contacting the authorities, the candidate verifies their permit status (must be C, i.e., domicile permit) and calculates equivalent years of residence according to the double counting system for periods between 8–18 years. Residence is counted from the date of first settlement in Switzerland, regardless of the canton or municipality of initial arrival.

Phase 2: Contact with the Municipal Naturalization Office The candidate contacts the naturalization office of the municipality of residence to receive: the complete list of required documents (extracts from residence registers, certificates of non-registration in the criminal record, valid identity documents); specific language and integration requirements of Canton Lucerne and the municipality; information on municipal and cantonal fees; an estimate of the average duration of the procedure in the specific municipality.

Phase 3: Collection and Preparation of Documentation Typically required are: passport or valid identity document; recent residence certificate; criminal record certificate; proof of integration (employment contract, registrations in local associations, completion of language courses, certificates of civic participation); application form legally signed.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the minimum federal requirements for ordinary naturalization?
The federal rule requires a C permit (residence permit) and at least 10 years of residence in Switzerland. However, periods of residence between 8 and 18 years of age count double in the calculation, allowing the requirement of 10 equivalent years to be achieved in less time if residence began during adolescence. Each canton and municipality may introduce additional requirements with respect to the federal minimum.
How does double counting of residency periods work?
Periods between 8 and 18 years of age are multiplied by two in the total calculation of equivalent years. For example, those who reside in Switzerland from 15 to 30 years accumulate: from 15 to 18 years = 3 years × 2 = 6 equivalent years; from 18 to 30 years = 12 years × 1 = 12 equivalent years; total = 18 equivalent years. This mechanism encourages permanent settlement and early integration of young foreigners.
What are the additional requirements of the canton of Lucerne?
In addition to federal requirements, the canton of Lucerne sets additional conditions: knowledge of the prevailing cantonal language (German), assessment of integration into the local community and respect for Swiss values, minimum duration of residence in the canton and in the specific municipality. The specific details and amount of the naturalization fees must be verified at the municipal offices and the cantonal administration.
How long does the naturalisation process take in Lucerne?
The overall duration of the procedure varies considerably between municipalities, typically requiring 6 to more than 12 months, depending on the complexity of the case and the administrative burden of the municipal office. The procedure includes: verification of status, contact with the municipal office, collection of documentation, submission of the application, cantonal verification and finally decision and formalization of citizenship.
