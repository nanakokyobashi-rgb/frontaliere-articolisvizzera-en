Lucerne naturalisation: 10 years and double counting

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 August 2026

Permit C and 10 years of residency are the federal requirement. Periods between 8–18 years count double. Discover procedures, times and costs for naturalisation in Lucerne.

Context

In brief

  • Ordinary naturalization requires a C permit and at least 10 years of residence in Switzerland
  • Periods between the ages of 8–18 count twice in calculating total residence
  • Each canton and municipality sets additional language, integration and length-of-residence requirements
  • Procedures and timelines vary significantly between cantons and municipalities

Key facts

  • What: Access to Swiss citizenship through ordinary naturalization
  • Who: Foreign nationals holding a C permit (settlement)
  • Where: Procedures managed by municipal and cantonal authorities (including Lucerne)
  • Basic federal requirement: C permit + 10 years of residence (with the possibility of double counting)
  • Double counting: Periods between 8–18 years of age multiplied by two for calculation purposes
  • Variable conditions: Language, integration, length of cantonal/municipal residence (specific requirements for the canton of Lucerne currently being defined)

The federal legal framework establishes that anyone holding a C permit (settlement permit) and having resided in Switzerland for at least 10 years may apply for ordinary naturalization. However, this minimum federal requirement does not exhaust the process: each canton may introduce stricter rules, and the same applies to municipalities that administer the process at the local level.

Particularly relevant for those seeking ordinary naturalization is the double-counting mechanism: periods of residence between the ages of 8 and 18 count twice in the final calculation of equivalent years. This mechanism encourages the permanent settlement of young foreign nationals residing in Switzerland, recognizing the value of early integration.

Federal legal framework vs. cantonal and municipal powers

At the federal level, the Federal Constitution and naturalization legislation establish the minimum requirements. However, the right to ordinary naturalization is exercised primarily by municipalities and cantons, which enjoy broad discretion in introducing additional conditions. In the canton of Lucerne, as in all Swiss cantons, these additional conditions are established by cantonal legislation and municipal ordinances. The specific details of the language, integration and cantonal/municipal residence-length requirements for Lucerne must be verified directly with the relevant municipal offices and the cantonal administration of Lucerne, as they vary according to local integration strategies.

Operational details

Mechanism for Accumulating Years of Residence

The residence-counting system for ordinary naturalization is structured according to the resident’s age: periods between the ages of 8 and 18 count twice, while all other periods (from birth to age 8 and from age 18 onward) count once. The total required remains fixed at the federal level: 10 equivalent years.

A hypothetical scenario clarifies the mechanism: a person who resides in Switzerland from the age of 15 until reaching the age of 30 (15 linear years) accumulates residence calculated as follows: from 15 to 18 years = 3 years × 2 = 6 equivalent years; from 18 to 30 years = 12 years × 1 = 12 equivalent years; total = 18 equivalent years. This person reaches the threshold of 10 equivalent years before turning 30 thanks to the double-counting bonus, despite having resided in Switzerland for 15 linear years.

This mechanism has an important practical implication: it allows those who arrived in Switzerland during adolescence or early adulthood to accumulate the required years in less time than those who arrive later. A forty-year-old who moves to Switzerland will have all years counted only once and will have to wait until the tenth actual year of residence; by contrast, someone who moves at age 15 will have an advantage in the calculation.

Varying Cantonal and Municipal Requirements

In addition to the federal requirement of 10 years of residence (with possible double counting), cantons and municipalities introduce additional conditions that reflect their integration priorities:

  • Language test: knowledge of the predominant cantonal language (for Lucerne: German)
  • Integration test: assessment of the ability to integrate into the local community and respect Swiss values
  • Length of cantonal residence: some cantons require a minimum period of uninterrupted residence in the specific canton, not only in Switzerland
  • Length of municipal residence: some municipalities require permanent residence in the municipality itself for an additional period beyond the cantonal requirements
  • Naturalization fees: varying amounts between cantons and municipalities (not yet specified for the canton of Lucerne in this guide)

These additional requirements reflect the Swiss principle of subsidiarity: municipalities and cantons define integration standards consistent with the local context and the needs of their community.

Practical Implications for Those Residing in Switzerland

For a foreign resident in Switzerland aspiring to ordinary naturalization, the practical implications include: checking their personal residence calculation (linear versus equivalent, with double counting for periods between 8–18 years); obtaining information from the municipality of residence about Lucerne’s specific cantonal and municipal requirements; planning the integration and language-learning process well in advance (at least 1–2 years before applying); budgeting for the naturalization fees required by the relevant municipality and canton.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure for ordinary naturalization

The path to ordinary naturalization follows standardized cantonal stages, although there are administrative variations from one municipality to another.

Phase 1: Verification of status and prerequisites Before contacting the authorities, the applicant verifies their permit status (it must be C, i.e. a settlement permit) and calculates the equivalent years of residence according to the double-counting system for periods between 8–18 years. Residence is counted from the date of first settlement in Switzerland, regardless of the canton or municipality of initial arrival.

Phase 2: Contact with the municipal naturalization office The applicant contacts the naturalization office of their municipality of residence to receive: the complete list of required documents (extracts from residence registers, certificates of no entry in the criminal record, valid identity documents); the specific language and integration requirements of the canton of Lucerne and the municipality; information on municipal and cantonal fees; an estimate of the average duration of the procedure in the specific municipality.

Phase 3: Collection and preparation of documentation Typically, the following are required: valid passport or identity document; recent residence certificate; criminal record certificate; proof of integration (employment contract, memberships in local associations, completion of language courses, certificates of civic participation); legally valid signed application form.

Phase 4: Submission of the application The formal application is submitted to the competent municipal authority. Some municipalities require a preliminary information meeting, an oral interview to verify knowledge of the cantonal language, or a presentation to the local community. This phase is crucial because it establishes the official date on which the application is received.

Phase 5: Verification by the cantonal authority After submission to the municipality, the application is forwarded to the competent cantonal authority of the canton of Lucerne. During this phase, the integration requirements, language proficiency, completeness of the documentation and compliance with additional cantonal regulations are verified. The cantonal authority may request additional interviews or clarifications.

Phase 6: Decision and formalization of citizenship If approved, the decision is notified to the municipality, which communicates the result to the applicant. This is followed by official registration with the Civil Status Office and the issuance of the naturalization certificate.

Timelines, costs and next steps

The overall duration of the procedure varies considerably between cantons and municipalities, on average taking several months (typically from 6 to more than 12 months depending on the complexity of the case and the municipality’s administrative workload). Municipal and cantonal fees for ordinary naturalization vary significantly between administrations. For specific amounts in the canton of Lucerne and the municipality of residence, it is advisable to contact the competent municipal office directly during Phase 2.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the minimum federal requirements for ordinary naturalization?
The federal rule requires a C permit (residence permit) and at least 10 years of residence in Switzerland. However, periods of residence between 8 and 18 years of age count double in the calculation, allowing the requirement of 10 equivalent years to be achieved in less time if residence began during adolescence. Each canton and municipality may introduce additional requirements with respect to the federal minimum.
How does double counting of residency periods work?
Periods between 8 and 18 years of age are multiplied by two in the total calculation of equivalent years. For example, those who reside in Switzerland from 15 to 30 years accumulate: from 15 to 18 years = 3 years × 2 = 6 equivalent years; from 18 to 30 years = 12 years × 1 = 12 equivalent years; total = 18 equivalent years. This mechanism encourages permanent settlement and early integration of young foreigners.
What are the additional requirements of the canton of Lucerne?
In addition to federal requirements, the canton of Lucerne sets additional conditions: knowledge of the prevailing cantonal language (German), assessment of integration into the local community and respect for Swiss values, minimum duration of residence in the canton and in the specific municipality. The specific details and amount of the naturalization fees must be verified at the municipal offices and the cantonal administration.
How long does the naturalisation process take in Lucerne?
The overall duration of the procedure varies considerably between municipalities, typically requiring 6 to more than 12 months, depending on the complexity of the case and the administrative burden of the municipal office. The procedure includes: verification of status, contact with the municipal office, collection of documentation, submission of the application, cantonal verification and finally decision and formalization of citizenship.
