Popular initiative to ensure cash payment on public transport in Graubünden

Context

In brief - Popular initiative for cash payment on public transport in Graubünden - More than 8,000 signatures for the reintroduction of cash payment - Point of view: "If companies are funded by taxpayers, they must accept both payment methods" ## Key facts - What: Popular initiative for cash payment on public transport - When: Launched in recent days - Where: Graubünden - Who: Promoters of the initiative - Amount: More than 8,000 signatures collected In recent days in Graubünden the popular initiative "Cash payment of public services (anti-discrimination initiative)" was launched. The promoters oppose digital payment on public transport and ask the Canton to guarantee cash for municipal and cantonal services and infrastructure. According to the initiators, access to public services such as buses, parking lots or toilets becomes more difficult if payment can only be made in digital form. Different groups of people are thus “discriminated against”: the elderly, those with mental disabilities, children, socially disadvantaged people and those who do not want to expose themselves to cybercrime. “If businesses and services are (co)funded by taxpayers, both options must be possible: digital payment and cash payment”, the request that promoters make on their webpage. Practical analysis ## # Implications for travellers The initiative has sparked debate on how the Canton can ensure cash payment for public services. If the initiative is successful, it could affect cantonal and federal legislation regarding the means of payment for public services. This could lead to more flexibility for travelers who prefer to pay in cash, especially for security or accessibility reasons. However, the success of the initiative will depend on the Canton's ability to quickly implement the necessary changes. ### Comparison with other regions In other regions of Switzerland, digital payment has become the norm for public services. For example, in Ticino and Zurich, cash payment has been gradually reduced or replaced by digital solutions. The initiative in Graubünden could therefore represent a unique case, in which the request to keep the cash payment could be supported by a significant part of the population. ### Future scenario If the initiative is approved, it could pave the way for a revision of cantonal and federal regulations regarding means of payment for public services.
