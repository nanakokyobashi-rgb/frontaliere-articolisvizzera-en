In the Canton of Solothurn, as in every Swiss canton, the labor market reflects a professional structure divided into training bands and salary levels defined by federal law and cantonal ordinances. Searching for a job and determining salaries follow precise rules that directly affect the pay slip, social security contributions and compulsory insurance.

Salary ranges by level of training

The Swiss employment market is historically divided into three qualification levels. The first is professional apprenticeship (AFC - Federal Certificate of Skills), which trains technicians and skilled workers in sectors such as manufacturing, construction, agriculture and services. During an apprenticeship, pay is lower than that of a skilled employee; upon completion, the salary increases according to the qualifications acquired and the sector.

The second level is higher professional training: high school technical school diplomas, administration and management studies and branch specialisations. These titles place the worker in an intermediate salary range, with progression prospects.

The third level is university education (bachelor's, master's, doctorate) and specialized postgraduate courses. University graduates enter the market with an average salary higher than professional school graduates, with significant margins for managerial and senior technical advancement.

Each band has its own salary band. A company that hires in the Canton of Solothurn must respect the minimum wages required for each qualification level if it adheres to a collective labor agreement. In the absence of a CCL, the remuneration is established by individual contract between employer and employee.

Collective labor agreements

In Switzerland there is no uniform federal minimum wage. However, many sectors and cantons provide for collective labor agreements (CCL), negotiated between employers' associations and trade unions, or established by cantonal law in specific areas (public health, construction, cleaning, municipal services). A CCL defines minimum wages, maximum hours, vacation days, allowances and social security contribution rates (including the Second Pillar, LPP).

In the Canton of Solothurn, as in the other cantons, an employer can join CCLs for the following categories: metalworking industry, crafts, commerce, hotels and restaurants, healthcare, education, public administration. Membership requires compliance with minimum wages and contractual conditions. If the canton provides a cantonal minimum wage specifically for certain sectors, this must be verified at the cantonal employment office. This guide focuses on the Swiss federal and cantonal structure.

Operational details

Social security contributions and compulsory insurance

The salary of an employee in the Canton of Solothurn is made up of a gross part (the contractual salary) and a series of mandatory deductions. These reflect federal and cantonal contributions and insurance obligations established by Swiss laws.

First pillar: AVS/AI/IPG Old-age and survivors' insurance (AVS), disability insurance (AI) and compensation for loss of earnings (IPG for maternity and paternity) constitute the first pillar of Swiss social security. The employee pays 5.3% of the gross salary; the employer pays a total of 10.6% (with its own share of 5.3%). These contributions finance public pensions: starting from the age of 64 for women (65 for men), the worker receives a monthly AVS income based on the contributions paid and the years of work.

Unemployment Unemployment contributions (AD - Unemployment insurance; AC - Insurance against involuntary unemployment due to worker's fault) total 1.1% up to an annual maximum, divided between employee and employer. These payments guarantee a daily allowance if the worker involuntarily loses his job.

Accident insurance Insurance against accidents at work (LAINF) is paid by the employer to an insurance company (SUVA or recognized private insurers) and varies from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector of activity. The employee does not contribute directly; the employer includes the cost in the operating budget.

Second pillar: occupational pension provision (BVG/BVG)

The Pension Fund (LPP — Law on Occupational Pension for Old Age, Survivors and Disability) is mandatory for employees with an annual salary above a coordinated threshold (not included in this analysis, as it is indexed annually). The LPP works on a defined contribution basis: the employer pays a percentage of the coordinated salary (gross salary minus a deduction), the employee another percentage. Rates increase with age:

  • Age 25–34 years: 7%
  • Age 35–44 years: 10%
  • Age 45–54 years: 15%
  • Age 55+ up to the reference age (65 men, 64 women): 18%

These percentages apply to your coordinated salary, not your entire salary. The LPP fund accumulates individual capital during working life, paid out as a monthly pension upon retirement or as a one-off capital (depending on the fund's choices).

KVG: Mandatory health insurance

Every resident in Switzerland must take out health insurance (KVG/KVG) within 3 months of arriving or starting the employment relationship. Unlike AVS, BVG and unemployment contributions, the KVG is NOT a salary contribution. It is a private policy taken out individually with a health insurance company (Helsana, CSS, Sanitas, Allianz, etc.) with a monthly or quarterly premium. The premium varies by canton, age group, gender and choice of annual deductible (CHF 300, 500, 1,000, 1,500, 2,000 or 2,500). Many Swiss cantons, including Solothurn, reduce premiums for low-income residents through a cantonal subsidy.

Practical scenario: Paycheck impact

An employee in the Canton of Solothurn with a gross salary of CHF 60,000 per year and aged between 35 and 44 bears the following costs:

Key points

Search and apply in the Solothurn job market

Searching for work in the Canton of Solothurn can take advantage of multiple channels. The Swiss national portal job listings hosts thousands of open positions in all sectors and geographical areas. Local companies also publish offers on dedicated cantonal platforms, on the website of the Solothurn–Nidwalden Chamber of Commerce, and through Swiss placement agencies specialized by sector.

Once the suitable position has been identified, the application procedure includes:

1. Drafting the motivation letter (in German, the main language of the canton; sometimes in English or Italian for international roles) 2. Preparation of an updated CV with photos, contacts, certified professional experience, languages spoken and technical skills 3. Sending via email or online platform indicated in the announcement 4. Preliminary interview (often via video call) 5. Meeting in person at the company headquarters (if the interview is passed) 6. Signature of the individual employment contract (written document, mandatory)

The employment contract must include: description of the role and duties, place of work, maximum weekly working hours (45 hours for industry, offices, sales; 50 hours for other sectors), gross monthly or annual salary, start date, duration of the probationary period (maximum 3 months), guaranteed holiday days (minimum 4 weeks per year, or 5 if the worker is under 20 years old), notice periods (1 month during the first year; 2 months from the second to the ninth year; 3 months from tenth year onwards).

If the employer adheres to a collective sector agreement, the individual contract must align with the minimum wages and conditions established by the CCL.

Rights and obligations during the employment relationship

During employment in the Canton of Solothurn, the worker has rights guaranteed by Swiss federal law:

  • Paid holidays: minimum 4 weeks per year (20 working days); 5 weeks if under 20 years old
  • Maternity leave: 14 weeks at 80% of salary (funded by loss of earnings allowance - IPG)
  • Paternity: 2 weeks paid (federal law from 2021)
  • Social security: automatic enrollment in AVS/AI paid via payslip; enrollment in LPP if salary exceeds the coordinated threshold; mandatory private KVG subscription
  • Protection from discrimination: the employer cannot distinguish by sex, origin, religion, opinion, sexual orientation
  • Right to regular termination: the worker can terminate the relationship by respecting the contractual terms (1, 2 or 3 months depending on seniority)

Social security payments (AVS/AI/IPG, unemployment, LPP) are made via the monthly payslip. The KVG is paid directly by the employee to the chosen health insurance company, not through the employer.

Deadlines and administrative obligations

Upon signing the contract, the worker must complete the obligations in the following terms:

Within 3 months of starting work:

  • Signing the KVG with a recognized health insurance company (federal law KVG/KVG). In case of delay, the fund retroactively covers the costs but applies an administrative penalty.

Within 1 month of starting:

  • The employer registers the employee with a pension fund recognized by the canton (Second pillar LPP). This fulfillment is automatic; the worker receives the registration documents subsequently.
  • The employer pays AVS/AI/IPG contributions to the federal (SECO) and cantonal tax authorities.

In your tax return (annual):

  • The worker declares the gross income from employment, the social security contributions paid, and benefits from personal deductions (family situation, professional deductions, mortgage interest, donations) according to the Solothurn cantonal ordinance.

To check the tax specifications, the cantonal KVG subsidies, the provisions on any cantonal minimum wages in Solothurn and the methods of registration in the social security system, it is advisable to contact:

  • Labor Office of the Canton of Solothurn
  • Cantonal Tax Service (Finance Directorate Solothurn)
  • A pension fund certified by the canton (for information on LPP)
  • A reliable health insurance company (for KVG quotes and subsidies)

For those who transit from another Swiss canton, the transfer of domicile also involves the change of health insurance and tax rates. Anyone who is a resident foreign citizen must register with the cantonal migration office (SEM) and sign a KVG within 3 months, under penalty of an administrative penalty.

Frequently Asked Questions
Does the Canton of Solothurn have its own cantonal minimum wage?
There is no single federal minimum wage in Switzerland. Some Swiss cantons have their own minimum wages for specific sectors (trade, construction, health, cleaning) often set through collective labour agreements (CLAs) signed between employers' associations and trade unions. The situation varies significantly by canton. To check whether the canton of Solothurn has a cantonal or sectoral minimum wage, it is advisable to contact the cantonal Labour Office directly or consult the cantonal ordinances.
What are the monthly social security contributions deducted from the paycheck?
Mandatory contributions deducted from gross salary include: AVS/AI/IPG (5.3% for employee), AD/AC Unemployment (1.1% up to annual ceiling), and LPP on coordinated salary (percentage variable with age: 7–18%). Direct federal, cantonal and municipal tax is deducted at cantonal rates. LAMal (health insurance) is a private policy paid directly to the sick fund, not through a pay slip. The employer also pays its share of AVS/AI (5.3%), unemployment (1.1%) and LAINF (0.7-1.5% according to sector).
How long do I have to take out health insurance (LAMal) after the first day of work?
Every resident of Switzerland must take out health insurance within 3 months of arrival or the start of the employment relationship. If you do not insure yourself within this period, the sick pay fund covers the costs retroactively by applying an administrative penalty. LAMal is not a salary contribution, but a private policy with variable premiums by canton, age group and annual deductible (CHF 300-2,500). The Canton of Solothurn offers subsidies for low-income residents.
What are the days of holidays and leave guaranteed by Swiss federal law?
Federal law guarantees a minimum of 4 weeks of annual leave (20 working days), or 5 weeks if the worker is under 20 years of age. In addition to holidays, the law provides for: maternity (14 weeks at 80% of salary via loss of earnings allowance — IPG) and paternity (2 weeks from 2021). Many collective bargaining agreements offer additional holidays and more generous forms of leave. The terms of cancellation are: 1 month first year, 2 months from the second to the ninth year, 3 months from the tenth year onwards.
How do I calculate payroll tax deductions in the canton of Solothurn?
Tax deductions are made at three levels: federal (IFD), cantonal and municipal (variable rates per municipality in the Canton of Solothurn). The amount depends on gross income, marital status, number of dependent children, real estate, and donations.
