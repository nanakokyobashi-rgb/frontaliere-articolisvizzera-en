According to NZZ am Sonntag, the University Psychiatric Clinics of Basel (UPK) have for years largely delegated ADHD assessments to students. At the UPK there are currently no cases where this has led to an incorrect diagnosis, as they communicated on Sunday. The newspaper claims, citing some former interns, that the latter would have carried out, practically alone, the assessments for ADHD (Attention Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder) and formulated diagnostic proposals. A psychologist would then assume responsibility for the diagnosis, despite not having personally visited the patients.

Operational details

The UPKs will carefully evaluate the results of the investigations and inform in a transparent manner about any further corrective measures to be taken. The UPKs also intend to clarify the issues related to invoicing raised in the article published by the press. According to the NZZ am Sonntag, the association of health insurers Prioswiss considers the billing of the basic insurance LAMal inadmissible if the benefits have been provided by students instead of by fully qualified professionals and independently qualified to exercise the health profession.

However, before the article, the UPKs would have initiated significant improvements in this specific management and administrative area, according to their official statement. The professional accompaniment of students has been markedly intensified and discussions on individual clinical cases are now conducted directly by the new psychological co-direction established in the structure. In addition, there is a further strengthening of specialist and medical psychological resources to ensure high quality standards and compliance with current regulations in the Swiss health sector.

Key points

An ADHD diagnosis often has far-reaching consequences for those affected, profoundly impacting the daily, academic, and professional lives of the patients involved. It may entail the right to compensatory measures for disadvantages in education or studies, guaranteeing specific protections provided for by current regulations. Furthermore, it often involves taking medication for years, requiring constant and rigorous medical monitoring by qualified specialists.

Between 2020 and 2024, the prescription of ADHD medications in Switzerland almost doubled, highlighting a steady and significant growth in the demand for targeted evaluations and treatments nationwide. However, medications are prescribed exclusively by physicians and not by psychologists or trainees, limiting the responsibility for pharmacological therapy to the structured medical body.
