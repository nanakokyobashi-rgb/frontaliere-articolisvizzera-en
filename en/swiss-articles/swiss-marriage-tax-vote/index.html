The proposal put to a vote

In view of the November 29 vote, the Federal Council and Parliament recommend rejecting the Centre's initiative «Enough with marriage discrimination». For direct federal tax, the text provides for maintaining joint taxation of married couples, who should not receive less favorable treatment than unmarried couples.

The proponents' proposal is limited to this level of taxation, but contains two elements. The first is confirmation of joint taxation for married couples. The second is an alternative calculation method, whereby the tax authorities would automatically apply the tax amount most advantageous for the couple.

The text therefore places the comparative treatment of couples at the forefront: it calls for married people to remain subject to joint taxation and for the result not to be less favorable than that for unmarried couples. The section on automatic calculation complements this request by indicating how the amount should be determined.

The position of the Federal Council

Karin Keller-Sutter, the Federal Councillor responsible for the Department of Finance, considers the proposal obsolete. The reason given is the move to individual taxation, approved by the people in March 2026. For the Federal Councillor, this move makes the Centre's proposal obsolete.

The Government raises both a financial and an institutional objection. In its view, approval would introduce different systems between the Confederation and the cantons. In addition, tax revenue could decrease by a maximum of three billion francs per year, depending on how it is implemented.

The figure is therefore tied to the implementation scenario and is not presented as an amount already defined. The political comparison remains between the solution supported by the proponents, based on automatic calculation of the most advantageous tax, and the individual taxation referred to by Keller-Sutter.

Operational details

The practical issue: three tax levels

To understand the practical effect of the text, the levels of Swiss taxation must be kept separate. The system comprises direct federal tax, cantonal taxes and municipal taxes. Each canton has its own law and its own multiplier; municipalities apply a multiplier to the cantonal level. The initiative described by the source concerns the first level, while the Government warns of the risk of different systems between the Confederation and the cantons.

A proposal, not a single method for every tax

This distinction changes how the promised benefit should be understood. For a married couple, the promoters' method would automatically calculate direct federal tax based on the most advantageous amount. Based on the available content, this does not result in a single method for every Swiss tax: the text refers to the DFT, and the very objection raised by the Government presupposes the possible coexistence of different systems.

The practical comparison breaks down as follows. On the one hand, there is the principle: married couples remain taxed jointly and not less favorably than unmarried couples. On the other, there is the mechanism: the tax authority automatically applies the most advantageous amount. In the background is the political alternative mentioned by Karin Keller-Sutter, namely individual taxation approved by the people in March 2026.
