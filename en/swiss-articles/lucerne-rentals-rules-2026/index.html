Three months' rent is the maximum security deposit provided for rental agreements in Switzerland. In the 2026 real estate market, anyone looking for a home in the canton of Lucerne starts from a national framework: rentals are governed by CO art. 253 et seq., and federal law is the same in every canton.

The rule is straightforward: the deposit may amount to no more than three months' rent and must be deposited into a blocked account held in the tenant's name. When comparing Lucerne with the other cantons, this limit therefore remains the common reference point specified for rentals. The amount to have ready depends on the rent stipulated in the contract, but the legal ceiling indicated by the source does not change from one canton to another.

The common point between Lucerne and the other cantons

The cantonal focus makes it possible to assess the local real estate market without confusing the location of the accommodation with the source of the rule. For the deposit and the framework governing rentals, the reference remains federal. This distinction also helps anyone comparing housing options in multiple cantons: checking the deposit always starts with the rent and stops at three months' rent.

The household budget, however, also includes items regulated at different levels. Swiss taxation has three levels: direct federal tax, cantonal taxes and municipal taxes. Each canton has its own law and its own multiplier; municipalities apply a multiplier to the cantonal tax. The AFC/ESTV is responsible for IFD and VAT, while the cantonal administrations manage cantonal and municipal taxes. The UST/BFS has a statistical function and does not set tax rates.

For those who want to consider the rent alongside other expenses, costo della vita in Svizzera must therefore be read by separating the federal rules governing rentals from cantonal and municipal tax responsibilities. In the canton of Lucerne, as in the rest of Switzerland, this separation prevents assigning to the rental agreement rules that instead belong to the tax system.

Operational details

The real budget: beyond rent

The national comparison of rents goes beyond the rent itself. A practical assessment for the canton of Lucerne must separate the initial deposit from expenses that affect disposable income. LAMal/KVG is compulsory for residents, must be taken out within three months of arrival, and operates with per-capita premiums: it is neither a tax nor a salary contribution. Premiums vary according to canton and region, while premium reductions are a cantonal subsidy.

For adults, the deductibles indicated are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 or 2500. This item must therefore be considered together with the rent, without turning it into a deduction from salary. To get an overview of healthcare costs, you can consult guide LAMal e cassa malati.

From gross salary to the margin for housing

Disposable income requires an examination of the payslip. AVS/AI/IPG contributions amount to 5.3% for the employee and 10.6% overall with the employer. AD/AC is 1.1% up to the annual maximum, while LAINF/LAA ranges from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector.

LPP/BVG applies to the coordinated salary, with rates of 7% between the ages of 25 and 34, 10% between 35 and 44, 15% between 45 and 54, and 18% from age 55 until the reference age. To check how these items are reflected in net pay, busta paga svizzera is available.

Comparing Cantons then requires keeping the responsibilities distinct. Federal direct tax, cantonal and municipal taxes are not a single levy; the municipal multiplier is applied to the cantonal one. UFAS/BSV concerns social security, including AVS/AI/LPP, and does not set taxes. For statistics, the institutional reference is UST/BFS. The minimum wage must also be viewed with caution: there is no federal minimum wage, while some Cantons have their own. These differences affect the margin available for covering the cost of living, without changing the federal limit on the deposit.

Key points

Preparing for the rental without confusing the items

The most straightforward procedure starts with the contract and leads to disposable income. For a search in the canton of Lucerne, this sequence can be used, keeping tenancy rights, health coverage, salary contributions and taxes separate.

Four steps before signing

1. Separate rent and deposit. Note the rent amount specified in the contract and verify that the security deposit does not exceed three months. Also check that the sum is to be placed in a blocked account in the tenant's name. These are the two financial elements directly indicated for the rental.

2. Place the contract within the correct framework. For accommodation in the canton of Lucerne, the reference is CO art. 253 et seq.; federal tenancy law is the same in every canton. Offers can therefore be compared starting from this common basis, without attributing a different rule on security deposits and blocked accounts to the canton.

3. Reconstruct the income actually available. On the payslip, distinguish AVS/AI/IPG, AD/AC, LAINF/LAA and LPP/BVG contributions from rent. LAMal should not be treated as a salary contribution: for residents, it is compulsory insurance with per-capita premiums.

4. Arrange health coverage by the deadline. Anyone arriving in Switzerland must take out LAMal within three months of arrival. In the calculation, consider one of the indicated adult deductibles and check the premium reduction provided for at cantonal level; premiums and subsidies depend on the cantonal and regional premium framework.

Check the areas of responsibility

For taxes, contact the appropriate institutional authority: AFC/ESTV covers IFD and VAT, the cantonal administrations cover cantonal and municipal taxes, while UFAS/BSV concerns AVS/AI/LPP and does not set rates. UST/BFS is used for statistics, not to establish a tax. If the budget depends on a salary, there is no need to look for a federal minimum threshold: some Cantons have their own minimum wage, but none exists at federal level.

This checklist avoids adding the same item twice or treating the security deposit as a tax. For those who want to translate income into a more readable monthly margin, the final step is calcolatore stipendio.

Frequently Asked Questions
How much is the maximum security deposit for rentals in Switzerland?
The security deposit provided for the lease in Switzerland and in the canton of Lucerne can reach a maximum of three months of rent. The sum must be placed in a escrow account in the tenant's name. This rule is part of the federal lease law, governed by the Code of Obligations in articles 253 et seq., and is the same in each canton.
What are the deadlines and rules for LAMal insurance in Switzerland?
LAMal or KVG health insurance is mandatory for residents of Switzerland and must be taken out within three months of arrival. It works with per capita premiums that vary by canton and region. For adults, the deductibles indicated are 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 or 2500 francs, while the reduction in premiums is a subsidy managed at the cantonal level.
How are tax and social security responsibilities divided in Switzerland?
Swiss taxation is divided into three levels: direct federal tax, cantonal taxes and municipal taxes. AFC and ESTV deal with direct federal tax and VAT, while cantonal administrations handle cantonal and municipal taxes. For social security, including AVS, AI and LPP, the reference body is the UFAS and BSV, while the UST and BFS perform statistical functions and do not set rates or taxes.
