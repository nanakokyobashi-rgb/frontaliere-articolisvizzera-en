SNB leaves guide rate at 0% and raises GDP estimates 2026

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 24 September 2026

The SNB guide rate remains at 0% from June 2025; August inflation at 0.8%; GDP 2026 forecast between 1.5% and 2%.

Context

In short

  • SNB: key rate stuck at 0% from June 2025
  • Inflation in August at 0.8%, driven by energy
  • 2026 growth expected between 1.5% and 2%

Key facts

  • Decision → monetary policy unchanged
  • Guide rate → 0%
  • August inflation → 0.8%
  • Growth 2026 → 1.5%-2%
  • Growth 2027 → approximately 1.5%

The Swiss National Bank's key rate remains at 0%. The decision, widely expected, leaves monetary policy unchanged and confirms the level at which the rate has been since June 2025. The news was released with a press release taken from Keystone-ATS.

The remuneration of sight deposits held by banks at the SNB also does not change. Up to a certain limit, assets will continue to be remunerated at the key interest rate; for amounts exceeding that limit, the level of 0.25 percentage points indicated in the press release will remain unchanged.

Prices and inflation forecast

Since June, inflation has grown further and in August it was 0.8%. The SNB mainly links the movement to higher energy prices. In the medium term, however, inflationary pressure has increased only slightly. The institute therefore considers monetary policy to be appropriate to maintain inflation in the area of ​​price stability and support the performance of the economy.

The conditional forecast indicates a further increase in inflation in the fourth quarter, followed by a decline during 2027. According to the SNB, energy price increases, currently very high, are expected to slow down in the coming quarters.

Global scenario and Swiss growth

In its baseline scenario, the SNB expects moderate global growth in the coming quarters, but signals high uncertainty linked especially to the situation in the Middle East. Energy prices significantly higher than expected would further push inflation and significantly slow economic growth. The international trade policy framework also remains uncertain.

For Switzerland, the institute expects moderate growth, supported by positive impulses from the world economy, monetary policy and the depreciation of the franc. For the whole of 2026 the estimate is now between 1.5% and 2%, compared to around 1% expected three months ago. For 2027, a growth forecast of around 1.5% remains.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the SNB's decision on the lead rate?
The Swiss National Bank has left the guide rate unchanged at 0%, a level maintained since June 2025. Even the remuneration of the banks' overnight assets at the SNB remains the same: up to a certain limit they follow the guide rate, beyond that limit it remains 0.25 percentage points.
What are the inflation forecasts for the coming years according to the SNB?
Inflation in August 2024 is at 0.8%. The SNB expects a further increase in the fourth quarter of 2024, followed by a decline in 2027. Official projections indicate inflation at 0.7% in 2026, 0.8% in 2027, and 0.8% in 2028, assuming the guide rate remains at 0%.
How has the estimate of Swiss GDP growth for 2026 and 2027 changed?
For 2026, the SNB now forecasts growth of between 1.5% and 2%, improved from the approximately 1% indicated three months earlier. For 2027, the estimate remains around 1.5%. Both forecasts are conditional on the assumption that the SNB lead rate remains at 0% for the entire forecast horizon.
