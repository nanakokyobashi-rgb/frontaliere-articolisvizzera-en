BERN - The Federal Council wants the national poison information service, 145, not to be funded exclusively by the public sector. To achieve this objective, it intends to amend the Chemicals Act and the Therapeutic Products Act, so as to require the chemical and pharmaceutical industries to co-finance Tox Info Suisse.

The project was confirmed in a statement issued on Wednesday after the government meeting. The text states that the chemical and pharmaceutical industries are unwilling to provide lasting and substantial support for the service on a voluntary basis. According to the statement, this position emerged in discussions between the Federal Department of Home Affairs (FDHA) and delegations from the two sectors.

The Federal Council justifies the obligation by citing the role of producers in poisonings. Producers in the two sectors are identified as direct and indirect contributors to more than 70% of poisonings that occur every year in Switzerland. For the government, this link justifies the industry's financial involvement.

The Federal Council will submit the amendments for consultation until mid-February. According to the data provided, the future obligation would concern around 5'500 companies in the chemicals sector and around 300 companies in the medicines sector.

The text also indicates the average annual costs: around 240 francs for producers of chemicals and almost 5'300 francs for holders of medicinal product authorizations. The difference between the two figures reflects the distinction between the two groups concerned.

The point announced in Bern

The decision described by the source is a regulatory intention: the Federal Council wants to shift the model from one that is not exclusively publicly funded to mandatory co-financing by the two sectors. The step indicated now is consultation on the two laws; the text does not yet report an outcome of the procedure.

Key points

The precedent for public funding

The federal proposal is part of a funding history dating back about sixty years. Initially, Tox Info Suisse could rely on support from private organizations and on revenue secured through contracts with hospitals, donations and public contributions. In recent years, however, most private organizations have progressively withdrawn their support.

From the historical model to parliamentary debate

The withdrawal of private funders is the point that the Federal Council highlighted in talks with industry. The note states that the voluntary support of the two sectors does not appear sufficient to guarantee lasting and substantial support for the poison-case advisory service.

Parliament had already addressed the issue: during the 2025 winter session, it had allocated the resources needed to ensure the continuity of the national emergency number for poisonings, but the Federal Council had rejected the increase.

Speaking in the Council of States, Federal Councillor Karin Keller-Sutter had warned that Parliament’s approval of the increase in federal funds would reduce the Confederation’s negotiating room. According to reports, the government aimed to obtain co-financing for Tox Info from private organizations. Keller-Sutter had also reported that the Federal Council considered the withdrawal of private organizations from the emergency service regrettable.

What remains open

The available content does not specify the precise closing date of the consultation, its outcome or the date on which the amendments will enter into force. Nor does it explain how the costs would be distributed among the individual companies or how average values would be converted into an individual amount.

The source therefore presents a proposal submitted for consultation, not a regulation that has already entered into force. It reports the parliamentary funding secured for the continuation of the service and the Federal Council’s intention to make the two sectors participate on a mandatory basis, but does not document any further changes to the operation of number 145.
