The leadership change

As of 1° January 2027, Philipp Brunner will be the new CEO of the Stadler Rail group. The appointment, announced by Stadler in Bussnang, entrusts leadership of the Swiss railway manufacturer to a manager who rose through the company. The choice leverages in-house expertise and knowledge of international markets.

The 42-year-old will succeed Markus Bernsteiner, 60, who has been in operational charge of the group since the beginning of 2023. Bernsteiner intends to focus on board mandates. Stadler specified that the change had been planned for some time. In the release, Peter Spuhler is identified as the current chairman of the board.

Bernsteiner joined Stadler in 1999. During his career, he held, among other positions, the roles of head of the Bussnang plant, head of the Switzerland division, head of the Components division and deputy CEO of the group.

Brunner's career path

Brunner has worked for Stadler since 2010. He studied business administration at the University of St. Gallen and earned an MBA at ETH Zurich. Before joining the group, he gained his first experience at UBS and in the consumer goods sector. The current chairman of the board, Peter Spuhler, personally hired him.

He started out as a project manager in India. A year later, he took charge of a difficult situation in Algeria, where he set up a maintenance site. Starting in 2012, he led the construction of the Minsk plant in Belarus, which he headed as CEO from 2016. Stadler credits that period with transforming the site into one of the group's most successful companies, with almost 2.000 employees. Due to sanctions, the plant's operations were subsequently sharply reduced.

Today, Brunner heads the Central Europe division and, since January 2026, also the German one on an interim basis. He has been part of the group management since 2021. According to the company, he expanded the Central European business, which has more than 2.600 employees, making it profitable.

Operational details

The signal for the Swiss economy

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the appointment should first and foremost be read as a change in the leadership of a Swiss railway group. The published text focuses the announcement on the succession to the CEO, managerial responsibilities and financial results; no other operational effects are presented. The connection with everyday life is therefore indirect, but concrete in terms of the company, stated employment and profitability.

Continuity to be assessed

The first element to observe is internal continuity. Brunner is familiar with the production of railway vehicles, the global supplier industry and markets in Europe, Asia and North Africa. The source describes expertise built within Stadler and knowledge of international markets developed within the group. For the 2027 transition, this indicates leadership with direct experience in the company's international activities; it does not yet indicate how future responsibilities will be distributed.

The second element concerns the phase managed by Bernsteiner. His tenure coincided with the consequences of the pandemic, the war in Ukraine, the 2024 floods and disruptions in supply chains. Stadler maintains that management and employees increased resilience and laid the foundations for greater profitability. The reference describes the management of the group, not a general assessment of the Swiss economy.

Numbers and caution

The clearest figure is revenue in the first half of 2026: 2,0 billion francs, up 40 percent. For the full year 2026, Stadler still expects revenue well above 5,0 billion and an EBIT margin above 5,0 percent. These figures make it possible to track the group's trajectory, but do not by themselves describe the performance of every plant or employment in Switzerland.

The reference to more than 2.600 employees concerns Central Europe. It is a useful figure for those following aziende che assumono, but it is not an announcement of new hires. The succession in the Central Europe and Germany divisions is at an advanced stage; the names of the new managers will be announced later.

Key points

How to follow the transition

The reader can follow the succession with a four-step check, sticking to the information provided by Stadler. The source does not describe forms, questions or procedures for the public. The date to keep separate from the other time references is that of the new CEO's taking office.

The checklist

1. Mark January 1, 2027. This is the date on which Brunner will take office as CEO of the group, while Bernsteiner will leave operational leadership.

2. Separate the group from the divisions. The appointment concerns Stadler; Central Europe and Germany have a separate succession process. Stadler says that the procedure is already at an advanced stage, but the names will be announced later.

3. Read the results with the correct period. The available comparison concerns the first half of 2026, with 2.0 billion francs and 40 percent growth. The forecast of revenue well above 5.0 billion and an EBIT margin above 5.0 percent, on the other hand, concerns all of 2026.

4. Distinguish employment data from geography. The more than 2,600 employees mentioned belong to Central Europe; the nearly 2,000 employees were associated with the Minsk plant, whose activity has been greatly reduced because of the sanctions. The text does not present these figures as new hires in Switzerland.

This reading avoids two errors: confusing the appointment date with the forecast for 2026 and mistaking the managerial succession for an employment announcement. Those who want to check available opportunities separately can consult the annunci di lavoro, without attributing to Stadler positions that the source does not mention.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who is Stadler's new CEO and when will he take office?
The new CEO of Stadler Rail will be Philipp Brunner, 42. His appointment has been formalized by the Bussnang-based group and he is expected to take office on January 1, 2027. Brunner, who has been part of the group's management since 2021, boasts a long corporate career that began in 2010, during which he managed projects in India, Algeria and Belarus, eventually leading the Central Europe division.
What are Stadler's recent financial results?
For the first half of 2026, the Stadler group recorded a turnover of CHF 2.0 billion, marking a growth of 40 percent compared to the previous period. For the full year 2026, the company expects to exceed CHF 5.0 billion in turnover, with an expected EBIT margin of more than 5.0 percent. These data reflect the current trajectory of the group in a complex market context.
What will happen to operational guidance after 2027?
From 1 January 2027, Markus Bernsteiner, who has been Chief Operating Officer since the beginning of 2023, will leave the leadership of the group to devote himself to new terms on the boards of directors. The handover to Philipp Brunner has been planned for some time. Stadler said the succession in the Central Europe and Germany divisions, currently managed by Brunner, is at an advanced stage and the names of future managers will be communicated later.
