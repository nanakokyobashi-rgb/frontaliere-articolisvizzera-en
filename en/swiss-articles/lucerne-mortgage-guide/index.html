Operational details

How equity funds work in the Swiss market

There is no federal law that imposes a minimum percentage of equity to purchase a house in Switzerland. However, the market has developed an established practice: banks usually require at least 20% of the purchase value as equity capital. This rule is not accidental.

If you invest your 20%, the bank covers the remaining 80% via mortgage loan. From the bank's point of view, you have "already lost" 20% if the property drops in value, so you are motivated to repay the mortgage. However, if you wanted to buy with 10% or 5% of your own funds, the bank would accept but ask for additional mortgage insurance (an additional cost for you) to cover the higher risk.

In some particular cases — for example, if you are young with a good stable income or if you have a long-standing banking relationship — you will be able to negotiate percentages lower than 20%, but you will have to expect higher interest rates and compulsory insurance.

The sustainability test: how banks decide

This is the phase that determines whether you will receive the mortgage or not. It doesn't happen "automatically". When you apply for financing, the bank asks you:

  • Bank statements from the last few months (to check that you manage your money well)
  • Tax returns for the last 2-3 years (to ensure earnings stability)
  • Employment contract (to evaluate employment prospects)
  • List of all debts (car, credit cards, other mortgages)
  • A professional appraisal of the property

With this data, the bank applies internal prudence tests. There is no single federal formula: each bank has its own guidelines. However, the main criteria are similar: your net income must cover the annual mortgage repayment, interest, property taxes, maintenance costs, and you must leave a margin for ordinary expenses. If the test fails — for example, the mortgage would take up 40% of your gross income — the bank may refuse, offer a lower mortgage amount, or charge higher interest rates.

The death tax: a cantonal and often surprising voice

Many buyers discover too late that transfer tax is an exclusively cantonal responsibility. There is no uniform federal tax throughout Switzerland. The Canton of Lucerne decides autonomously on the rate, exemptions and payment methods.

This means that if you buy in the Canton of Lucerne you will pay the Lucerne rate. If you had purchased in the Canton of Zurich, the figure would be different. You cannot negotiate the tax and you cannot avoid it (except for a few cases of exemption, which depend on cantonal legislation).

The tax is calculated on the sale value of the property and must be paid within the deadline indicated by the cantonal administration (usually 30 days) after registration in the land register. It is a cost that is added to the price already agreed and that you must budget for from the beginning. If you forget to include it in your budget, you will find yourself in trouble when the invoice arrives.

Notary fees: variable by canton and amount

The notary draws up the deed of sale, verifies the ownership in the land register, ascertains that the seller has the right to sell, and calculates the taxes. All this has a cost: notary fees.

These charges are not a fixed national percentage. They vary according to:

  • The amount of the property (the more expensive the house, the more expenses increase)
  • Lucerne cantonal rates (each canton has its own guidelines)
  • The complexity of the deed (if there are ownership problems, pre-existing mortgages, etc., the cost increases)

You can ask the notary for an estimate before signing the compromise. This way you will know exactly how much you will have to pay for registration. It is an amount that you CANNOT negotiate — it is established by cantonal law — but at least you can calculate it in advance.

Key points

Step by step procedure: from idea to registered deed

Buying a house in Lucerne follows a logical sequence. Understanding the order of the steps helps you avoid costly mistakes.

Phase 1: Financial preparation (before looking for a home)

Don't wait until you find the perfect property to think about money. Start by asking one or more banks for pre-approval guidance: “With my income and the funds I have, how much can I borrow?” Banks offer this service free of charge and without obligation. They will give you a realistic indication of your financing capacity. So you already know what price range you can afford.

Related to this: Check how much equity you can raise. If you have 20%, you are in a standard position. If you have 15%, you should expect an additional insurance cost. If you have less than 10%, many banks will say no.

Phase 2: Search for the property and preliminary appraisal

Once you know your price range, look for a home. You can use online real estate portals, local agents, advertisements. When you find a property that interests you, before making an official offer, consider asking for an independent appraisal (expensive, but very useful). A professional appraiser assesses whether the asking price is in line with the local market. Avoid unpleasant surprises when getting your mortgage approved.

Phase 3: Purchase promise and mortgage request

When you agree on the price, sign a promise to purchase (or compromise). This document binds you: if you withdraw without legal justification, you may lose a deposit. At the same time, it also binds the seller to sell to you at the agreed price.

Immediately afterwards, start the formal loan application with the chosen bank. The bank will carry out the viability test (examining your documents), commission an appraisal of the property on behalf of the bank (different from your private appraisal), and notify you of the outcome. If everything is OK, the bank issues the mortgage offer with interest rate, duration, amount, conditions.

Warning: the promise to purchase usually contains a release clause conditional on financing. It means that if the bank denies the mortgage, you can get out of the compromise without penalties. Have this point checked by a lawyer before signing.

Phase 4: Notarial deed and registration

Now the notary draws up the deed of sale on the basis of the purchase promise. The notary verifies the property data in the land register, verifies that the seller is actually the owner, calculates the transfer tax and notary fees. It provides you with a detailed estimate of all cost items.

The deed is signed by both parties in front of the notary (his authentication is required). At this point the deed is legally binding.

Phase 5: Payment of taxes and taking out the mortgage

The deed is registered in the land register. From this moment on you are the legal owners. The cantonal administration of Lucerne issues the invoice for the transfer tax. The notary's office issues the invoice for notary fees. You must pay both within the indicated period (usually 30 days).

Once the property is registered and the taxes are paid, the bank takes out the mortgage (transfers the money to your account). You can use this money to pay off the balance to the seller through the notary.

Practical tools for calculating your total budget

To plan the total amount you need:

1. mortgage sustainability calculator: enter the price of the property, your own funds, your income, and the calculator tells you if the mortgage is sustainable according to banking criteria of prudence.

2. Check the transfer tax rates of the Canton of Lucerne: the cantonal administration website shows the exact rate. Calculate the amount by applying this rate to the purchase price.

3. Request an estimate of notary fees: Contact a notary in the Canton of Lucerne directly and provide the approximate value of the property. It will give you an estimate of the costs of drafting the deed and registering it.

4. Put all the items together: property price + transfer tax + notary fees + mortgage insurance (if necessary) + maintenance costs and property taxes (annual, to be expected). This is your actual total budget.

The sooner you calculate these figures, the more knowledgeable you will be when searching and negotiating the property.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum percentage of own funds required to buy a home in Switzerland?
There is no minimum federal legal constraint, but banks usually require 20% of the purchase value as equity. Lower percentages are possible, but will result in additional mortgage insurance (which increases costs) and perhaps higher interest rates. Check with your bank what minimum percentage they accept in your case.
How does the mortgage sustainability test work and what documents do I need to submit?
The sustainability test evaluates whether you can repay the mortgage with your gross income, considering all fixed expenses, taxes, insurance and home maintenance. Banks ask for: account statements, tax returns for the last 2-3 years, employment contract, list of existing debts, property appraisal. There is no single federal formula; each bank has its own internal prudential criteria.
Is the transfer tax the same in all Swiss cantons?
No, absolutely not. The transfer tax is a cantonal responsibility: each canton (including Lucerne) decides the rate independently. If you buy in Lucerne you will pay the Lucerne rate; if you buy in another canton, the rate will be different. You must check the cantonal administration rate before calculating the total budget. It is a non-negotiable cost.
Are notary fees a fixed percentage of the house price?
No. Notary fees vary according to the amount of the property and the cantonal rates of Lucerne. It is not a uniform national percentage. You can ask the notary for an estimate before signing the compromise, so you know exactly how much you will have to pay. It is an amount established by cantonal law, non-negotiable, but quantifiable in advance.
If the bank declines the mortgage after I sign the purchase promise, can I get out of the compromise without loss?
Usually yes, if the purchase promise contains a conditional dissolution clause to the loan. In this case, if the bank denies the mortgage, you can get out of the compromise without penalty. But you need to check the text of your contract: not all compromises have this protection. Have the document checked by a local lawyer before signing it.
