Slab Naturalization: requirements and procedure (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 September 2026

How to become a Swiss citizen in Solothurn: years of residence, language and integration tests, municipal taxes and process times.

Context

In brief

  • Permit C + 10 years of residence: federal basis
  • Solothurn has specific language and integration criteria
  • Language test and administrative checks are mandatory
  • Municipal and cantonal taxes vary by territory

Key facts

  • What: Ordinary naturalization to become a Swiss citizen
  • Where: Canton of Solothurn and municipalities of northwestern Switzerland
  • Federal requirement: Residence permit (C) + 10 years of residence
  • Who manages: SEM (State Secretariat for Migration), cantonal administration, municipalities
  • Procedures: Language test, integration, security checks

Naturalization in the Canton of Solothurn represents the path through which a foreign resident acquires Swiss citizenship and gains full political and civil rights. The process is governed by three normative levels: federal law, Solothurn cantonal provisions, and local municipal procedures. At the federal level, the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) establishes migration criteria, while the Canton of Solothurn and its municipalities apply additional integration and administrative verification requirements.

One of the fundamental prerequisites is the possession of a residence permit, category C, which represents the most stable residence title available for foreigners in Switzerland. At the federal level, this permit is normally granted after ten years of continuous residence. In certain contexts, the years between the second and seventh year of residence can count as double towards reaching the ten-year threshold if the applicant has demonstrated solid professional and social integration.

Operational details

Practical implications of naturalization

Once Swiss citizenship has been acquired in the Canton of Solothurn, the new citizen gains access to rights previously inaccessible as a foreign resident. At the federal level, Switzerland practices direct democracy with four voting dates per year for federal consultations: the new citizen acquires the right to participate in these votes and in the cantonal and municipal votes of Solothurn. Additionally, they can sign a popular initiative (which requires 100,000 signatures in 18 months) or contribute to an optional referendum (which requires 50,000 signatures in 100 days).

From an economic and professional standpoint, Swiss citizenship removes any professional restrictions related to migratory status. A new Swiss citizen in Solothurn gains unrestricted access to the national job market, public competitions, and regulated professions. Additionally, they gain access to the complete social security system without temporal contribution constraints.

Step-by-step: what to do

Before submitting the naturalization application at the municipal level, the candidate must verify that they possess a valid C permit from the SEM, accumulate the ten years of residence required by federal law, and gather documents of linguistic integration such as language certificates and work references. Subsequently, they must obtain a declaration of absence of security problems from the Canton of Solothurn, complete the application form of the municipality of residence, and submit the entire documentation to the competent municipal office.

Key points

Where to go and required documents

The first point of contact is the population office (Einwohneramt) or the municipal integration office in your municipality of residence in Solothurn. There you can request an information meeting to verify if you meet the preliminary residence requirements and if your administrative situation is in order. The municipal office will provide you with an exact list of the required documentation and guide you through the next steps.

Typically required documents include a residence certificate or extract from the municipal registers documenting 10 years of continuity, valid identification documents (passport, ID card), language certificates for Swiss German (TELC, GOETHE diplomas or equivalents), a certificate of no criminal record requested by the Swiss authorities, socio-professional integration certificates (employer references, letters of recommendation), and the naturalization application form provided by the municipality.

Timelines and deadlines

Submitting the application to the municipality is the first formal step. Once submitted, the file moves to the Canton of Solothurn for the evaluation of integration criteria, then to the SEM at the federal level. The entire procedure from the municipal application to federal approval generally takes several weeks or months, depending on the completeness of the file and the administrative workload. Once naturalization is approved, the municipality of residence issues the Swiss citizenship certificate. This document is the legal basis for renewing the Swiss passport at the federal passport offices.

Frequently Asked Questions
How long do I have to wait before applying for naturalization in Solothurn?
The minimum federal requirement is ten years of continuous residence in Switzerland and the possession of a residence permit (category C). However, Canton Solothurn may have additional residency requirements at cantonal or municipal level. We recommend that you contact the population office of your municipality directly to confirm if you are eligible.
What is the cost of naturalization in Solothurn?
Naturalization fees are determined by the Canton of Solothurn and local municipalities and vary according to the local cantonal tax structure. Costs have not yet been specified. Contact the relevant municipal office to obtain an accurate estimate of the costs of naturalisation.
What language and integration tests do I need to pass?
Canton Solothurn requires a demonstration of knowledge of German (local language) and a mastery of the core values of Switzerland. The specific tests and levels required vary by municipality. Contact the cantonal integration office for precise information on the tests (e.g. TELC diplomas or equivalent).
Do I get federal and cantonal political rights after naturalization?
Yes. Once naturalized as a Swiss citizen, you acquire federal, cantonal and municipal voting rights. In Switzerland there are four federal voting dates each year for direct consultations. You can also subscribe to popular initiatives (100,000 signatures in 18 months) and optional referendums (50,000 signatures in 100 days).
Do I need to renew the C permit during the naturalization procedure?
No. During the naturalization procedure, your C permit remains valid and you must continue to comply with all Swiss laws. Naturalization does not interrupt the validity of your permit until the procedure is completed and approved by the federal authorities.
