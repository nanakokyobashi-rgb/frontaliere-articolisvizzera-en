The job market in the canton of Aargau follows the federal Swiss structure, where there is no national minimum wage, but some cantons have introduced their own cantonal minimum wages. Those working in Switzerland must know the fundamental rights: maximum working hours, minimum holiday duration, social contributions, and income tax.

The maximum working hours are set at 45 hours per week for industry, offices, and commerce, while other sectors can apply 50 hours. The minimum holidays are 4 weeks per year for adult employees, with 5 weeks for those under 20 years. Maternity is covered for 14 weeks at 80% through the maternity allowance (IPG), while paternity is 2 weeks.

Salary structure and training

Salaries in Switzerland vary significantly based on the level of education. Those with vocational training (CFC) or a high school diploma have access to different positions compared to those with a university degree. Companies operating in Aargau often follow collective labor agreements that set minimum wages and employment conditions for specific sectors.

Termination of the employment contract follows federal timelines: 1 month during the first year, 2 months from the 2nd to the 9th year, and 3 months from the 10th year onwards.

Operational details

Taxation of earned income in Switzerland works at three levels: federal (direct federal tax, IFD), cantonal and municipal. The canton of Aargau applies its own cantonal rates, while the municipalities have additional multipliers on the cantonal tax. A worker in Aargau sees both tax and social security contributions deducted from his paycheck.

Mandatory social contributions

Social contributions are deducted directly from the gross salary. The AVS/AI (old age and disability insurance) absorbs 5.3% of the employee's salary; the employer pays an additional 5.3%. Unemployment insurance (AD) and company insolvency compensation (AC) are 1.1% up to an annual limit. Accident insurance (LAINF/LAA) varies from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector of activity.

The second pillar (LPP, compulsory occupational pension) provides contributions as a percentage of the age group: 7% from 25 to 34 years, 10% from 35 to 44, 15% from 45 to 54, and 18% from 55 years up to the reference age for retirement. These contributions are deducted from your paycheck. For a complete analysis of your net salary, consult our payslip calculator which explains each salary item.

KVG (health insurance) is mandatory for all Swiss residents and must be taken out within 3 months of arriving in the canton. It is not a salary contribution, but rather a private insurance with per capita premiums that vary by canton and region. Average premiums in Aargau differ compared to other cantons; the canton provides premium reductions (subsidies) to those who are entitled to them.

Net vs gross

To calculate the net salary, tax withholdings (federal, cantonal, municipal) and social contributions must be subtracted from the gross amount. The resulting net amount depends on the personal situation (marital status, children, family dependents) and the place of domicile. In the canton of Aargau, as in the others, it is useful to use specific salary calculators to obtain a realistic estimate of net salary.

Key points

Anyone looking for work or negotiating a new contract in the canton of Aargau must know their rights and standard procedures. Here are the fundamental steps to orient yourself in the Swiss job market.

Check the applicable collective agreements

Before signing a contract, check whether your sector is subject to a collective bargaining agreement (CCL). These agreements, made between employer associations and unions, set minimum wages, hours and benefits for specific industries. If a collective agreement is in force in your sector, the employer is obliged to respect its terms. CCLs represent a protection tool for workers, ensuring that pay does not fall below sector standards.

Rights in case of dispute

If you believe that your salary is lower than what is foreseen by the collective agreement or by law, you can contact the competent cantonal authority. In the canton of Aargau, the cantonal employment office handles disputes relating to employment law. Many disputes are also resolved through out-of-court conciliation before resorting to the courts. This route is often faster and less expensive than the judicial route.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a cantonal minimum wage in Aargau?
Canton Aargau, like most Swiss cantons, has not introduced its own minimum wage. However, collective bargaining agreements, where in force, set sectoral minimum wages. There is no Swiss federal minimum wage either. If your contract is covered by a CCL, the minimum wage is the one provided for in the contract.
How do you calculate net from gross in Aargau?
From the gross salary are subtracted: federal, cantonal and municipal taxes; AVS/AI contributions (5.3%); AD/AC contributions (1.1% up to the maximum); LAINF/LAA (0.7–1.5% depending on the sector); and the contribution to the second LPP pillar (variable by age group). The resulting net also depends on the personal situation and the canton of domicile. Use a specific calculator for an accurate estimate.
What are the minimum holiday rights in Switzerland?
Each employee is entitled to a minimum of 4 weeks of vacation per year. Anyone under the age of 20 is entitled to 5 weeks. These are federal legal minimums; collective agreements and individual agreements may provide for longer periods.
What to do if the salary is not paid correctly?
If the salary does not correspond to the contract or the law, ask the employer for clarification. If the problem persists, you can contact the cantonal labour office in Aargau for free mediation, or consult a lawyer specialising in labour law.
