The labor market in the canton of Aargau follows the federal Swiss rules for determining wages, supplemented by cantonal regulations. Unlike some European countries, Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage: wage determination occurs through collective labor agreements (CLA) and company agreements.

Salary Structure by Education Level

Salary levels in Aargau are strongly correlated with professional education level. Apprentices in vocational training (VET — Federal Vocational Baccalaureate) have salary scales defined by CLAs and progress from the first to the third year of apprenticeship. A vocational training diploma accesses positions of skilled worker or clerk, with higher compensation than apprentices. Holders of higher specialist diplomas (federal exams) or university degrees receive even higher compensation.

The salary structure reflects the educational investment: an engineering graduate earns on average more than a technical profession graduate, who in turn earns more than an apprentice. Collective contracts specify minimum wages for each education level and professional category.

Key points

If you are looking for a job in Aargau or evaluating a salary offer, here are the concrete steps to verify if the compensation is adequate and which tools to use.

Step 1: Identify the applicable collective agreement

The first step is to identify the collective agreement (CA) that applies to the role and sector. Most positions in Aargau fall under a specific sector CA (e.g., CA for chemistry, pharmaceuticals, metallurgy, commerce). You can find the text of the contract on the company's website offering the job, on the website of the sector's employers' association, or at the Aargau cantonal employment office. The CA defines the minimum salary for your level of education and professional category. If the offer is below the CA minimum, it could be a sign of contractual issues.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum wage in Aargau?
Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage, but compensation is determined through collective bargaining agreements (CLAs) by sector. Each CCL in force in Aargau establishes the minimum wage by level of training and professional category. If it does not fall under a CCL, the wage offer is determined by the direct agreement with the company. We recommend that you always check the CCL applicable to your industry before accepting an offer.
How does the salary change depending on the degree of training?
In Aargau, as in all of Switzerland, wage levels progress with the degree of training. An apprentice in vocational training (CFC) starts with a minimum remuneration set by the CCL. A CFC graduate accesses skilled worker positions with salaries 40-50% higher. A higher specialist degree (federal exams) and a university degree result in further salary increases, reflecting the higher qualification.
What contributions are deducted from my gross salary?
In Switzerland, the employee automatically pays 5.3% for AVS/AI/IPG (old-age pension, disability, loss of earnings benefit), 1.1% for unemployment contributions (AD/AC) up to the annual ceiling, and 0.7–1.5% for accident insurance (LAINF/LAA) depending on the sector. Added to these are the federal direct (IFD), cantonal and municipal taxes, which vary according to income, canton and personal situation. The [calcolatore stipendio](nav:calculator) quantifies the impact on net.
What are the main sectors they hire in Aargau?
Aargau is an important centre for manufacturing, chemistry, pharmaceuticals, machinery, finance, construction, healthcare, technology and telecommunications. The region is home to some of the largest pharmaceutical and chemical multinationals in Switzerland. These sectors generally offer competitive wages and well-structured collective agreements with clear career progressions.
How do I check if the salary offered is adequate?
First step: check the collective agreement applicable to your industry and profession — the CCL defines the minimum wage. Second step: use the [calcolatore stipendio](nav:calculator) to compare your offer with the average salary range by profession, experience, degree of training and canton. Step three: Calculate the net available and evaluate supplementary benefits. If the offer is less than the minimum CCL, contact the cantonal labour office in Aargau.
