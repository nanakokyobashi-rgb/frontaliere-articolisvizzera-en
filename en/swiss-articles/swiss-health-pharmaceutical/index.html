Switzerland continues to be a major hub for research and development of new drugs thanks to the growing use of artificial intelligence (AI). Swiss pharmaceutical companies are investing heavily to harness the potential of AI in discovering new treatments and optimizing clinical trials. In particular, giants like Roche are focusing on AI to develop innovative therapies against obesity and other pathologies. For example, Roche has announced an investment of 2.5 billion Swiss francs by 2026 to develop new treatments thanks to AI.

'AI represents a groundbreaking shift in pharmaceutical research, enabling us to accelerate the discovery of new treatments and personalize therapies,' says a Roche representative.

Swiss cities like Basel and Zurich have become centers of excellence for pharmaceutical research, thanks to the presence of important research institutes and leading companies in the sector. The Swiss regulations on clinical research, updated in 2014 with the law on the ordinance on clinical trials, support innovation while ensuring patient safety. The benefits of AI include:

  • Rapid analysis of large amounts of data
  • Identification of patterns and correlations not immediately apparent
  • Optimization of clinical trials
  • Reduction of costs and development times

Operational details

The Impact of AI on Pharmaceutical Research

The artificial intelligence is revolutionizing the way new drugs are discovered and developed. Thanks to AI, Swiss pharmaceutical companies can analyze vast amounts of data in record time, identifying potential candidates for new treatments with greater precision. This not only speeds up the discovery process but also reduces the costs associated with traditional clinical trials. For example, according to a report by Swissmedic, the Swiss agency regulating therapeutic products, the use of AI has reduced the time to develop new drugs by 30% in the last 5 years.

Strategic Investments

Roche, one of the leading players in the Swiss pharmaceutical sector with headquarters in Basel, is investing 3 billion Swiss francs in the development of new treatments for obesity. The company is using AI to identify new therapeutic targets and optimize candidate molecules. This approach not only improves treatment effectiveness but also reduces the time needed to bring them to market. Novartis, another major Swiss pharmaceutical company with headquarters in Basel, has invested 1.5 billion Swiss francs in an AI-based research platform to accelerate the discovery of new drugs.

More Efficient Clinical Trials

AI is also improving clinical trials, enabling a more precise selection of patients and efficient management of collected data. This leads to more reliable results and a reduction in the time to develop new drugs. For example, a clinical trial conducted by the University of Zurich used AI to select patients and reduced the recruitment time by 50%. The Swiss regulations on clinical trials, updated in 2020, support the use of AI, predicting an accelerated procedure for the approval of clinical trials that use innovative technologies.

Key points

What it means for Swiss patients

The use of AI in pharmaceutical research will have a significant impact on Swiss patients. Thanks to more effective and personalized treatments, patients will be able to benefit from innovative therapies for conditions such as obesity and mental health disorders. For example, according to a recent study, the use of AI in pharmaceutical research could reduce the development time of a new drug from 10-15 years to 5-7 years. In addition, the reduction in development and approval times for drugs means that patients will be able to access new therapies more quickly. In Switzerland, cities like Basel and Zurich have already become major hubs for pharmaceutical research thanks to the presence of leading pharmaceutical companies such as Novartis and Roche. The Swiss regulations on drug authorization, regulated by the Swiss Agency for Therapeutic Products (Swissmedic), provide for an approval process that can last up to 330 days. The use of AI could simplify this process, reducing approval times and increasing efficiency.

'The integration of AI in pharmaceutical research represents a great opportunity to improve public health in Switzerland' — said the director of Swissmedic.
