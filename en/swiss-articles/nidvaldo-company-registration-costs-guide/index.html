Opening a business in Switzerland involves a series of administrative and tax requirements that vary according to the canton. In the Canton of Nidvaldo, as in all of Switzerland, those who intend to start a business must deal with three levels of taxation: federal, cantonal and municipal. The choice of legal form — sole proprietorship, partnership, partnership, limited liability company or public limited company — determines both the administrative obligations and the tax burden. Each form has different advantages and constraints, and requires specific skills in management and financial reporting.

Registration in the Trade Register is mandatory for all professional activities. It is not optional: it is the first step to operate legally in Switzerland. Each canton maintains its own register, and Nidvaldo is no exception. Once

Operational details

Once the activity is registered in the commercial register of Nidwalden, the entrepreneur enters the three-tiered fiscal system that characterizes Switzerland. It is not a complicated overlap, but a parallel structure: direct federal tax (IFD) calculated on income and assets through tax return, cantonal tax, and municipal tax. Each level has its own rates, and the overall burden depends on both income and geographic location of the activity. The canton of Nidwalden and its municipalities apply their own rates on the cantonal and municipal components. The municipal multiplier (applied to the cantonal rate) varies from municipality to municipality, even within the same canton of Nidwalden. Whoever opens a business in Stans, the capital of the canton, will have a different fiscal burden compared to Buochs, Beckenried, or Hergiswil. This municipal variability is crucial in the choice of operational headquarters: a difference of a few percentage points in the multiplier can mean thousands of francs in difference in net annual balances. ### Mandatory social security contributions Whoever hires their first employee must register with the federal social security offices. AVS/AI/IPG contributions amount to 5.3% on gross salary for the employee, while the employer pays an equivalent amount (5.3% additional). In addition, there are: - Unemployment insurance (AD): 1.1% up to a maximum annual amount (annually revised amount). - Accident insurance (LAA/LAINF): varies from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the activity sector and risk profile. - Pension fund (LPP, second pillar): from a certain salary threshold, the contribution scales by age bracket: 7% (25-34 years), 10% (35-44), 15% (45-54), 18% (55+ until reference age). These contributions are paid to the cantonal AVS office or a delegated compensation organ, according to the deadlines communicated (usually monthly or quarterly). They are not deductible from the employee's salary for the mandatory pre-retirement component. ### Mandatory health insurance Mandatory health insurance (LAMal/KVG) is required for all residents in Switzerland, including entrepreneurs. It is not a tax or a salary deduction, but a private premium paid directly to the chosen insurance fund. It must be concluded within 3 months of arrival or registration of residence. Premiums vary by canton, region, and age bracket; the canton of Nidwalden offers subsidies (reduced premiums) for those with incomes below certain thresholds. The deductible (annual amount that the patient pays out of pocket before the insurance intervenes) ranges from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 for adults, depending on the chosen coverage level. The higher the deductible, the lower the monthly premium. In addition to the mandatory LAMal, it is recommended to evaluate supplementary coverages (injuries, professional civil liability, loss of earnings) according to the nature of the activity.

Key points

Seven Key Steps to Launching Your Business: A Guide to Nidvaldo's Procedures

To start your activity in Nidvaldo, you must follow a precise sequence of obligations. The first step is to choose the legal form: consult a tax consultant, accountant, or notary to decide between individual company, Sarl, SA, or other forms. The choice conditions responsibility, taxation, administration, and management costs.

Next, verify the availability of the company name. The activity name must be available at the Nidvaldo Commercial Register and not confuse with other companies already registered in the same canton. Also, check the availability of the internet domain and social media, especially if online communication is central to the business.

The third step is to open a dedicated bank account. Even for an individual company, it is practical to open a separate company account from the personal account to track cash flows, invoices, and payments. Many Swiss banks offer dedicated accounts for start-ups with reduced fees.

Presenting the registration application to the Nidvaldo Commercial Register is the next step. The form and required documents vary depending on the legal form. Normally, you need: a copy of the statutes (for SA/Sarl), identification of the legal representative, proof of business address, and a certificate of concession (for certain sectors). Submit the original or a notarized copy to the competent office.

Once registered, you must register with the cantonal and municipal tax offices. The cantonal tax administration will open a file and ask for periodic tax declarations (usually annual, except for CAA income — annual acquired accounting).

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the easiest legal form to open a business in Nidvaldo?
The sole proprietorship is the simplest form: it does not require minimum capital, does not require written bylaws and does not involve shareholder meetings. However, the owner is liable for debts with personal assets (unlimited liability). A limited liability company (Sarl) limits liability to paid-in capital, but requires more administrative fulfillment. Consult an accountant to choose the best solution for your specific case and industry.
How much does it cost to register in the Nidvaldo Trade Register?
Registration fees vary depending on the canton and legal form chosen. In general, a sole proprietorship entails lower costs than a Sarl or SA. To know the exact amount and any consultancy costs (notary, accountant), contact directly the office of the Commercial Registry of the Canton of Nidvaldo. Processing times can range from a few weeks to several months.
What social security contributions do I have to pay as an entrepreneur with employees?
When you hire your first employee, you need to sign up for Social Security. AVS/AI/IPG contributions are 5.3% of the gross salary for the employee (plus 5.3% from the employer); unemployment insurance (AD) is 1.1% up to an annual ceiling; accident insurance (LAA) varies from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector. From the second pillar (PPI), contributions range by age group: 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54), 18% (55+).
Is health insurance mandatory if I open a business in Nidvaldo?
Yes, health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory for all residents in Switzerland, including entrepreneurs. It is not a tax or a salary deduction, but a private premium paid to the insurance fund. It must be stipulated within 3 months of arrival or registration of domicile. Premiums vary by canton and region; Canton Nidvaldo offers income subsidies below certain thresholds. The deductible ranges from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 for adults, depending on the level of coverage.
What are the three levels of taxation in Switzerland and how do they affect my business?
Swiss taxation is on three parallel levels: direct federal tax (DFI), cantonal tax and municipal tax. Each canton (and each municipality) applies its own rates. In Canton Nidvaldo, the municipal multiplier varies from municipality to municipality, affecting the final tax burden. A difference of a few percentage points in the multiplier can mean thousands of francs of difference in the annual budgets. Consult a local accountant to estimate the impact on your business.
