The issue of child education is a central issue for border workers active in Switzerland, especially following the entry into force of the New Agreement on Border Workers on 1 January 2024. The decision to enroll their children in Swiss or Italian institutions has different implications in terms of logistics and documentation. In Switzerland, access to compulsory education is regulated by the individual cantons, who manage the educational offer according to local directives. For border crossers, the key principle lies in the distinction between tax residence and place of work. While the worker is subject to withholding tax in Switzerland, the application of the tax credit in Italy, through the EC framework of Model 730, guarantees the evasion of double taxation as provided for in the Convention of 9 March 1976. School enrolment in Switzerland usually requires the demonstration of an effective link with the territory, although the procedures vary greatly from canton to canton. For the purposes of Article 9 of the Agreement,” old border crossers " are those who complied with the tax requirements and who worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais on 17 July 2023 or in the period from 31 December 2018 to 17 July 2023. For income from work covered by Article 9, taxation remains exclusively at source in Switzerland; for other workers the ordinary regime applies, with taxation in Italy, credit for Swiss tax and Swiss withholding tax at 80% of the ordinary rates. The 10,000 euros of Article 4 of L. 83/2023 are only the income limit referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of L. 147/2013 and not a general deductible for new border crossers. School access dynamics

The integration of children into the Swiss school system requires an accurate assessment of the educational path. The cantons independently define the admission criteria for non-residents, often related to the place of work of the parents. It is essential to monitor the official communications of the Federal Statistical Office (BFS) to understand migration and school trends, although tax rates and access rules are not determined by bodies such as the UFAS or the BFS, but by the competent cantonal administrations.

Operational details

The practical analysis of homeschooling for the children of border residents reveals significant logistical challenges. When opting for Swiss schooling, the scheduling must align with the parent's work hours, often characterized by heavy transborder traffic flows. It is important to consider that, unlike Italy, the Swiss system often includes a return home for lunch or management through local school cafeterias, with varying costs and arrangements depending on the municipality. Planning must also include necessary insurance coverage. Frontier residents with G permit, for instance, retain the right to choose LAMal, the mandatory health insurance, which may have varying deductibles between CHF 300 and CHF 2500. The choice of sickness fund is a critical step that impacts family living expenses. For those working in Switzerland, take-home pay reflects mandatory deductions such as the AVS/AI/IPG (5.3% off the employee's pay), unemployment insurance (AD/AC) at 1.1% up to a CHF 148,200 income cap, and LAINF (0.7-1.5%) and LPP (7-18% based on age) contributions. These elements, combined with the tax burden, define the net income available for educational and extracurricular expenses. The fundamental difference between the two systems lies in the educational organization and the involvement of the local community. While Italy guarantees continuity in education through a national system, Switzerland's flexible cantonal organization allows for a quicker adjustment to local labor market needs, which is regularly monitored by the SECO. Frequently Asked Questions
Is it possible to enroll children in school in Switzerland if they are resident in Italy?
Enrolment in compulsory education in Switzerland for children of non-resident border workers is subject to the specific regulations of each Canton. Generally, access is subject to residence in the Swiss Municipality. It is necessary to contact the competent municipal school office to verify the possibilities of derogation or the specific procedures for frontier workers.
What are the taxes that the border worker pays in Switzerland?
The border crossing is subject to income tax at source in Switzerland. Rates are set by federal and cantonal laws and administered by the Federal Tax Administration (FTA). Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit (EC framework of 730), in accordance with the Convention of 9 March 1976.
What changes for new frontier workers from 2024?
From 1 January 2024, the New Border Arrangement (signed on 23 December 2020 and ratified by Law 83 of 13 June 2023) is in force. For Article 9 of the Agreement, “old frontier workers” are people who met the tax requirements and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais on 17 July 2023 or during the period from 31 December 2018 through 17 July 2023. Article 9 keeps covered employment income taxable exclusively at source in Switzerland; other workers follow the ordinary regime, with ordinary taxation in Italy, a credit for Swiss tax and Swiss withholding at 80% of the ordinary rates. The €10,000 figure in Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 is only the income limit referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013; it is not a general allowance for new frontier workers.
