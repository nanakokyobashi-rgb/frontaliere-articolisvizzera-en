New car sales down in August, almost one in three is electric (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 1 September 2026

Sales of new cars in Switzerland fell by 0.6% in August, with almost one in three cars sold electric.

Context

In brief

  • New car sales in Switzerland decreased by 0.6% in August.
  • Almost one in three cars sold is electric.
  • Hybrid cars account for 37% of sales.

Key facts

  • What: Decline in new car sales
  • When: August 2026
  • Where: Switzerland
  • Who: Auto-Switzerland
  • Amount: 16,000 vehicles

New car sales in Switzerland recorded a 0.6% decrease in August, with 16,000 vehicles registered, according to the importers' association Auto-Switzerland. This figure represents a decline compared to the same period last year, which was already weak. Alternative drives reached a market share of 79%, with a significant increase in electric cars, which account for 30% of sales, up from 20% the previous year. Conventional hybrid cars are at 37%, while gasoline cars have fallen to 16% and diesels to 5%.

Sales Details

In detail, conventional hybrid cars maintained a 37% share, a slight increase from 36% the previous year. Electric cars saw a significant increase, rising from 20% to 30% of the market, with 4800 cars registered, a 49% increase. Plug-in hybrids remained stable at 12%. Gasoline cars fell from 24% to 16%, while diesels decreased from 7% to 5%.

Overall, in the first eight months of 2026, 151,600 vehicles were registered, an increase of 1.8% compared to the same period last year. Hybrid cars reached 55,600 units, an increase of 2%, while electric cars saw a 23% increase with 37,600 units. Gasoline cars decreased by 16% to 31,200 units, while plug-in hybrids increased by 17% to 18,800 units. Diesels decreased by 24% to 8400 units. The share of 4x4s increased from 50% to 53%.

Operational details

Practical Implications

New car sales in Switzerland showed a downward trend in August, with a 0.6% decrease compared to the same period last year. This data reflects an overall weak market situation, which has a significant impact on the prospects of the automotive sector. The market share of alternative propulsion cars has increased significantly, reaching 79%, with a particular focus on conventional hybrids and electric cars.

Market Analysis

The increase in electric cars is particularly significant, with a market share of 30%, up from 20% last year. This trend is in line with the growing demand for eco-friendly and sustainable vehicles. However, gasoline and diesel cars continue to lose market share, with 16% and 5% respectively. Plug-in hybrid cars remained stable at 12%.

Future Prospects

The director of Auto-Switzerland, Thomas Rücker, emphasized the urgency of deregulation and greater flexibility in CO2 emission regulations, in line with the rest of Europe. He also warned that new taxes or regulatory burdens on electric vehicles would have a counterproductive effect, stating that "any additional tax is out of place."

Brand Ranking

Volkswagen led with 17,000 vehicles sold between January and August, followed by Skoda with 14,200, BMW with 12,500, Mercedes with 12,100, Audi with 10,200, Toyota with 8,800, Volvo with 5,700, Seat/Cupra with 5,700, Renault with 5,400, Dacia with 4,900, Hyundai with 4,600, KIA with 4,000, BYD with 3,900, Tesla with 3,400, Ford with 3,200, and Peugeot with 3,200. All other manufacturers did not reach 3,000 units.
