The first authorization

In Zurich, Finma authorized a fund open to the public that invests directly in Bitcoin, the first of its kind. In a statement issued on Tuesday, the authority justified the decision by citing the growing interest in cryptocurrency investments, especially in Bitcoin.

For the first time, Finma explains, this is a publicly traded fund for the public that invests directly in the cryptocurrency. The product is referred to in the text as a Bitcoin ETF. The decision therefore concerns an instrument intended for the public and traded on the stock market, not merely an announcement about investors' interest.

Finma assumes that the approval may increase investor protection. At the same time, the authority highlights the risk associated with investments in Bitcoin and accompanies the authorization with an explicit reminder to exercise caution regarding market risk.

The protection indicated in the statement also stems from the measures required of supervised institutions. These must reduce operational risks and custody-related risks. According to Finma, applying the rules of conduct contributes to greater protection compared with direct investments in cryptocurrencies.

The news therefore contains two distinct elements: the authorization of the first publicly traded fund with direct investment in Bitcoin and the obligation for supervised institutions to adopt measures concerning operational and custody risks. In the same statement, however, Finma keeps the warning about cryptocurrency risk at the forefront.

Operational details

Two aspects not to be confused

Operational safeguards and market risk

Finma's communication separates investor protection from the risk associated with Bitcoin's performance. The first aspect concerns how supervised institutions must organize the management of the investment, reducing operational risks and those related to custody. The second, by contrast, concerns the value of the invested capital, which is exposed to the sharp fluctuations of cryptocurrencies.

For the Swiss public, the documented new feature is therefore the existence of an investment channel available to the public and traded on an exchange, with direct investment in Bitcoin. The comparison with direct investment does not concern a promise of return, but the level of protection associated with the conduct rules applied by supervised institutions.

Finma also draws attention to the effect of fluctuations on capital. According to the statement, this aspect must be taken into account in the investment decision, especially in relation to the size of the amount invested. The distinction remains clear: the authorization concerns the structure and safeguards of the investment, while Bitcoin's market risk remains very high.

The scope of the news

No provider identified

Finma's statement does not mention a specific ETF provider. At the request of the news agency AWP, a spokesperson explained that several fund providers had submitted or announced applications. The authority, however, does not comment further on the ongoing authorization procedures.

This leaves the main fact established, but does not attribute the product to a specific company. The source documents the first authorization of a public investment fund that invests directly in Bitcoin and clarifies that the fund is traded on an exchange. It does not, however, indicate which of the various providers submitted the authorized product.

The informational limitation is accompanied by a very precise warning about risk. Finma does not reduce the issue to the custody or organization of institutions: it also draws attention to the market risk directly associated with Bitcoin and defines it as very high. The assessment also applies to an approved ETF.

The clearest part of the warning is this:

Strong fluctuations in cryptocurrencies can significantly reduce the value of the invested capital.

Finma therefore classifies investments in Bitcoin as highly risky, including those made through the new ETF. In the statement, the amount invested is identified as an element to be taken into consideration in the investment decision. No numerical threshold is provided, and no expected return is presented.

The verifiable picture thus remains limited: Switzerland has authorized for the first time a public fund traded on an exchange that invests directly in Bitcoin; supervised institutions must take measures regarding operational and custody risks; market risk remains very high, and the provider's name has not been disclosed.
