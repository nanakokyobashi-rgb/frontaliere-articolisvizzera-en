Axpo has begun construction in Suhr, in the canton of Aargau, of a solar plant on arable land. The project has a precise objective: to show whether agricultural use and electricity production can coexist on the same area.

An area divided between energy and agriculture

The plant will have a power output of 600 kilowatts peak, indicated by the abbreviation kWp. The experimental area measures 2,8 hectares, but the modules will be installed on 1,2 hectares. They will be positioned approximately three metres above the ground and can be oriented according to the season.

The area underneath will not be taken away from cultivation: it will continue to be used for agriculture. This is the operational focus of the experiment, because the project maintains both the productive function of the field and the energy function of the modules in the same space.

The comparison entrusted to FiBL

Axpo, the canton of Aargau and FiBL, the Research Institute of Organic Agriculture based in Frick, are collaborating on the project. The institute will assess the effects of the modules on yields, water balance and biodiversity.

The results from the area equipped with the modules will be compared with those obtained in a nearby field without panels. Emmi will purchase all the solar energy generated and use it directly at its plant in Suhr.

Axpo will be responsible for both the construction and operation of the plant; the farmer who already managed the field will continue to cultivate it. FiBL will handle the field research, Axpo the technical aspects, the farmer the cultivation and Emmi the use of the electricity. In Suhr, the project thus brings cultivation, business activity and electricity generation together on the same area.

Operational details

The practical value of the project lies not only in the 600 kWp. It lies in the fact that the same area is being studied as an agricultural field and as a surface for solar modules. For those following energy transformations in Switzerland, the question remains concrete: how do crops, water balance and biodiversity change when modules are installed above part of the land?

The nearby field without modules serves as a benchmark. This makes it possible, in terms of the experimental design, to separate the effects of the presence of the modules from those linked to cultivation. However, it does not anticipate the result: the project indicates which aspects will be studied, not what the outcome will be.

The distribution of the surfaces is also a fact to be read carefully. The modules cover 1,2 hectares of the total 2,8; the surface beneath them remains agricultural. The experiment therefore does not describe a complete replacement of the field with panels, but a coexistence to be observed through data on yields, water and biodiversity. The possibility of orienting the modules depending on the season is another variable stated by the project.

At the industrial level, the electricity already has a recipient: Emmi will purchase all of it and use it directly at the Suhr plant. The chain is also clear in terms of responsibilities: Axpo builds and manages, the farmer continues to cultivate, and FiBL studies the effects. These are useful elements for following any updates without confusing production, research and use of the energy.
