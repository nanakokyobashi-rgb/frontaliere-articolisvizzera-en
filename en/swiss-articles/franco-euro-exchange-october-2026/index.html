Key points

The safe-haven role

The source attributes part of the franc's strength to its role as a safe-haven currency. When economic or geopolitical uncertainty increases, international investors tend to seek assets considered relatively safe. The Swiss franc is described as one of the historic destinations for this capital.

According to the analysis, the franc's reputation as a safe-haven currency is supported by the political stability of the Swiss Confederation, the strength of its institutions, the credibility of its monetary policy, and the traditional prudence in managing public finances. These factors help explain demand for the currency, but they do not constitute a guarantee against every exchange-rate movement.

The limit of protection

International tensions have refocused attention on defensive currencies and encouraged renewed interest in the franc. As long as the international environment remains characterized by uncertainty, demand for instruments considered safe could continue to support it. But the text also sets a limit: a safe-haven currency is not immune to fluctuations. The franc can weaken significantly when risk appetite improves in the markets.
