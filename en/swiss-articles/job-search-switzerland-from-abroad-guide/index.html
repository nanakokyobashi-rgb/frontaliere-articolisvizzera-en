Searching for a job in Switzerland from abroad can be a complex process, but with the right preparation and information, you can find interesting opportunities. The New Cross-Border Workers' Agreement, signed on 23 December 2020 and in force since 1 January 2024, introduced new rules for cross-border commuters. The deductible for new cross-border commuters has been increased to €10,000, while old cross-border commuters benefit from a transitional regime until 2033 with an exemption of €7,500.

Job Portals

There are several job portals in Switzerland that can help you find opportunities. Some of the most popular include JobScout24, Indeed, and LinkedIn. These portals offer a wide range of job openings in various industries and can be a great starting point for your search.

CV preparation

Preparing a CV in Swiss format is crucial to increase your chances of landing an interview. The Swiss CV should include detailed information about your career path, skills, and education. It is also important to translate your CV into one of the official languages of Switzerland, such as German, French or Italian, depending on the region you want to work in.

Operational details

Practical Implications of the New Frontier Agreement

The New Frontier Agreement, in force since 1 January 2024, introduced several changes that may affect frontier workers. One of the main innovations is the increase in the deductible for new frontier workers to €10,000. This means that new frontiersmen can benefit from a higher deductible than old frontiermen, who have a deductible of €7,500 until 2033.

Comparisons between old and new frontiersmen

Old frontier workers, i.e. those who were already such before 17 July 2023, benefit from a transitional regime that allows them to maintain the exemption of €7,500 until 2033. This transitional regime has been introduced to ensure a smooth transition to the new rules.

Procedure for obtaining a work permit

To obtain a work permit in Switzerland, you must follow a specific procedure. First, you need to find a job offer and get an employment contract. Subsequently, it is necessary to apply for a work permit from the competent authorities. The procedure varies depending on the country of origin and the type of permit you need.

Key points

Procedure for obtaining a work permit in Switzerland

Obtaining a work permit in Switzerland is a process that requires attention to detail and knowledge of the local rules. Here's a step-by-step guide to help you navigate the process:

Step 1: Find a job offer

The first step in obtaining a work permit in Switzerland is to find a job offer. You can use job portals like JobScout24, Indeed, and LinkedIn to search for opportunities. Once you get a job offer, you will need to get an employment contract.

Step 2: Apply for the work permit

After obtaining an employment contract, you will need to apply for your work permit with the relevant authorities. The procedure varies depending on your country of origin and the type of permit you need. It is important to find out about local practices and the expectations of Swiss employers.

Step 3: Register with the local authorities

Once you have obtained your work permit, you will need to register with the local authorities. This includes opening a Swiss bank account and, if necessary, finding accommodation. It is also important to find out about taxes and social security contributions.

Step 4: Health insurance

Cross-border commuters have the right to opt out for health insurance (KVG) in Switzerland. Deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500. It's important to choose a deductible that fits your financial and healthcare needs.
