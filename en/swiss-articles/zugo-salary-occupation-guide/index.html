The labour market in the canton of Zug is a dynamic and diverse Swiss employment centre. Anyone seeking work in the canton or negotiating contractual conditions must be aware of the rights and duties established by federal and cantonal legislation. From the calculation of salaries to statutory holidays, from compulsory social contributions to protections against dismissal: the rules are uniform for all Swiss employees, regardless of the sector.

Sectors and employment opportunities

The canton of Zug offers opportunities in various sectors: agriculture, industry, construction, commerce, services, tourism, health, education and technology.

Operational details

How they calculate salary and mandatory contributions

When negotiating a salary in the canton of Zug, it is crucial to understand the deductions applied to the gross salary. Each employee automatically pays contributions to:

AVS/AI/IPG (Old Age and Survivors Insurance, Disability Insurance, Loss of Earnings Allowance): 5.3% of gross salary. The employer pays an equivalent amount (10.6% total with employee contribution).

Unemployment Insurance (AD): 1.1% up to an indexed annual ceiling.

Accident insurance (LAInf/LAA): 0.7–1.5% according to the sector of activity, paid mainly by the employer.

Occupational pension (LPP/BVG): Contributions calculated on a coordinated salary (gross salary minus annual deduction) vary from 7% for age 25–34 years, 10% for 35–44, 15% for 45–54, up to 18% for 55+ years up to the reference age.

Direct federal tax: Withdrawn at source by the employer according to personal rate, varies by canton and municipality of residence.

This facility is legal and mandatory throughout Switzerland. The net salary the worker receives depends on the gross, social security contributions, payroll tax and any union or private insurance deductions.

Key points

Looking for work and negotiating in the canton of Zug

Those wishing to seek employment in the canton of Zug can contact the cantonal office of labour and economy of Zug for information on sectors, availability and employment data. Consulting Swiss job search sites is another effective option. It is essential to check whether the sector of interest is covered by a collective agreement (CCL) and what minimum wage it provides. When collecting multiple offers, the comparison should not be limited to the gross amount: consider place, hours, additional holidays, bonuses and complementary insurance.

Calculate your net salary and plan your finances

Once you have received an offer, it is advisable to use a salary calculation tool to estimate the net. Check how AVS/AI contributions (5.3%), LPP (7–18% according to age), unemployment insurance (1.1%) and direct federal tax vary affect pay. The cost of living in the canton of Zug includes rent, public transport, LAMal health insurance, food and energy: UST/BFS data provide reliable comparisons on regional and national costs.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a mandatory minimum wage for all sectors in the canton of Zug?
No, the canton of Zug does not have a uniform cantonal minimum wage applicable to all sectors. However, many sectors are covered by collective bargaining agreements (CLAs) that set specific minimum wages by grade and level of training. It is essential to check if your industry has a CCL. Contact the cantonal labour office of Zug, the trade union of your category or the employer to check the specific contractual conditions.
How much is withheld from my gross salary for contributions and taxes?
From the gross salary are withheld: AVS/AI/IPG (5.3%), unemployment insurance (1.1% up to the annual maximum), LPP/BVG (7–18% according to age, calculated on coordinated salary), and direct federal payroll tax. The exact amount depends on the gross, age, canton of residence and municipality. Use an online salary calculator to get an accurate estimate of your net salary.
What are the minimum guaranteed rights for holidays and working hours?
In the canton of Zug, as throughout Switzerland, every employee is entitled to: a minimum of 4 weeks of holiday per year (5 for workers under 20), a maximum of 45 hours per week for industry, offices and retail (50 hours for other sectors), 14 weeks of maternity leave at 80% of pay and 2 weeks of paternity leave.
Can I be fired for no reason in the canton of Zug?
No. The employer must comply with statutory cancellation terms: 1 month during the first year of employment, 2 months from the second to the ninth year, 3 months from the tenth year onwards. An unjustified or discriminatory dismissal may be challenged at the cantonal conciliation authority within 30 days. The union of your category can assist in the dispute and protection of your rights.
Where can I find information on wages and collective agreements for my industry?
Contact the cantonal office of labour and economy of the canton of Zug, the trade union of your professional category, or consult the data of the Swiss Statistics Office (UST/BFS). If your industry has a collective agreement, the employer must provide you with a copy. To calculate your expected net salary, use an online pay calculation tool.
