The tax system in Aargau

The Swiss tax system, also applied in Aargau, provides for three levels of taxation: federal, cantonal and municipal. The Federal Council sets the federal direct tax (IFD), while each canton has its own laws and multipliers. Municipalities, in turn, apply a multiplier to the cantonal rate. This tripartite system creates significant geographic differentiation between the various Swiss regions, with Aargau presenting competitive rates compared to other cantons.

Operational details

Job opportunities in canton Aargau

Canton Aargau offers a dynamic labour market with opportunities in different sectors. According to official data, the maximum working time is 45 hours/week for the industrial, office and sales sectors, and up to 50 hours for other sectors. Workers are entitled to a minimum of 4 weeks of holiday per year (5 for workers under 20), while maternity covers 14 weeks at 80% of salary (IPG) and paternity 2 weeks.

Access to the labour market for foreigners requires the appropriate permits. The L permit is for short periods (up to 1 year), the B permit is renewable, while the C permit, required for long-term residence, normally requires 10 years of residence (reduced to 5 for EU/EFTA citizens). Border workers obtain the G permit, specific for those who reside abroad but work in Switzerland.

Economic and social aspects

The cost of living in Switzerland remains one of the highest in Europe, with considerable differences between cantons. LAMal health insurance is mandatory for all residents and must be taken out within 3 months of arrival. Premiums are calculated on an individual basis and vary by region, with deductibles ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 for adults. Many cantons, including Aargau, offer subsidies to reduce the cost of premiums.

Key points

Practical procedure for job search in Switzerland

For those seeking employment in Switzerland, including candidates for the canton of Aargau, it is essential to follow a structured procedure. It will start with preparing a curriculum vitae that meets Swiss standards, highlighting education, work experience, language skills and references. The CV should be written in at least two languages, preferably German and English for Aargau.

Job search can take place through various channels: specialized online portals, temporary employment agencies, job fairs and direct contacts with companies. It is important to note that Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage, although some cantons have introduced specific minimum rates. The interested company will then start the procedures to obtain the necessary work permit for a candidate.

Documents and deadlines

The documents required to work in Switzerland include: passport or identity card, work or residence permit, social security number (AVS), proof of residence, and professional qualification documents. Regarding housing, federal law provides a maximum security deposit of three months' rent, to be paid into a blocked account in the tenant's name. The landlord's termination is valid only on an official cantonal form, and any disputes must be submitted within 30 days to the conciliation authority.

For those moving to Switzerland, it is essential to take out LAMal health insurance within three months of arrival. Frequently Asked Questions
Which sectors offer the most job opportunities in Switzerland in 2026?
In 2026, the Swiss labour market concentrates opportunities especially in the fields of engineering, technology, health and logistics, both for Swiss citizens and for foreign workers in possession of the required permits.
How is the tax system structured in Aargau?
The tax system of Aargau follows the Swiss model at three levels: direct federal tax established by the Federal Council, cantonal tax with its own laws and municipal tax that applies a specific multiplier on the cantonal, creating geographical differences between municipalities.
What work permits are required for border workers?
Border workers residing abroad and working in Switzerland must obtain the G permit. Other permits include L for short periods (up to one year), B renewable for longer stays and C for long-term residence, normally after 10 years of stay (5 for EU/EFTA citizens).
