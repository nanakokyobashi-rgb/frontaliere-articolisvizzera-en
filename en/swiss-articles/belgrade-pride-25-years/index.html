The Pride in Belgrade celebrates 25 years, an event that returns to its origins. The event, which began in 2001, was marked by violence and attacks. Today, Pride 25 has gathered the same slogan 'Ima meta za sve nas' ('There is room for all of us'). The event is authorized, protected, and well-attended, but the demands of the Lgbtq+ community, from civil unions to gender recognition, remain unanswered.

Achievements and challenges

In the last 25 years, the Lgbtq+ community in Serbia has made significant progress. In 2009, the Serbian Constitutional Court declared discrimination based on sexual orientation unconstitutional. However, many of the community's demands remain unmet. For example, Serbia has not yet adopted a law on civil unions, despite international pressures and recommendations from the Council of Europe.

Comparison with Switzerland

Switzerland, a country known for its neutrality and progressiveness, has made significant strides in Lgbtq+ rights. In 2007, same-sex marriage was legalized in some cantons, such as Geneva and Zurich. In 2021, Switzerland legalized same-sex marriage nationwide, becoming the 29th country in the world to do so. This comparison highlights the differences in Lgbtq+ rights progress between Serbia and Switzerland.

Operational details

In Belgrade, Pride celebrated its 25 years of history, returning to its origins with an event that crossed the heart of the city. The march left and arrived at Manjež Park, passing in front of the government building and in view of Parliament. Protected by police cordons in some critical points, the procession marched peacefully and festively through the streets of the centre. The godmother of the year, journalist Danica Vučenić, spoke in front of the government headquarters, stressing that Pride takes place in a period of glorification of violence and serious human rights violations.

Not far away, in front of the square of the Church of the Ascension, some religious and lay people protested the protesters, brandishing crucifixes and icons and also showing an image of Ratko Mladić. This event drew attention to the tension still present in Serbian society regarding LGBTQ+ rights.

Swiss Context

In Switzerland, LGBTQ+ rights have made significant strides in recent years. In 2021, the country legalized same-sex marriage, becoming the 30th country in the world to do so. This regulatory change has had a positive impact on the LGBTQ+ community, offering greater legal protection and social recognition.
