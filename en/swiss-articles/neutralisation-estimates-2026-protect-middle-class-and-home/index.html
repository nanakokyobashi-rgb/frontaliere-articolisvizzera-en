Neutralising estimates: a vote that affects the pockets of cross-border workers

On 14 June 2026, the people of Ticino will be called to the polls to decide on an initiative that, more than others, will directly enter the homes of those who live and work on the border: the initiative "Yes to the neutralisation of the increase in estimated values". Behind this technical term lies a decision that could change the tax pressure, access to social benefits and the quality of life of thousands of cross-border workers. In Ticino, where wages are often low, health insurance premiums are among the highest in Switzerland and the cost of living is among the highest, every small increase in taxes weighs like a millstone. And the tax estimates risk precisely this: making citizens who, in reality, are already struggling at the end of the month appear "richer" on paper.

For those who live and work on the border, between Como, Varese and Ticino, the consequences would be immediate. An increase in estimated values would result in an increase in taxable assets, with a consequent increase in taxes to be paid. For a cross-border worker who owns their own home, this would mean seeing the tax value of the property increase and, consequently, paying more taxes even on the home in which they live. Not only that: exceeding decisive thresholds for access to benefits such as reductions in health insurance premiums, scholarships or other social benefits would become more difficult. Here is the paradox: being "too rich" to receive support, but not rich enough to live without difficulty.

A tax system that does not correspond to reality

The neutralisation of estimates is not an ideological proposal, but a response to a system that risks moving further and further away from the concrete lives of people. Today, tax estimates consider the home as a source of income, when in reality it is the result of years of sacrifice: a security, a rooting, an investment that does not produce income but is taxed as if it did. For cross-border workers, who have often built their stability with responsibility and sacrifices, this mechanism is particularly unfair. The house, for them, is not a luxury but the result of daily work that crosses the border every morning.

The issue worsens when considering that, in Ticino, salaries are generally low compared to the rest of Switzerland. An increase in wealth taxes or the taxable value of property would particularly affect those who have already struggled to build their future. The risk is fueling an increasingly widespread sense of injustice: why should those who have already paid taxes on their income have to pay more on the value of a home that has already been financed?

The impact of property estimates on cross-border workers: a practical example

Imagine a cross-border worker who owns a house in Chiasso or Stabio, purchased after years of working in Switzerland. Today, the fiscal value of the property is calculated according to parameters that could be updated upwards. If the estimates increase, the taxable value grows, and so do the wealth taxes. But that's not all: exceeding a certain threshold could also prevent access to reductions in health insurance premiums or other benefits. For a worker who crosses the border every day, this means seeing their purchasing power eroded without a concrete reason. This is why on June 14, 2026, we are not just voting on a technical mechanism, but on a choice of equity.

The alternatives: what happens if the initiative passes or fails

  • If it passes: The estimates remain unchanged, avoiding increases in property taxes and maintaining access to social benefits for those in need. The tax burden on the middle class is lightened, and the primary residence is not further penalized.
  • If it fails: The estimates could increase, leading to a consequent rise in wealth taxes and a reduction in the available taxable income. For cross-border workers who own a home, this would mean paying more on a property that has already been financed with previously taxed income.

The neutralization of the estimates goes in the opposite direction, to restore balance, avoid distortions, and prevent a theoretical value from becoming an additional tax burden.

Why this vote also concerns Italian cross-border workers

Even those who work in Ticino but live in Italy could be affected by this decision. If the estimates increase, property taxes in Ticino could rise, indirectly affecting disposable income. Furthermore, the reduction in access to social benefits in Ticino could have repercussions on family members living across the border. This is not just a local issue, but a choice with concrete cross-border implications.

How the estimation system works in Ticino today

Currently, fiscal estimates in Ticino are periodically updated to reflect the trends in the real estate market. However, this update does not always correspond to the economic reality of taxpayers. For cross-border workers, who often have intermediate incomes and modest properties, an increase in estimates can have disproportionate effects. This is why the initiative of June 14, 2026, proposes to freeze these updates, at least until the system is revised to be more equitable.

The next steps: what cross-border workers need to know

Cross-border workers who own a home in Ticino or whose family members own one should carefully consider the implications of this vote. If the initiative passes, they could avoid an increase in property taxes and keep access to essential social benefits. If it fails, however, the risk is seeing fiscal pressure rise right when wages remain low and living costs are high. Find out how to calculate the impact on your taxes with our dedicated tool.

Why tax assessments are hitting cross-border workers hard

The tax assessment system in Ticino is designed to reflect the real value of properties. However, when these assessments are increased without a corresponding match to the purchasing power of taxpayers, the result is a mechanism that penalizes those with the least financial flexibility: cross-border workers. Let's examine in detail why this vote on June 14, 2026, is crucial for those living and working at the Italian-Swiss border.

The tax assessment mechanism: how it works and why it's dangerous

Tax assessments are values assigned to properties for calculating property taxes. In Ticino, these values are periodically updated to reflect market trends. However, an increase in assessments doesn't necessarily mean the taxpayer has more available wealth. For a cross-border worker who might have purchased a home years ago with an ongoing mortgage, this update translates into higher taxes without a real economic benefit.

The paradox of the middle class: too rich for help, too poor to live

In Ticino, the middle class is increasingly squeezed between two extremes. On one side, low salaries compared to the rest of Switzerland and high health insurance premiums. On the other, one of the highest living costs in the country. When assessments increase, the taxable value of the property grows, and so do the taxes to be paid. But it's not all: exceeding key thresholds for accessing health insurance reductions or scholarships becomes more difficult. The result? A family already struggling at the end of the month finds itself paying more, without being able to rely on any support.

Practical consequences for cross-border workers who own a home

For a cross-border worker who has purchased a home in Mendrisio, Varese, or Como, an increase in assessments would have immediate effects:

1. Higher wealth taxes: The home, already financed with previously taxed income, would be further burdened by new taxes. This means less money for daily expenses or for children. 2. Reduced access to aid: If the taxable value exceeds a certain threshold, the family could lose the right to health insurance premium reductions or other benefits. A hard blow for those who already spend a significant portion of their salary on health insurance. 3. Financial instability: With income already squeezed between Ticino wages and Italian costs, an increase in property taxes could lead to debt or the need to sell the home.

Comparison with the previous situation: why the initiative is necessary

Until today, tax assessments in Ticino have been updated without fully considering the economic situation of taxpayers. This has created a system where those who own a home are penalized twice: first with income taxes, then with property taxes. The initiative of June 14, 2026, proposes to stop this mechanism, at least until the system is revised to be fairer and more sustainable.

Proposed alternatives: what changes if the initiative passes

If the initiative 'Yes to the neutralization of the increase in assessment values' passes, cross-border workers will be able to:

  • Maintain the current value of assessments: Avoiding an increase in property taxes and preserving purchasing power.
  • Maintain access to social aid: The thresholds for health insurance premium reductions and scholarships would remain unchanged, ensuring support for those in need.
  • Avoid fiscal distortions: A fairer system that adheres to the economic reality of taxpayers.

Criticisms of the current system: why neutralization is a solution

The tax assessment system in Ticino is often criticized for its lack of transparency and for the fact that it does not reflect the economic reality of taxpayers. For cross-border workers, this means paying taxes on a value that does not correspond to their disposable income. The neutralization of assessments is therefore a concrete response to a real problem: making the tax system fairer and more sustainable.
