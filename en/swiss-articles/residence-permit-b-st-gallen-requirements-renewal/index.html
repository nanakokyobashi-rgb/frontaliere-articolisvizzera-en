Residence Permit B St. Gallen: Requirements and Renewal (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 September 2026

Practical guide to residence permit B in the Canton of St. Gallen: requirements, documents, duration and renewal procedure at the cantonal migration office.

Context

In short

  • Permit B: Federal authorization renewable for residence and work in Switzerland
  • Duration: up to 5 years, renewable upon request within 3 months of expiration
  • Management: Migration Office of the Canton of St. Gallen (coordination SEM)
  • Requirements: employment, accommodation, health insurance, federal documents

Key facts

  • What: Residence permit B (renewable authorization for residence/work)
  • Issued by: SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) + St. Gallen cantonal office
  • Maximum duration: up to 5 years renewable
  • Renewal: request within 3 months of expiration
  • Where: Migration Office of the Canton of St. Gallen
  • Basic requirements: employment contract, accommodation, LAMal (mandatory health insurance)
  • Who can obtain it: foreign citizens with confirmed employment in Switzerland

The residence permit B is the federal authorization that allows foreign citizens to reside and work in Switzerland for a determined and renewable period. In the Canton of St. Gallen, the management of this permit is handled by the cantonal migration office, in coordination with the SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) at the federal level.

Unlike the permit L (valid up to 1 year and mainly for temporary stays), the permit B is designed for those who intend to settle and work continuously in Switzerland. This permit can be renewed provided that the reasons for granting it still exist: ongoing employment, valid accommodation, health insurance, and absence of federal rejection reasons.

Duration and renewability of the permit B

The residence permit B has a maximum duration of 5 years, but it can be granted for shorter periods (2, 3 years) at the discretion of the cantonal authorities based on specific circumstances. The renewability depends on the persistence of the initial requirements: stable employment, adequate accommodation, regular payment of social security and tax contributions, no criminal or administrative obstacles.

Unlike the permit C (domicile), which represents a permanent authorization and is normally granted after 10 years of continuous residence (or 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens), the permit B remains linked to the need for the persistence of the prerequisites, particularly employment. Therefore, if a person loses their job, the authorities may revoke the permit or impose an emergency situation (employment within a short term).

Differences between foreign permits in Switzerland

In Switzerland, there are four main types of permits for foreigners: the permit L (short duration, up to 1 year), the permit B (renewable residence), the permit C (domicile), and the permit G (cross-border). Clarifying the differences is essential to navigate the procedures correctly.

The permit L is suitable for short stays and does not allow automatic renewal; it represents the minimum authorization. The permit B, the subject of this article, is aimed at those who work and reside stably but have not yet met the requirements for the permit C. The permit C is the most stable and represents the first step towards ordinary naturalization, with benefits in terms of fiscal and social security stability. Operational details

Requirements and documents to obtain the B permit

To obtain the B residence permit in the Canton of St. Gallen, you must meet a series of federal requirements and submit the required documentation. The main requirements are: a valid employment relationship (signed contract with a Swiss employer), adequate and appropriate housing according to local standards, mandatory health insurance (LAMal, which must be subscribed within 3 months of arrival in Switzerland with a health insurance provider of your choice), and no criminal or administrative obstacles that prevent entry.

In terms of documentation, the initial application requires: a valid passport or identity document, a certificate of employment relationship (letter from the employer confirming employment and contractual conditions), proof of housing (rental contract, letter from the landlord, or statement from the employer if they provide housing), a criminal record extract from the country of origin (translated and certified according to Swiss standards), proof of health insurance subscription (confirmation document from the health insurance provider), and federal application forms (available from the SEM or the St. Gallen Canton Migration Office).

The cost of living in Switzerland, including health insurance premiums, varies significantly depending on the canton, community, and franchise choices (from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 for adults). Regarding housing, it is important to note that Switzerland does not have strict federal regulations on minimum square footage, but the canton and municipality can set conditions on habitability and compliance with local standards. A regular rental contract is sufficient, keeping in mind that the security deposit cannot exceed three months' rent and must be deposited into a blocked account in the tenant's name (federal regulation according to the Code of Obligations).

Application procedure at the St. Gallen Migration Office

Once the documents are gathered, the application must be submitted to the St. Gallen Canton Migration Office. You can appear in person, send the documents by mail, or (depending on the available options) use an online system if the canton provides it. It is advisable to contact the office in advance to verify the preferred submission channels and to receive an updated checklist.

After submitting the application, the cantonal migration office coordinates with the SEM for evaluation. Processing times vary depending on the volume of applications and can range from a few weeks to several months. During this period, the applicant remains in Switzerland with a provisional status (if already residing in the territory) or cannot enter until the permit is issued (if the application is submitted from abroad). A decision of refusal must be justified, and the applicant has the right to appeal to the competent cantonal authorities within the deadlines set by law.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure for the first application

1. Secure employment: The first step is to ensure a work contract with a Swiss employer. The contract must specify the job, gross salary, weekly hours, and probation period if applicable.

2. Obtain accommodation: Find and rent an apartment or room in Switzerland. The rental contract is essential for the permit application. Ensure the security deposit does not exceed 3 months' rent.

3. Subscribe to health insurance: Within 3 months of arrival in Switzerland, it is mandatory to take out health insurance (LAMal). Choose a health insurance company, select an insurance model (deductible chosen by the insured, typically 300–2500 CHF for adults), and sign the policy. The insurance company will send a subscription certificate.

4. Gather documentation: Prepare your passport, employment certificate, rental contract, criminal record extract (from your country of origin), proof of health insurance, and federal SEM forms.

5. Submit the application: Go to the Migration Office of the Canton of St. Gallen with all the documentation (or send it by mail). Ensure you meet any administrative deadlines.

6. Receive the permit: Once approved, the B permit is delivered in person or sent by mail. The document is valid from the date of issue until the expiry date indicated.

Procedure for renewing the B permit

The renewal is a simplified procedure compared to the first application. It must be submitted at least 3 months before the current permit expires. The documents to be produced are essentially the same as the initial application: updated passport copy, employment certificate (letter from the current or new employer), proof of valid accommodation, proof of payment of social security and tax contributions (if required), and proof of continuous health insurance subscription (updated document from the health insurance company).

An important aspect is that the permit remains valid until a decision is made on the renewal application, even if the expiry date indicated on the document has passed, provided the application is submitted on time. This means there is no interruption of legal status if the application is submitted 3 months before the expiry date.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between residence permit B and residence permit C?
The B permit is a renewable authorization for temporary residence and work, with a duration of up to 5 years. The C permit is the residence permit, which represents a permanent authorization and is normally granted after 10 years of continuous residence (5 years for EU/EFTA citizens). Those who have a B permit can apply for a C permit once they have reached the required residence time and maintained the requirements of economic and social stability.
When do I need to apply for a renewal of permit B?
It is advisable to submit your renewal request at least 3 months before the expiry date of your current B permit. Report to the St. Gallen Migration Office with all the necessary documentation (updated employment contract, proof of accommodation, health insurance). If you submit the request in good time, the permit remains valid until the decision even if the technical expiry date has already passed.
What documents are essential to obtain the B permit?
The main documents are: valid passport or ID, employment relationship certificate, rental agreement or proof of accommodation, criminal record extract (from country of origin, translated and certified), proof of health insurance (LAMal), and federal application forms (available from the SEM). The St. Gallen Migration Office provides a complete checklist at the submission address.
Where to contact the Migration Office of the Canton of St. Gallen?
The Migration Office of the Canton of St. Gallen handles B permit applications at the cantonal level. You can show up in person at their counters, mail the documents, or contact the office by phone to check the available presentation channels. The federal SEM toll-free number is +41 58 464 16 16 for questions about federal proceedings. The www.sem.admin.ch portal provides contacts and updated forms.
What if my B permit expires before renewal?
If you have submitted your renewal request at least 3 months before the expiry date, your permit remains valid until the decision of the competent authorities. However, it is important to meet the deadline of 3 months before the deadline to avoid legal complications. Don't wait until the last minute: apply for renewal in advance to ensure continuity of legal status in Switzerland.
