Health insurance 2027: a difference of up to 10'000 francs

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 29 September 2026

In 2027, the national average will be 16'450 francs, but the difference between Geneva and Zug for the same family is close to 10'000 francs.

Context

In brief

  • In 2027 the average Swiss family will be around 16’450 francs.
  • In Geneva the bill will approach 22’000 francs a year.
  • In Ticino the typical family will pay 21’346 francs.
  • There is a difference of almost 10’000 francs between the most expensive and least expensive canton.

Key facts

  • Typical family → two adults, one young person and one minor
  • Year → 2027
  • National average → around 16’450 francs
  • Ticino → 21’346 francs
  • Geneva → almost 22’000 francs
  • Basel-Stadt → 20’458 francs
  • Zug and Appenzell Innerrhoden → 11’702-11’988 francs a year
  • Maximum difference → almost 10’000 francs a year

In 2027, a family consisting of two adults, one young person and one minor will spend an average of around 16’450 francs on health insurance premiums. The calculation was made based on the premiums announced today, Wednesday, by the Federal Office of Public Health, the FOPH. The national figure, however, conceals very wide differences between the various regions of Switzerland.

The premium ranking

Geneva is at the top of the ranking: a typical family will have to budget for almost 22’000 francs a year in 2027, net of any subsidies. It is followed by Ticino, with 21’346 francs, and Basel-Stadt, with 20’458. These are the three most expensive cantons in the Confederation and the only ones to exceed the 20’000-franc threshold.

At the opposite extreme are Zug and Appenzell Innerrhoden. For two parents and two children, the annual premium remains below 1’000 francs a month, and the annual total indicated for these two cantons is between 11’702 and 11’988 francs. The source thus describes a gap of close to 10’000 francs between the most expensive canton and the most affordable one, for the same LAMal coverage.

For many households, a similar sum is equivalent to several months’ rent. The comparison with Grisons, however, narrows the gap to around 5’000 francs a year compared with the least expensive cantons. The geography of premiums therefore affects costo della vita in Svizzera and makes the canton of residence a decisive variable when drawing up the family budget.

The picture concerns not only the level of premiums, but also their evolution. The ranking of the increases announced for 2027 differs from that of overall costs: Ticino remains among the most expensive cantons, but does not record the steepest increase.

The data thus paint a picture of a Switzerland that is highly fragmented also when it comes to healthcare spending. The figures explain why the announcement of yet another increase reignites the debate over the need for a reform of the system.

Operational details

The level and the change do not coincide

To understand the impact on the household budget, two measures must be separated: how much is paid overall and how much it increases compared with this year. The Ticino case highlights the difference. A family residing in the Canton spends on average over 4’100 francs more than the Swiss average and almost 9’400 more than a family residing in Zug.

The daily burden for families

Converted into a daily measure, spending in Ticino amounts to around 56 francs per day for basic health insurance. For a Zug family, it is around 31 francs: the gap is about 25 francs per day, more than many people spend on food, according to the comparison reported by the source. The figure makes the burden of premiums visible even when one does not look at the annual total.

The ranking changes when the increase expected for 2027 is considered. In Ticino, the additional cost for the typical family is estimated at 741 francs and 60 centimes, compared with a national average of around 801 francs. The increase in Ticino is around 3,6%, a significant rise but more contained than that observed in recent years and in several other Cantons.

The strongest increases are elsewhere

The record for increases goes to Jura, with 1’067 francs more than this year for an average family. Solothurn, Schaffhausen and Neuchâtel are around 940 francs; Zurich also exceeds the national average. In Graubünden, however, the additional cost for 2027 remains below 600 francs.

For those preparing the budget, the comparison shows that a Canton can have high premiums overall and, at the same time, an annual increase below the average. The percentage or amount of the increase does not erase the starting level. It is this combination, together with the distance between regions, that fuels the debate over the need for a reform of the system. To navigate the healthcare item in the Swiss budget, the reference remains LAMal e cassa malati.

Key points

Four steps to reduce spending

The source indicates a concrete strategy for families who want to contain premiums: start by comparing, check the alternatives and verify possible access to subsidies. The process helps put one's own premium into the context of the canton where one lives.

1. Compare premiums

The first step is to compare the available premiums. The comparison must be based on the same insurance need, because the service compares families with identical LAMal coverage. Looking only at the national total is not enough: the source shows that spending varies greatly from one canton to another. The comparison must therefore be linked to the situation of one's household and place of residence.

2. Choose the cheapest health insurer

After the comparison, the advice is to choose the cheapest health insurer. This step turns the data into a budget decision: the annual premium can amount to many months' rent, especially in the cantons where spending is higher. The choice must be made without confusing the Swiss average with the amount applied in one's own region.

3. Assess the alternative models

The third check concerns the alternative models. The source lists them among the possibilities for reducing spending. In practical terms, it is advisable to compare the model's effect on the premium before settling on the first proposal and to keep the comparison based on the same LAMal coverage.

4. Check subsidies

Finally, it is necessary to verify whether one is entitled to subsidies. The service specifies that the amounts are shown net of any subsidies in the case of Geneva; for this reason, the check can change the way the household bill is understood. How can health insurance premium costs be reduced?
