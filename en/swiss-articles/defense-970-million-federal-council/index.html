The Federal Council is asking for 970 million for air defence: 250 million short range, 650 million average, 60 million radar, 10 million drones. Self-protection 100 mln by 2029.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The Federal Council is asking for CHF 970 million to accelerate the purchase of air defence systems.
  • The funds are divided between: 250 million short range ground-to-air defence, 650 million medium range, 60 million radar, 10 million anti-drone.
  • An additional 100 million over the next few years for self-protection of over 60 military bases by 2029.

Key facts

  • What: Additional credit request for air defense systems
  • When: 19 August 2026 (press release today)
  • Where: Bern, Swiss Confederation
  • Who: Federal Council and Parliament (for approval)
  • Amount: CHF 970 million + 100 million multi-year
  • Motivation: Increased threats, long delivery times in the armaments market

# Accelerating air defense

Bern accelerates air defense. The Federal Council has announced an additional appropriation of CHF 970 million to be submitted to Parliament in order to ensure the procurement lead times needed to upgrade Switzerland's airspace protection systems. The maneuver is part of a context of increased geopolitical threats and increasingly long delivery times in the arms market.

Operational details

The multi-year self-protection plan

But the request for €970 million is only part of the project to strengthen Switzerland's defence. In the coming years, the Government has planned to allocate another 100 million francs to strengthen the army's self-protection at more than 60 military locations scattered throughout the country. These measures include the improvement of fences, the installation of video surveillance systems, electronic access controls and the construction of security rooms.

The timetable is tight: the Federal Council intends to carry out all these interventions at the latest by the beginning of 2029. Again, to speed up implementation times, the executive has already anticipated that it will request additional credit from Parliament in the context of the 2025 buildings programme.

Because acceleration now

The changing international security environment has made it urgent to upgrade Switzerland's defensive capabilities. While the country maintains its traditional stance of neutrality, the Confederation recognizes the need to equip itself with modern and reliable tools for the protection of airspace. The tense situation on the global arms market — with suppliers requiring substantial advance payments — represents a further element of pressure on the timing of investments.

Key points

What are the next steps

After today's announcement, the federal council's message will be transmitted to Parliament for examination and voting. It is not yet clear if the credit request will follow the ordinary procedure - i.e. preliminary discussion in committees, then scrutiny and voting in the plenary session of the Chamber and Senate - or if it will benefit from an accelerated procedure due to the strategic urgency. However, it is certain that Parliament will have the final word on the granting of the 970 million, as well as on the additional credit request for the 2025 real estate program.

Once approval has been obtained from both Chambers, the acquisition process can proceed quickly. International producers require significant advance payments before production and delivery, so each month of delay risks determining a chain effect on the overall delivery times. The federal council therefore has a pressing need to obtain the green light as soon as possible.

Implications for Swiss citizens

For Swiss taxpayers, the approval of these credits involves the use of public federal resources dedicated to defense. Although the federal council has referred to better forecasts for Confederation revenues, the financing of these new investments will still have an impact on the federal budget and future spending priorities.

Frequently Asked Questions
How much is the Federal Air Defence Council asking for?
The Federal Council is asking for an additional loan of CHF 970 million from Parliament. The funds will be allocated to: 250 million for short-range surface-to-air defence, 650 million for medium-range surface-to-air defence, 60 million for a partially mobile medium-range radar, and 10 million for defence against mini-drones. Another 100 million are planned in the coming years to strengthen self-protection at more than 60 military bases.
Why is the Federal Council asking for this money now?
The Government cites increased geopolitical threats and increasingly long delivery times in the arms market. Manufacturers require substantial down payments before manufacturing; without them, even urgent purchases risk delays of several years. The Swiss army currently does not have the necessary means to adequately protect the country from air threats.
When will the credit come into effect if approved by Parliament?
Once Parliament has approved the credit, the Federal Council can immediately pay the advances to the suppliers. The self-protection plan of the military bases must instead be implemented at the latest by the beginning of 2029.
What is short- and medium-range surface-to-air defense?
These are defense systems against air threats at different distances: short-range defense protects against threats at short distances, while medium-range defense offers protection at greater distances. The Federal Council invests 250 million in short-range defence and 650 million in medium-range defence.
What military self-protection measures are envisaged?
The plan includes upgrading fences, installing video surveillance systems, electronic access controls, and building security rooms at more than 60 military locations. The planned investment is 100 million francs in the coming years, with implementation by the beginning of 2029.
