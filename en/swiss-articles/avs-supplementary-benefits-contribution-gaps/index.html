AVS and supplementary benefits: contribution gaps (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 3 September 2026

How the first pillar works in the Swiss system: calculation of the AVS pension, contribution gaps, cantonal compensation fund and supplementary benefits for the minimum living wage.

Context

In brief

  • AVS is the first pillar of the Swiss social security system: pension calculated on years of contributions
  • Contribution gaps (years without payments) reduce the monthly pension amount
  • Supplementary benefits intervene when AVS does not cover the minimum living standard
  • Cantonal compensation fund manages the system at the local level

Key facts

  • What: Three-pillar pension system (mandatory AVS, professional LPP, private third pillar)
  • Contributions: 5.3% employee + 5.3% employer (10.6% total on salary); self-employed pay both shares
  • Management: UFAS oversees at the federal level; cantonal compensation funds manage operationally
  • Minimum living standard: Amount set by cantons to ensure dignity in retirement
  • Right: At least 1 year of contributions to access AVS pension

The first pillar of the Swiss social security system rests on AVS (Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance), a mandatory contributory system managed through cantonal compensation funds. Each canton has its own fund — a public body that collects contributions, maintains individual accounts, calculates pensions, and pays them out monthly for the beneficiary's lifetime.

The AVS pension is not uniform: it depends on the number of years of contributions paid and the average amount of salaries declared during the working career. Those who accumulate contribution gaps — periods in which payment was absent, reduced, or interrupted — receive a lower pension compared to those with a complete contribution career. These gaps are the main factor in reducing the benefit in the first pillar.

Operational details

Complementary services: the role of the State when AVS is not enough

When the AVS pension — calculated on actual contributions — does not reach the level of the minimum living allowance set by the cantonal authorities, supplementary benefits intervene. These are not the result of a new contribution: they are tax transfers, financed by general taxation (direct federal, cantonal, municipal tax, VAT). Their purpose is to guarantee a minimum standard of living to the elderly and disabled who, due to circumstances of their work biography, cannot count on a full AVS income.

The supplementary benefits cover the gap between the income received and the certified cost of living (rent, essential services, basic necessities). Each canton sets its own living minimum and access criteria; each municipality applies the cantonal criteria to its territory. The disparity between cantons and municipalities is significant: the living minimum in Geneva can differ significantly from that in a mountain canton, reflecting different living costs.

Who is eligible? Conditions vary, but generally access is granted to those who receive an AVS or disability (AI) pension below the local minimum, have capital resources within cantonal thresholds and reside permanently in the canton/municipality. Complementary services are NOT generic assistance: they are a legal right for those who meet objective criteria.

Key points

Check your own contribution career: concrete procedures

The first step is to contact the cantonal compensation fund of your canton of residence. You can do this:

  • Via the official website of the fund (almost all cantons allow online requests)
  • By mail, sending a request form
  • In person at the local office of the fund

Request a free extract of your AVS contribution career. The document will arrive within 2-4 weeks and will contain:

  • A year-by-year list of declared incomes
  • Confirmation of paid contributions
  • Explicit identification of gaps
  • Calculation base for the estimate of future pension

Take the time to carefully check each line. Verify that:

  • All work periods are recorded (compare with your memory of employers)
  • Salary amounts match your pay slips
  • There are no erroneous gaps (years that should be covered but are not)
  • Years of work in other cantons or abroad have not been omitted (if applicable)

If you find discrepancies, report them to the fund within 30 days: it is obliged to investigate and correct the official data. After this period, corrections are more difficult and require lengthy administrative procedures.

Frequently Asked Questions
What exactly are AVS contribution gaps and how do they affect income?
Contribution gaps are years of your working career in which you have not paid AVS contributions, or have paid them to a small extent. The most common cause is unemployment. The AVS pension is calculated on the arithmetic average of ALL the years since the first contribution to retirement age (65 for men, 64 for women). How do I find out if I have contribution gaps in my account?
Contact the cantonal clearing house of your canton of residence (the official cash register website has the online application form or postal address). Ask for a contributory career excerpt (free). The document, sent within 2-4 weeks, lists year by year your declared income, contributions paid and explicitly identifies gaps. Check carefully that there are no registration errors.
What is the difference between AVS and complementary services?
AVS is an annuity earned through mandatory contributions paid during your working life: it is a vested right, not assistance. Supplementary benefits are tax subsidies of the State (canton and municipality) provided when the AVS income does not cover the living minimum calculated locally. Supplementary services depend on your capital resources and cantonal criteria.
What is the role of the cantonal clearing house and how can I contact it?
The clearing house is the local public body in charge of managing the first pillar in your canton. It collects contributions, maintains your individual accounts, calculates annuities and pays them monthly. You can contact her via the cashier's official website, by post or in person at her office. Each checkout has a dedicated site with contact details and request forms.
Can I fill the contribution gaps and how much does the AVS ransom cost?
Yes, some periods can be redeemed (years of training, unemployment), but redemption is optional and comes at a cost. The cashier will calculate a lump sum based on the years to be redeemed and historical income. The cost can range from hundreds to thousands of francs. Before redeeming, ask the cashier for the permanent increase in monthly income that would result, so you can assess whether the investment is economically convenient in the long term.
