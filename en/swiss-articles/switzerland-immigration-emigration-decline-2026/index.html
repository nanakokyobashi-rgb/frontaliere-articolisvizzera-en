Switzerland: immigration falls and emigration grows in 2026

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 16 August 2026

Analysis of data from the State Secretariat for Migration on the foreign permanent resident population in Switzerland in the first half of 2026.

Context

In a nutshell

  • 40,000 foreign residents left Switzerland in the first half of 2026
  • 74,112 new arrivals registered in the first six months, down 2.9%
  • Foreign emigration up 4.3% compared to 2025
  • Net migration balance down by 12.5% to 29,884

Key facts

  • What: Change in the foreign permanent resident population
  • When: First semester 2026
  • Where: Swiss Confederation
  • Who: State Secretariat for Migration (SEM)
  • Amount: 2.43 million foreign residents at the end of June
  • Percentage: 12.5% decrease for net migration
  • Permits: 33,219 permits for border workers issued

Almost 40,000 permanent foreign residents left Switzerland during the first half of 2026, registering an increase of 4.3% compared to the same period of the previous year. According to the Ministry of Migration (SEM), this phenomenon is part of a broader trend in which emigration by foreign residents is constantly growing from 2023. At the same time, the inflows show signs of slowing down. During the first six months of 2026, exactly 74,112 foreign nationals joined the permanent resident population of Switzerland, marking a decrease of 2.9% compared to the previous twelve months. The combination of these factors resulted in a 12.5% contraction in net migration to 29,884.

Operational details

Official data published by federal authorities call into question the common perception that migratory movement towards the Confederacy represents a one-way path. A significant share of highly skilled workers from nations such as the United States, China, Japan, and Canada tend to move temporarily and then leave again. Among migrants from OECD countries outside the EU and EFTA, it is estimated that between 70% and 80% will leave Switzerland again within three to five years. Historically, based on long-term cohort data, about 20% of people who arrived in Switzerland in 2011 had already left the country within two years. The figure increases significantly over time: after five years the starting rate reaches 35%, while after ten years the percentage reaches almost half. Many workers arrive knowing that their stay will be temporary, often sent by multinational companies for the duration of a single project or with a temporary assignment, at the end of which they return to their country of origin or move to new professional destinations.

Key points

The indicators of the national labour market show a phase of general cooling of the related flows. Switzerland issued 33,219 new border permits during the first half of 2026, a decrease of 10% compared to the 36,876 permits issued in the same period of the previous year. On the other hand, short-term work permits increased from 164,779 to 169,514, an increase of 3%. A further turning point in outflows is retirement. A Swiss pension guarantees greater purchasing power in countries with a lower cost of living than Switzerland. This dynamic is particularly evident among Portuguese citizens who arrived in Switzerland during the 1980s: many of them are now returning to Portugal, where the proceeds of their Swiss pensions offer greater economic sustainability. Frequently Asked Questions
How many foreign residents left Switzerland in the first half of 2026?
In the first half of 2026, almost 40,000 permanent foreign residents (to be exact 39,251 people) left Switzerland, registering an increase of 4.3% compared to the same period of the previous year, according to data from the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM).
What is the impact of foreign workers on total new arrivals?
Employment remains the main motivation: over four-fifths of immigration from the European Union and EFTA is work-related. In the first six months of 2026, 41,321 workers from these countries moved to Switzerland for long-term employment.
How have the permits for border workers and for short-term work changed?
In the first six months of 2026, Switzerland issued 33,219 new permits for border workers, a decrease of 10% compared to 36,876 the previous year. In contrast, short-term work permits increased by 3%, rising to 169,514 units.
