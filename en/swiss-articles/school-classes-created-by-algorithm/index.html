The city of Uster has implemented software to create more balanced school classes. The goal is to give equal opportunities to all students. The first results seem to be positive. The software takes into account the origin and place of residence of students to create more balanced classes.

The head of primary schools in Uster, Patricia Bernet, explains: "The origin and place of residence are decisive for the educational path." “We see that the classes are more balanced, it means that the social and linguistic aspects are considered more,” Bernet continues.

The intent of this method is not to bring 'children from one side of the city to the other just to mix up classes, pupils go to school on foot. Only in exceptional cases do we carry out transports." For the distribution of classes, the offer and the path to go to school are taken into account first and then the social heterogeneity.

Operational details

Using software to create more balanced classrooms can have important practical implications for students and schools. First, it can help reduce inequalities among students and create a more inclusive learning environment.

Second, it can help improve the quality of education and increase student satisfaction. Finally, it can help reduce tensions between students and create a more serene and peaceful learning environment.

The composition of school classes is an important aspect to ensure that all students have equal learning opportunities. The use of software to create more balanced classes seems to be a step in this direction.
