Operational details

The SSIC (Swiss Construction Company) argues that delays and interruptions to construction work due to extreme heat are rarely taken into account and are not compensated. Construction companies are forced to meet increasingly tight deadlines, which puts workers' health at risk.

According to the SSIC, most construction companies cannot meet deadlines due to extreme heat, which can reach temperatures of over 35°C. This leads to delays and interruptions in work, which can have serious consequences for workers' health. The SSIC argues that construction companies should be exempted from penalties for delays due to extreme heat.

Concrete example: in July 2022, in Lucerne, a team of masons was forced to stop for three days due to extreme heat. The team couldn't keep working because of the high temperature and humidity. The SSIC argues that construction companies should be exempted from penalties for delays in cases like these.

The SSIC argues that current regulations are not sufficient to protect construction workers from heat injury. Federal labor law states that workers must be protected from heat injury, but the law is unclear on how to enforce this provision.
