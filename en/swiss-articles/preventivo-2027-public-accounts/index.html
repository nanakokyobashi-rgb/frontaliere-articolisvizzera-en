2027 budget: deficit and public accounts in Ticino

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 1 October 2026

The Canton's 2027 Budget forecasts a deficit of 98.5 million. Between bureaucracy, personnel and taxation, here are the reactions and the debate in Bellinzona.

The Canton's 2027 Budget, with a deficit of 98.5 million, has sparked the debate on public finances. In Bellinzona, tio.ch bloggers reacted with distrust to the numbers and with a common question: where to find the money?

On Tuesday, the news was the increase in health insurance prices; on Wednesday the deep red of the Budget arrived. The tone of the comments remained the same: those who pay taxes are asking for solutions, not just new news. In the background, the bill for initiatives on health insurance premiums also returns.

"The same news every time. Some real solution every now and then would also please those who pay taxes."

Disputed numbers and cited choices

Many readers do not believe the numbers and one comment argues that, in the final balance, the deficit could become about half. The comparison is with a private company: those who do not know how to prepare a budget, they observe, would risk losing their jobs. Others describe the Canton as a family that lives beyond its means and leaves debts to children and grandchildren.

In the debate, a public debt of about 3 billion is mentioned, equal, according to the reported commentary, to over 8,000 francs per person. More than one reader takes up or reformulates the position of the director of the DFE Christian Vitta: in words everyone agrees, but when the time comes to decide, crossed vetoes appear.

The requests also concern sacrifices from above. In the comments appear the Canton's car fleet, events with costs defined as stratospheric, road construction sites done and redone and expenses for agriculture. Someone proposes independent technicians; another ironically recalls the bailiffs.

On taxation, one group points to Zug, Schwyz and Lucerne as models for attracting wealthy taxpayers. Others point out that Ticino's taxes are already much lower than those in the Canton of Bern. Among the reactions there is also the reference to the 1.7 billion equalization received from Bern and the different tax burden between modest and high incomes. The issue of cassa malati remains linked to the discussion on the public account.

Operational details

Divisive choices

Read as a whole, the comparison is not only about those who want to cut and those who defend spending. The comments show three different criteria: reducing the administrative apparatus, asking politicians to set an example, or using the tax authorities to attract wealthy taxpayers. Each criterion brings with it an objection that has already emerged among readers.

| Option discussed | Topic indicated | Contrast emerged | | Reducing staff and bureaucracy | Rules and procedures produce paper | In services for elderly homes and special education it is said that the belt is already tight | | Starting from the top | Cantonal cars, events and construction sites are cited as expenses to be reviewed | Agriculture and other items are considered difficult to touch | | Tax action | Lower tax rates to attract wealthy taxpayers | Competition can favour big capital while wage earners remain |

From linear cut to political choice

The first fracture concerns staff. On the one hand, the comments call for fewer employees and less bureaucracy. On the other, those who work with nursing homes and special education services warn that not all offices have the same margin. The practical question is therefore not only how many places to reduce, but where to place the intervention without affecting activities that, according to those testimonies, are already containing expenses.

The second line concerns the example of politics. The car fleet, events with stratospheric costs and repeated road construction sites become symbols of a broader request: before asking for sacrifices, the Canton should show that it has examined its priorities. The proposal to rely on independent technicians stems from this mistrust, while the joke about the bailiffs signals the provocative nature of the confrontation.

On the tax front, the problem is even more complex. The reference to Zug, Schwyz and Lucerne focuses on attracting wealthy taxpayers; the reference to the Canton of Bern, on the other hand, calls into question the extent of the comparison. The equalization cited by readers and the difference between low and high incomes add two elements to the same discussion. Key points

How to follow the discussion

The reactions of readers do not constitute a single financial plan. However, they are a useful grid for following the dossier without confusing a figure in the Budget, a political proposal and an opinion on the management of the Canton.

Five steps to read the dossier

1. Separate the figure from the forecasts. The deficit indicated in the Budget must be kept distinct from the comment according to which, in the final balance, it could be reduced to about half. 2. Divide the proposals by area. Personnel and bureaucracy belong to a block; cars, events, construction sites and agriculture to that of spending priorities; the tax authorities constitute a separate chapter. 3. Check the status of the initiative. The one recalled by readers is popular and interparty, but is described as being examined by the Management and Finance Commission of the Grand Council. 4. Compare tax arguments. On the one hand, there are Zug, Schwyz and Lucerne; on the other, the comparison with the canton of Bern, the aforementioned equalization and the different pressure on incomes. 5. Keep the topic of premiums separate. The bill of health insurance premium initiatives remains in the background and should not be confused with individual proposals on personnel, bureaucracy or taxes.

This order helps to understand what is already written in the Budget and what belongs instead to the political debate. It also helps not to treat as an acquired decision a request still under examination by the Management and Finance Commission.
