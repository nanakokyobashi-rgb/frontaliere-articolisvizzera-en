Glarus C Permit: Requirements, Procedures and Application (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 September 2026

Permanent residence in Canton Glarus: guide to C permit requirements, language skills, integration and application procedure with cantonal authorities.

Context

In brief

  • The C permit allows permanent residence in Switzerland without periodic renewal
  • Requires 10 years of residence (5 for EU/EFTA citizens)
  • Application at the foreigners' office of the Canton of Glarus
  • Requires local integration criteria and language skills

Key facts

  • What: Permanent residence permit C
  • Who: Foreign citizens residing in Switzerland
  • Where: Canton of Glarus
  • Required residence duration: 10 years standard; 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens
  • Competent authority: Foreigners' Office of Glarus and State Secretariat for Migration (SEM)
  • Additional requirements: Integration, local languages, stable economic situation

For those living in the Canton of Glarus and intending to settle permanently, the permanent residence permit C represents the longest-term residence title provided by Swiss law. Unlike the residence permit B, which is renewed every 5 years, the permit C offers lasting legal stability, subject to compliance with specific federal and cantonal conditions.

According to the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM), the permit C is granted to foreign citizens who have resided continuously in Switzerland for a certain period: normally 10 years for non-European citizens, reduced to 5 years for citizens of the European Union and the European Free Trade Association (EU/EFTA). In the Canton of Glarus, as in other Swiss cantons, the procedure for issuing the permit follows a specific administrative path that includes the evaluation of local integration criteria, acquisition of local language skills, and verification of the applicant's economic and tax situation.

Operational details

The transition from permit B to permit C directly affects many aspects of life in Switzerland, primarily the tax regime. Permanent residents in the Canton of Glarus are subject to ordinary federal, cantonal, and municipal taxation, just like any other resident, without temporary restrictions. Federal direct tax (IFD), cantonal and municipal taxes are calculated on the same scale as long-term residents, without discrimination based on migratory status. Furthermore, permit C determines the mandatory participation in Swiss pension schemes. Those who work are automatically enrolled in the Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance (AVS) and the Disability Insurance (AI), with contributions amounting to 5.3% of gross salary for the employee, totaling 10.6% with the employer's contribution. If the employment contract includes a pension fund (LPP), the coordination itself depends on the duration and age class: the coordination deduction and the amounts of mandatory contributions vary according to federal criteria. Mandatory enrollment in health insurance (LAMal) must occur within 3 months of arrival in Switzerland and remains an obligation even with permit C. Unlike social security systems, LAMal is a private insurance with per capita premiums, with annual deductibles that each canton regulates in part, and with the possibility of reducing premiums according to cantonal income criteria. ### Access to Credit and Real Estate Ownership With permit C, access to bank credit for the purchase or renovation of real estate becomes significantly easier. Swiss banks consider permit C as an indicator of stability and credibility, increasing the willingness to grant mortgages at favorable rates. For the rental of housing, permit C does not eliminate federal tenant protection rules (Code of Obligations art. 253 ff.), but increases the landlord's trust at the time of signing a contract, reducing the likelihood of being rejected for temporary reasons. Permanent residence, finally, facilitates access to public services and cantonal benefits — scholarships, housing subsidies, integration programs — often reserved for stable residents.

Key points

The application for the C residence permit must be submitted to the competent foreigner's office of the Canton of Glarus. Although the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) sets federal guidelines, each canton adopts specific procedures and forms; it is therefore essential to contact the Glarus administration directly to know the exact documentation required and the processing times. In general, the documentation to be collected includes a residence certificate, extracts from the civil register, documentation relating to work activity or source of income (contracts, payslips, tax declaration), local integration certificates, proof of adequate knowledge of the local German language, a declaration of absence of debts towards the cantonal and municipal tax administration, and certificates of good conduct. Processing times vary depending on the administrative workload of the cantonal office and the completeness of the documentation submitted. Generally, a complete file is processed within 2-4 months, but additional weeks may be required during peak periods. If the application is accepted, the C permit is issued, valid for the entire duration of residence in Switzerland, as long as the holder maintains tax residence in the country. In case of refusal, the cantonal authority is required to communicate the reasons; there is the possibility to appeal within set deadlines to a higher administrative authority. Frequently Asked Questions
How many years of residence are required for the C permit?
Normally 10 years of uninterrupted residence in Switzerland for non-European citizens; 5 years for citizens of the European Union and the European Free Trade Association (EU/EFTA). The Canton of Glarus may apply additional integration criteria, but the minimum limit remains the federal one established by the SEM.
What is the difference between permit B and permit C?
Permit B (residence) is renewable every 5 years and linked to a cause of stay such as work or family reunification. The C (domicile) permit is permanent, does not require renewal and represents a permanent residence. Both require mandatory subscription to LAMal and payment of federal, cantonal and municipal taxes.
What documents are needed for permit application C?
Certificate of residence, attestations of local integration, proof of knowledge of the local German language, documentation of stable source of income, declaration of absence of tax debts and certificate of good conduct. The exact list varies by canton: check with the foreign office in Glarus.
Does the C permit allow for Swiss naturalisation?
No directly. The C permit is a permanent residence permit, not citizenship. However, the Federal Constitution allows for ordinary naturalization after 10 years of residence in Switzerland (with double counting for previous periods), combined with the C permit and specific cantonal and municipal requirements.
