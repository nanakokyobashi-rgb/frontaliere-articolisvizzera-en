Eurozone inflation: new three-year peak

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 October 2026

Eurostat's first estimate puts eurozone inflation at 3,8% in September, up from 3,2% in August. Energy rises to 18,8%.

Context

In brief

  • Eurozone inflation rises to 3,8% in September.
  • It was at 3,2% in August.
  • Energy: increase of 18,8% versus 14,3%.
  • The ECB's main rate is at 2,5%.

Key facts

  • Period → September

In September, inflation in the euro area reached a new three-year peak. Eurostat's first estimate puts the increase in prices in the 21 countries that share the single currency at 3,8%, compared with 3,2% in August.

The jump came as energy prices accelerated. According to Eurostat, the increase in energy prices reached 18,8% in September, compared with 14,3% in the previous month. The source links the surge in energy prices to the conflict in the Middle East.

Energy and the ECB's response

The overall figure thus rose to almost twice the 2% target set by the ECB. The acceleration weighs on European households and businesses, according to the description provided along with the first estimate.

In recent months, price pressures have pushed the ECB to tighten monetary policy. The institution has already raised its key interest rates twice during the year, bringing the main rate to 2,5%.

The central bank has not formally ruled out further monetary tightening in the coming months. The stated reason is price pressures, while the report attributes September's acceleration to the surge in the energy component.

The RG 12.30 report is dated 2 ottobre 2026 and presents a first estimate for September. The geographical reference remains the euro area, with the 21 countries cited by Eurostat.

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the news therefore offers an indicator for the euro area, but not a measure of national inflation. The source does not report Swiss data, price changes in Switzerland, or decisions by the BNS/SNB.

Operational details

The practical reading for the Swiss public

The move from 3,2% to 3,8% should be read as an acceleration of inflation in the euro area between August and September. The comparison does not say how much prices have changed for a single family or a single company: the text provides an aggregate figure for the 21 countries.

Key points

A four-step method

To use the data correctly without attributing effects to it that the source does not describe, it is advisable to follow a simple procedure.

Four operational steps

1. Note the period: the reading concerns September and is contained in report RG 12.30 of 2 ottobre 2026. 2. Separate the source: this is a preliminary Eurostat estimate referring to the 21 countries sharing the single currency. 3. Compare the two available figures: overall inflation and energy, using August as the previous reference. 4. Keep European monetary policy separate from any personal or Swiss assessment.

After this verification, anyone with a budget in francs can record euro expenses separately. comparatore CHF/EUR is used to read the relationship between the two currencies, while the Eurostat figure remains a measure of the euro area. It is not advisable to apply 3,8% to every item in one’s budget without an expense item linked to the area concerned.
