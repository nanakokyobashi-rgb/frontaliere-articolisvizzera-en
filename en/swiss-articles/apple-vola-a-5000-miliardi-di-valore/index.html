Apple breaks through $5 trillion in market capitalization, becoming the second company in the world to reach the historic threshold after Nvidia. Cupertino's shares rose 1.8% on Wall Street, allowing it to surpass $5 trillion in value for the first time and be, at the moment, the largest company in the S&P 500.

Apple's market capitalization has increased dramatically over the years, rising from about $350 billion in 2010 to over $5 trillion today. This is largely due to the exponential growth of the mobile device market and strong demand for Apple products such as iPhones and Macs.

Competition with other technology companies such as Google, Amazon and Microsoft is increasingly fierce, but Apple has managed to maintain its leading position thanks to its strong presence in the mobile device market and its ability to innovate and create high-quality products.

An iPhone can be rented for $17.99 per month, while a Mac will cost $24.99. The move was decided after Apple's recent price hike to offset the surge in memory chip costs.

According to the most recent data, Apple's value has risen to $5 trillion, ranking second in the ranking of the most valuable companies after Nvidia. This increase in value was determined by the growth in demand for technological devices, especially smartphones and laptops.

The 'Upgrade' program was launched in response to the increase in prices of Apple devices, which have increased by 15% over the last 6 months. This increase was caused by the rising costs of memory chips, which are a key component of Apple devices.
