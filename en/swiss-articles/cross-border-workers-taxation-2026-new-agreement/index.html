Border taxation 2026: the new rules after the tax agreement

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 23 June 2026

Find out how the taxation for border workers changes from 2026 with the new tax agreement between Switzerland and Italy.

Context

TL;DR

  • New tax rules for cross-border workers from 2026
  • Differences between old and new cross-border workers
  • Tax-free allowance and tax credit with hypothetical scenarios

Key facts

  • What: New tax rules for cross-border workers
  • When: From January 1, 2026
  • Where: Switzerland and Italy
  • Who: Cross-border workers who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy
  • Amount: Variable tax-free allowance and tax credit

From January 1, 2026, the new tax rules for cross-border workers who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy will come into force. These rules were established by the new tax agreement signed on December 23, 2020, which came into force on January 1, 2024. The main changes concern the differences between old and new cross-border workers, the tax-free allowance and the tax credit.

Differences between old and new cross-border workers

Cross-border workers who started working in Switzerland before January 1, 2024, are considered “old cross-border workers” and will continue to be taxed according to the previous rules. “New cross-border workers,” on the other hand—those who started working from January 1, 2024, onward—will be subject to the new tax rules.

Tax-free allowance and tax credit

The tax-free allowance is the amount exempt from taxation in Italy. For new cross-border workers, the tax-free allowance has been increased, meaning that a larger portion of their income will not be taxed in Italy. The tax credit, on the other hand, is the amount that can be deducted from the taxes owed in Switzerland.

Operational details

Practical analysis of the new tax rules

The new tax rules for cross-border workers will have a significant impact on their taxation. To better understand how they work, it is useful to consider some hypothetical scenarios.

Scenario 1: Cross-border worker with an average income

A cross-border worker with an average income could benefit more from the new tax allowance. For example, if they were previously taxed on an income of 50.000 CHF, with the new tax allowance they might be taxed only on 45.000 CHF, thereby saving on Italian taxes.

Scenario 2: Cross-border worker with a high income

For a cross-border worker with a higher income, the tax credit could be more advantageous. For example, if they previously paid 10.000 CHF in taxes in Switzerland, with the new tax credit they could deduct 2.000 CHF, thereby reducing their overall tax burden.

Comparison with the previous situation

Before the new tax agreement, cross-border workers were taxed both in Switzerland and in Italy, with no possibility of deducting taxes paid in one country from those owed in the other. Under the new rules, however, there is greater coordination between the two tax systems, which should reduce the risk of double taxation.

Key points

What to do to comply with the new tax rules

To comply with the new tax rules, cross-border workers should follow a few practical steps. Here is a step-by-step guide:

Step 1: Verify your status

The first step is to check whether you are considered an 'old cross-border worker' or a 'new cross-border worker'. This depends on the date you started working in Switzerland. Those who started before January 1, 2024 are an 'old cross-border worker'; those who started after are a 'new cross-border worker'.

Step 2: Calculate the allowance

The second step is to calculate the new allowance. To do this, it is useful to consult a tax expert or use an online calculator. The allowance depends on income and family situation.

Step 3: Apply the tax credit

The third step is to apply the tax credit. In this case too, it is useful to consult a tax expert or use an online calculator. The tax credit depends on the taxes paid in Switzerland and the allowance applied in Italy.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main differences between old and new frontiersmen?
'Old frontier workers' are those who started working in Switzerland before 1 January 2024 and will continue to be taxed according to the previous rules. The 'new frontier workers' are those who started working from 1 January 2024 onwards and will be subject to the new tax rules.
How does the deductible for new frontier workers work?
Deductible is the amount exempt from taxation in Italy. For new frontier workers, the deductible has been increased, which means that a larger part of their income will not be taxed in Italy.
What is the tax credit and how does it work?
The tax credit is the amount that can be deducted from the taxes due in Switzerland. This credit depends on the taxes paid in Switzerland and the deductible applied in Italy.
