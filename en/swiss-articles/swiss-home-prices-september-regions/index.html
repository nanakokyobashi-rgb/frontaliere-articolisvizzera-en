House and apartment prices in Switzerland: stable in September

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 October 2026

Single-family homes at -0,1% and apartments at +0,2% in September: Zurich records the sharpest decline, while Ticino grows in condominium ownership.

In Zurich, the September snapshot of residential properties for sale shows substantial stability across Switzerland, at least on a monthly basis. The data come from an analysis of real estate listings published by the Immoscout platform in collaboration with real estate consultancy IAZI. The survey looks at asking prices set by sellers and distinguishes between single-family homes and apartments.

The first segment recorded a decline of 0.1% compared with August. For apartments, on the other hand, prices rose by 0.2%. The comparison is therefore between two movements in opposite directions, although within an overall picture defined as substantially stable.

The regional map of houses

The Swiss figure is not repeated in the same way in all regions. For single-family homes, the sharpest decline was recorded in Zurich, where the drop was 2.7%. Decreases were also recorded in the Mittelland, Central Switzerland and Ticino. Increases, on the other hand, concerned the Lake Geneva region, Eastern Switzerland and Northwestern Switzerland.

Condominiums follow a different distribution. Prices rose in Ticino, Northwestern Switzerland, the Lake Geneva region and Central Switzerland. They fell instead in Zurich and Eastern Switzerland.

This dual reading is the central point of the analysis: the national monthly direction is not enough to describe every area. The result changes when moving from single-family homes to condominiums and when shifting the observation between Zurich, Ticino, the Lake Geneva region and the other regions mentioned. For this reason, the figures should be read while keeping the type of property, the location and the comparison between September and August separate. The geographical reference is not a secondary detail, because the source records increases and declines in the same categories depending on the area considered. The distinction is therefore an integral part of the monthly data. This is not a single uniform movement.

Two filters to avoid confusing the data

The national stability indicated for September is a starting point, not a figure to apply indiscriminately to every listing. The source cross-references two variables: the type of property and the region. A correct reading must retain both until the end of the comparison.

Key points

Four operational checks

The data collection can be turned into a simple sheet. The objective is not to add estimates, but to keep the scope of the data intact: listings, asking prices, September, comparison with August, category and region.

1. Define the category. Indicate whether it concerns a single-family house or a condominium. Do not combine the two entries into a single row, because the source records different trends.

2. Indicate the region. Report the area associated with the listing: Zurich, Mittelland, Central Switzerland, Ticino, Lake Geneva region, Eastern Switzerland or Northwestern Switzerland. The location determines which regional comparison to use.

3. Set the time frame. Write September as the period observed and August as the monthly reference. This keeps the comparison tied to the interval indicated by the analysis.

4. Transcribe the reference. Keep the seller's asking price in the listing and do not replace it with a generic value. The subject of the price is also part of the interpretation.

After this recording, use the corresponding change without transferring it to another category. The 0,1% concerns single-family houses compared with August; the 0,2% concerns apartments. The 2,7% identifies the sharpest decline in house prices, in Zurich. These are three different references and must be kept in their respective boxes.

The sheet should also retain the source information: the listing analysis is published by Immoscout with IAZI and concerns sellers' asking prices. This way, the reader does not lose the meaning of the number when comparing it with a different figure.

A final check consists of rereading the regional row: for houses, do not confuse the decline in Ticino with the rise in the Lake Geneva region; for condominiums, do not confuse the rise in Ticino with the decline in Zurich. The source provides precisely this distinction, and preserving it prevents a comparison based solely on the national headline.
