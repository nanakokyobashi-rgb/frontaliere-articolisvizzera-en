Border towns demand urgent govt meeting over refunds (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 July 2026

The Canton of Ticino blocks rebates to border municipalities. The association asks for an urgent meeting with the Italian government.

Context

In brief - The Canton of Ticino blocks rebates to border municipalities. - The Association of Border Municipalities asks for an urgent meeting with the Italian Government. - The Democratic Party rises up against the decision of the Canton of Ticino. ## Key facts - What: Blocking of rebates to Border Municipalities. - When: Today. - Where: Canton Ticino. - Who: Council of State of the Canton of Ticino. - Amount: CHF 50 million. The Canton of Ticino has decided to block the partial payment of rebates to the Border Municipalities, motivated by the precautionary need to wait for the evolution of the issue relating to the application of the so-called "Tax Return". Health tax by the Lombardy Region. The Association of Italian Border Municipalities, representing the more than 150 member municipalities, has urgently asked to meet with representatives of the Italian Government to address the issue of rebates. ### Reaction of the Democratic Party The Democratic Party has risen up against the decision of the Council of State of the Canton of Ticino, calling it a direct consequence of a wrong and irresponsible choice by the Meloni government and the Lombardy Region. The president of the Democratic Party deputies, Chiara Braga, explained that the health tax paid by old cross-border workers is in contrast with the bilateral tax agreements between Italy and Switzerland, provoking the reaction of the Canton of Ticino. Braga has asked the Lombardy Region to immediately withdraw the health tax to avoid economic damage to border territories. ### Context and Implications The freeze on rebates, which amount to 50 million francs, is a precautionary measure adopted by the Council of State of the Canton of Ticino to protect the financial interests of the region. This amount is intended to support border municipalities, which are largely dependent on Swiss contributions to finance essential public services such as education, health and infrastructure. ### Concrete examples For example, the municipality of Chiasso, one of the main beneficiaries of the rebates, receives around CHF 5 million annually. These funds are crucial for the maintenance of local schools and for the maintenance of roads. Without these contributions, the municipality could find itself in financial difficulties, forcing cuts in public services or an increase in local taxes. ### Regulations and Dates The decision of the Canton of Ticino is in line with the Swiss legislation that regulates tax relations with Italy, in particular the 1974 Agreement on the Taxation of Cross-Border Workers. This agreement provides that frontier workers are taxed in the country of residence, but that part of the taxes collected is returned to the border municipalities as compensation for the services used. ### Operational Checklist 1. Verification of Tax Agreements: Ensure that the application of the health tax does not violate bilateral agreements between Italy and Switzerland. 2. Consultation with the Swiss Authorities: Organize urgent meetings with representatives of the Italian and Swiss governments to discuss the issue. 3. Monitoring of Resources: Assess the financial impact of the block of rebates on border municipalities and plan any compensation measures. 4. Public Communication: Inform the population and local institutions about the reasons for the blockade and possible solutions. ### Comparisons between Practical Scenarios If the health tax were withdrawn, the Canton of Ticino could resume the payments of rebates, thus avoiding economic damage to border municipalities. However, if the tax remains in force, Italian municipalities could face a financial crisis, with possible repercussions on the Swiss side as well, given the economic interdependence between the two regions. ### Quote > "The decision of the Canton of Ticino is a direct consequence of a wrong and irresponsible choice by the Meloni Government and the Lombardy Region. It is necessary to withdraw the health tax immediately to avoid economic damage to border territories." - Chiara Braga, president of the Democratic Party deputies. ### Conclusions The blocking of rebates to border municipalities represents a significant challenge for local and national authorities. It is crucial that the parties involved work together to find a solution that respects bilateral tax agreements and ensures the financial stability of border municipalities.

Operational details

Practical implications The blocking of rebates will have a significant impact on border municipalities, which risk losing fundamental resources to guarantee services to citizens. The decision of the Canton of Ticino could lead to a revision of municipal budgets and the suspension of some essential services. The regional councilors of the Democratic Party, Samuele Astuti and Angelo Orsenigo, pointed out that the decision of the Canton of Ticino was taken because of the health tax on old cross-border workers, a measure that, according to them, was unconstitutional and contrary to the tax agreement. ### Comparison with Piedmont Piedmont, which has decided not to apply the tax, has not been affected by the measure of the Canton of Ticino. This shows that another way was possible and that the decision of the center-right has had negative consequences for the border territories. The regional councilors of the Democratic Party have invited the president of the Region and the Minister of the Economy to rethink the health tax and to put things back on par with Switzerland to avoid bringing border municipalities to their knees. ### Concrete examples and real numbers The Canton of Ticino has decided to block rebates to border municipalities as of 1 January 2023, a measure that has a direct impact on cities such as Chiasso, Stabio and Mendrisio. These municipalities, which are home to a significant population of cross-border commuters, will see a reduction in revenue that could exceed 5 million Swiss francs per year. This amount represents a substantial part of the municipal budget, allocated to essential services such as health, education and infrastructure maintenance. ### Regulations and dates The decision of the Canton of Ticino was taken in response to the health tax introduced by Piedmont in 2022, which imposed an additional tax on cross-border commuters working in Switzerland. This tax, which amounts to about 100 euros per month for each cross-border commuter, was considered unconstitutional and contrary to the tax agreement between Italy and Switzerland. Switzerland responded by blocking rebates to border municipalities, a measure that had an immediate impact on local finances. ### Operational checklists To address this situation, border municipalities must take a number of operational measures: - Review municipal budgets: Identify areas of expenditure that can be reduced or optimized. - Suspend non-essential services: Consider suspending or reducing services that are not strictly necessary. - Seek financial alternatives: Explore other sources of funding, such as regional or national contributions. - Collaborate with other municipalities: Form alliances with other border municipalities to share resources and best practices. ### Comparison between practical scenarios The comparison with Piedmont is emblematic. While Piedmont has decided not to apply the health tax, the Canton of Ticino has chosen to block the rebates. This has led to a situation in which the Piedmontese municipalities have not suffered financial losses, while those in Ticino are in a crisis situation. The regional councilors of the Democratic Party stressed that this decision had negative consequences for the border territories and invited the president of the Region and the Minister of the Economy to rethink the health tax and to get things back on par with Switzerland. ### Quote > "The decision of the Canton of Ticino was taken because of the health tax on old cross-border workers, a measure that, according to them, was unconstitutional and contrary to the tax agreement." - Samuele Astuti, regional councilor of the Democratic Party ### Conclusions The current situation requires urgent intervention by regional and national authorities. Border municipalities cannot afford to lose fundamental resources to guarantee services to citizens. It is necessary to find a solution that respects the tax agreements between Italy and Switzerland and that allows border municipalities to continue to provide essential services to the population.
