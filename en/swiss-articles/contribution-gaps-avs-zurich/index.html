AVS and complementary services: compensation fund

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 25 August 2026

First pillar in the Canton of Zurich: compensation fund, calculation of AVS annuity, contribution gaps and supplementary benefits for the minimum living wage.

Context

In brief

  • AVS is the first pillar of Swiss social security, funded by mandatory contributions of 5.3% for employees
  • The cantonal compensation office manages contributions and pensions in the area
  • Contribution gaps directly reduce the final monthly AVS pension
  • Complementary benefits provide a safety net for those with insufficient pensions

Key facts

  • What: Pillar-based social security system with AVS as the mandatory foundation
  • Employee contribution: 5.3% of gross salary (10.6% including the employer's share)
  • Management: Cantonal compensation office for collection and payment
  • Contribution gaps: Years without payments proportionally reduce the pension
  • Complementary benefits: Monthly supplement to cover the minimum subsistence level
  • Reference age: 64 years for women, 65 years for men

The Swiss social security system is structured around three pillars. AVS, the first pillar, is the mandatory foundation: every employee pays 5.3% of their salary, to which an equivalent employer contribution is added (10.6% of the payroll overall). Self-employed workers also pay contributions calculated on their professional income.

The cantonal compensation office collects these payments and manages the related benefits. It is not a personal account where the money remains frozen: it is an administrative institution operating according to solidarity-based principles, where the contributions of those who work finance the pensions of those who are already retired. Each canton has its own compensation office, while following uniform federal rules.

Contribution gaps: how they affect the pension

Not every year of working life sees regular payments. Periods of unemployment, illness, maternity covered by loss-of-earnings compensation (IPG), unpaid leave or other events can create gaps. These gaps REDUCE the final AVS pension, because the calculation is based on the average contributions paid during the years of the career (typically from age 21 to 64 for women, 65 for men).

The system provides for 44 years of full payments to qualify for the maximum pension. Each missing year proportionally lowers the monthly pension amount. A worker who completes their career with 5 years of uncovered gaps will receive a lower pension than someone with uninterrupted payments over the same working period.

Some periods, however, can be covered through equity contributions: unemployment with benefits, illness, recognized maternity — these situations are often considered years of contributions even without direct payment. It is essential to check the career statement with the compensation office to verify whether all gaps are legitimate.

The role of complementary benefits

If the calculated AVS pension is insufficient to cover the minimum subsistence level, complementary benefits (PC) come into play. These are funded by the canton and municipalities and provide a guaranteed monthly supplement to ensure a decent minimum income. They do not derive from your personal payments, but are a form of public support based on actual need.

Operational details

How the compensation fund is structured

The canton’s compensation fund (as in every Swiss canton) is the body that centralizes the management of AVS at the territorial level. It receives payments from companies and self-employed workers, maintains individual contribution records, calculates pensions when the reference age is reached (64 for women, 65 for men), and makes monthly payments to pensioners.

The fund is NOT a bank where your money remains deposited in a personal account — it is a public administration that distributes the pension wealth collected according to federal rules and the principle of solidarity between generations. Each canton has its own compensation fund, often managed by the cantonal department of social insurance or the finance office.

Pension calculation: components and variability

The AVS pension is not simply a refund of what was paid in. It is calculated based on three main components:

1. Years of contributions: the completeness of the working career (ideally 44 years for the maximum pension). Anyone with gaps receives a proportionally lower pension.

2. Average salary: the average of the incomes declared during the career, adjusted according to federal indices. It is not only the final salary that counts, but the entire employment history, which makes it important to verify the accuracy of the recorded data.

3. Contribution gaps: each year without a payment reduces the final calculation. Some gaps can be covered (illness, unemployment with benefits, maternity IPG), others cannot.

In addition to the calculation structure, AVS pensions are indexed annually by the Confederation according to inflation and the national average salary. They are not fixed — they increase every year, at least nominally, ensuring an adjustment to the cost of living.

Supplementary benefits: the activation mechanism

Supplementary benefits (PC) come into play when the AVS pension is not sufficient to cover the “minimum subsistence level.” This limit varies by canton and family composition: a single widower will have a different minimum subsistence level from a couple, which will have different figures from someone living with other family members.

The canton (and the municipality of residence) verify:

  • Established monthly AVS pension
  • Available assets (with deductible allowances)
  • Other sources of income (LPP pensions, withdrawals from the third pillar, income from work still being performed)
  • Actual housing costs and specific needs (health, disability)

If the total is below the established minimum subsistence level, the PC covers the monthly shortfall. It is an assistance-based, non-contributory benefit: it does not derive from your personal contributions, but is public support granted on the basis of a means test.

Key points

Checking your rights: a practical step-by-step guide

If you are approaching the reference age (64-65 years) or wish to check your AVS entitlements, follow these concrete steps:

1. Request your career record (Versicherungsverlauf) Contact your cantonal compensation office or visit the website of the Federal Social Insurance Office (FSIO, German acronym UFAS, French OFAS). The record shows all registered payments, gaps, the length of the credited career and personal data as they appear in the federal archives. It is free and you can request it online or by post.

2. Calculate a pension estimate Many cantons and the FSIO offer online calculators where you enter the data from the record and receive a projection of your future AVS pension. This estimate is useful for planning retirement and understanding whether supplementation with other sources of income (LPP, third pillar) will be necessary.

3. Assess an appeal for unjustified gaps If you discover unjustified gaps (periods during which you should have paid but no payment is recorded), you can appeal within a limited time frame (usually 1-2 years) to have them filled or corrected. Some periods of illness, unemployment with benefits or other events may be covered by equitable contributions — check with the office.

4. Plan the third pillar If the pension estimate seems insufficient, consider making payments into a conto del pilastro 3a up to the annual maximum (variable amount, to be checked with your bank each year). This supplements AVS and reduces federal, cantonal and municipal taxation.

How to apply for supplementary benefits

In the 3-6 months before accessing the pension (do not wait until the first day of retirement), contact:

  • Cantonal supplementary benefits office: in the Canton of Zurich, consult the official cantonal website and search for "AVS/AI supplementary benefits"
  • Municipality of residence: many municipalities process applications locally in coordination with the canton

Documents required (typical list, check with the canton):

  • Official cantonal application form (template downloadable from the cantonal website)
  • Latest AVS career record
  • Documentation of assets (bank statements, property certificates, life insurance policies with surrender value)
  • Documentation of current income (if you still work or have other sources)
  • Rental agreement or certificate of ownership of the home
  • Certificate of residence

Processing time varies (on average 2-3 months), so it is advisable to start the procedure in advance.

Frequently Asked Questions
What if I have contribution gaps in the AVS?
Gaps reduce the final rent proportionally to the missing years. If you complete 44 years of contributions you receive the maximum annuity; each missing year you lower the amount. Some periods (sickness, unemployment with allowance, maternity covered by IPG) can be recognised as covered even without direct payments. Check the career statement at the clearinghouse to see if all the gaps are correct.
Are the supplementary benefits automatic or do I have to apply?
Complementary services are on request, not automatic. You must apply to the canton or municipality of residence before you retire. The clearinghouse does not process it automatically — it is up to you to activate it. Income and equity documents are needed for the resource test that verifies entitlement.
Can I know in advance what my future AVS annuity is?
Yes. Request your career statement from your canton's clearinghouse (free of charge, online or by post), then use the UFAS or canton calculator for an estimate. Your payments are recorded in the federal archives and the projection is reasonably reliable, barring future regulatory changes or extraordinary indexing.
Who runs the clearinghouse in my canton?
Each canton has its own compensation fund for the AVS — it is a cantonal institution, often managed by the department of social insurance or finance. It's not a private bank. To contact her, check the canton's official website (e.g. canton-zurigo.ch) and search for 'AVS clearinghouse' or 'ausgleichskasse'.
If I access my pension early (64 instead of 65), is the pension lower?
Yes, with early access the annuity is permanently reduced by a percentage established by law (malus of anticipation). Each year in advance involves a fixed reduction in the monthly allowance. The clearinghouse communicates the exact reduction when you request early access, allowing you to evaluate whether it is economically convenient.
