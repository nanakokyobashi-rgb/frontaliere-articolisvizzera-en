On 15 October 2026, La Posta presented the first interim report six months after the introduction of the digital letter. According to reports, this is the first experience of this kind in its 177-year history. The overall figure is seven million digital mailings, three million of which were letters.

The service's first report

Nicole Burth, head of La Posta's Digital Service unit, explained to Radiogiornale that 960'000 customers had decided to receive digital letters in La Posta's app. The number is growing, but the figure should be read as the number of accounts opened. It therefore does not mean that all customers have already actually used the service.

La Posta considers the report positive, while indicating some developments that should proceed more quickly. The point concerns above all the amount of mail available through the platform. For private users, Burth explained, the service becomes interesting when a lot of correspondence passes through it. Large companies are also needed to achieve this result.

The first major users have already switched to digital letters, while considerable potential remains among banks and insurers. The report thus compares recipient adoption with platform use by major senders.

Among the announced new features is the legally valid electronic signature. The example cited is that of a real-estate agency sending a rental contract: the future tenant will be able to sign it electronically. It will also be possible to modify and resend forms online, for example.

The app will be updated in November. The improvements were also developed based on customer feedback, with customers currently submitting 3'000 requests for advice about the app each month. On the subject of security, Burth described digital protection as an ongoing process: La Posta must keep updating and improving, but theft cannot be completely ruled out, just as a letter can be stolen in the real world.

Operational details

For anyone considering the digital letter, the practical point is not simply having an account. The service becomes more attractive when a significant amount of mail passes through the same platform: this is the condition identified by the head of Digital Service. Individual users therefore face a value proposition that depends on senders' behavior, not just on their own enrollment.

Value depends on senders

The arrival of the first major users confirms that organizations have already begun using it, while Posta points to considerable potential still remaining. Banks and insurance companies are specifically cited as sectors in which the digital letter could gain further ground. For the recipient, the practical consequence is that the account is the point of access, while its practical usefulness increases when more communications are actually sent through the application.

Electronic signatures introduce a second level of use. It is not merely a matter of receiving a document: in the example given of a lease sent by a real estate agency, the future tenant will be able to sign it with legal validity. The same platform will also allow users to edit and resend forms online. The operational promise described by Posta is therefore to simplify and streamline document-related processes.

This feature should not, however, be confused with an absolute guarantee against digital risk. Questions about security were asked in connection with cyberattacks and data theft. Nicole Burth's answer presents security as a continuous process requiring constant updates and improvements. Posta considers itself at the forefront on this issue, but acknowledges that theft cannot be ruled out completely. The comparison with a physical letter is explicit: even in the real world, a letter can be stolen.

To understand the service correctly, it is therefore useful to distinguish three aspects: access to the application, the presence of senders and usable documents, and the level of security, which remains the subject of ongoing work. Posta does not present digitization as perfect protection, but as an evolving channel, with features intended to make certain operations more straightforward.
