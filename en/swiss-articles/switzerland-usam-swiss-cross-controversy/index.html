Operational details

The new practice has been criticised by polls showing opposition from SMEs and citizens. USAM said this practice could lead to a weakening of the 'Switzerland' brand and a loss of consumer confidence. The organisation denounced relocations and called for the restoration of exclusivity for domestic producers.

The controversy arises from the fact that some foreign products are using the Swiss cross as a quality mark, despite not being produced in Switzerland. This has led to a situation where consumers can buy products that do not meet Swiss standards, but still use the Swiss brand.

USAM conducted a survey of Swiss citizens to understand their opinion on the matter. The survey revealed that 70% of participants are against the possibility of using the Swiss cross as a quality mark for foreign products. In addition, 60% of participants said they were willing to pay more for a quality Swiss product than a foreign product that uses the Swiss cross as a quality mark.

USAM ALSO pointed out that the possibility of using the Swiss cross as a quality mark for foreign products could lead to a loss of consumer confidence in Switzerland. “Switzerland is known for its high quality and attention to detail,” he said.

Key points

To better understand the situation, it is advisable to consult the USAM website. The organisation provides detailed information on the issue and the possible effects of the new practice.

The Swiss Union of Mutual Aid Associations (USAM) recently launched a campaign to raise awareness among the Swiss about the issue of foreign products that do not comply with Swiss regulations. USAM claims that the Swiss cross, a symbol of assistance and solidarity, is misused by some foreign companies to sell products that do not meet the quality and safety requirements required in Switzerland.

USAM released a press release stating that "the Swiss cross is not a mark of quality, but a symbol of assistance and solidarity." The organization says its mission is to protect Swiss people from substandard products and to promote a culture of care and solidarity.

The issue is particularly sensitive in Switzerland, where product safety and quality are core values. USAM highlighted that the new practice of using the Swiss cross by foreign companies could have serious consequences for Switzerland's reputation and consumer safety.
