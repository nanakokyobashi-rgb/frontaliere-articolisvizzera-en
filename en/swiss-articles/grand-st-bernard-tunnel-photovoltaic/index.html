One thousand solar panels, tilted at 60 degrees, are at the centre of the project being carried out by Tunnel du Grand-St-Benard SA in Bourg-Saint-Pierre, on the Swiss side of the Great St Bernard. The system will cover the roof of the gallery providing access to the tunnel.

The decision was made to increase reliance on renewable energy. The planned total area is 7000 square metres; the text describes the panels as currently being installed.

A schedule divided into tranches

The schedule proceeds in tranches. Installation of the thousand panels will begin at the end of 2026 and continue in 2027. The commissioning of approximately 3000 square metres is expected by the end of this year, while the remaining 4000 will be activated during the following year. Before moving to the installation phase, the company carried out a testing period between 2022 and 2025.

The expected result is stated precisely: 450 MWh of electricity produced each year, equal to half the tunnel's energy needs. The source therefore links the new production to the consumption of the tunnel's facilities.

“We have a large flat roof area,” said Baptiste Cavin, engineer responsible for maintenance and projects at Tunnel du Grand-St-Bernard SA.

Cavin specified that the solar farm will provide a winter supplement to the production already available. Electricity demand covers lighting, electromechanical systems and ventilation, and is described as considerable.

The total cost of the work is approximately 2 million francs. The work is carried out by company employees, and the project is 100% in-house.

The source finally specifies that this project has no connection with the floating solar farm on Lac des Toules, located at an altitude of 1810 metres in Bourg-Saint-Pierre territory (VS) and belonging to the electricity company Romande Energie. This distinction clarifies the scope of the announcement: the project concerns the tunnel and its roof on the Swiss side.

Operational details

A practical look at the investment

The new plant is part of a renewable production system already operating at the company. Inside the tunnel, a hydroelectric power station operates, powered by a small dam built along the road to the Great St Bernard Pass.

An additional source for the tunnel's systems

The photovoltaic system is presented as a winter supplement to current production. The choice of a flat roof makes it possible to use a surface already associated with the tunnel, while the panels' inclination is given as 60 degrees. However, the source does not provide a separate output for each panel.
