Apprenticeship and vocational training in the Canton of Zurich (cross-border guide)

Practical guide to finding a traineeship, employment contract, apprentice's salary and professional maturity in the Swiss system.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The Swiss system offers dual school-business training.
  • The search for the place takes place through official platforms and applications.
  • The internship contract is governed by the Code of Obligations.
  • Professional maturity can complement the academic course.

Key facts

  • What: Dual vocational training (AFC/CFP).
  • When: I usually start training in August.
  • Where: Canton of Zurich and Swiss territory.
  • Who: Cantonal vocational training offices.
  • Amount: Variable salary according to sector and year of course.

The vocational training system in the Canton of Zurich is based on the dual model, which allows apprentices to combine practical experience in the company with theoretical education at a vocational school. This pathway is structured to respond to the needs of the Swiss labour market, offering nationally recognised certifications such as the Federal Certificate of Capacity (AFC) or the Federal Certificate of Practical Training (CFP). The search for an internship generally begins one year before the start of the training course. Students should consult the official cantonal platforms, where companies publish open positions. The application procedure follows standard practices: sending curriculum vitae, motivation letters and school certificates. Operational details

The practical analysis of the apprenticeship system in the Canton of Zurich reveals significant implications for family budget management and financial planning. Although the apprentice receives a monthly salary, the latter varies considerably according to the economic sector, the year of the course and the directives of the trade associations. It is important to point out that this salary is subject to the social contributions provided for by Swiss law. In particular, the employer must deduct the fees for the AVS/AI/IPG and, depending on age and income, for the occupational pension LPP. As far as taxation is concerned, the apprentice is considered a full taxpayer. The income received must be declared in the annual tax return, contributing to the determination of direct federal, cantonal and municipal tax. Managing these aspects requires a clear understanding of your tax and social security obligations from day one.

Social security and health management scenarios

An often overlooked aspect is the integration of the apprentice into the Swiss healthcare system. Every person residing in Switzerland is obliged to take out a LAMal policy within three months of arrival or commencement of residence. Key points

For those wishing to undertake a professional training path, the procedure requires rigor and attention to deadlines. The first step consists of exploring the offers available through the official portals of the Canton of Zurich, which serve as the main clearinghouse for apprenticeship positions. Once a position of interest has been identified, it is necessary to prepare a complete application dossier. This must include a cover letter specific to the company, an updated curriculum vitae with references to previous school or internship experiences, and copies of the latest school certificates. Precision in the documentation is often the first filter used by companies to select candidates. After securing an interview, it is essential to arrive prepared regarding the company's activities and the professional profile required.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the documents required to apply for an apprenticeship?
To apply for an internship position in the Canton of Zurich you need to prepare a complete dossier that includes a personalised motivation letter, an up-to-date curriculum vitae highlighting your school or internship skills and experiences, and copies of the latest school certificates you have obtained. The accuracy of these documents is essential to pass the first stages of company selection.
Does the apprentice have to pay taxes on the salary received?
Yes, in Switzerland the apprentice is considered a taxpayer. Salary received during vocational training is subject to taxation. Therefore, the income must be correctly declared in the annual tax return, which will determine the direct federal, cantonal and municipal tax due based on the three-tier tax system in force in the Canton of Zurich.
What happens if the apprenticeship contract is not respected?
The traineeship contract is governed by the Swiss Code of Obligations. In the event of disputes or non-compliance with the contractual clauses, the parties may contact the cantonal vocational training office, which acts as supervisory authority. This body ensures that working and training conditions comply with federal and cantonal laws, intervening to mediate or resolve irregularities.
