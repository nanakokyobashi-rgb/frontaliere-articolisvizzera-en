Factors affecting salaries in Uri

The labor market of Uri Canton follows federal and cantonal principles that regulate wages, hours and pensions throughout Switzerland. Although Uri is not among the cantons with its own legislative minimum wage (federal law does not provide for a national minimum wage), salary depends on several factors: level of education, economic sector, collective labor agreements (CCL) in force and the cantonal tax regime.

For those working in Uri, some common national thresholds apply. The maximum working hours are set at 45 hours per week for industry, offices and commerce; other sectors can agree up to 50 hours. Minimum vacation is four weeks per year, five for employees under 20 years old. Contract terminations follow precise timelines: 1 month during the first year, 2 months from the second to the ninth year, 3 months from the tenth year onwards.

Operational details

Evaluate the contractual conditions in Uri

When moving or changing jobs in Uri, the first assessment is about the level of training and experience required. A high nominal salary can be deceptively low once taxes, contributions and insurance premiums are deducted. A worker receiving a gross salary often finds that the net is significantly lower when the three tax rates (federal, cantonal, municipal) and social insurance contributions are subtracted.

In URI, as in all of Switzerland, a first discriminant between contracts is the adherence to a collective labour agreement. If your employer is a member of an employer association that adheres to a CCL in your industry, the contract is subject to federal and sectoral minimum standards: hours, holidays, night/holiday supplements, salary supplements are already defined. An individual contract, by contrast, requires case-by-case negotiation and leaves fewer protections established by law or agreement.

Social security and taxes: the impact on net income

Compulsory social security (AVS/AI) weighs directly on the salary: 5.3% is automatically paid from the paycheck; the employer pays an additional 5.3%. Unemployment insurance (AD) is another 1.1% until a ceiling is reached. If your contract includes an LPP/BVG fund, the contribution varies by age group and can reach 18% for workers over 55.

Key points

Concrete steps to start working in Uri

Before accepting a job offer in Uri, gather information on three fronts. First: verify the applicable collective labor agreement (CLA) in your sector by contacting the trade union or employer; the CLA defines sectoral minimum wage, working hours, benefits and forms of protection. Second: calculate the tax effect using the SECO calculator or the Uri tax office; enter the proposed gross salary and immediately discover your monthly/annual net income. Third: check LAMal premiums for your profile (age, gender, franchise) by visiting the official comparison tool and verify if you are entitled to cantonal subsidies.

When signing the contract, ensure it contains the start date, net/gross amount, duration (definite/indefinite), weekly hours, days of leave, probation period and termination clauses. If the contract refers to a CLA, request a copy of the applicable text. Keep the documentation: it will be useful for future verifications and for any disputes with the employer.

Registration, taxes and deadlines

Within 3 months of arriving in Uri, register with the municipal residence authorities (population office). Registration is mandatory and opens access to cantonal public services. At the same time, you must register with a LAMal health insurance fund; you can do this online or by directly contacting an insurance company.

If you are a tax resident in Uri as of December 31, you are subject to federal, cantonal and municipal income tax. The federal tax return usually expires on March 31 of the following year; Uri may have its own deadlines. Keep pay slips, receipts and pension documents to support your return.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a legislative minimum wage in uri?
No. Switzerland does not have a mandatory federal minimum wage; some cantons have introduced it separately. Uri does not currently set a minimum wage by law, but many sectors operate under collective bargaining agreements (CCLs) that set sectoral minimum wages. Checking the CCL applicable to your field of work is essential to know the contractual protections in force.
How is the net salary calculated from the gross salary in Uri?
From the gross salary are subtracted: AVS/AI/IPG contributions (5.3%), AD/AC unemployment insurance (up to 1.1%), LPP if applicable (7%-18% by age group), and federal + cantonal + municipal taxes. The three tax levels operate in cascade: the cantonal Uri multiplier and the municipal one determine the final rate. Use the salary calculator for an accurate estimate.
Is health insurance (LAMal) mandatory in Uri?
Yes. All residents of Switzerland, including those of URI, must take out LAMal coverage within 3 months of arrival. Premiums are individual and vary by canton/region and deductible chosen (from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500). Lower-middle income earners benefit from cantonal subsidies that reduce the premium; contact your municipality's social office to apply.
What are the minimum holiday and maternity rights in Switzerland?
Minimum holiday is 4 weeks per year (5 for employees under 20). Maternity covers 14 weeks at 80% of salary via loss of earnings allowance (IPG); paternity is 2 weeks. Cancellations of the contract: 1 month in the first year, 2 months from the second to the ninth year, 3 months from the tenth year onwards.
What changes if I work under CCL compared to an individual contract?
A CCL sets sectoral minimum wage, standard hours, night/holiday pay, additional vacation days, and other protections. The individual contract leaves room for negotiation but fewer safeguards. Check if the employer is a member of an employer association that adheres to a CCL in your industry; if so, the contract is automatically governed by the CCL.
