Rumors about Morgan Stanley, UBS rises above 3% (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 September 2026

UBS shares are up more than 3% in Zurich and close to 41 francs after rumours about a possible merger with Morgan Stanley.

Context

In brief

  • UBS gains over 3% on the Swiss stock exchange
  • The share price returns to nearly 41 francs
  • The rumors concern Morgan Stanley
  • In Zurich, the reaction remains cautious

Key facts

  • Company: UBS
  • Increase: over 3%
  • Price nearly reached: 41 francs
  • Bank mentioned: Morgan Stanley
  • Source of the rumors: Semafor Business
  • Former CEO mentioned: Colm Kelleher
  • Other possible candidates: Standard Chartered and Deutsche Bank
  • Financial agency mentioned: Awp

This morning on the Swiss stock exchange, UBS shares were in high demand. The shares of the company led by Sergio Ermotti gained over 3%, returning to nearly 41 francs. The reaction comes after several difficult days following the latest parliamentary decisions concerning capital requirements.

The origin of the rumors

Trading was reignited by rumors of a possible merger with the American investment bank Morgan Stanley. The source cited is a brief article by Semafor Business. The author writes that Morgan Stanley has long shown interest in UBS.

The text also highlights the role of Colm Kelleher, former CEO of the US institution and chairman of UBS’s board of directors. The connection is presented as something that would not be accidental. Among the other possible candidates for a merger are Standard Chartered, an English bank, and Deutsche Bank, a German bank.

The material cited links the recovery to the rumors and does not describe a merger that has already been finalized. The names of the other banks are likewise presented as possibilities: the source indicates neither a decision that has been reached nor the terms of any potential merger.

Caution in Zurich circles

In Zurich stock-market circles, reports the financial agency Awp, the rise is being greeted with a degree of caution. Similar speculation had already circulated about a year ago. At that stage, there was also talk of the possibility that UBS might move its headquarters to the United States, London or Singapore.

The source also reports a political interpretation of the rumors: some circles would think that the rumors serve to create a threatening atmosphere for political circles in Bern. The matter thus comes with two distinct elements: on the one hand, the morning’s performance, and on the other, the precedent of rumors that had already circulated.

Key points

Per seguire the matter in an orderly way, a four-step check can be used, based exclusively on the reported data.

1. Identify the type of information

The first question is whether one is reading a decision or a rumor. Here, the text discusses rumors and speculation about a possible merger. The source indicated is a short article from Semafor Business; the text does not report an announcement from UBS or Morgan Stanley. This distinction must also be maintained when the stock rises.

2. Separate names from decisions

Morgan Stanley is the bank associated with the hypothesis, while Standard Chartered and Deutsche Bank are indicated as other possible candidates. The presence of these names does not mean that a bank has been chosen. The reference to Colm Kelleher describes an existing connection within UBS's senior leadership, but does not in itself constitute proof of a corporate plan.

3. Note what can be verified

The observable fact is the rise in the share price on the Swiss stock exchange, with a return close to 41 francs. It should be noted together with the fact that UBS was coming off several difficult days, linked to the latest parliamentary decisions on capital requirements. The source does not indicate a deadline, an offer price, a document to sign or a procedure to initiate. Therefore, based on this article, there is no personal action to take.

4. Check what follows without anticipating it

Updates should be compared with the two elements that prompted caution in Zurich: similar speculation that had already circulated about a year earlier and the previous hypothesis concerning the relocation of the headquarters to the United States, London or Singapore. The material does not say that the relocation took place and does not present a new decision about the headquarters. The reader can therefore note any developments without turning a hypothesis into a fact.
