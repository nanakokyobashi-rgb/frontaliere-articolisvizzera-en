The protest in French-speaking newsrooms

LAUSANNE - Tamedia's French-speaking newsrooms oppose the new cuts announced on Monday by the Zurich-based publisher. Employees are calling for the layoffs to be abandoned and are considering possible forms of mobilization.

Tuesday, the day after the announcement, newsroom members met in a general assembly. The measure affects 41 employees in Switzerland, 13 of them in Lausanne and Geneva. In the press release issued on Wednesday, the staff describes the number of positions affected as considerable: nearly 10% of the company's French-speaking staff.

The entities mentioned include 24 Heures, La Tribune de Genèv, Le Matin Dimanche, Femina and Bilan. The list also includes the Digital, Print and Visual Desk areas, and thus different publications and functions within the company.

The 2024 precedent

The new staff reduction was unanimously rejected by the assembly, just four days after the end of the moratorium on layoffs agreed in 2024. At that time, 25 positions had already been eliminated in western Switzerland.

In the statement, employees argue that the new cuts would have devastating consequences for the publications. They also criticize the use of artificial intelligence and automation: in their view, these technologies could improve working conditions, but the company would also use them to replace people. The criticism also concerns the growing use of articles not produced on site, simple translations and contributions from abroad, sometimes poorly adapted to local realities.

The staff describes a situation of exhaustion and anger. The layoffs of those who have worked to provide quality journalism are in the firing line, while the managers identified by the employees as responsible for the situation remain in their positions.

Employees consider it unacceptable that the people who have spared no effort to ensure quality journalism should be laid off, while the managers who have led them into a dead end remain in their positions.

For the employees, TX Group — which Tamedia is part of — has the means to invest in journalism. The newsrooms therefore call on Tamedia's management and TX Group's board of directors to abandon the layoffs and open a transparent dialogue about the future of journalism in western Switzerland.
