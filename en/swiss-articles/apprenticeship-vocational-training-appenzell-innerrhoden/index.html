Apprenticeship in the canton of Inner Appenzell

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 25 August 2026

Practical guide: how to find an internship, enter into the contract, understand salary and professional maturity in the Inner Appenzell Canton.

Context

In brief

  • Apprenticeships in Switzerland last 3-4 years, combining company-based training (70%) and vocational school (30%)
  • In the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden, federal regulations and cantonal training ordinances apply
  • Apprentices are entitled to a written contract, minimum wage (if set by the canton), and at least 4 weeks of vacation
  • Completion with an apprenticeship certificate or additional vocational baccalaureate

Key facts

  • What: Dual vocational education and training system (apprenticeship)
  • When: After compulsory school, usually from age 15-16
  • Where: Company (practical training) + Cantonal vocational school
  • Who: Apprentice, vocational trainer, vocational school, employer
  • Duration: 3-4 years depending on the occupational profile
  • Regulation: Federal Code of Obligations (Arts. 344-346) + cantonal ordinances

In the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden, as in all Swiss cantons, apprenticeships represent the main path toward a nationally recognized vocational qualification. The system operates according to the federal dual model: the young person works in a company for approximately 70% of their time and attends the cantonal vocational school for the remaining 30%, thus ensuring both theoretical and practical skills.

Apprenticeships are regulated by federal law (Code of Obligations, Articles 344-346) and by the cantonal ordinances of the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden. The duration depends on the occupational profile chosen and is usually 3 or 4 years. During this period, the young person is protected by Swiss labor regulations: a maximum of 45 hours per week (except for specific sectors), a minimum of 4 weeks of vacation per year (5 weeks if under 20), and the right to a salary that complies with any established cantonal minimum wages.

The apprenticeship contract must be drawn up in writing and signed before the apprenticeship begins, clearly indicating duties, duration, remuneration, working hours and other rights. The apprenticeship concludes with a final examination leading to the award of the apprenticeship certificate (Federal Certificate of Practical Training), a document recognized nationwide. The most motivated can add a vocational baccalaureate, which opens access to higher education.

How to find an apprenticeship in the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden

Finding an apprenticeship is a structured process that usually begins as early as 1-2 years before compulsory school ends. In the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden, the main channels are:

  • Cantonal Office for Vocational Education and Career Guidance: provides personalized counseling, updated lists of companies, and cantonal regulations
  • National online portals (berufsberatung.ch): complete catalog of professions and companies offering positions
  • Industry associations and regional chambers of commerce: sector-specific lists
  • Direct contact with local craft and manufacturing companies
  • Career orientation days and job fairs organized by the canton

The apprenticeship contract and the apprentice's rights

The contract must obligatorily contain: the names of the apprentice, trainer and company; a description of the profession and main duties; start date and total duration; gross monthly remuneration; weekly working hours; vocational school days; vacation duration (legal minimum plus any additional days); clauses on early termination during the probationary period.

The apprentice has the right to terminate the contract without notice during the probationary period (usually the first 3 months). After the probationary period, early termination requires notice (depending on the cantonal ordinances, often 1-2 weeks). In the event of disputes over rights or interpretation of the contract, the cantonal conciliation office mediates between the parties.

Operational details

Apprentice remuneration and social contributions

Remuneration varies significantly depending on the professional sector and the year of apprenticeship. In the first year, an apprentice typically earns between 30-50% of the salary of a qualified worker in the same trade; in subsequent years, remuneration increases progressively, often following salary scales defined by sector-wide collective agreements or specific cantonal ordinances.

In the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden, as in other cantons, the main sectors (agriculture, commerce, crafts, industry, services) have remuneration tables publicly available from the cantonal vocational training office or professional associations. These amounts are binding for the apprenticeship contract.

Various mandatory social contributions at federal level are deducted from the apprentice's gross salary:

  • AVS/AI (Old-age and disability provision): 5.3% of gross salary (the employer pays an additional 5.3%, for a total of 10.6%)
  • Unemployment fund (AD/AC): 1.1% up to the established annual ceiling
  • Health insurance (LAMal): the apprentice must take it out independently within 3 months of starting (mandatory for residents), with premiums varying by canton and choice of deductible
  • Accident insurance (LAINF): managed and financed by the employer (a contribution of 0.7-1.5% depending on the sector), part of the labor cost not visible on the payslip
  • LPP (Occupational pension provision): not always applicable to apprentices, but in certain sectors or with larger employers, contributions are required on a percentage of the coordinated salary

AVS, unemployment and LAMal contributions are deducted directly from the payslip. Consult your calcolatore dello stipendio to estimate how much will remain net after all deductions.

Vocational baccalaureate: pathways and opportunities

The vocational baccalaureate is a post-apprenticeship diploma that provides direct access to the Swiss tertiary education system, including higher schools, universities of applied sciences and master's programs. It is a different and higher-level qualification than the standard apprenticeship certificate.

In the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden, the vocational baccalaureate is offered by the cantonal vocational school and can be pursued through two pathways:

1. Integrated vocational baccalaureate: completed during the apprenticeship (1-2 additional days per week compared with regular school), allowing students to obtain the diploma upon completion of the apprenticeship 2. Specialized vocational baccalaureate: completed after the apprenticeship, through a dedicated pathway lasting an additional 1-2 years

Candidates must pass written examinations in general subjects (German, mathematics, history, science, foreign languages) and specialized subjects related to the professional profile. The vocational baccalaureate certificate is issued by the canton and recognized nationwide, opening the door to higher education and the skilled labor market.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure for applying for an apprenticeship

Phase 1: Explore the available occupational profiles Consult the complete federal catalog of vocational training profiles on berufsberatung.ch (administered by SECO) and the portal of the cantonal vocational training office of the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden. Identify 3-5 professions that match your interests and abilities. Access detailed descriptions of duties, sectors and required skills.

Phase 2: Prepare the application dossier Write in German (the official language of the canton) a clear and concise CV, a cover letter addressed to the chosen employer, copies of your last two school report cards showing your academic performance, and language proficiency certificates (if required by the sector). Some sectors (e.g. healthcare, public services) may require a certificate of physical fitness or a background check.

Phase 3: Identify companies in the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden Contact craft, manufacturing, agricultural and service companies operating directly in your sector of interest. The Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden has chambers of commerce and industrial associations that provide updated lists and company addresses. Ask for information by phone before sending the dossier.

Phase 4: Submit applications and attend interviews Send your dossier by email or in person, attaching the cantonal apprenticeship application form (available from the vocational training office). The selected companies will contact you for an interview. Be punctual, professional and ready to describe your motivations and expectations.

Phase 5: Sign the contract If the interview outcome is positive, the company will propose a preliminary apprenticeship contract. Read all clauses carefully, verify the pay, schedule, school days and rights. Sign the contract within 4 weeks before the planned start date, which is usually August or September.

Deadlines and calendars for 2026

  • Search: start 1-2 years before completing compulsory schooling (therefore between the ages of 13-14)
  • Applications: usually from September to March of the previous year
  • Contract signing: by April for starts in September
  • Apprenticeship start: generally August/September (cantonal calendar)
  • Final exam: during the last 3 months of the apprenticeship
  • Certificate issuance: within 30 days of passing the final exam

Official resources in the Canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden

  • Cantonal vocational training office: counseling, company addresses, school calendars, official contract forms
  • School career guidance office: support for students in choosing a profile and employer
  • berufsberatung.ch portal: national catalog of professions, job descriptions, training courses, companies
  • Regional craft and industrial associations: sector-specific lists, networking events, information on minimum wages
  • SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs): federal regulations on vocational training and the protection of young workers

Tips for increasing your chances of acceptance

  • Contact companies early: many select candidates 1-1.5 years before the planned start
  • Attend orientation days, job fairs and events organized by the canton
  • Request a short apprenticeship experience (1-2 weeks) at a company of interest to become familiar with the sector and show motivation
  • Improve your basic German skills (if it is not your native language): this is a common selection criterion
  • Maintain good grades in mathematics, science and language: many companies check them on school certificates
  • Prepare for the aptitude test: some employers (especially in administration, healthcare and finance) administer one

Frequently Asked Questions
At what age can you start an apprenticeship in the Inner Appenzell Canton?
Typically after completing compulsory schooling, around the age of 15-16. In the Inner Appenzell Canton, as in all cantons, it is necessary to have regularly completed the cycle of compulsory education (three years of secondary school). There is no upper age limit, but the search is usually simpler by completing it within 17-18 years. The canton's vocational guidance office recommends starting the exploration of trades as early as age 13-14.
What is the average salary of an apprentice in the Inner Appenzell Canton?
It varies greatly by industry and year of apprenticeship. In the first year it typically ranges from CHF 400 to CHF 700 per month (gross); in subsequent years it gradually increases to CHF 800-CHF 1,500. These amounts are gross; from them are deducted AVS/AI contributions (about 5.3%), unemployment (about 1.1%) and health insurance costs. Consult the official wage curves of your sector at the cantonal training office or through our salary calculator.
Can I terminate the internship contract before it expires?
Yes. During the trial period (usually the first 3 months) you can withdraw without notice. After the probationary period, early termination requires notice (usually 1-2 weeks by both parties, depending on the cantonal ordinance). If there is just cause (abuse, harassment, serious breach of contract), you can terminate at any time. In the event of a dispute, the cantonal conciliation office will mediate between you and the employer.
Is professional maturity mandatory to work as a qualified person?
No. The traineeship certificate (federal certificate of practical training) is sufficient to be recognized as a skilled worker and enter the labor market in your trade. The professional maturity is additional and optional, but we strongly recommend it if you want to continue with higher education (specialized schools, universities of applied sciences, masters) or if you are aiming for managerial or expanded career roles in your industry.
How does insurance coverage work during an apprenticeship?
The accident insurance (LAINF) is fully covered and financed by the employer (mandatory, not paid in the envelope). Health insurance (LAMal) must be taken out independently within 3 months of the start of the apprenticeship (mandatory for residents of Switzerland), with premiums varying by canton, region and franchise chosen. AVS, disability and unemployment are automatically deducted from the gross salary. Check the exact amount in your monthly paycheck.
