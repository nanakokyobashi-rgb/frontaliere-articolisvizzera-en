Apprenticeship in the canton of Inner Appenzell (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 25 August 2026

Practical guide: how to find an internship, enter into the contract, understand salary and professional maturity in the Inner Appenzell Canton.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The apprenticeship in Switzerland lasts 3-4 years combining business training (70%) and vocational school (30%)
  • In the Inner Appenzell Canton, federal regulations and cantonal ordinances on training apply
  • The apprentice is entitled to a written contract, minimum wage (if set in canton), minimum holiday of 4 weeks
  • Conclusion with certificate of internship or additional professional maturity

Key facts

  • What: Dual vocational training system (apprenticeship)
  • When: After compulsory school, usually from 15-16 years old
  • Where: Company (practical training) + Cantonal vocational school
  • Who: Apprentice, trainee teacher, vocational school, employer
  • Duration: 3-4 years depending on the professional profile
  • Regulation: Federal Code of Obligations (art. 344-346) + cantonal ordinances

In the Inner Appenzell Canton, as in all Swiss cantons, apprenticeship represents the main path towards a nationally recognized professional qualification. The system works according to the federal dual model: the young person works in a company for about 70% of his time and attends the cantonal vocational school for the remaining 30%, thus guaranteeing both theoretical and practical skills.

Operational details

Compensation and Social Contributions of the Apprentice

The compensation varies significantly depending on the professional sector and the year of apprenticeship attended. In the first year, an apprentice typically earns between 30-50% of the salary of a qualified worker in the same profession; in subsequent years, the compensation increases progressively, often following salary curves defined by sectoral collective contracts or specific cantonal ordinances.

In the Canton of Appenzello Interno, as in other cantons, the main sectors (agriculture, commerce, craftsmanship, industry, services) have publicly available remuneration tables at the cantonal office of education or professional associations. These amounts are binding for the apprenticeship contract.

From the apprentice's gross salary, various mandatory social contributions at the federal level are deducted:

  • AVS/AI (Old Age and Disability Insurance): 5.3% of the gross salary (the employer also pays 5.3%, total 10.6%)
  • Unemployment insurance (AD/AC): 1.1% up to the annual maximum established
  • Health insurance (LAMal): the apprentice must conclude it autonomously within 3 months from the start (mandatory for residents), with premiums varying by canton and by choice of deductible
  • Work accident insurance (LAINF): managed and financed by the employer (contribution of 0.7-1.5% depending on the sector), part of the invisible labor cost in the pay slip
  • LPP (Professional Old-Age Insurance): not always applicable to apprentices, but in certain sectors or larger employers, contributions are provided on a percentage of the salary coordinated

The AVS, unemployment, and LAMal contributions are deducted directly from the pay slip. Consult your salary calculator to estimate how much will remain net after all deductions.

Key points

Step-by-step procedure for applying for an internship

Phase 1: Explore available professional profiles Consult the complete federal catalogue of training profiles on berufsberatung.ch (managed by SECO) and the cantonal portal of the Appenzell Innerrhoden Office of Vocational Training. Identify 3-5 professions that match your interests and abilities. Access detailed descriptions of tasks, sectors, and required skills.

Phase 2: Prepare the application dossier Write a clear and concise curriculum vitae in German (official language of the canton), a motivation letter addressed to the chosen employer, copies of the last two school reports certifying your performance, and language proficiency certificates (if required by the sector). Some sectors (e.g. healthcare, public services) may require a physical fitness certificate or a background check.

Phase 3: Identify companies in Appenzell Innerrhoden Contact artisanal, manufacturing, agricultural, and service companies directly that operate in your area of interest. The canton has trade associations and industrial associations that provide updated lists and addresses of companies. Ask for information by phone before sending the dossier.

Phase 4: Send applications and participate in interviews Send your dossier via email or in person, attaching the cantonal internship application form (available at the Office of Vocational Training). The selected companies will contact you for an interview. Be punctual, professional, and ready to describe your motivations and expectations.

Frequently Asked Questions
At what age can you start an apprenticeship in the Inner Appenzell Canton?
Typically after completing compulsory schooling, around the age of 15-16. In the Inner Appenzell Canton, as in all cantons, it is necessary to have regularly completed the cycle of compulsory education (three years of secondary school). There is no upper age limit, but the search is usually simpler by completing it within 17-18 years. The canton's vocational guidance office recommends starting the exploration of trades as early as age 13-14.
What is the average salary of an apprentice in the Inner Appenzell Canton?
It varies greatly by industry and year of apprenticeship. In the first year it typically ranges from CHF 400 to CHF 700 per month (gross); in subsequent years it gradually increases to CHF 800-CHF 1,500. These amounts are gross; from them are deducted AVS/AI contributions (about 5.3%), unemployment (about 1.1%) and health insurance costs. Consult the official wage curves of your sector at the cantonal training office or through our salary calculator.
Can I terminate the internship contract before it expires?
Yes. During the trial period (usually the first 3 months) you can withdraw without notice. After the probationary period, early termination requires notice (usually 1-2 weeks by both parties, depending on the cantonal ordinance). If there is just cause (abuse, harassment, serious breach of contract), you can terminate at any time. In the event of a dispute, the cantonal conciliation office will mediate between you and the employer.
Is professional maturity mandatory to work as a qualified person?
No. The traineeship certificate (federal certificate of practical training) is sufficient to be recognized as a skilled worker and enter the labor market in your trade. The professional maturity is additional and optional, but we strongly recommend it if you want to continue with higher education (specialized schools, universities of applied sciences, masters) or if you are aiming for managerial or expanded career roles in your industry.
How does insurance coverage work during an apprenticeship?
The accident insurance (LAINF) is fully covered and financed by the employer (mandatory, not paid in the envelope). Health insurance (LAMal) must be taken out independently within 3 months of the start of the apprenticeship (mandatory for residents of Switzerland), with premiums varying by canton, region and franchise chosen. AVS, disability and unemployment are automatically deducted from the gross salary. Check the exact amount in your monthly paycheck.
