Operational details

The European Union has approved a reform for unemployed frontier workers, which could cost Switzerland up to a billion francs a year. Switzerland is particularly affected by this reform as it is home to the largest number of border workers in Europe.

The European reform would overturn the principle that today a border worker employed in Switzerland contributes to Swiss unemployment insurance, but in the event of job loss receives benefits from his country of domicile. This could lead to a significant increase in costs for Switzerland, which already hosts around 1.3 million border workers, of whom around 700,000 are workers.

Switzerland is one of the top destinations for frontier workers, with 60% of them working in the agricultural sector, 20% in the manufacturing sector and 10% in the services sector. Border workers are crucial to the Swiss economy, contributing around CHF 10 billion per year to GDP.

The EU reform could lead to an increase in costs for Switzerland of about 700 million francs per year, due to the increase in benefits for unemployed border workers. This could be covered by an increase in taxes or an increase in contributions for frontier workers.

Key points

The recent European reform on the management of unemployed frontier workers is creating significant burdens for Switzerland. The key question is how to cope with these additional expenses and what measures to take to minimise the impacts on our economy.

Switzerland may consider increasing its resources to meet the additional costs arising from this reform. According to estimates, the cost could vary between 500 and 1 billion Swiss francs per year. This increase in expenses could be covered by an increase in taxes or the contribution of policyholders.
