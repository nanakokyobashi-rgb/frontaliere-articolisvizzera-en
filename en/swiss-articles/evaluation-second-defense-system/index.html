Air defense: evaluation of Bodluv GR 2 completed

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 September 2026

Armasuisse has completed the evaluation for the second Bodluv GR 2 ground-to-air defense system. The choice goes to the Federal Council.

The evaluation phase closes

The Federal Office for Defence Procurement, Armasuisse, has completed the evaluation of the second ground-based air defense system intended for the Swiss Army. The choice of model now lies with the Federal Council, as communicated by Armasuisse to Keystone-ATS. The announced step therefore concerns the model to be selected, not a decision already awarded to a single bidder.

The procurement, internally designated Bodluv GR 2, is intended to fill the gap created by the known problem with the delivery of the US Patriot system. Delivery will be delayed by five to seven years. Kaj-Gunnar Sievert, spokesperson for Armasuisse, described the situation as follows:

«Until one of the two longer-range Bodluv systems is introduced, Switzerland has no protection».

Armasuisse considers a new delivery deadline for the Patriot by 2032 realistic. Defense Minister Martin Pfister therefore intends to proceed with the purchase of the second system as quickly as possible. Sievert also confirmed that the military and arms-policy evaluations of the bids received have been completed, as SonntagsBlick had reported in advance.

For the second system, Armasuisse gathered information from manufacturers in France, Israel and South Korea. All bidders contacted submitted binding offers. SonntagsBlick identifies the system of the Franco-Italian company Eurosam as a possible winner, but Armasuisse provided no information on the individual systems under evaluation.

Elements mentioned include the Federal Council's desire to purchase, if possible, more armaments material in Europe and the politically sensitive situation of a possible purchase from Israel or South Korea. The contract should also help improve relations with France, considered strained after the choice of the US F-35 over the French Rafale. Operational details

Three figures, three readings

According to Bern, five billion francs are earmarked for the purchase of the second air defense system. The sum is fully covered by the army's supplementary funding, amounting to 24 billion. Armasuisse states that no additional funds will be needed as part of the regular armaments program. To read the picture without mixing different items, the comparison reported by the source is as follows:

The first comparison concerns the Patriot. Armasuisse attributes the increase over the initial estimate to the redefinition of priorities by the US government, inflation, adjustments for obsolescence, and the expansion of capabilities. The source does not present a combined total for the two systems: the maximum cost indicated for the Patriot and the five billion earmarked for the second system therefore remain figures relating to separate items.

A choice still open

The evaluation of the offers is complete, but the selection of the model still belongs to the Federal Council. The binding offers from manufacturers in France, Israel, and South Korea describe the field of bidders; they do not by themselves constitute the announcement of a winner. The reference to Eurosam, too, remains the one reported by SonntagsBlick, while Armasuisse has chosen not to provide details on the individual systems.

The timetable presents a second level of interpretation. The Patriot could be delivered by 2032, according to the timeframe considered realistic by Armasuisse, while Martin Pfister wants to accelerate the purchase of the second system. The source does not, however, indicate a delivery date for the new system. The procedure to follow

For those who want to follow the dossier without attributing to it data that were not communicated, the sequence consists of five checks.

1. Consider the evaluation phase concluded: Armasuisse explained that the military and arms-policy evaluations of the offers have been completed.

2. Await the decision on the model: the choice now lies with the Federal Council. Until this decision arrives, the name of Eurosam remains a possibility reported by SonntagsBlick and not an official choice indicated by Armasuisse.

3. Check the Patriot schedule: the expected delay is five to seven years, and Armasuisse considers a new delivery deadline by 2032 realistic.

4. Separate the two financial items: five billion francs are earmarked for the second system, covered by the army's supplementary financing amounting to 24 billion. The text specifies that no additional funds will be needed in the normal armaments program.

5. Check for any new information on the systems: upon request, Armasuisse did not provide information on the individual systems under evaluation. Any further detail must therefore be distinguished from the rumors reported by SonntagsBlick.
