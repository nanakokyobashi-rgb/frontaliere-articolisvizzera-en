Mandatory helmet for scooters: Federal Council plan (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 20 August 2026

The Federal Council proposes mandatory helmet for electric scooters after the increase in accidents. Find out what's going to change in Switzerland.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The Federal Council wants to make the helmet mandatory for scooters
  • The measure will follow an upcoming review of the federal ordinance
  • The government refuses registration and insurance
  • Reason: Accidents increase in Switzerland

Key facts

  • What: Helmet requirement for electric scooters (proposal)
  • Who: Swiss Federal Council, in response to a motion by Jacques Nicolet (UDC/VD)
  • When: Next revision of the ordinance (date not yet specified)
  • Where: All of Switzerland
  • Reason: Increase in accidents, especially in pedestrian areas
  • Implications: No to compulsory registration and insurance

The Swiss Federal Council is preparing to impose a helmet requirement as a safety measure for electric scooters. The decision comes after a motion presented by Jacques Nicolet (UDC/VD) and responds to a growing concern about the increase in accidents related to this means of transport, particularly in urban pedestrian areas.

However, the Swiss government has clarified its position on other aspects of the proposal: it categorically rejects a registration and insurance obligation for electric scooters. According to the Federal Council, such measures would result in disproportionate administrative burdens for consumers, cantons and industry.

Operational details

Why the helmet and the license plate?

The Federal Council's choice to support only the helmet obligation (rather than registration and insurance) reflects a pragmatic assessment of the costs and benefits. The obligation of the helmet is a low administrative cost measure: it does not require a national database, it does not require periodic reviews in the cantons, and it can be applied through a simple safety regulation.

On the contrary, the registration of scooters would bring with it a series of organisational complexities. Each canton would have to operate a registration system, municipalities would have to coordinate controls, and industry would face significant bureaucratic burdens. The government quantified this in terms of disproportionate administrative burdens for consumers, cantons and industry.

In addition, there is the issue of retroactivity. Applying the license plate obligation to scooters already in circulation would mean forcing owners to make a mandatory registration. This would represent what the Federal Council calls an interference with vested rights — in other words, a violation of the principle that a rule cannot retroactively oblige owners of already legitimately owned property.

Key points

What to do now as an e-scooter user

If you own or regularly use an electric scooter in Switzerland, the current situation does not yet make helmet use mandatory (barring stricter local regulations that some cantons may have already adopted). However, the Federal Council's decision clearly signals that this obligation will arrive in the future. Scooter users may want to consider adopting a helmet early, in line with best safety practices already widespread in other European countries.

The challenge now is timing. The Federal Council has indicated that it will introduce the helmet obligation during an upcoming ordinance revision, but has not specified when this revision will take place. There is no publicly communicated deadline for the moment.

This means that over the coming months, the regulatory framework will remain substantially unchanged. In the medium term, cantonal consultation on an ordinance revision could begin. In the long term, once it enters into force, the obligation will become binding for all users on Swiss territory.

The cantons will have to coordinate with local police on the enforcement of the new rule once the federal revision is communicated. The cantonal role will be crucial to ensure the uniform implementation of the measure and to educate users on compliance with the new regulation.

Where to follow updates

To stay updated on this measure, check the Federal Council press releases available on the governo.admin.ch website. In addition, check your canton's administration website for any supplementary cantonal ordinances that might accompany the federal revision. Stay informed through national news sources such as RSI, SRF, and RTS.

Frequently Asked Questions
When will helmets become mandatory for scooters?
The Federal Council has indicated that it will submit the helmet obligation at a forthcoming review of the ordinance, but has not specified a precise date. Timing will depend on the timing of the federal review procedure. Currently, there is no publicly communicated deadline.
Why doesn't the government want to force registration?
According to the Federal Council, registration would entail disproportionate administrative burdens for consumers, cantons and industry. Furthermore, applying it retroactively to scooters already in circulation would represent an interference with the acquired rights of current owners, violating the principle of non-retroactivity.
What type of helmet will be required?
The source does not specify the exact type of helmet required. The technical details (certifications, safety standards) will be defined in the ordinance when it is reviewed by the Federal Council. Generally, cycling helmets with EN 1078 certification are considered suitable for this type of activity.
Can I already be subject to the obligation in my canton?
The source does not specify whether the cantons will be able to introduce local obligations before the federal review. You should check directly with your cantonal administration or consult the cantonal website for any specific rules on scooters already in place locally.
