Operational details

On 15 June, the Swiss Federal Council approved the new Collective Labour Agreement (CCL) for Swiss ground staff, which provides for a reduction in weekly working hours from 42 to 40 hours. This time change applies to all Swiss employees, including those in Zurich, Bern and Geneva, which are among the most important cities in the country.

In addition, the new CCL introduces significant economic improvements, such as an allowance of CHF 7.50 per hour for Saturday work and an increase in surcharges for Sunday, holiday and night employment. These improvements were achieved thanks to negotiations between Swiss and the unions, which represented the interests of the employees.

According to estimates, the new CCL will bring a 10% increase in the income of Swiss employees, who work in the air transport sector. This means that an employee who currently earns CHF 60,000 per year, could increase his income by CHF 6,000 per year, thanks to the economic improvements introduced by the new CCL.

The new CCL also provides for an increase in surcharges for night work, which will go from 10% to 15% of the hourly wage. This means that an employee who earns 50 francs per hour could earn 7.50 francs more per hour if they work at night.

Key points

The new Collective Agreement for Ground Personnel (CCL) will be valid for five years and provides for an increase in the salary table of 200 francs both on the minimum and on the maximum. For 2027, a minimum charge of 0.5% is guaranteed.

The new CCL has been negotiated between the social partners and the Swiss government, with the aim of improving the working conditions of employees in the tertiary sector. The contract also provides for a reduction of the working week to 40 hours, with the aim of improving the quality of life of employees.

According to statistics, in 2022, the tertiary sector employed around 2.5 million people in Switzerland, representing 65% of the total workforce. With the new CCL, productivity is expected to increase by 10% and labour costs to be reduced by 5%.

The new CCL also provides for an increase in employee pay, with an increase of 10% for minimums and 5% for maximums.
