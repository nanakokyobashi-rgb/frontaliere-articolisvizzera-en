Voting in nine cantons: tax and voting mobility (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 20 September 2026

On Sunday, September 27, nine cantons are voting: tax in Vaud, contraception in Geneva and cross-border mobility of 39.5 million.

Context

In brief

  • Votes in nine Cantons on Sunday, September 27
  • Themes: taxation, civil rights, education, and mobility
  • Focus on tax cuts in Vaud and the vote on 16-year-olds' voting rights in Graubünden
  • Investments for cross-border mobility in Geneva

Key facts

Date: September 27 Locations: Nine Cantons Geneva mobility credit: 39.5 million Swiss francs Geneva traffic target: -15,000 vehicles per day

On Sunday, September 27, voters in nine Swiss Cantons will be called to the polls to decide on a series of reforms affecting the economy, civil rights, and social policies. The consultations cover a wide spectrum of topics, ranging from land use planning to education, including issues related to contraception, hooliganism, and infrastructure for cross-border mobility.

The main cantonal consultations

Among the most relevant themes are the initiative for a substantial reduction in taxes in the Canton of Vaud and the proposal to extend the right to vote to 16-year-olds in the Canton of Graubünden. In Basel-Country, the vote will concern the law on compensation for capital gains from land use planning, following a Federal Court ruling that found part of the current regulation non-compliant. In this case, voters will have to choose between an initiative by the Association of Landowners (HEV), which aims for minimal contributions and strict limits on the use of funds, and a counter-proposal by the Grand Council that provides for slightly higher contributions.

In the Canton of Basel-City, the SVP (Swiss People's Party) has launched the 'anti-chaos' initiative, which aims to make participants in unauthorized demonstrations responsible for police costs and damages caused. The proposal includes extending such liability to those who resort to violence during authorized gatherings and asks the Government for a plan to reduce unauthorized demonstrations. In parallel, citizens of Basel-City will vote on an initiative to use a trade fair pavilion as a storage facility for the Historical Museum, the State Archives, and the University Library, to avoid new constructions near the University.

In the Canton of Bern, attention is focused on education. A popular initiative requests that the right to quality education be enshrined in the cantonal constitution, obliging the Canton and Municipalities to guarantee high-level teaching and qualified staff throughout the territory. Operational details

Analysis of Tax and Social Reforms

The votes scheduled for September 27th reveal a divergence in approaches among the various Cantons, particularly regarding the management of public finances and social services. In the Canton of Vaud, the call for a substantial tax reduction represents an attempt to lighten the tax burden on taxpayers, a theme with direct implications for the purchasing power of residents. This contrasts with the situation in Basel-Country, where the debate shifts to the redistribution of land-use planning surpluses, highlighting the tension between the interests of landowners (HEV) and the funding needs of municipalities.

On the front of civil rights and health, the Socialist Party's (PS) initiative in Geneva for free contraception raises questions about equity in access to sexual health. While the left and the Centre support the text, the Council of State, along with the PLR, MCG, and UDC, contests the proposal, deeming it not sufficiently targeted at lower-income brackets and potentially costly. This debate reflects the complexity of implementing social subsidies in a system where the efficiency of public spending is constantly monitored.

Impact on Mobility and Infrastructure

A case of particular relevance for those commuting between Switzerland and France is the proposed credit of 39.5 million francs in Geneva. The investment is intended to co-finance infrastructure in French territory, including 1,600 new parking spaces in P+R facilities, bus priority lanes, and the expansion of the tram network. The operational goal is to reduce daily traffic by 15,000 vehicles on the roads of the Canton. This project demonstrates how traffic management and pollution require coordinated cross-border solutions to improve urban quality of life.

For those residing in Switzerland, these changes directly influence the cost of living and the efficiency of public services. Smoother mobility in Geneva could reduce travel times, while decisions on education in Bern or voting rights in the Grisons redefine the social contract at the local level. Key points

Operational Guide for the Voter and Taxpayer

For residents of the nine involved Cantons, the voting procedure for Sunday, September 27, follows the standard modalities of cantonal consultation. Voters will receive the informational material and ballot papers at their home address. It is fundamental to carefully read the differences between the popular initiative and any potential counter-proposal, as in the case of Basel-Country, where the option proposed by the Grand Council differs in the amount of contributions requested compared to the proposal by the HEV.

For those interested in tax reforms, especially in the Canton of Vaud, it is advisable to analyze their current tax situation. Since the Swiss tax system is structured on three levels (federal, cantonal, and municipal), a reduction in cantonal taxes can significantly vary the net annual amount depending on the municipal multiplier applied.
