Asylum applications: SEM lowers estimates for 2026 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 25 August 2026

In the first half of 2026, fewer than 10,000 asylum applications (the first drop since 2022). SEM forecasts 22,000 for the full year, vs. 26,000 initially estimated.

In the first half of 2026, for the first time since 2022, Switzerland registered fewer than 10,000 asylum applications. This is reported by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM), the federal body responsible for migration policy.

The data suggests a trend reversal: in the previous four years (2022-2025), the number of applications had remained stable or growing. Now the decline is marked.

The SEM has simultaneously revised downwards the projections for the whole of 2026: the Confederation expects 22 thousand requests by the end of the year, against the 26 thousand estimated in the initial plan. This is a reduction of about 4,000 applications, almost 15% less than the previous forecast.

Operational details

Practical implications for Swiss cantons and municipalities

The decline in asylum applications has concrete repercussions at the local level. Although the responsibility for migration and asylum lies with the federal government (and specifically the SEM), the operational management — housing, assistance, integration, medical care — falls on the cantons and municipalities.

Fewer requests mean:

  • Reduced pressure on local social and housing services: municipalities and cantons will need fewer temporary reception facilities. Some reception centres may be downsized or converted.
  • Less impact on cantonal funds: integration charges (language courses, starting work, legal assistance) for asylum seekers are borne or co-financed by the cantons. Fewer people to accommodate means fewer expenses.
  • Regional variability: the decline is not uniform. Cantons such as Geneva, Zurich and Bern, which have historically hired more asylum seekers, will experience more marked differences than cantons with smaller flows.

By contrast, some industries and manufacturing sectors rely on foreign labor to make up for skill shortages. A structural decline in migrants could, in the long term, create further pressures on wages and availability of workers in certain sectors (agriculture, construction, health, services). However, the source does not provide data on the sectors specifically concerned.

How the SEM Handles Asylum Applications: Procedures and Timelines

Those arriving in Switzerland who wish to request international protection must file an asylum application. The procedure follows well-defined steps:

1. Initial Registration: The applicant presents to the authorities (often at the border or at a SEM office) and is registered in the system. 2. Assignment to a Canton: The SEM distributes applicants among the cantons according to quotas established by law. 3. Reception and Interview: The competent canton provides temporary accommodation and the SEM conducts a thorough interview with the applicant to assess the validity of the application. 4. Decision: The SEM issues an asylum decree (approval), rejected asylum, or assisted repatriation. Average decision times range from a few months to over a year, depending on the complexity of the case and the SEM's workload. 5. Appeal: Those who receive a rejection may file an administrative appeal within specified timeframes (timelines vary by canton).

The decline in applications facilitates this procedure: fewer applications in the queue mean potentially faster processing times, reducing the period of uncertainty for the applicant and easing the administrative burden on the SEM.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the "S status" reserved for Ukrainians?
Statute S is a form of special protection established by Switzerland following the Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Unlike traditional asylum, Statute S does not require individual proof of political, religious or other persecution, but recognizes a situation of generalized conflict that endangers the civilian population. The SEM expects about 12,000 requests for articles of association by the end of 2026, a slight decrease compared to the previous year.
Why did the number of asylum applications fall in the first half of 2026?
The SEM attributes the main decline to two external factors: the decrease in Mediterranean crossings and the lower flow along the Balkan route. These changes are not due to stricter regulations in Switzerland, but to global dynamics (safety conditions on the routes, economic and war situation in the countries of origin, international migration control interventions). The absolute number of migrants on the move fell globally during 2025 and early 2026.
Fewer asylum seekers what impact do they have on services and the labour market?
A structural decline in asylum reduces the pressure on cantonal social services (housing, language courses, legal assistance) and on local public funds. In some economic sectors (agriculture, construction, health), where foreign labour is historically important, a lower inflow of workers could result in staff shortages and pressure on wages. The source does not specify sectoral economic impact figures, but the phenomenon is monitored by federal authorities.
How is the number of asylum seekers distributed among the cantons?
The SEM allocates applicants among the cantons according to a national distribution formula established by law. Historically, cantons such as Geneva, Zurich and Bern have taken on higher shares. With the overall decline, these cantons will also experience a decline, but the breakdown remains proportional to their respective quotas and reception capacities.
Where can I find official data on asylum applications in Switzerland?
The State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) regularly publishes statistics on asylum applications, decisions and nationality of applicants on the admin.ch website. Detailed statistics are also available from the Federal Statistical Office (FSO/BFS), which publishes quarterly and annual reports on migration, integration and asylum applications at the national level.
