How the first pillar works in Canton Uri: compensation fund, calculation of the AVS annuity and supplementary benefits for those who do not reach the minimum living wage.

Operational details

How supplementary benefits work in the Canton of Uri

Supplementary benefits (SB) to OASI/DI are regulated at the federal level by the Supplementary Benefits Act (LPC), but their payment and operational management is entrusted to the cantons. In Uri, responsibility lies with the cantonal compensation fund in collaboration with the social services, which process the application, collect the documents and calculate the monthly amount due.

The calculation formula

The SB amount is given by the difference between recognised expenses and computable income. Recognised expenses include the amount for basic needs (distinguished for single persons, couples, families), the actual rent (up to a recognised maximum), medical and paramedical expenses not covered by other insurances, and other items provided for by the LPC. Computable income, on the other hand, consists of the OASI/DI pension, any income from work, income from assets (assets above an exemption threshold are converted into notional income), third-party benefits and family allowances. If the difference is positive, the applicant is entitled to SB for the corresponding amount.

Who is entitled to SB

Persons who receive an OASI or DI pension, or who — within a certain time horizon — would be entitled to a pension, are entitled to SB, provided their economic situation meets the legal criteria. The application may also be submitted by persons who have not yet reached the reference age, if they receive a DI pension. For recipients of modest pensions, SB is often the difference between a poor old age and a dignified old age: in many situations the OASI pension alone does not even cover the rent and basic expenses.

Key points

Practical procedure: how to submit your application in Uri

Obtaining the AVS (Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance) pension and, if necessary, supplementary benefits (PC) in the Canton of Uri requires a few precise steps. The procedure is defined at the federal level, but the point of contact is the cantonal compensation office of Uri (Ausgleichskasse Uri).

Step 1 — Check your contribution record

Before submitting any application, it is useful to request an extract of the individual account (CI) from the FSIO (UFAS/BSV) or through the compensation office. The individual account lists all contributions paid, recorded income, and any gaps. If missing years appear, discuss with the compensation office the possibility of a retroactive payment.

Step 2 — Applying for the AVS pension

The AVS pension is not paid automatically: it must be requested from the Uri compensation office, usually a few months before reaching the reference age. The office sends the forms, collects the required documents (family record, salary certificates, any periods of work abroad) and forwards the file for calculation. The first pension is normally paid retroactively from the month of application, not earlier.

Step 3 — Possible application for supplementary benefits (PC)

If the AVS pension — alone or together with other income — does not cover the vital minimum needs, the PC phase opens. The application is submitted to the Uri compensation office with the following typical documents: AVS/AI pension decision, rent certificate and lease agreement, bank and securities statements, insurance policies (life, 3a), and spouse's income certificates. The office checks the recognised expenses and computable income and issues the decision.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Canton Uri Compensation Fund?
It is the cantonal body designated to manage the first AVS/AI/IPG pillar in Canton Uri: it collects contributions, settles AVS and AI annuities and manages applications for supplementary benefits (PC) for residents who do not reach the living minimum with just the annuity.
How is the AVS annuity calculated?
The annuity depends on the number of years of contribution and the average of the determining incomes. The scales provide for full annuities for those who have full contributions and partial annuities reduced in proportion to the missing years. Contribution gaps lower the annuity and, in some cases, can be filled with retroactive payments within the legal deadlines.
When are you entitled to supplementary benefits?
Beneficiaries of AVS or AI annuities (and, in certain cases, persons close to the annuity) whose economic situation does not cover the recognised expenses are entitled to the CP. The CP is calculated as the difference between recognised expenses (living expenses, rent, medical expenses) and computable income (income, substance plus deductible, other income).
What is the PC application like in Canton Uri?
The application must be submitted to the cantonal compensation fund of Uri, with the forms provided by the entity. The AVS/AI annuity decision, lease and rent amount, bank statements and securities, life and 3a policies, and spouse's income are typically needed. The cashier checks the situation and notifies the decision.
Should PCs be returned?
No, supplementary benefits are not loans and should not be repaid. However, any significant change (income, substance, rent, family composition) must be communicated to the cashier: failure to communicate may result in the revocation or reduction of the CP with recovery of the amounts already paid.
