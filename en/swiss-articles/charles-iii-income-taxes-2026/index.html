Practical Analysis of Real Tax Transparency and Implications for Switzerland The publication of King Charles III's tax return and the disclosure of taxes paid represent an important signal in the international tax transparency landscape. Even in Switzerland, where the issue of taxes and taxation is central to citizens, workers and businesses, similar initiatives can influence public debate and expectations of openness on the part of institutions and relevant subjects. In Switzerland, taxation is a federal and cantonal competence, with taxes such as direct federal tax, VAT, and contributions for AVS/AHV, LPP/BVG and LAMal/KVG. Tax returns are generally public only in specific cases, and tax transparency is often balanced with individual privacy. The gesture of the British Crown could renew the focus on these dynamics also in the Swiss context, where tax pressure and the cost of living are constant themes. Frequently Asked Questions
Why did King Charles III decide to publish his tax return?
King Charles III chose to make his tax return public as a gesture of transparency, breaking the British Crown's tradition of secrecy. This allowed to show the amount of taxes paid on private income since 2022.
What are King Charles III's main sources of income?
The main source of private income is the Duchy of Lancaster, which generates around £26.8 million in the 2024-2025 fiscal year thanks to farmland rents and the management of commercial and residential properties.
How can border workers manage their tax return in Switzerland?
Border workers must collect all tax documents, calculate taxable income including salaries and annuities, complete and file the cantonal tax return by the deadlines, consider deductions and pay taxes due. Tools like the payroll calculator are useful for estimating taxes.
