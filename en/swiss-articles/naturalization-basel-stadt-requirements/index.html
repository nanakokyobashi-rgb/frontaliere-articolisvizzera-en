The federal framework of ordinary naturalization

Ordinary naturalization in Switzerland is based on stable federal criteria: those who wish to acquire Swiss citizenship must have had a C (domicile) permit for at least ten years. In the Canton of Basel-City, the competent municipalities (Basel, Riehen, Bettingen and others) add their own requirements in addition to federal legislation.

The procedure begins with an application submitted to the municipality of residence, which then forwards the application to the cantonal authorities for final assessment. Integration and German language tests constitute ordinary components in the assessment, although the details and modalities vary according to the municipality concerned.

Operational details

Cantonal and Municipal Procedures in Basel-Stadt

In the Canton of Basel-Stadt, the naturalization procedure is not uniform for all municipalities. Each municipality (Basel, Riehen, Bettingen, Birsfelden, and others) defines specific modalities and timelines, although the reference framework remains federal. The application must be submitted with a series of standard documents: extract from the residents' register, certificate of good conduct, evidence of integration (work, volunteering, civic participation), proof of language knowledge. Some municipalities may require additional certificates related to economic, family, or housing situations. Once the application is submitted to the municipality, it undergoes a publication phase (opposition period) during which citizens and authorities can raise objections. If there are no significant objections, the file is passed to the cantonal authorities for final approval.

Naturalization Fees and Amounts

The fees for naturalization vary significantly between Swiss municipalities and even within the same canton. In the Canton of Basel-Stadt, each municipality applies its own amounts, which generally range from a few hundred to a few thousand Swiss francs. It is essential to verify the current amounts directly with the municipality of residence, as these can change annually. Some municipalities offer the possibility to pay the fees in installments, but this option varies from municipality to municipality. Contact your municipal office to learn about the available payment methods.

Key points

Step-by-step operational procedure

Step 1: Verify federal prerequisites. Ensure you have held a C permit (residence) for at least ten years. If your residence in Switzerland is less than this limit, you will have to wait. Certain periods (e.g., 8-18 years) count double according to federal regulations, thus reducing the overall time.

Step 2: Contact your municipality. Identify the competent office in the municipality where you reside (civil registry office, naturalization administrative office, or similar). Basel-Stadt includes Basel, Riehen, Bettingen, Birsfelden, Bottmingen, Münchenstein, and other municipalities: each has a specific point of contact. Request the required documentation and submission methods.

Step 3: Gather the necessary documents. Prepare a recent extract from the residents' register (generally not older than 3 months), a certificate of good conduct, proof of work and civic integration, and certificates of knowledge of Swiss German. The municipality may request additional documents based on your situation.

Step 4: Submit the application. Submit the complete application to the municipality, preferably in person or according to the communicated methods. Ensure you include all required documentation and receive a submission receipt.

Step 5: Wait for the publication phase. The municipality will publish your application for a set period (generally 30-60 days). During this period, anyone can raise justified objections regarding your eligibility or integration issues.

Step 6: Pass the tests (if required). If the municipality requires it, you must take the Swiss German language test and the integration test according to the methods and dates communicated by the office.

Frequently Asked Questions
How long does it take to get naturalization in Basel City?
The timing varies according to the municipality of residence and depends on the completeness of the documentation, language and integration test results, objections during the publication phase, and workload of the administration. On average, the process takes from a few months to over a year. Contact your municipality for a more realistic estimate based on the current situation of your file.
What exactly are language and integration tests?
Knowledge of Swiss German (level A2-B1) is generally required to communicate daily, work and participate in civic life. The integration test verifies familiarity with Swiss values, institutions and norms of coexistence. The precise methods, formats and minimum scores vary from municipality to municipality in the Canton of Basel-City. Your municipality will provide specific details during the initial consultation.
How much does ordinary naturalization cost in Basel City?
Taxes vary significantly between municipalities. In the Canton Basel-City, the amounts generally range from a few hundred to a few thousand Swiss francs. Each municipality applies its own amounts and can offer different payment methods or installments. Check the current costs directly with your municipality, as these may vary annually.
What residence permit do I need to apply for naturalization?
A C (domicile) permit is required for at least 10 years. However, certain residency periods (between 8 and 18 years) count as double, which means you can reach federal requirements in less real time. If you currently have a B permit, you will need to wait for renewal in C or direct transfer. Please check your current permit with the relevant authorities.
What should I do if my naturalization application is rejected?
If you receive a refusal, you can appeal to the competent cantonal authorities within about 30 days of notification of the decision. The appeal must explain in detail why you feel the decision is wrong or unfair. The cantonal authorities will review your file according to the formal procedure. It is advisable to consult an expert in Swiss administrative law to maximise the chances of a successful appeal.
