Operational details

The five-year analysis of the Federal Statistical Office (FSO) shows a slowdown in the Swiss labour market. In 2022, the number of vacancies was around 123'000. At the end of 2025, the number dropped to 86'000. This change is due to several factors, including global uncertainties and the transformation caused by artificial intelligence (AI). Swiss companies are more cautious in their hiring decisions. One of the main reasons is increasing automation, which is reducing the demand for skilled labour in sectors such as the office, IT and financial professions. For example, in the canton of Zürich, one of the country's main financial hubs, companies are investing heavily in AI technologies to automate complex processes, thereby reducing the need for human staff. Skilled labour is less in demand, but there are still opportunities in emerging sectors such as sustainability and technology. For example, the canton of Glarus saw a 15% increase in new job creation in the sustainability sector between 2020 and 2025. This is due to the growing demand for energy and environmental professionals. Swiss regulations play a crucial role in labour market management. The Temporary Work Act was recently reformed to facilitate the integration of temporary workers into rapidly expanding sectors.

Key points

The Swiss government has initiated a series of measures to support the labour market. The creation of new jobs is one of the main objectives. Companies are encouraged to consider hiring new employees and investing in vocational training. Workers can access training and refresher programs to improve their skills and increase their job opportunities. ## Government Measures The Swiss government has announced an economic stimulus plan that envisages the creation of 300,000 new jobs over the next five years. This objective is part of a broader professional retraining and business support plan. 📊 The measures include tax incentives for companies hiring new employees and investment in vocational training. For example, the canton of Zug has already implemented a training program that has seen the training of over 5000 workers in the technology sector. 💡 ## Training Programs Workers can access government-funded training and refresher programs. These programmes cover a wide range of sectors, including technological innovation, renewable energy and tourism. 📊 For example, the 'Training for the Future' programme offers vocational refresher courses at a reduced cost or free of charge for Swiss workers.
