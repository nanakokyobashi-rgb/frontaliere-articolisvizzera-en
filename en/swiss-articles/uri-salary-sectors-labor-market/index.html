The labour market in Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage, although some cantons — including URIs — may have established their own regulations at cantonal or municipal level. Salary levels are mainly determined through national and cantonal collective agreements, the degree of training (AFC for vocational training, high school diploma, university), professional experience and the sector to which they belong.

Each Swiss canton independently manages its own taxation, social security (AVS/AHV, LPP/BVG) and, in many cases, specific regulations on the labour market. In the Canton of Uri, as in other central cantons, wages reflect this federal structure: a technician with vocational training (AFC)

Operational details

The Role of Collective Bargaining Agreements in Determining Salaries

In Switzerland, collective bargaining agreements (CBAs) are negotiated primarily between trade unions and employers' associations at the national or sectoral level. These agreements define not only the minimum salary for each professional category and level of education, but also binding norms: maximum weekly working hours (45 hours for industry, offices, and sales; 50 hours for other sectors), minimum annual leave (4 weeks standard, 5 weeks for workers under 20 years old), maternity leave (14 weeks at 80% through income replacement, IPG), and paternity leave (2 weeks).

Some cantons, including Uri, have enacted their own cantonal or municipal minimum wages, which, where applicable, prevail over national agreements. It is essential to check with the cantonal labor office which collective bargaining agreements are in force in your professional sector and whether Uri has specific applicable regulations.

Key points

Job Search and Verification of Conditions in the Canton of Uri

If you are looking for employment in the Canton of Uri, start by consulting the main job platforms: LinkedIn Jobs, jobs.ch, Kijiji.ch, sector-specific portals (e.g., for healthcare, ICT, craftsmanship), and official websites of the cantonal labor office. Ensure that each job offer specifies the minimum required level of education (AFC, diploma, degree) and, if possible, the indicative salary range.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Evaluating a Job Offer

Step 1 — Identify the applicable collective agreement: Determine if your sector (e.g., metalworking industry, public healthcare, retail trade) is covered by a national collective agreement or if Uri has specific cantonal regulations. Contact the cantonal labor office if you are unsure.

Step 2 — Compare with national standards: Search for median salary data for your profession and education level on the UST/BFS website. Identify whether the proposed salary is aligned, below, or above the cantonal and national average.

Step 3 — Calculate the monthly net: Do not confuse gross and net. A gross annual salary of CHF 80,000 does not correspond to CHF 6,667 monthly net because various factors (progressive taxation, pension contributions, personal situation) reduce the available amount. Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a federal minimum wage in Switzerland?
No, Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage. However, some cantons have enacted their own minimum wages at the cantonal or municipal level. The Canton of Uri may have specific regulations: check with your local Uri labour office. Wage levels are mainly regulated by national and cantonal collective agreements negotiated between trade unions and employers' associations by professional sector.
How are deductions from gross salary calculated in Switzerland?
From the gross salary are deducted: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3%, unemployment insurance (AD/AC) 1.1%, accident insurance (LAINF) between 0.7–1.5% depending on the sector, variable LPP/BVG contributions by age groups (7-18%), and progressive federal/cantonal/municipal taxes. The final net depends on the Canton of residence, marital status and number of family charges. Use Frontier Ticino's calculator for an accurate estimate.
What are the factors that influence salary in Switzerland?
The salary depends on: degree of training (AFC, diploma, university), years of professional experience, economic sector (ICT generally pays more than sales), canton of residence and work, business size, individual performance and specialized skills. Cantons like Uri may have different wage structures than metropolises like Zurich or Basel because of the different costs of living.
Do I need to take out health insurance if I am employed in Switzerland?
Yes, LAMal/KVG is mandatory for all residents in Switzerland. You must stipulate it within 3 months of arrival or hiring. It is not withheld from the salary but is a private expense. You can choose the deductible (CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 or 2500 per year) and the canton of residence determines the average premium. Possibility of cantonal subsidies for low incomes.
When does occupational pension (LPP/BVG) start and how much do I contribute?
The LPP/BVG (second pillar) starts when the annual salary exceeds about CHF 22,680. The contribution depends on age: 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54), 18% (55+ up to the reference age). It is mandatory and finances the old-age pension in addition to the basic benefit of the AVS/AHV. Contributions and returns are managed by the foundation/collective institution designated by the employer.
