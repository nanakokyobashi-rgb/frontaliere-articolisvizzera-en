ST. GALLEN - The Federal Administrative Court (FAC) has upheld Swisscom's appeal in the dispute over the expansion of the fiber-optic network and annulled the decision by which the Competition Commission (Comco) had fined the company 18 million francs. In the market under examination, Swisscom does not hold a dominant position.

The FAC considers that Comco incorrectly applied cartel law. The measures ordered by the Commission also concerned areas where Swisscom had not yet begun expanding the network. Since it could not offer services in those areas, the judges held that the operator could not hold a dominant position either.

From P2P to P2MP

The case arose at the beginning of 2020, when Swisscom announced the switch from the point-to-point architecture, referred to as P2P, to the point-to-multipoint architecture, or P2MP. Under the previous system, each connection was linked directly to the central office via a dedicated fiber, which could also be leased by competing operators.

The new configuration instead provides for a shared line up to an intermediate distributor. From that point, the signal is split among the various connections. For other telecommunications providers, the change entails using Swisscom's signal and technology. At the end of 2020, Comco opened an investigation and adopted provisional measures to block installation of the new technology. After the FAC dismissed Swisscom's appeals in 2021, the Federal Supreme Court dismissed them in turn in 2022. On December 4, 2023, in the main proceedings, Comco imposed conditions for continuing the expansion and levied the penalty. The judgment of September 21, 2026, annuls that decision, but it can still be appealed before the Federal Supreme Court.

Operational details

What changes in practice

The decision does not focus solely on the fine. The decisive point is how the relevant market is defined: the TAF's assessment depends on the technology present in a given area and on whether Swisscom can offer services through that network.

The distinction gives rise to two scenarios described in the judgment. In areas where the expansion had not yet begun, the TAF holds that Swisscom could not have held a dominant position: without the possibility of offering services, according to the judges, the condition identified by Comco was missing. In areas already equipped with the P2MP system, by contrast, the Court states that Swisscom does not operate in the market defined by the Commission, because that market essentially comprises dedicated and continuous fiber connections between the exchange and the buildings.

For telecommunications operators, the transition from P2P to P2MP therefore changes the technical relationship with the network. Previously, there was a dedicated fiber that could be leased; afterward, other providers must use Swisscom's signal and technology. The source does not, however, indicate any changes in prices, contracts, speeds or the work schedule for individual connections. The judgment annuls Comco's decision, but does not announce a commercial change for anyone using a fiber connection.

The proceedings also remain open judicially. Since the judgment has not yet become final, a possible appeal to the Federal Supreme Court could reopen the discussion. Frequently Asked Questions
What decision did the TAF make regarding Swisscom's fine?
The Federal Administrative Court, in a judgment dated September 21, 2026, upheld Swisscom's appeal and annulled the decision by Comco that had imposed a fine of 18 million francs on the company. The court held that the Commission had misapplied cartel law, particularly with regard to the definition of the relevant market.
Why did the TAF consider Comco's application of cartel law incorrect?
The Federal Administrative Court explained that Comco had defined the market on the basis of dedicated and continuous fiber connections (P2P). With the transition to P2MP technology, such dedicated connections no longer exist; in areas where expansion had not yet begun, Swisscom could not offer services and therefore could not hold a dominant position, whereas in areas already using P2MP, the company does not operate in the market defined by Comco, which exclusively comprises point‑to‑point fiber.
What are the next legal options after the TAF ruling?
The Federal Administrative Court's judgment of September 21, 2026, has not yet become final and binding, so it can still be challenged before the Federal Supreme Court. If Comco decided to appeal, the Federal Supreme Court could reopen the discussion; until a final decision is issued, the annulment of the fine remains provisional.
