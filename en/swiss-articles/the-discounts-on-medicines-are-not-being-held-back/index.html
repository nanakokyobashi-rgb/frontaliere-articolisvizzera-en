The health sector has rejected allegations of withholding discounts on drugs, stating that anything that is eventually withheld is done solely for the improvement of the quality of health facilities.

In Switzerland, the health system is known for its efficiency and its ability to offer high quality services to citizens. However, the Federal Financial Controller's report raised doubts about the management of drug discounts, which amount to CHF 88 million out of a total of CHF 743 million.

This report caused a stir in the healthcare industry, which responded to the allegations with an official statement. The director general of the health sector said: "Anything that is eventually withheld is done solely for the improvement of the quality of health facilities. There is no intent to withhold discounts for personal or profit purposes.”

According to the health sector, discounts on medicines are a necessary measure to ensure access to high-quality medicines for citizens

According to the rules established by the Federal Council in 2019, Swiss hospitals have the possibility to grant discounts to patients for the drugs needed for their care. This legislation has been applied throughout the country, with some exceptions in the Canton of Ticino and the Canton of Valais, where local authorities have established specific limitations for imported drugs.

Key points

If you are a border worker in Switzerland, it is important to be aware of this situation and how it can affect your health. You need to check the current situation and how it may be affected by your presence in Switzerland.

In 2020, the Swiss Federal Assembly passed the law on medicines, which provides significant discounts for Swiss and border citizens. According to the law, drugs are classified into three categories: A, B and C. Category A drugs are the cheapest and are covered by a maximum of 20 Swiss francs per month. Category B drugs are those that require a prescription and are covered by a maximum of 30 Swiss francs per month. Category C drugs are those that require a prescription and are covered by a maximum of 50 Swiss francs per month.

However, it is important to note that these discounts are not automatic and must be claimed by the citizen or border crossing concerned. In addition, discounts are not available for all medications and do not cover shipping and delivery costs.

For example, if a border citizen lives in Lugano and has to buy a category A drug, they can claim a discount of 20 Swiss francs per month. However, if the drug is category B, you can claim the discount of 30 Swiss francs per month.
