Zurich Airport: Driverless Self-Driving Shuttles (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 9 September 2026

Two fully autonomous electric shuttles at Zurich Airport starting in September. First level 4 project, over 15,000 kilometres tested.

Context

In brief

  • Zurich Airport: two driverless shuttles from September 2026
  • Level 4 automation, 15,000+ km tested in 1.5 years
  • Operators reskilled from drivers to remote controllers
  • Pilot project until end of 2026

Key facts

  • What: First fully autonomous shuttle system in operation
  • When: Beginning of September 2026
  • Where: Zurich Airport, apron
  • Who: Flughafen Zürich, WeRide, Swissport, Krummen Kerzers
  • Test: Over 15,000 kilometers in 1.5 years
  • Duration: Pilot project until end of 2026

Zurich Airport has reached a historic milestone for automated mobility. Two electric shuttles travel the apron completely autonomously, without any driver on board, monitored exclusively by a remote control center.

The first fully autonomous trips, corresponding to level 4 automation, started at the beginning of September 2026. The project was developed in collaboration with WeRide, the specialized technology partner. Before launch, an intensive testing period of one and a half years was necessary, during which the two vehicles traveled over 15,000 kilometers.

Flughafen Zürich expresses satisfaction with the result. Coralie Klaus Boecker, manager of the airport in the innovation sector, emphasizes the strategic importance: 'Gradual automation of vehicles can make an important contribution to airports, given the growing shortage of specialized labor. For us it was therefore essential to develop the necessary foundations promptly.'

Initial phase and operational expansion

For the first four weeks, the two shuttles will circulate empty on the airport apron. This validation period serves to further test the system under real operational conditions. Subsequently, the vehicles will be used as shuttles for employees on a defined route that does not cross aircraft taxiways, ensuring the safety of airport operations.

Operational details

Transformation of the Swiss Labour Market

The Zurich Airport project highlights a concrete challenge faced by Swiss companies: a shortage of specialized labour. According to Flughafen Zürich, this deficit represented one of the main obstacles to the adoption of more efficient technologies. Automation does not mean the disappearance of jobs, but rather the transformation of professional roles. The drivers of Swissport and Krummen Kerzers were not laid off, but rather retrained to fill a new position: that of remote control operators.

This transition represents an important model for the Swiss labour market. Rather than reducing employment, the project has created a new professional competence — remote management of autonomous systems — that requires new digital and supervisory skills. For those working in Switzerland in the transport and logistics sector, this evolution signals a concrete trend: the skills required are shifting from manual driving tasks towards monitoring, diagnostics, and remote supervision competencies.

Professional Retraining and Operational Efficiency

Companies that invest in professional retraining benefit both from a reduction in operational costs and from the preservation of internal human capital. In the case of Zurich Airport, former drivers become supervisors of multiple vehicles simultaneously, significantly increasing overall efficiency. Employees acquire more sophisticated skills, which are typically associated with higher salaries and career progression.

Key points

Timeline and Operational Phase Until the End of 2026

The project follows a well-defined timeline. From the first weeks of September until the end of 2026, operational data will be collected systematically. Every journey, every remote command, every interaction between the autonomous system and the surrounding environment will feed a database that will be used to improve the reliability and safety of vehicles.

By the end of 2026, Zurich Airport will have accumulated concrete experience on: operational reliability in real conditions, interaction between remote operators and autonomous systems, integration with airport security procedures, feedback from users using the shuttles, organizational impact on the staffing structure and actual operating cost savings.

From Pilot Phase to Expansion in Swiss Transport

Once the pilot project is concluded, the data will lay the groundwork for expansion at the same airport and potentially at other Swiss infrastructures. Freight transport companies, automated warehouse operators, railway stations and urban logistics services will see this experience as a reference for their innovation strategies.

For those working in the transport sector in Switzerland, the message is clear: professional retraining has become a strategic priority. Companies like Swissport and Krummen Kerzers have already demonstrated that the transition from traditional roles to remote supervision roles is feasible and advantageous. Anyone working in this sector should consider acquiring digital and remote supervision skills as an investment in their professional future.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is' automation level 4 '?
According to the source, level 4 corresponds to the first level in which vehicles circulate completely autonomously without any safety person on board, monitored exclusively by a remote control center. Zurich Airport reached this milestone with the two electric shuttles in September 2026, after more than 15,000 kilometers of intensive testing.
When will autonomous shuttles start carrying passengers?
For the first four weeks, the two shuttles will circulate empty on the yard. Subsequently, they will be used as shuttles for airport employees on a defined route that does not cross the taxiways of the aircraft. The pilot project will continue until the end of 2026.
What happened to the original shuttle drivers?
The drivers of the partner companies Swissport and Krummen Kerzers have not been dismissed, but retrained for the new role of remote control operators. They manage the shuttles remotely and, in the future, a single employee will be able to supervise several buses at the same time, increasing the overall efficiency of the operation.
Will the project extend to other Swiss airports and infrastructure?
The pilot project at Zurich Airport will continue until the end of 2026. The data collected will lay the foundation for the expansion of autonomous vehicles in the airport and potentially in other Swiss strategic infrastructures (ports, railway stations, warehouses, urban logistics) in the future.
