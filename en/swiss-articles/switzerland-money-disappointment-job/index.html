Anyone who comes to Switzerland just for the money will be disappointed.

80% of vacancies in Switzerland are filled by local staff. Christian Atkinson, who coordinates Robert Walters' Swiss personnel consultancy activities, emphasizes this to put into perspective the idea that companies primarily look across the border for highly qualified professionals.

According to the headhunter, when a company has to fill a position, it starts with the domestic market. It looks for people already rooted in the country, with suitable qualifications and knowledge of the local work culture. Direct hires from abroad perhaps account for 20%. For the employer, the risk is real: a candidate may withdraw at the last moment, visa procedures may drag on, or integration may not work. In that case, whoever has arrived leaves at the first opportunity.

The high salary remains an attraction, but it is not enough to explain the choice. Atkinson describes an enormous costo della vita in Svizzera capable of reducing the salary advantage. After the first bills for rent and health insurance, actual savings may turn out to be almost the same as those attainable, for example, in the United Kingdom. For this reason, anyone who changes jobs exclusively for the salary is, in his view, a warning sign.

Rent, family and first impressions

Housing is now companies' main concern and the most stressful factor for those who want to relocate. In urban centers, the vacancy rate is extremely low; many expats have to move several times during the first few months. For a family, an apartment with a dedicated home office space can quickly cost up to 6000 francs per month.

Atkinson, 46, lives with his family in Zurich. Raised in the United Kingdom, he says that upon his arrival he booked an Airbnb on Langstrasse, when he had an infant: the choice placed him in the middle of a nightlife district. The episode explains why, in his view, without someone capable of guiding newcomers, people easily make wrong decisions. Switzerland remains attractive for its safety, nature, quality of life and environment for children, but relocation requires stronger motivations than mere earnings.

Operational details

The sector changes the value of the offer

The same professional offer does not carry the same weight for everyone. Atkinson distinguishes mainly between IT, finance and pharmaceuticals, and also links the assessment to the age of those relocating. The comparison therefore concerns not only the figure on the payslip, but the way work fits into everyday life.

| Profile | Element that attracts or puts off | | IT | Specialists ask for broad opportunities to work remotely; in Switzerland, being present in the office often remains the rule. | | Finance | More agile corporate structures than in London are an attraction. | | Pharmaceuticals | The Basel region is described as a natural draw. | | Younger people | The policy on free movement and the social climate carry weight. | | Older people | The choice focuses more on school and family. |

For those looking in annunci di lavoro, the first practical comparison is therefore between job duties and work organization. In the IT sector, recruitment from abroad becomes more difficult when specialists ask for broad opportunities to work remotely and office presence remains strong in Switzerland. Atkinson cites Germany, where salaries are lower but models are often more flexible. In finance, by contrast, the identified lever is the agility of corporate structures compared with London. For pharmaceuticals, the geographical reference is the Basel region.

The most unexpected difference may emerge as early as preschool. In the United Kingdom, Atkinson explains, nursery school focuses on academic learning from the outset. In a Swiss preschool, his son told him that he had learned to light matches and fend for himself in the woods: it was a shock for his father.

Behind the episode lies a different educational philosophy. The model described by Atkinson promotes autonomy and individual responsibility; academic achievement, in his experience, evens out later. His children grew up happy, healthy and extremely independent. For an international family, the question to ask is not only what salary to expect, but what view of childhood and schooling one will encounter after the move.

Language, too, should be considered without making assumptions. The Swiss approach to languages is open and, in many international companies, English is more than sufficient. This can facilitate entry into professional life, but it does not eliminate the other obstacles to integration mentioned in the interview. The choice therefore depends on the combination of work, age and family, not on a single ranking of attractiveness.

Key points

A checklist before accepting the transfer

The decision can be turned into an orderly assessment, without treating salary as the only variable. The criteria that emerged from the interview help prepare a choice less exposed to errors.

Four practical steps

1. Clarify the motivation. Before responding to an offer, write down what you are looking for beyond money: security, contact with nature, quality of life and an environment favorable to children. If salary is the only reason for the change, the interview suggests considering this motivation a warning sign.

2. Calculate the real margin. Set net salary alongside the first expenses cited by Atkinson: rent and cassa malati. The comparison must concern what remains for savings, investments or retirement, not the isolated salary figure. The assessment also serves to measure the difference compared with the situation in the United Kingdom, given as a point of comparison in the interview.

3. Prepare the home search. Define the family's needs immediately, including space for a home office, and do not choose an address without understanding its surroundings. The Airbnb experience on Langstrasse shows the risk of a decision made without guidance. Since many expats change accommodation several times in the first few months, the housing search should be treated as part of the professional decision.

4. Check work and school together. Ask how much office presence the role requires and how compatible the arrangement is with family needs. Then compare expectations regarding early childhood education: the Swiss system described by Atkinson prioritizes independence and responsibility, while the British experience starts with a more academic focus. The question is not which model is better, but which one is suitable for the children.

If one assessment remains open, it is advisable to pause the comparison and formulate a precise question for the company or the family: the transfer must hold up together in terms of work, housing and educational expectations. This checklist does not replace evaluating the offer: it makes it clear before departure. For the final comparison, use calcolatore stipendio.

Frequently Asked Questions
What percentage of job positions are filled by local personnel in Switzerland?
According to Christian Atkinson, Robert Walters' head of Switzerland, 80% of vacancies are filled by local staff. Only about 20% of hires take place directly from abroad, with significant risks such as last-minute withdrawal or visa procedures taking longer.
How much can family accommodation with a home office cost in Switzerland?
A family-suitable apartment, with space for a home office, can quickly reach 6000 francs per month. In urban centers, the vacancy rate is extremely low, forcing many expats to move several times in the first few months.
How do the Swiss and British education systems differ according to the interviewee?
Christian Atkinson describes a fundamental difference: in the United Kingdom, kindergarten focuses on academic learning from the outset. In Switzerland, by contrast, the system promotes autonomy and individual responsibility, with practical experiences such as learning to light matches or fend for oneself in the forest. The academic level evens out later in the process.
