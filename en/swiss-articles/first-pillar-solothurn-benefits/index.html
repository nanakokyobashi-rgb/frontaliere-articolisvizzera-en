The first pillar of Swiss social security (AVS - Old Age and Survivors Insurance) represents the basis of the pension for every worker in Switzerland, including residents of the Canton of Solothurn. The management of this system is the responsibility of the cantonal compensation fund, an operational body that administers contributions, calculates annuities and guarantees benefits to those who have reached the reference age.

The financing of the system depends on the contributions paid during your working life. Employees pay 5.3% of their salary (plus 5.3% paid by the employer, for a total of 10.6%), plus other contributions for disability insurance (AI) and maternity daily allowance (IPG). In the Canton of Solothurn, as throughout Switzerland, these payments fuel a system of intergenerational solidarity, where today's contributions finance today's income.

What does the cantonal compensation fund do

The cantonal compensation fund is not an independent institution, but part of a coordinated federal system. Its role is crucial: it collects contributions from pay slips, records the contribution history of each insured person, calculates the AVS income based on contributions paid and age at retirement, and provides monthly benefits.

One or more compensation funds operate in the Canton of Solothurn (federal structure, but cantonal implementation). Every insured person has the right to consult their contribution history and check if there are gaps: periods not covered by mandatory payments which reduce the amount of the future annuity. The fund provides free advice on the contribution situation and social security rights.

Contribution gaps: how to detect them

A contribution gap is any year of working life (from 17 to 64 years for women, from 17 to 65 years for men) in which no contributions were paid, or in which payments were lower than the income ceiling established by law. The gaps reduce the total contributions credited, proportionally lowering the future AVS income.

What if there are gaps? The annuity calculated by the compensation fund will be less than the expected maximum. In this case, if the total income (AVS income + other sources) does not cover the living minimum, the additional benefits intervene. This is a fundamental supplement that guarantees the right to the subsistence minimum for those who have contributed insufficiently.

Operational details

Complementary benefits (PC) are the supplement that the State (in the forms of KVG/cantonal subsidy and social assistance) provides when the AVS income is not sufficient to guarantee the minimum subsistence. In the canton of Solothurn, as in all cantons, the right to supplementary benefits is regulated by a federal coordination law, but implementation remains cantonal.

How the AVS pension is calculated

The AVS annuity is based on three main elements. The first is the average annual insured income: the average of the coordinated annual incomes (deducting the minimum, adding a credit for periods of inactivity such as maternity, illness, unemployment) during the years of payment. The second element is the number of years of payments: it counts from the moment the payments begin (age 17) up to the reference age (64 years for women, 65 years for men). The third is the conversion rate: the percentage with which the average income is transformed into annual income. The rate varies based on the starting age of the pension.

The AVS system does not set a pre-established amount, but calculates the annuity individually for each insured person. Two people with the same income will be able to receive different incomes depending on the number of years of payment. This mechanism guarantees fairness: those who have paid contributions for more years receive a proportionately higher income.

Importance of contributory continuity

A continuous career without gaps is essential to obtain a full income. If years of payments are missing (for example, periods of unemployment, prolonged illness, or periods abroad without payments), the calculation of the annuity will only take into account the years actually paid. A gap of 5 years, for example, will reduce the annuity by approximately 5% (calculated over 48 total years of payments).

In the canton of Solothurn, as in the whole of Switzerland, it is possible to fill the gaps by paying outstanding contributions (so-called "voluntary payments") up to a certain time limit. The compensation fund provides advice on voluntary payments and their effects on future income. This possibility represents an important opportunity for those who have had periods of non-insurance during their working life.

Complementary benefits and minimum subsistence

When the AVS pension, added to other resources (such as LPP pension, investment income, real estate) does not cover the living minimum, the supplementary benefits come into play. They are managed by the canton and financed jointly by the canton and the Confederation. In the canton of Solothurn the subsistence minimum is set on the basis of a federal formula that takes into account vital needs (housing, food, sanità, clothing) and the composition of the family unit. A single person, a couple and a family with children will have different vital minimums.

Key points

If you are resident in the Canton of Solothurn and are about to retire, or you want to check your AVS contribution position, here is the concrete procedure for moving correctly.

Step 1: Check your contribution history

At least 2-3 years before retirement, contact the cantonal compensation office of the canton of Solothurn. Request an extract of your contribution history (called a "payment certificate" or "insurance situation"). The fund will send you a document listing all the payments recorded from your age of 17 up to the date of the request.

Examine the certificate carefully. Check for missing years or lower payment periods. If you find gaps, report them to the fund: there may be legitimate reasons (periods covered by disability benefits, parental leave covered by the system) to take into account. The fund has the obligation to help you clarify your position.

Step 2: Fill in the gaps (if necessary)

If you discover contribution gaps without legal coverage, you can submit an application for voluntary payment. This right exists up to a maximum of past years (typically 5 years, but federal law allows extensions in certain cases). The compensation fund will inform you of the amount due to cover each year of the gap.

Voluntary payments increase the total contributions credited, increasing future income. Calculate the return: investing in covering a gap today reduces the risk of requesting additional benefits tomorrow. This is particularly relevant from a long-term perspective.

Step 3: Calculate the expected income

The compensation office offers a consulenza pre-pensionamento free of charge. During this consultation you will receive an estimate of your income based on your current contributions and different pension starting age hypotheses (for example, whether you will retire at 64 or 65). You will be able to evaluate whether voluntary payments would sufficiently increase the income, or whether you will have to rely on complementary benefits.

Step 4: Request additional services (if necessary)

If the estimate of your AVS pension does not cover the subsistence minimum, contact the social assistance or insurance office of your municipality in the canton of Solothurn. They will manage the request for additional services. You will need to provide documentation of the calculated AVS income, declaration of other resources (real estate, accounts, investment income), and documentation of necessary expenses (rent, healthcare, transportation, etc.).

The additional benefits are paid starting from the first day of the month following the approval of the application. Don't miss the deadlines: the application must be submitted within the deadlines indicated by the canton.

Important deadlines

Submit the request for a contribution statement at least 2-3 years before your expected retirement. If you intend to make voluntary payments, do so within the legal deadlines (usually 5 years from the event that caused the gap, but check with the fund). Submit the application for complementary benefits within the cantonal deadlines to avoid delays in disbursement.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the cantonal clearing house and what role does it play?
The cantonal compensation fund is the federal body that manages the first pillar of social security (AVS) at the cantonal level. It collects contributions from payrolls (5.3% paid by the employee, 5.3% by the employer), records the contribution history of each insured, calculates the individual pension on retirement and pays it monthly. Each canton has at least one clearing house coordinated by the federal level (UFAS/BSV).
How are AVS contributions calculated and how much do they amount to?
Employees pay 5.3% of their salary for AVS/AI/IPG; the employer pays an additional 5.3% (total 10.6% jointly paid). Independents pay 7.8% of net income. These contributions feed into the intergenerational solidarity system and are recorded year by year by the compensation fund.
What are supplementary benefits and who is entitled?
The supplementary benefits (PC) are the supplement provided by the canton (jointly financed by the canton and the Confederation) when the AVS pension does not cover the minimum living allowance. They cover vital needs (housing, food, health, clothing) based on the composition of the household. AVS policyholders residing in the canton are entitled when the annuity added to other resources does not reach the living minimum.
Is it possible to fill the contribution gaps and how?
Yes. If your tax history has gaps (years without payments), you can request a voluntary payment to the compensation fund. This entitlement exists within a maximum of past years (typically 5 years, with federal exceptions). The cashier will let you know the amount due. Voluntary payments increase the total contributions credited and therefore the future income.
When do I need to start verifying my tax status?
You will need to contact the clearinghouse at least 2-3 years before your expected retirement. Request an excerpt of your tax history (certificate of contribution). Examine the document carefully to identify any gaps. If necessary, consider voluntary payments or the possibility of requesting supplementary benefits.
