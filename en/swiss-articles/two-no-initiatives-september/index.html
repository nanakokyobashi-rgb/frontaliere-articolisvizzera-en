The initiative on neutrality and the one on nutrition are heading towards a rejection at the polls on 27 September. This is what emerges from a survey conducted by the Leewas institute between 12 and 13 August on behalf of Tamedia and 20 Minuten, on a sample of 13,154 people with a margin of error of about 1.5 percentage points.

The neutrality initiative gets 36% yes against 62% no. In detail, 30% are clearly against, 57% are sure against, 5% are quite against, 6% quite favourable and 2% undecided. The opposition has strengthened since June, when the no vote was 54% and the yes vote was 34%.

Operational details

Deep rift along policy lines

The vote on 27 September will draw a sharp rift between the Swiss parties. On the neutrality initiative, the split is as much on the substance as on the vision of the country: the UDC sees the need to constitutionally shield a historical principle of Switzerland, while the other parties fear that this would limit the country's diplomatic response capabilities in an increasingly complex international context. This explains the electoral gap: 82% UDC in favour against 91% PS, 82% Greens, 79% Centre and 72% PLR against.

On power, the division is equally clear: only the Greens collect a favorable majority with 69%, while the SP is split 50-50. All the other parties — PLR (83% against), Centro (75% against), UDC (74% against), PVL (66% against) — oppose it. This political geography will determine the final outcome of the vote next month and a half.

Voter profiles: who votes yes and who does not

Beyond the party line, clear sociodemographic profiles emerge that reveal different anxieties. On neutrality, opponents typically have higher education and high incomes, and reflect anxiety about the loss of Swiss influence internationally. On the contrary, yes gathers strength in rural areas and in Italian Switzerland, where a more traditionalist view of Swiss neutrality seems to prevail.

Key points

Five and a half weeks to decide: how to prepare

From the date of the survey (August 13) to the vote (September 27) there are about 5 and a half weeks left. This is enough time to delve into the two texts and their concrete implications for Switzerland. The first step is to read the official material of the Swiss Federal Administration, which explains the two initiatives in a neutral and balanced way.

You can consult the full texts of the initiatives on the Federal Chancellery's website to assess how each could impact the country in the coming years. The voting methods vary slightly between cantons: some send the ballots at home, others allow voting by mail or at the polling station. Please check with your local municipality for deadlines and arrangements specific to your region.

The Leewas survey is right in predicting two double noes. But Swiss democracy works like this: surprises are always possible, and every vote counts in determining the final outcome.

How the Swiss federal vote is organised

Switzerland votes on federal initiatives through a national ballot in which all cantons participate on the same date. Each citizen is entitled to one vote on each federal initiative.

Frequently Asked Questions
When exactly do you vote for the two initiatives?
September 27, 2026 is the federal ballot date for the two initiatives: the one on neutrality (promoted by the UDC) and the one on food. All Swiss cantons participate on the same day. Citizens residing abroad can vote by correspondence according to the procedures of their consular district.
Why does no seem so strong in two-initiative surveys?
According to the Leewas survey of 13,154 people, no to the neutrality initiative is supported by 62% because many fear that constitutionalising neutrality would limit Switzerland's room for manoeuvre internationally and in diplomatic relations. On food, 65% voted no mainly due to the fear of a "state-imposed menu" and the reduction of animal products.
Which parties support the two initiatives?
On neutrality, only the UDC supports it (82% of its voters). On food, only the Greens garner a favourable majority (69%). The PS is evenly split on power. All the other parties — Centre, PLR, PVL — oppose one or both of the initiatives according to the survey data.
How does a Swiss federal ballot work exactly?
An initiative is approved only if it SIMULTANEOUSLY collects both a majority of the popular votes (>50%) AND a majority of the cantons (at least 12 out of 26). If even one of the two criteria is not met, the initiative is rejected. Because polls predict net noes in both vote and cantonal distribution, an initiative is unlikely to overcome this double hurdle.
Where do I find the full texts and neutral material to decide?
The Swiss Federal Chancellery publishes official texts, explanatory messages and neutral material on all federal votes free of charge on its institutional website. You can check it online to read exactly what the two initiatives are asking for before deciding on September 27 how to vote.
