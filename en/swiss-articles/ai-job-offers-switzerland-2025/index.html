The Swiss job market is setting a new record in artificial intelligence, with a notable surge in openings for AI-related roles. According to the AI Jobs Barometer by PwC Switzerland, the number of positions requiring specific AI skills has reached 25,000 in 2025—a level never seen before. This marks an increase of about 9,000 jobs compared to 2024.

Despite this growth, AI jobs still represent a small share of the overall market: only 1.8% of all Swiss job offers are connected to this technology. The trend, however, is clear: artificial intelligence is reshaping the world of work far beyond the tech sector, engaging new industries and redefining the skills employers are seeking.

Adrian Jones, an expert at PwC Switzerland, says AI is changing how careers begin and evolve. Routine tasks, which used to be part of onboarding and learning, are increasingly managed by artificial intelligence. As a result, discernment, adaptability, and leadership are becoming essential from the very start of a career. Companies must rethink their talent development strategies.

Operational details

The expansion of AI-related job offers in Switzerland in 2025 concerns not only the tech sector but has tangible impacts on various national economic sectors.

Practical implications for workers and companies

For those living and working in Switzerland, the figure of 25,000 AI offers signals clearly that demand for advanced digital skills is growing. Although the share of the overall market remains modest (1.8%), the trend suggests that AI will become increasingly central to employment strategies, even in traditional sectors such as health and energy.

Workers with AI skills can expect above-average salaries, with especially pronounced pay premiums in the mentioned sectors. This phenomenon helps redefine the salary landscape, affecting cost of living and choices in professional training.

Since 2019, professions highly exposed to AI have integrated an average of 248 new skills per profession. This requires continuous skill updates, both for those already in the job market and for those intending to enter it.

Comparison and national scenarios

Compared to 2024, the increase of 9,000 AI positions indicates an acceleration in the digitalisation of Swiss companies. Cantons such as Zurich, Geneva and Basel, where innovative companies are more prominent, show a significant impact. The national trend implies that even non-traditional tech sectors are realigning their personnel management strategies.
