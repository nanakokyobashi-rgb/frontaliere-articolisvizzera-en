Swisscom Layoff After 38 Years: The Case Goes to Bern

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 30 September 2026

Sébastien Perriard, a 54-year-old from Bulle, was fired by Swisscom after 38 years of service. More than 530 people have signed a petition calling for his reinstatement.

A Dismissal That Has Reached the Federal Level

In Bulle, Sébastien Perriard’s story has moved from a personal matter to a public debate. The 54-year-old from Fribourg was fired by Swisscom after 38 years for helping an elderly customer who wanted to cancel some subscriptions. According to the account provided by the source, the employee had been reported by the store manager.

After the story became public, Perriard received an outpouring of solidarity—not just from customers. An online petition calls for him to be reinstated to his former position. At the time of this report, more than 530 people had signed it. The petition expresses outrage over what it calls a brutal dismissal and also calls for a review of sales practices at Swisscom stores to protect the most vulnerable customers.

Solidarity from Former Colleagues

Among the comments on the petition is one from another former Swisscom employee. The former colleague recalled his excellent relationship with Perriard and explained that he struggled to understand the reasons for his dismissal. He described Perriard as collaborative and attentive, noting that this was consistent with his character—for instance, he had accompanied a neighbor to the Bulle store to clarify a situation that was deemed unclear.

The 54-year-old said he was also moved by the support of people who do not know him personally. He explained that this solidarity gives him strength.

“It makes me feel less alone. I sincerely thank everyone.”

The message from his former colleague touched him particularly deeply, as it came after years without contact.

The case has also reached the federal level. Vaud National Councilor Benoît Gaillard raised the issue during question time in the Federal Council, asking whether a dismissal after nearly 40 years, without any warning, was compatible with modern and socially responsible personnel policies. In its response, the government remained cautious because it was not familiar with the specific case.

Gaillard then explained the nature of his efforts to the residents of the Fribourg town. He intends to submit a parliamentary question to inquire whether there is a problem with Swisscom and criticized the Federal Council’s response, claiming that he had already heard reports of unlawful practices at the company.

Operational details

What changes, and what does not change, for the labour market

The case does not, on its own, introduce a new rule for people working in Switzerland. The source does not report a judicial decision, a reinstatement agreement or a change already approved in sales procedures. Perriard's dismissal, the customers' petition and the parliamentary initiative announced by Gaillard therefore remain distinct.

The petition should therefore be presented as a request, not as reinstatement already obtained.

It is an issue raised by the signatories, linked to the incident at the Bulle store. The petition's text thus broadens the discussion: not only the fate of an employee, but also the way some customers' needs are handled.

The third implication is political. A member of the National Council brought the case up during Question Time and announced an interpellation.
