Cassis in Düdingen: Switzerland does not fall from the sky, but must be cultivated

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 August 2026

Switzerland does not fall from the sky, but must be cultivated

Context

In brief

  • Ignazio Cassis spoke during a brunch at a farm in Düdingen, in the canton of Fribourg.
  • The Federal Councillor used farmers’ work as an image for the care required to build Switzerland.
  • He also addressed the difficulties caused by drought and heat and the meaning of neutrality.

Key facts

  • Article date: 1 August 2026
  • Location: Düdingen (FR)
  • Occasion: a farm brunch
  • Speaker: Ignazio Cassis, head of the FDFA

Cassis’s message

In Düdingen, Ignazio Cassis spoke about Switzerland through a concrete scene: a farm, families gathered together and the daily work of the people who cultivate the land. The phrase that sums up his intervention is that Switzerland does not fall from the sky, but must be cultivated with patience. The comparison does not introduce a programme for growing a particular crop. It describes a country that requires attention, effort and continuity.

The speech also recognises the difficulties faced by farmers. Drought and heat make work in the fields more uncertain and show how much it depends on conditions that no one can fully control. For that reason, patience is not presented as passivity. It is the ability to prepare, protect and pass on the things that hold a community together.

The farm setting

The brunch gave Cassis a starting point close to everyday life. Families came together, children played and farms opened their doors. From this scene, the speech turned to a shared responsibility: Switzerland is made up of villages, families and people who get up early to work, not only of institutions in the capital. The Federal Councillor therefore stressed cooperation and care for the places where people live.

Operational details

Patience as a political image

The speech in Düdingen connects agricultural work with a broader reflection on the country. A good harvest is not improvised: the land has to be prepared, protected and entrusted to those who come after us. In Cassis’s view, the same is true of Switzerland. Prosperity and cohesion are not natural guarantees, but the result of repeated actions and shared responsibility.

This perspective begins with villages and families. The head of the FDFA recalled that Switzerland does not begin only in Bern. It also takes shape in places where people get up early, work and care for their surroundings. The farm in Düdingen therefore becomes the concrete setting for a message addressed to the whole country.

A less predictable world

Cassis placed the message in an unstable international context. Wars and missiles are reminders that the world can change quickly and that security cannot be taken for granted. In that situation, the patience mentioned in the speech does not mean ignoring risks. It means staying clear-headed, preparing and protecting what allows society to remain free.

Neutrality as responsibility

The same idea is applied to Swiss neutrality. The article recalls the debate around an initiative by the Swiss People’s Party that was due to be put to a vote on 27 September. Cassis presents neutrality not as an end in itself, but as a means in the service of the country’s freedom, security and prosperity.

In the speech, being neutral does not mean looking away. It means observing what is happening carefully while preserving the freedom to act. Neutrality is therefore described as a choice requiring judgement and responsibility, not as an automatic formula.

From the field to the community

The move from the land to politics is not a statistic or an announcement of a new farming plan. It is an analogy: caring for a field and caring for a country both require time, work and transmission. The metaphor allows Cassis to discuss security and neutrality without separating them from the ordinary life of the people he met in Düdingen.

Key points

What it means to cultivate Switzerland

In the article, cultivating Switzerland means caring for the conditions that make shared life possible. The starting point is the work of farmers, exposed to the changing climate and essential to the country. The destination is a wider responsibility: preserving freedom, security and prosperity with patience and attention.

The brunch scene makes this idea visible. Families gather, children play and farms welcome visitors. This is not a list of administrative measures, but an image of everyday Switzerland. The country is built by people and communities that cooperate, preserve what they have received and pass it on to the future.

A clarification about the title

Cassis is the surname of Federal Councillor Ignazio Cassis. The article is not about cultivating cassis, the blackcurrant, and it does not report a Swiss plan to plant fruit in Düdingen. The verb “cultivate” is used figuratively: Switzerland, like land entrusted to someone’s care, has to be built with patience.

The thread running through the speech

The thread connects three observations:

  • farmers face drought and heat while continuing their work;
  • families and villages are a concrete part of Switzerland;
  • neutrality should serve freedom, security and prosperity while leaving the country free to observe and act.

These points are not a new regulation or an economic forecast. They are the themes of a speech delivered in a rural setting and reported by swissinfo. Its conclusion is an invitation to active patience: prepare, protect and pass on the country, just as one cares for a field that will have to bear fruit tomorrow as well.
