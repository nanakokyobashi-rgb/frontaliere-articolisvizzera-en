Concrete examples

In Switzerland, farmers work hard to produce high-quality food. According to 2022 data, Swiss farmers produced over 3.5 million tons of fruit and vegetables, with a value of more than 2.5 billion francs. However, farmers also face challenges such as competition with imported products and the need to adapt to climate change.

Switzerland's neutrality

Switzerland is known for its political and military neutrality. This principle was enunciated in 1815 with the signing of the Treaty of Vienna. Switzerland's neutrality has also been recognised by the UN, which accepted its membership in 2002. Switzerland's neutrality is a fundamental value for its foreign and domestic policy.

More Cassis in Düdingen: Neutrality as the key to Swiss prosperity

Switzerland is not a nation that develops on its own, but is the result of hard work and constant patience. Federal Councillor Ignazio Cassis stressed the importance of being neutral and working hard to achieve the goals. Neutrality is not an end in itself, but a means to serve our freedom, security and prosperity.

Switzerland is a unique example of how neutrality can be an asset for the country. According to the 2020 report of the Federal Office for Foreign Policy (UPEP), Switzerland has maintained its neutrality for more than 150 years, avoiding joining any military or political bloc. This choice has allowed Switzerland to maintain its independence and to act as a bridge between the different nations.

Neutrality is not only a matter of principle, but also a matter of practice. Switzerland maintained its neutrality even during the two world wars, when many other countries were involved in conflicts. According to the 2019 report of the Federal Office for Foreign Policy (UPEP), Switzerland has maintained diplomatic relations with more than 180 countries, despite its neutrality.

Neutrality is not only an advantage for Switzerland, but also for other countries. Switzerland is an important financial and commercial center, and its neutrality allows it to operate as an intermediary between different nations. According to a 2020 report by the Swiss Bank, Switzerland is the second largest financial center in the world, after the United Kingdom.
