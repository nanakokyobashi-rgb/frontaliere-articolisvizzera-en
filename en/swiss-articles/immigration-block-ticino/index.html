Ticino: Vote on restrictions for foreign residents (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 15 June 2026

The Canton of Ticino approves restrictions on foreign residents, but with limited effects, while Switzerland rejects the Swiss People's Party initiative with 54.79%.

Context

In brief

  • The Canton of Ticino approves the restriction on foreigners with 50.66% support
  • Switzerland rejects the SVP initiative with 54.79%
  • Voter turnout in Ticino reaches 50.72%
  • The SVP proposal is rejected at the national level
  • Management of migration flows remains a key topic in political debate

Key facts

  • What: The Canton of Ticino has approved a referendum against immigration and demographic growth.
  • When: The vote was held recently.
  • Where: Throughout the Canton of Ticino.
  • Who: Ticino residents, supported by parties like Lega dei Ticinesi and UDC.
  • Result: The 'yes' votes received 50.66%.

This outcome reflects a strong local sensitivity to migration issues, influenced by Ticino's position as a border territory with Italy. The approved proposal aims at restricting foreigners and cross-border workers, but its practical impact remains limited at the federal level.

The referendum saw a turnout of 50.72%, the highest among federal votes in this cycle, indicating high civic engagement. The decision fits into ongoing political and social tensions surrounding immigration, a topic promoted by right-wing parties such as Lega dei Ticinesi and UDC.

A border territory and voting

Ticino, as a border region with Italy, has a long history of significant migration flows. Managing migration and the local labor market has always been a central issue for residents, who feel directly involved in these dynamics. Although the proposal was approved, it will have no immediate or concrete effects at the federal level, where the majority rejected the initiative.

This result highlights how migration sensitivities can influence voting decisions, even in border areas. Ticino's stance differs from other cantons, where the majority rejected the proposal.

Operational details

The outcome of this referendum in Ticino highlights how the management of migration flows remains a highly topical and politically sensitive issue in Switzerland. Even though the block proposal approved by the Canton of Ticino has a limited impact at the federal level, its result indicates a shift in public sentiment, which is also reflected in labour market dynamics and immigration policies.

If the situation before and after the vote is examined, it can be seen that the position of Ticino citizens has shifted slightly towards greater attention to controlling migration flows. This trend could lead to a revision of policies for managing cross-border workers and immigrants, although final decisions remain the competence of federal authorities.

The result also has a practical impact on cross-border workers, who may face new checks or restrictions in entry procedures. The growing sensitivity on this issue is reflected in increased border controls and possible changes to permit quotas.

For companies and workers, it is essential to monitor authorities' decisions and stay updated on the procedures for applying for and renewing work permits. It is advisable to consult official guides and work with specialised advisers to ensure compliance with the new regulations.

Furthermore, the increasing focus on migration management could also affect integration policies and support measures for cross-border workers. At the market level, possible variations in hiring dynamics and salary policies are foreseen, requiring careful planning and constant monitoring.
