Operational details

The minister's reaction: violation of the spirit of LAMal

Health Minister Elisabeth Baume-Schneider described salaries and especially bonuses linked to turnover as "hardly acceptable". He pointed out that these remunerations are largely financed by basic insurance funds, the mandatory one that covers Swiss citizens. The minister said: "If there are financial incentives to carry out more examinations, we are in total contradiction with the spirit of the health insurance law and with the containment of costs. You can see that this is not legal. " Its position highlights a structural conflict: while LAMal aims to contain healthcare spending and offer medically necessary services, turnover bonuses create an opposite incentive, pushing towards an increase in diagnostic volumes.

The association of Prioswiss health insurance funds and the association of FMH doctors have also expressed criticism of the bonus systems linked to turnover. Both recognize that this practice contradicts the principles of conscious management of health resources. LAMal, as a mandatory insurance system for Swiss residents, finances much of these benefits through premiums paid by workers and employers.

Procedure in practice: how to verify the costs of your radiological services

If you are a Swiss resident undergoing magnetic resonance or other radiological exams, you can request detailed information from your hospital or private clinic. Ask explicitly if the structure uses bonus systems linked to revenue and if specialists receive variable incentives based on the number of exams performed. This information is crucial to understand if your prescriber (primary care physician or specialist) may be subject to economic pressure to prescribe additional diagnostic tests. Each canton has specific health surveillance offices: contact them if you suspect conflicts of interest in your medical services.

Regarding your health insurance premiums, transparency is essential. Check how much of your LAMal premiums goes to finance radiological structures like 3R. Many health insurance companies publish annual reports that describe how funds are allocated. If you discover that your health insurance company finances clinics with bonus systems linked to revenue, you can file a formal complaint or switch health insurance companies during the annual change period (usually October/November for effect from January 1st of the following year).

Why do radiologists make so much money in Switzerland?
Radiologists in Switzerland have high salaries because magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is a very expensive and profitable service in the healthcare system. According to data leaked by the company 3R, in 2023 the turnover from NMR alone was CHF 77.4 million. Basic salaries start at CHF 300,000 gross per year. How do turnover bonuses affect my health insurance premium?
Radiologists' bonuses are mainly funded by basic insurance (LAMal), the same one that funds your premiums. If radiologists earn bonuses for performing multiple examinations, this increases the total healthcare expenditure, which is transferred to the LAMal premiums of all Swiss residents. According to Minister Baume-Schneider, these financial incentives contradict the spirit of LAMal and cost containment. Each additional exam that is not medically necessary drains resources from your sick fund.
What is the median salary of Swiss radiologists according to this survey?
According to data leaked by the company 3R, the standard salaries for specialist radiologists are 300,000 gross francs per year. A head office radiologist, at 60% occupancy, received CHF 285,000 (equivalent to CHF 475,000 full-time). A colleague at 25% employment received 90,000 francs: more than the Swiss median salary of 84,300 francs while working only 1.5 days a week. In 2025, a radiologist received 400,000 base francs plus almost 200,000 bonus francs.
What does the law say about the legality of these bonuses?
Health Minister Elisabeth Baume-Schneider said the turnover bonuses may not be legal. He said: “If there are financial incentives to carry out more examinations, we are in total contradiction with the spirit of the health insurance law and cost containment. You can see that this is not legal." The WFH association (doctors) and Prioswiss (sick funds) also criticised the system. A formal legal evaluation is underway, and new regulations may follow.
What can I do if I suspect I have been prescribed a useless MRI?
You can request a second medical opinion from another radiologist at a different facility. In addition, you can ask your primary care physician for a written medical justification for prescribing the exam. If you believe that the prescription was motivated by economic incentives rather than clinical necessity, you can lodge a complaint with the health supervisory office of your canton. Sick funds are obliged to investigate abnormal and potentially unnecessary prescriptions.
