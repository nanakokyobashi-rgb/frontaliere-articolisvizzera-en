Open Internal Appenzello activity: costs and registration (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 September 2026

Complete guide to opening a business in Appenzello Interno: registration, minimum capital and AVS, LPP, LAMal obligations.

Context

In brief

  • Registration with the commercial register is mandatory for every business
  • Three legal forms: sole proprietorship, general partnership, joint-stock company
  • Federal social security obligations: AVS (5.3% employee), LPP (7–18% depending on age), LAINF (0.7–1.5%)
  • Health insurance LAMal within 3 months from the start of business activity

Key facts

  • What: Opening a commercial business
  • Where: Canton Appenzell Innerrhoden
  • Competence: Cantonal commercial register + Federal administration (AVS/LPP/LAINF)
  • Tax levels: 3 (federal, cantonal, municipal)
  • LAMal deadline: Within 3 months from the start of business activity

Anyone starting a commercial business in the canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden faces a regulatory framework structured on three levels: federal, cantonal, and municipal. The procedure for registering with the commercial register, the obligation to subscribe to certain social insurances, and the various startup taxes represent the fundamental steps to operate regularly in Switzerland.

Registration with the commercial register is the first mandatory step. Each canton manages its own register at a specific office, where the type of legal form (sole proprietorship, general partnership, joint-stock company) and the essential company data are formalized. The minimum capital required varies depending on the chosen form: for a sole proprietorship it may be lower, while a joint-stock company has specific thresholds defined by the Federal Code of Obligations. Appenzell Innerrhoden, like all Swiss cantons, applies this federal regulation and adds its own cantonal rules for costs and procedures. It is essential to contact the cantonal Commercial Register Office directly to learn the exact amounts and necessary documents.

In parallel with registration, anyone opening a business has the obligation to fulfill various insurance obligations at the federal level. These include registration with AVS/AI, occupational pension provision through LPP, accident insurance (LAINF) if the employee reaches a minimum salary threshold, and mandatory health insurance (LAMal). These aspects do not vary by canton: they are regulated by federal law and constitute uniform obligations throughout Switzerland.

Opening costs

Opening costs include registration fees for the commercial register (specific cantonal amount), minimum capital paid (varies depending on legal form), federal insurance contributions (AVS, LPP, LAINF), and LAMal premiums (private, per capita, not a tax or contribution). Timing is critical: registration with the commercial register must take place within a specific period defined by the canton (usually a few weeks from the start of operations), while health insurance LAMal must be taken out within three months from the start of business activity, otherwise there is a risk of official registration which entails additional costs.

Operational details

Federal Insurance Obligations: Non-Negotiable Expenses

When you start a business in Switzerland, the framework of insurance obligations is defined at the federal level and applies everywhere, including Inner Appenzell. It's not a matter of choice: these are constraints governed by federal law on benefits and insurance.

AVS/AI (Old-Age and Disability Insurance)

Anyone starting a business is required to contribute to the AVS from the beginning. If you have employees, the contribution is shared: the employee pays 5.3% of gross salary, while the employer adds another 5.3%, for a total of 10.6%. It's not a negotiable percentage: it's fixed by federal law. If you work as a sole proprietor (individual business), AVS contributions vary according to declared net income, but are mandatory nonetheless.

AD/AC (Unemployment Insurance)

Alongside the AVS, employees and employers also contribute to the AD: 1.1% of salary up to an annual maximum. This covers the risk of unemployment. Like the AVS, it's a mandatory and non-negotiable deduction.

LAINF/LAA (Accident Insurance)

If your employee reaches a minimum annual salary threshold, you are required to insure them against accidents. The contribution ranges from 0.7% to 1.5% of coordinated salary, depending on the sector. A craft business has a different contribution rate than an office or industrial business. SUVA is the main entity, but other insurers exist. Costs depend on the risk profile of your business.

LPP/BVG (Occupational Pension Law)

Occupational pensions are mandatory for employees earning above a certain threshold (coordination). The contribution depends on the employee's age and is shared between employee and employer. For employees aged 25–34 the contribution is 7%, 35–44 years 10%, 45–54 years 15%, 55 years until retirement age (normally 65) 18%. These rates apply to coordinated salary (gross salary minus a federal deduction, indexed annually). It's the main pension cost after the AVS.

LAMal/KVG (Mandatory Health Insurance)

Every resident in Switzerland is obliged to take out health insurance within three months of starting a business (or upon arrival in Switzerland if foreign). It's not a tax, but a private subscription with a per capita premium. Premiums vary by canton, region, and insurance company, and depend on age bracket and chosen deductible. Standard deductibles for adults start at CHF 300 annually up to CHF 2500. Inner Appenzell, like every canton, offers cantonal subsidies for premiums to people with low incomes. The deadline is critical: if you don't subscribe within three months, the canton proceeds with automatic enrollment (tacit registration) with potentially higher premiums and retroactive effect.

Key points

Practical Checklist for Opening a Business in Appenzell Innerrhoden

Following a logical order reduces the risk of errors and penalties. Here are the concrete steps to start your business properly.

Step 1: Choose the Legal Structure and Verify Minimum Capital

Contact the Commercial Register Office of Appenzell Innerrhoden for advice on sole proprietorship, partnership, or limited company. Each form has different minimum capital requirements and obligations. Do not proceed further until you have clarified this fundamental point with the competent office.

Step 2: Registration in the Commercial Register

Once you have the required documents (property register extract, lease contract for space, certificates, etc.), register with the cantonal office. Registration must take place within a period set by the canton (usually 30–60 days from the actual start of your business). The cost varies by canton and is set by the cantonal fees: contact the office for an exact quote.

Step 3: Registration with AVS/AI within 5 days

Immediately after registration in the commercial register, you must register with AVS at the federal single number (the Coordinated Federal Administrations). This is a non-negotiable federal obligation. If you hire employees, the registration includes them as well: each employee must have an AVS number.

Step 4: Registration with Accident Insurance (LAINF/LAA)

Contact a LAINF insurer (SUVA or authorized private companies) to insure your employees. Ask for the specific risk profile of your business: this determines the percentage premium (0.7%–1.5% of coordinated salary).

Step 5: Health Insurance (LAMal) — within 3 months

Choose an insurance company from those operating in Switzerland and take out a policy [nav:health]. The canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden (through the social insurance office) provides a list of authorized companies and their premium ranges. Compare prices: there are significant variations between companies even for the same canton. The minimum deductible is CHF 300 per year for adults. Remember that the deadline is non-negotiable: three months from the start of your business.

Step 6: Occupational Pension (LPP) for Employees

If you hire employees, register them with an LPP occupational pension fund [nav:pension] before the start of their employment. Contributions begin on the first day and depend on the employee's age (7% for ages 25–34, 10% for 35–44, 15% for 45–54, 18% for 55+ up to the reference age). Each fund has its own registration rules: contact it with the employee's details.

Critical Deadlines

The commercial register must be completed within 30–60 days from the start of your business. AVS must be registered within 5 days of commercial registration. Health insurance LAMal has an absolute deadline: within 3 months from the start of your business. LAINF must begin before the start of your business if you hire employees immediately.

Total Costs (Qualitative Estimate)

Fixed opening costs include commercial register registration fee (variable by canton), minimum capital payment (variable by legal structure), first LAINF premium (varies by sector), first LAMal premium (varies by canton and owner's age), LPP registration (usually free, but with mandatory contributions from the first month).

For an exact estimate, contact the Appenzell Innerrhoden cantonal administration or the official cantonal website (www.ai.ch).

Next Steps

Once your business is open, you must submit your tax return within federal and cantonal deadlines (usually 90 days after the end of the fiscal year). AVS and LPP contributions will become a regular monthly expense, as will LAMal premiums. Frequently Asked Questions
What minimum capital do I need to open a business in Appenzell Innerrhoden?
The minimum capital varies depending on the legal form you choose. A sole proprietorship can start with reduced capital, while a public limited company requires CHF 100,000 according to the Federal Code of Obligations. For a simple partnership, the requirements are lower. Contact the Commercial Registry Office of the canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden for specific details about your legal form.
How much will I pay in AHV and BVG contributions when I open a business?
If you are self-employed (sole proprietorship), the AHV contributions depend on your declared net income. If you hire employees, they pay 5.3% AHV while you pay 5.3% as an employer (total 10.6%). For BVG, the employee's contribution varies according to age: 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54), 18% (55+ to 65). You as an employer pay a parallel percentage of the coordinated salary.
When do I have to take out health insurance (KVG)?
The absolute deadline for subscribing to KVG is three months from the start of your business. If you do not insure yourself within this period, the canton proceeds with an automatic registration (silent registration) with potentially higher premiums and retroactive costs. It is therefore critical to contact a private insurance company as soon as possible.
What is the difference between the canton of Appenzell Innerrhoden and other cantons to open a business?
The federal legislation (legal forms, AHV, BVG, UVG, KVG) IS IDENTICAL IN ALL CANTONS. [EOS]The differences relate to cantonal costs: commercial register registration fee, cantonal and municipal tax rates, average KVG premiums. Appenzell Innerrhoden has its own tariffs and administrative procedures. For a specific estimate, please contact the competent cantonal office.
Do I have to pay for accident insurance (UVG) if I hire employees?
Yes, it is mandatory to insure your employees against accidents if their annual salary reaches a certain threshold. The UVG premium ranges from 0.7% to 1.5% of the coordinated salary, depending on the sector of activity. A SUVA or an authorized private insurance company will calculate the exact contribution based on your company's risk profile.
