Operational details

The crisis involving the Volkswagen Group reflects the structural difficulties that large companies in the manufacturing sector must manage in an ever-changing market. Among the main obstacles cited are the impact of US tariffs, the weakness detected in the Chinese market and the growing competition within the European market. These factors push management towards drastic decisions to protect the economic sustainability of the group. Previously, an agreement had been reached between the group and the unions that envisaged, by 2030, the cutting of 35,000 jobs, with the intention of avoiding operational layoffs and the closure of plants in Germany, accompanied by a production reduction of 700,000 vehicles. The new proposal marks a clear change of course compared to what was previously established, bringing the number of redundancies to 100,000. ### Market Scenario Analysis The Volkswagen Group has shown mixed signals in recent performance. Frequently Asked Questions
What is the total number of redundancies under the new Volkswagen plan?
According to rumours, the plan presented by CEO Oliver Blume foresees a total of 100,000 redundancies by 2030 out of a global workforce of 657,000 employees. This proposal doubles the cut plan previously agreed with the unions, which envisaged a reduction of 35,000 jobs.
Which factories are at risk according to the plan?
The restructuring plan foresees the possible closure of four factories located in Germany. The decision is part of a reorientation strategy of the group aimed at responding to the challenges posed by the weakness of the Chinese market, European competition and US tariffs.
What is the unions' position on the cuts?
The unions expressed firm opposition to the new cut plan. In a statement, they said they were ready to do what they could to prevent the implementation of these measures, defining the situation as a battle for the safeguarding of employment within the company.
