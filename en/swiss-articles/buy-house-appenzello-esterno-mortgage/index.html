Buying a home in Appenzello Esterno Canton follows standard Swiss procedures, with specific fiscal and administrative features of the canton. In Switzerland, the property tax system is structured on three levels: federal, cantonal, and communal. Each canton, including Appenzello Esterno, applies its own rates on transfer taxation and has administrative competence on urban planning and building permits.

At the federal level, there is no nationwide property tax. However, each canton and each municipality applies its own taxes on transfers (transfer tax) and annual property taxation. The Appenzello Esterno cantonal administration has jurisdiction over transfer tax rates, building permit criteria, and, if applicable, tax deductions for homeowners.

Role of the bank and financing

Mortgage financing is the most common solution in Switzerland to buy a home. Banks assess the sustainability of the mortgage with their own criteria, checking that the mortgage burden (annual mortgage rate) does not exceed a percentage of net income. A buyer must generally have funds available of at least 20% of the purchase price; this percentage is a widely followed standard by Swiss banks.

Operational details

Assessment of ability to pay

Once the property is identified, the bank makes a thorough assessment of the buyer's ability to pay. This process includes analysis of net income (monthly salary net of federal, cantonal, municipal taxes), current fixed charges (rents, LPP retirements, insurance, debts), and a conservative stress test with higher interest rates than the current ones.

The sustainability of the mortgage is calculated with a formula that varies slightly between banks, but the principle is constant: the annual installment of the mortgage must not exceed a percentage (typically 30-35%) of the annual net income. If the income is low or the fixed charges are high, the bank may refuse the loan or propose a loan of a lower amount. Fixed charges include LAMal. A buyer resident in Appenzello Esterno will pay LAMal premiums according to the regional rates of the canton. Unlike a salary contribution (such as the AVS/AI at 5.3% for the employee), LAMal is a private insurance at per capita premiums, not linked to salary.

Notary fees and purchase procedure

Notary fees are a significant component of the purchase cost. A notary registered in the canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden draws up the deed of sale, verifies the real rights to the property via the Land Registry, ensures that the transaction complies with federal and cantonal laws, and coordinates the registration of the property in the name of the buyer. Notary fees follow a schedule calculated on the basis of the value of the property and vary according to the cantonal fee.

Step-by-step towards purchase

1. Research and preliminary evaluation Identify the desired property. Consult the cadastral plans on the cantonal land registry website and request an independent estimate of the value of the property.

2. Mortgage pre-approval Contact local banks for a preliminary sustainability check. Prepare: pay slips for the last 12 months, VAT return (if self-employed), bank account statement.

3. Negotiation and preliminary contract Once you have identified the property, negotiate the terms of purchase. A preliminary contract is not required by law, but is recommended to protect both parties.

4. Consult the notary Contact a notary registered in the canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden. The notary draws up the deed of sale, verifies the rights to the property and registers the mortgage in the land registry.

5. Payment of the transfer fee The cantonal administration of Appenzell Ausserrhoden will require payment of the transfer tax within specific deadlines (usually 30–60 days after signing the deed). Check the deadlines at the cantonal tax office.

6. Registration in the Land Registry Once the formalities are completed, the property is officially registered in the name of the buyer.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum capital for buying a house in Switzerland?
In Switzerland, banks generally require equity of at least 20% of the purchase price. Higher funds reduce mortgage, reduce banking risk, and can improve financing terms. Some institutions may accept lower percentages (e.g. 10-15%) if the buyer has a stable income, but this practice is less common and subject to stricter conditions.
How is mortgage loan affordability calculated?
The bank assesses affordability by verifying that the mortgage burden (annual mortgage payment) does not exceed a percentage of net income (usually 30-35%). The bank analyzes net income after federal, cantonal, municipal and KVG taxes, current fixed charges (rents, loans, insurance), and conducts a stress test with higher interest rates to check affordability even in the event of a rate hike.
What is the transfer tax in Appenzell Ausserrhoden?
The transfer tax (real estate transfer tax) is a cantonal tax levied when you buy real estate. It is calculated on the basis of the purchase price according to the canton rate of Appenzell Ausserrhoden. This tax is paid only once during the purchase and is not deductible from federal income tax, although it may be deductible at the cantonal level according to local legislation.
What documents do I need to get the mortgage approved?
Banks generally require: pay stubs for the last 12 months, VAT return (if self-employed), bank statements, list of current debts (credit cards, car loans), and certificate of ownership of the current home (if owner). Some institutions also require a letter from the employer confirming the stability of employment.
What role does the notary play in the purchase of a property?
The notary is an essential professional. It draws up the deed of sale, verifies the rights in rem to the property via the Land Register, ensures that the transaction complies with federal and cantonal laws, registers the bank mortgage in the Land Register, and coordinates the official transfer of ownership in the name of the buyer. Notary fees vary depending on the cantonal rate and the value of the property.
