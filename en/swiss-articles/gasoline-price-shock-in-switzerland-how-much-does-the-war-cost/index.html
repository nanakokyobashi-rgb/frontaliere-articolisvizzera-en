Since February, petrol in Switzerland has risen from 1,64 to 2,11 francs per litre. The Touring Club Suisse figure translates the geopolitical crisis into an immediate expense: with 15'000 kilometres per year, average consumption of six litres per 100 kilometres and the indicated price, the additional outlay reaches 423 francs per year. Diesel and heating oil have recorded significant price increases since the start of the war in Iran, according to Avenergy.

The bill already visible for motorists and travellers

For those considering a trip, the risk mainly concerns routes to Asia. To bypass several Middle Eastern airspaces and comply with the ban on overflying Russia, airlines have fewer alternatives. Longer routes increase consumption and costs, with possible repercussions for passengers. Following the Houthi attacks on Riyadh airport, Swiss has not announced any further changes and confirms the resumption of flights to Dubai from October 27.

The price of kerosene, explains aviation expert Hansjörg Bürgi, has already doubled compared with last year. Fuel accounts for around one third of airlines’ expenses: if carriers were to extend their routes further, fares could increase. The attacks also risk delaying Riyadh’s development as an international hub by years, to the benefit of Istanbul, Addis Ababa, Paris and London.

The risk is also reaching shops. If energy remains expensive for a long time, producing and transporting goods will cost more, potentially leading to price increases on shelves and renewed pressure on inflation. Jan-Egbert Sturm, director of KOF, considers Switzerland relatively well placed: it depends less on heavy industry than other European countries, and the strong franc eases the cost of energy imports. The phenomenon also affects costo della vita in Svizzera.

However, KOF points to a risk for exports: a global slowdown would reduce demand for Swiss products. In a scenario with oil at 180 dollars per barrel, growth could slow in the following year and inflation temporarily exceed 2%, while remaining below 2% on an annual average. For Marcus Schneider, the conflict is now a regional crisis; according to the political scientist, a full-scale escalation is already under way, and it remains to be seen how much worse it could become.

Operational details

Three scenarios for the Swiss portfolio

The most immediate figure is the TCS figure, but it is not a rate that is the same for everyone. The 423 francs are based on a specific profile: 15'000 kilometres per year, six litres per 100 kilometres and the jump from 1,64 to 2,11 francs per litre. The useful comparison for a family is therefore between its own mileage, average consumption and the indicated change, without automatically extending that result to every motorist.
