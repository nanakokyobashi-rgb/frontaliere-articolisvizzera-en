Federal votes 27 Sept: Changes for Swiss residents & workers (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 21 July 2026

The upcoming federal votes will address key issues for the Swiss economy and society. Here are the practical implications for residents and border crossers.

Context

In a nutshell

  • What: Federal votes on national initiatives and referendums
  • When: September 27, 2026 (standard date for federal votes)
  • Where: All of Switzerland, vote by mail or on the spot
  • Who: The Federal Council and the cantons organise the procedures
  • Why: Decisions on tax policies, labour, environment and health

Key facts

  • What: Votes on popular initiatives and mandatory referendums
  • When: 27 September 2026 (4 times a year: March, June, September, November)
  • Where: National and cantonal Switzerland
  • Who: Swiss citizens with voting rights (including foreign residents in some cantons)
  • Amount: Not applicable (qualitative decisions on laws and policies)

The federal elections on 27 September are a crucial event for Swiss direct democracy. Although the specific topics of the upcoming votes are not yet detailed in the source, historically these appointments include decisions on:

  • Tax Policy: Changes to Direct Federal Tax or VAT
  • Labour market: Minimum wage regulations or collective agreements
  • Cost of living: Interventions on rent, energy or sick pay premiums (LAMal)
  • Social security: AVS/AHV or LPP/BVG reforms

Operational details

Practical implications for residents and border workers

The decisions of 27 September could have a direct impact on:

  • Taxation: Changes to the tax at source for border workers or cantonal deductions
  • Wages: Minimum wage increases in specific cantons (e.g. Ticino)
  • Healthcare: Increase/decrease in LAMal premiums or deductibles
  • Pensions: Adjustments to AVS/AI contributions (currently 5.3% for employees)

Examples of concrete scenarios

1. Reduced VAT: If you pass a reduced VAT initiative, food and essential services could cost less, increasing purchasing power. For example, an increase in VAT reduced from 7.7% to 5% could reduce the cost of a meal from CHF 100 to CHF 95, a saving of CHF 5 for a meal. 2. AVS Reform: If an AVS reform is passed, pension contributions could increase, reducing the net paycheck. For example, an increase in contributions from 5.3% to 6% for employees could reduce net salary by CHF 1,000 to CHF 940, a decrease of CHF 60. 3. Labor Deregulation: If a labor deregulation is rejected, collective agreements maintain protections on hours and overtime. For example, in the Canton of Geneva, an increase in non-contractual working hours from 10 to 15 hours could increase the risk of overwork and stress for employees.

Key points

What to do concretely

1. Inform yourself: Consult the official material of the Confederation (admin.ch) or your canton. For example, residents of Genova (Ticino canton) should check the specific regulations for foreign citizens.

2. Verify voting rights: Foreign residents can vote in some cantons after 3-10 years of residence. For example, residents of Vaud can vote after 3 years, while those of Neuchâtel after 10 years. It is essential to consult the canton's website to verify the specific regulations.

3. Calculate impacts: Use tools like the salary calculator to simulate the effects of changes in tax or pension contributions. For example, a 2% increase in salary could result in a 10% reduction in pension contributions for a worker earning 5,000 CHF per month.

4. Participate: Even without voting rights, contact communes or associations to express concerns. For example, residents of Zürich can participate in meetings with the commune to discuss new fiscal regulations.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I vote as a frontier?
No, federal voting rights are reserved for Swiss citizens and, in some cantons, for foreign residents with C permit and sufficient domicile. Border workers can, however, vote at municipal level in rare cases (e.g. Ticino for those who reside in the municipality).
What happens if I don't return the card?
Non-participation has no legal consequences, but reduces influence on decisions that impact the cost of living and work.
How do I know if a new law applies to me?
Check the cantonal websites or the federal portal. The laws passed always include practical explanations about who is affected and how to adapt.
