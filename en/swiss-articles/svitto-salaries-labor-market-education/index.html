The labor market in the Canton of Schwyz follows the Swiss occupational structure, where wages are determined mainly by level of education, experience and sectoral collective agreements. Unlike many European countries, Switzerland does not set a federal minimum wage. Some cantons establish their own minimum wages through legislation, while others rely on sectoral collective agreements and market dynamics.

Education is the main driver of wage differentiation. Those who complete a vocational education and training (VET diploma of 3–4 years) gain access to technical and craft qualifications with higher pay than unskilled work. Those who obtain an advanced vocational diploma (federal diploma or federal examination) or a university degree access managerial and specialist roles with higher salaries.

Operational details

Salary levels, training and collective contracts

The level of education remains the main differentiating factor in the Swiss labour market. Those who complete a vocational training (AFC) access typically higher salaries compared to unskilled work, with margins varying according to specialisation and market demand. Those who continue with higher education (federal diploma, federal examination, degree) access positions with greater responsibility and proportionally higher compensation.

Sectoral collective contracts (also called CCL or sectoral agreements) define minimum standards for salary, working hours and benefits for specific sectors: metallurgy, construction, commerce, pharmaceutical industry, advanced tertiary sector, and others. Even where there is no statutory cantonal minimum wage, collective contracts serve as an occupational reference and protect workers from compensation below certain levels.

Working hours, holidays and minimum rights

Swiss law sets a maximum working time of 45 hours per week for industry, offices and sales; 50 hours for other sectors. Minimum holidays are 4 weeks per year (5 for those under 20). Maternity covers 14 weeks at 80% through loss-of-earnings compensation (IPG); paternity is 2 weeks. Notice periods for termination are: 1 month during the first year, 2 months from the second to the ninth year, 3 months from the tenth year onwards.

Key points

How to navigate the Swiss labour market

For those seeking employment or negotiating a contract in Canton Schwyz, understanding how gross salary translates into net pay is essential. Many candidates do not correctly calculate the impact of mandatory insurance contributions on their actual take-home salary.

Assessing gross salary and converting it to net

The first step is to gather salary benchmark data for your role, sector, and level of experience. If the employer adheres to a collective agreement, salaries are typically indexed to that scale. Online, bodies such as the FSO (Federal Statistical Office) and SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs) publish median salary surveys by sector and canton.

Once you have identified a salary offer, it is essential to calculate the net amount: from gross salary you subtract AVS/AI contributions (5.3%), UI/ALV (1.1%), LAINF (0.7-1.5%), LPP (7-18% depending on age), and finally income taxes (federal, cantonal, municipal). A salary calculator helps you visualise the net amount from a specific gross figure, including all contributions and taxes applicable in the canton.

Choosing health insurance and reviewing the contract

Within 3 months of starting employment, the employee must take out a LAMal/KVG policy. Premiums vary significantly by canton and age bracket; choosing a higher deductible (up to CHF 2,500) reduces the monthly premium. Canton Schwyz offers premium subsidies for low-income earners, according to cantonal ordinances.

If the employer adheres to a collective agreement, request a copy of the applicable CLA (collective labour agreement), which specifies working hours, minimum wages, additional holidays, and benefits. If the proposed salary is below the collective agreement minimum, you have grounds to negotiate.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between gross and net salary in Switzerland?
Gross salary is the amount of the contract. Mandatory contributions are subtracted from the gross: AVS/AI 5.3%, AD/AC 1.1%, LAINF 0.7-1.5%, LPP 7-18% (by age group), and income taxes (federal, cantonal, municipal). Net is what the worker actually receives in paychecks.
Is there a minimum wage in the Canton of Schwyz?
Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage. Some cantons set their own wage minimums by law; others do not. In the canton of Schwyz, check whether a cantonal minimum is in force; otherwise, collective bargaining agreements serve as an employment reference. Check with the cantonal labour office or employer association in your industry.
How does occupational pension (LPP) work?
The LPP is mandatory for employees with coordinated income above a threshold. Contribution rates vary by age group: 7% (25-34 years), 10% (35-44), 15% (45-54), 18% (55+ up to the reference age). The sum includes employee and employer contributions. The objective is to establish a capital for the pension in addition to the AVS.
What does health insurance (LAMal) cover?
LAMal is mandatory for all residents in Switzerland. Covers medical expenses (outpatient care, hospital care, drugs) according to the chosen deductible (CHF 300-2500 per year). Premiums and coverages vary by canton and insurer. It is not a wage tax, but a private contract with per capita premiums.
How many holidays does an employee have in Switzerland?
The legal minimum is 4 weeks per year (20 working days). For those under 20, the minimum is 5 weeks. Many collective agreements and private employers offer extra days. Maternity covers 14 weeks at 80% via loss of earnings allowance (IPG); paternity is 2 weeks.
