Crans-Montana: last patient discharged from Niguarda (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 August 2026

The hospitalisation of the twelve patients injured in the New Year's fire has ended. Francesca leaves the Burn Centre in Milan after 224 days of specialist treatment.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Discharged the last patient admitted to Milan after the New Year's fire.
  • Francesca spent 224 days at the Niguarda Burn Center.
  • A total of twelve people were treated in the Lombard capital.
  • The Tissue Bank has supplied over 25 thousand square centimeters of skin.

Key facts

  • What: Discharge of the last patient admitted.
  • Where: Centro Burni del Niguarda, Milan.
  • Who: Francesca (patient), Guido Bertolaso (Lombardy Welfare Councillor).
  • Duration: 224 days of hospitalization.
  • Interventions: Over 50 surgical operations performed.

The fire that occurred in Crans-Montana on New Year's Eve involved twelve people, who were urgently transferred to Milan to receive high-level specialist care. The path of health rehabilitation reached a significant stage with the discharge of Francesca, the last patient still hospitalized at the Burn Center of the Niguarda Hospital. The news was officially confirmed by the Councillor for Welfare of the Lombardy Region, Guido Bertolaso, who called the operation a "mission accomplished" in compliance with the promise made to the families of the young people involved from the first moments of the emergency. The care provided required a coordinated effort on a large scale, involving over 100 professionals in the initial days, including doctors, nurses and specialised technicians. Operational details

The Impact of Emergency Management

The case of Crans-Montana is an example of cross-border collaboration in healthcare for the management of severe trauma. The Niguarda facility had to implement intensive protocols to ensure not only the survival, but also the functional recovery of the twelve young patients, ten of whom were very young. Councillor Bertolaso stressed that the care provided was not limited to the acute phase of the emergency, but included more than 50 total surgeries performed to manage the burns reported. The path of clinical healing, despite having reached a phase of hospital discharge, should not be considered as the end of the care process. For many of the boys, a further specialist process has already begun, focusing for example on hand surgery, necessary to recover mobility compromised by the formation of post-traumatic scars. Rehabilitation, in this scenario, is divided into two parallel fronts: the physical one, aimed at restoring motor skills, and the psychological one, essential to process the traumatic event. Despite returning home, medical monitoring continues over time, as burn wounds require constant care and specific attention to prevent long-term complications.
