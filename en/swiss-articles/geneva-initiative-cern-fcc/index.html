Geneva launches popular vote on CERN's future

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 October 2026

The FCC would be 91 km long, would cost 15 billion francs, and 5’696 signatures are needed by mid-February 2027; final decision by the 25 Member States scheduled for May 2028.

TL;DR

  • Co-CERNés calls for a popular vote on major CERN projects
  • The FCC under consideration would be 91 kilometers long

On Tuesday, in Geneva, a collective of Geneva-based associations and political figures launched the cantonal constitutional initiative promoted by the Co-CERNés coalition. The aim is to put all major infrastructure projects linked to CERN to a popular vote whenever federal legislation provides for the canton to take a position.

The text concerns cantonal opinions on CERN constructions or infrastructure requiring authorization. The result of the consultation would become the official position of the canton, but would retain a consultative nature: it would therefore not be a binding decision on the project.

The launch follows the recent consultation procedure on the Future Circular Collider (FCC), described by the promoters as partial and as a denial of democracy. Christina Meissner, a Centre MP, accuses the Council of State of leaving the Confederation in charge of the entire dossier, reducing the canton’s effective role.

“This is about bringing the decision-making process back to the local and democratic level,” Meissner told the press.

The disputed authority

The promoters dispute the FCC’s having been placed under the authority of the Confederation, as is the case with airport planning or military infrastructure. In their view, this choice would exclude the application of cantonal powers in matters of building law.

According to Co-CERNés, the project also risks jeopardizing sustainability objectives approved by the Geneva Grand Council and the population. Raphaël Mahaim, a Vaud national councillor from the Greens, considers it unlikely that a democratic rejection could be ignored by the Federal Council or the other CERN member states.

Mahaim also considers invalidation of the initiative unlikely, recalling that similar mechanisms already exist in the Canton of Vaud for nuclear-related matters.

The FCC is still under study: a 91-kilometer-long accelerator intended to succeed the LHC from 2045. The estimated costs are 15 billion francs, a figure that opponents believe could be higher. According to the coalition, it would be Europe’s largest construction site; the promoters fear heavy traffic for about ten years, millions of tonnes of excavation, the loss of agricultural land, and more than 100 kilometers of underground work.

The concerns include cooling water, greenhouse gases, and consumption: the electricity required would be comparable to that of a city of several hundred thousand inhabitants. CERN presents the FCC as benchmark infrastructure for fundamental research. The decision will fall to the 25 member states in May 2028, barring postponements. To bring the initiative to a vote, 5’696 valid signatures are needed by mid-February 2027: the committee acknowledges the tight timeline.

Operational details

What changes for the canton and the federal dossier

The practical point of the proposal is not the immediate approval of the FCC, but the way Geneva would formalize its position. The proposed rule would introduce a popular vote for every cantonal opinion concerning a CERN construction or infrastructure project requiring authorization. The result would remain advisory, but would become the official position of the canton. The change would therefore concern the local decision-making stage, not the final decision entrusted to the CERN Member States.

From the opinion to the consultation

The controversy arises from the relationship between local and federal powers. The proponents argue that the recent consultation on the FCC left the Geneva State Council with too little effective room to maneuver because the dossier was handled at the federal level. Their initiative seeks to bring that step back to the cantonal level through a vote, without eliminating the advisory nature of the outcome.

The proposed model has a concrete consequence: the canton's position would be defined after a popular consultation rather than solely through the institutional opinion challenged by the coalition. A clear distinction nevertheless remains between the cantonal position and the decision on the project. The Federal Council and the other CERN Member States would not be replaced by the Geneva vote; Mahaim nevertheless considers it unlikely that a possible democratic rejection could be ignored.

Territory, energy and timing

To assess the dossier, the elements cited by the proponents are mainly territorial and environmental: heavy traffic for about ten years, millions of tonnes of excavation, loss of agricultural land and more than 100 kilometers of underground works. Added to these are the water required to cool the facilities, greenhouse-gas emissions and electricity consumption comparable to that of a city with several hundred thousand inhabitants. The source does not quantify the latter needs; the available figure is the estimated cost of 15 billion francs, a figure disputed by opponents.

The timeline creates the main practical scenario. The committee must collect the signatures by mid-February 2027, while the 25 Member States are expected to decide in May 2028, barring postponements. The proponents admit that organizing a vote before that decision will be particularly tight. Key points

How to follow the procedure

The sequence to follow is defined by four steps. Each has a different effect and should not be confused with the final decision on the FCC. This distinction helps follow the procedure without anticipating the effects of a vote.

1. Subject of the initiative. The committee proposes that every cantonal notice concerning a CERN construction or infrastructure project requiring authorization be submitted to a popular vote. This is the scope of the requested amendment.

2. Collection of signatures. To bring the initiative to a vote, 5’696 valid signatures must be collected by mid-February 2027. The deadline concerns the sponsoring committee and represents the first measurable step in the procedure.

3. Cantonal consultation. If the signature stage makes it possible to proceed to a vote, the outcome becomes the Canton’s official position. The source specifies that the result would retain an advisory character: the vote therefore expresses Geneva’s position without being binding.

4. CERN decision. The 25 member states are expected to take a position in May 2028, barring postponements. The committee acknowledges that the window for organizing a vote before that date will be particularly tight.

Dates to keep separate

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the clearest way to follow developments is to distinguish the signature deadline from the cantonal vote and the international decision. The first is the set deadline, the second is the expected popular vote, and the third remains up to the 25 CERN member states. These are not three equivalent stages: the consultation defines the Canton’s position, while the decision on the future accelerator remains part of the organization’s process.

Any popular rejection should also be understood in this context. Mahaim considers it unlikely that it could be ignored by the Federal Council or the other member states, but the source does not present it as automatically binding. The practical point is therefore to follow two elements: the collection of the valid signatures and the timing of the vote in relation to May 2028.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the objective of the Co-CERNés initiative?
The initiative seeks to have every cantonal notice concerning CERN construction or infrastructure that requires authorization put to a popular vote. The result would become the Canton’s official position, while remaining advisory in nature, thereby bringing decision-making back to the local and democratic level.
How many signatures are needed and by when to bring the initiative to a vote?
To initiate the procedure, 5’696 valid signatures must be collected by mid-February 2027. Only once this threshold has been reached will the committee be able to organize the cantonal consultation on the FCC project.
What does the process entail after the collection of signatures, and what will be the role of the cantonal vote with regard to CERN’s final decision?
If the signatures are sufficient, the popular vote defines the Canton’s official position, which remains consultative. The final decision on the FCC lies with CERN’s 25 member states, scheduled for May 2028, barring any postponements.
