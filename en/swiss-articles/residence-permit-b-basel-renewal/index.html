Permit B Basel: Requirements and Renewal (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 24 August 2026

The B permit in Switzerland is renewable. Guide to requirements, documents and renewal procedure in the Canton of Basel-City.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Permit B is a federal renewable home category managed by the SEM
  • Basel-City has a cantonal migration office for local processes
  • Renewal requires verification by SEM and cantonal authorities
  • Crucial distinction between permit L (short), B (medium), C (domicile)

Key facts

  • What: Residence permit B, federal renewable category (SEM)
  • Where: Canton of Basel-City (Basel-Stadt), Switzerland
  • Who: State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) + cantonal authority Basel City
  • When: Renewal to be requested before the expiration of the document
  • Duration: Renewable according to federal criteria; specific duration refers to the SEM
  • Amount: Costs not yet specified in this guide

Permit B represents a federal category of residence in Switzerland, managed by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM). Unlike the L permit (short duration, up to 1 year) and the C permit (domicile), the B permit offers an intermediate position: it allows you to stay and work in Switzerland with the possibility of renewing your residence permit. In the Canton of Basel-City, as in all Swiss cantons, the B permit renewal procedure follows federal guidelines, but also requires interaction with the local cantonal migration office.

Whoever obtains the B permit, moreover, is subject to Swiss tax obligations — for this reason it is useful to familiarize yourself with the dichiarazione delle imposte already at the moment

Operational details

Scenario di rinnovo: come funziona

Immaginiamo uno scenario tipico. A non-EU worker has obtained a B permit with an expiration date in autumn 2026. A few weeks before the expiration date, they must contact the cantonal migration office in Basel City to signal their intention to renew. The federal procedure provides that all renewal requests are examined by the SEM in coordination with the cantonal authorities. It is not possible to automatically extend the B permit: each renewal requires a formal request and verification that the requirements are still met.

Differences between L and B permits in renewal

The L permit, with a maximum duration of 1 year, is not intended for repeated renewals of the same holder in the medium term. It is a solution for episodic stays (seasonal work, short training, temporary project). On the other hand, the B permit is specifically designed for those who intend to settle in Switzerland for one or more years, with the awareness that renewal is a right as long as the federal and cantonal conditions (employment, economic resources, absence of security problems) continue to exist. The C permit, finally, is the culmination of the scale: it requires a 10-year stay (5 for EU/EFTA) and represents a stable residence with almost the same rights as Swiss citizens.

Key points

Step-by-step towards renewal

The renewal process of the B permit follows some fundamental steps:

1. Check the expiration date of your B permit (visible on the document itself) 2. Contact the cantonal migration office of Basel City several months before the expiration date to notify the intention to renew 3. Prepare the required documentation (valid passport, proof of employment, proof of financial resources, proof of accommodation, certificate of non-existence of penal problems — check the exact list with the office) 4. Send or submit the renewal application to the cantonal office 5. Wait for the elaboration by the cantonal office and the SEM

Contacts and next steps

The SEM is reachable through the official website of the Swiss Confederation for questions on federal procedures. In the Canton of Basel City, the migration office is the local point of contact for specific questions on renewal, required documents, and costs. We recommend contacting them by phone or email to obtain detailed and up-to-date information. Office hours and contact details are available on the official website of the Canton of Basel City.

Practical implications

The renewal of the B permit is essential to maintain the legality of your residence and employment in Switzerland. A missed renewal application within the terms can lead to administrative complications, loss of the right to work, and obstacles for future permit applications. For those aspiring to the C permit (after 10 years of residence), maintaining compliance with B permit renewals is a crucial step towards stable residence.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the B permit in Switzerland?
Permit B is a federal renewable residence category, issued by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM). It allows a foreigner to reside and work in Switzerland with the possibility of renewing the permit before the expiry date. It is distinguished from the L permit (short duration, up to 1 year) and the C permit (domicile, usually from 10 years of residence).
When do I need to renew my B permit?
You must contact the cantonal migration office in Basel-City several months before the expiry of your B permit to indicate your intention to renew. The expiration date is listed on your permit document. Waiting too long risks compromising the continuity of your authorisation.
What documents are required for renewal?
Renewal generally requires: a valid passport, proof of employment (employment contract), proof of economic resources, proof of accommodation, and a certificate of non-existence of criminal problems. The exact list remains to be verified directly at the cantonal office of Basel City, which will provide the updated list specific to your case.
How long does renewal take?
Processing times vary depending on the workload of the cantonal office and SEM. Contact the office for a specific estimate in your case and to find out the average processing times.
Can permit B lead to permit C?
Yes. After 10 years of continuous residence in Switzerland (or 5 years if you are an EU/EFTA citizen), you can apply for the C (domicile) permit, which represents a permanent residence. Maintaining the B permit during these years is essential to building the continuity of residence required by federal law.
