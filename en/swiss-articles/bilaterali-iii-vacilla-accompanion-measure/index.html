Operational details

The decision of the Economic and Tax Commission of the Council of States could have concrete implications for Switzerland. If unions oppose the whole package, there could be an impact on national politics and the economy. In addition, the decision could affect relations with the European Union and could have consequences on the protection of workers.

The accompanying measure in question is the flexibility scheme for companies operating in Switzerland and with employees abroad. This regime was introduced with the bilateral agreement III, signed in 2011. The aim is to facilitate the mobility of workers and to reduce administrative barriers for companies operating in Switzerland.

However, trade unions have expressed concerns about the possibility that this regime could be used to bypass Swiss labour rules. In particular, they criticised the possibility that companies could use this regime to reduce labour costs and not secure employees' rights.

The Economic and Taxation Commission of the Council of States has proposed a change to the flexibility regime to address these concerns. The proposal envisages introducing a cap on the number of employees that can be hired through this scheme and increasing penalties for companies that do not comply with labour standards.

Key points

The Economic and Tax Commission of the Council of States recently announced the decision to monitor the consequences of worker protection, one of the accompanying measures of Bilateral III. This decision was taken in consideration of the possible impact on national politics and economy.

It is important to note that the protection of workers is one of the most important measures of Bilateral III, which aims to establish a balance between the protection of workers and the competitiveness of the economy. The decision of the Economic and Taxation Commission of the Council of States to monitor the consequences of this measure could have a significant impact on national policy and economy.

A concrete example of how the protection of workers can influence national politics and economy is the case of the Canton of Ticino. In 2020, the Canton of Ticino introduced a law that obliges companies to pay an additional tax for foreign workers. This law was passed to protect Ticino workers and to reduce demographic pressure on the health system. However, the law also had a negative impact on the local economy, as many businesses decided to move their headquarters to other cantons.
