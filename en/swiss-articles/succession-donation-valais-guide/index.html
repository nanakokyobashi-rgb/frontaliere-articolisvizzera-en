In the Canton of Valais, as in all Swiss cantons, inheritance tax is governed by specific cantonal laws. Unlike most European countries, Switzerland does not have a federal inheritance tax: the competence is exclusively cantonal and communal. This means that each canton – including Valais – autonomously defines rates, exemptions, allowances, and declaration procedures. The Swiss federal system ensures that each canton can adapt inheritance tax to its own needs and fiscal values.

Inheritance tax in Valais applies when an inheritance is transferred to heirs. The main elements of the system include:

How inheritance tax works in Valais

Rates according to degree of kinship: The rates vary significantly depending on the relationship between the deceased and the heir. The classic Swiss system provides categories of heirs (spouse, descendants, collaterals, strangers), with progressive rates that increase with the distance of the family relationship.

Operational details

Why inheritance tax rates vary between cantons

Inheritance tax in Switzerland reflects the federalist principle of cantonal autonomy. While other aspects of taxation – such as federal direct tax and VAT – are harmonized at the national level, inheritance tax remains entirely under cantonal control. This creates significant differences between cantons, which can influence the estate planning decisions of those who own property or investments in Switzerland.

A crucial element is the degree of kinship. In the standard Swiss system:

  • The spouse benefits from very low rates or total exemptions
  • Children (direct descendants) have low rates
  • Siblings (collateral relatives) face moderate rates
  • People unrelated to the family pay the highest rates

In Valais, as in many other cantons, this progression is present, although the amount of rates and exemptions varies compared to other cantons.

Practical implications for heirs: If a parent residing in Valais has heirs who live or work in Switzerland, the calculation of inheritance tax will depend on the Valais rules. For those who live as cross-border commuters or have property in multiple cantons, it may be advisable to understand how tax residence affects inheritance tax.

Estate planning: In the Swiss context, where competence is cantonal, those with significant assets might consider how tax residence affects inheritance tax. Tools like the third pillar can be relevant for long-term estate planning, in coordination with inheritance tax rules.

Exemptions and reductions: Exemptions for the spouse and descendants in Switzerland are a central element of tax policy. The intention is to encourage the preservation of family wealth within the immediate family, reducing the tax burden on inheritances destined for the closest relatives.

Key points

How to declare an inheritance in Valais

When a person residing in Valais dies, the heirs have a legal obligation to submit an inheritance declaration. This process is essential to regularize the tax situation of the inheritance and determine the tax due.

Main steps of the declaration:

1. Gather documentation: The heirs must collect the will (if any), the death certificate, the list of inherited assets (real estate, bank accounts, investments), the deceased's debts, and any other relevant documents to determine the net value of the inheritance.

2. Complete the declaration form: The inheritance declaration in Valais must be completed according to the form provided by the Valais tax administration. The form requires detailed information about the identity of the heirs, the degree of kinship, the value of the inheritance by category of asset, and other relevant data.

3. Submit the declaration: The declaration must be submitted to the competent tax office of Valais within the deadlines set by law. The deadlines vary depending on the circumstances (inheritance with a will, legal inheritance) but must generally be strictly respected.

4. Calculate and pay the tax: Once the tax is calculated based on the declaration, the heirs receive a tax notice and have the obligation to pay the tax within the indicated deadlines.

Deadlines and procedures: The deadlines for the declaration are set by Valais law. Missing the deadlines can result in penalties and interest. Therefore, it is important to act promptly after the death.

Frequently Asked Questions
In Valais, does the spouse pay inheritance tax?
No, in most Swiss cantons, including Valais, the spouse benefits from a total or partial exemption from inheritance tax. This means that the transfer of assets from the deceased to the surviving spouse is generally not subject to taxation, or is subject to a very low rate. The specific rules depend on the current Valletta legislation.
What are the terms for declaring a succession in Valais?
The terms for the declaration of succession in Valais are established by cantonal law. Generally, heirs must file the declaration within a period defined by the canton, which may vary depending on the circumstances (presence or absence of will). It is important to check the specific deadlines with the Valais tax administration, as exceeding the deadline can result in penalties.
How are inheritance rates calculated in Valais?
Rates of succession in the Valais vary according to the degree of kinship between the deceased and the heir. The classic Swiss system provides for lower rates for spouses and descendants, moderate rates for collaterals, and higher rates for strangers. Each canton, including Valais, has its own table of rates defined by cantonal law.
Do children also pay inheritance tax in Valais?
In Switzerland, children (descendants in a straight line) often have a privileged tax treatment compared to other categories of heirs, with reduced rates or partial exemptions. In the Valais, as in many cantons, children benefit from lower rates than collateral and strangers. The specific rules depend on the Walloon legislation.
