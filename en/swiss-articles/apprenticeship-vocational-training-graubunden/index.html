Vocational training in Switzerland is regulated at the federal level by the Law on Vocational Training (LFPr), which defines its principles, and finds concrete application in each Canton, including Graubünden. The Swiss system is based on the so-called "dual model": the apprentice carries out practical training in a company and theoretical training in a vocational school at the same time, with intercompany courses that complete the path.

Professional maturity: an additional opportunity

Professional maturity (MP) is a secondary qualification II that is obtained in parallel with the internship. It is divided into seven areas (health, social, visual and applied arts, economics, technique, architecture and natural sciences) and is open to holders of a federal certificate of ability or, in some cases, during the last year of the internship for candidates who meet the school requirements.

Obtaining the MP allows access, without further examination, to professional university schools (SUPs) in the respective subject area. To enroll in a university (ETH or cantonal university), on the other hand, gymnasial maturity or the passing of a complementary exam is required.

Apprentice living costs and conditions

The cost of living in Switzerland is high and has a real impact on an apprentice's monthly budget. Accommodation is one of the main items, regulated by federal law (art. 253 et seq. CO) the same in each canton. The security deposit can be a maximum of three months' rent, it must be paid into a restricted account in the tenant's name. The cancellation of the landlord requires the official cantonal form, and the tenant can dispute it within thirty days to the conciliation authority.

Health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory for anyone residing in Switzerland. People moving for internship must take out coverage within three months

Concrete procedure: how to proceed step by step

Finding an apprenticeship position in the Canton of Graubünden requires following a reasoned sequence of steps, which should ideally be started well in advance of the beginning of the apprenticeship.

The first step is to identify the desired sector and profession, by consulting the professional profiles published by the Federal Office for Professional Education and Technology (UFFT/BFTB). For each profession, the description of the skills, the duration of the training, and the list of schools involved are available.

The second step consists of preparing a complete application, made up of a cover letter, an updated CV, copies of school certificates and, where available, internship certificates or references. The cover letter should be tailored to the company and the chosen profession. In the most sought-after sectors, it is useful to apply to several companies, both directly and through job placement portals.

The third step is attending an exploratory interview, during which the company assesses the candidate's aptitudes and the candidate evaluates the work environment. Once the interview is passed, the parties sign an apprenticeship contract drawn up in writing and compliant with cantonal law.

The fourth step is the registration of the contract with the cantonal office for professional education in Graubünden, which verifies its compliance and enrolls the apprentice in the school pathway. The office also provides information on the planned inter-company courses.

Frequently Asked Questions
How does the dual system work in Graubünden?
The apprentice simultaneously carries out practical training in a company and theoretical training in a vocational school, with intercompany courses that complete the course. The system is regulated at the federal level by the Law on Vocational Training (LFPr) and is concretely applied in the Canton of Graubünden under the supervision of the Cantonal Office of Vocational Training. The final qualification is the federal certificate of ability (AFC) or the federal certificate of practical training
What is the salary of an apprentice in Switzerland?
The monthly salary of the apprentice varies according to the year of internship, the sector and the canton: the salary of the first year is lower than that of the following years, to reflect the progressive skills. The employer also pays the mandatory social contributions (AVS/AI/IPG, AD, LPP for those subject, and LAINF according to the sector). There is no federal minimum wage: wages are regulated by collective bargaining agreements or defined individually in the internship contract.
What is professional maturity and how do you achieve it?
Professional maturity (MP) is a secondary qualification II that is obtained in parallel with the internship. It is divided into seven areas: health, social, visual and applied arts, economics, technology, architecture and natural sciences. It is open to holders of a federal certificate of ability or, in some cases, during the last year of the internship. Obtaining the MP allows access without further examination to professional university schools (SUPs) in the respective subject area.
What are the LPP contribution rates for workers?
For workers subject to the LPP contribution obligation, the coordinated salary is subject to levies that vary by age group: 7% from 25 to 34 years old, 10% between 35 and 44 years old, 15% between 45 and 54 years old and 18% from 55 years old up to the reference age. These percentages apply to both the employee's and the employer's share, according to the breakdown provided for by law. The AVS/AI/IPG withholding is 5.3% for the employee, plus 1.1% for unemployment insurance.
What permits are required for a foreign apprentice in Switzerland?
The foreign apprentice must have a permit appropriate to their status: the B permit (residence) for residents, the C permit (domicile) for those who have completed the required requirements, the G permit (border) for those who cross the border daily. For holders of a B or C permit, the right of access to the traineeship is equal to that of Swiss citizens; for a G permit, the traineeship is only possible if the employment takes place mainly in the canton of employment.
