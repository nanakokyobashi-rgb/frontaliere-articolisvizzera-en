Basel City Inheritance and Gift Tax: Rates (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 2 September 2026

Guide to inheritance and gift taxes in the Canton of Basel-City: rates for degree of kinship, exemptions and reporting procedures.

Context

In brief

  • Inheritance tax is a cantonal competence in Switzerland, not federal
  • Basel-Stadt has its own rules on rates and exemptions
  • Spouses and direct descendants generally enjoy benefits

Key facts

  • What: Tax on inheritance and donations
  • Where: Canton of Basel-Stadt, Switzerland
  • Who: Beneficiaries of inheritance and people making donations
  • Competence: Cantonal tax administration
  • Structure: Rates by degree of kinship (specific to the canton)

In the Swiss tax system, the taxation of inheritances and donations is exclusively a cantonal competence. Unlike the federal direct tax, which is managed at the federal level by the Federal Tax Administration (FTA), inheritance tax does not exist at the national level. Each canton—including the Canton of Basel-Stadt—autonomously regulates its own rules and rates applicable to inheritances and donations.

The Swiss Confederation organizes taxation according to three levels: federal, cantonal, and communal. For inheritances, the responsibility lies entirely with the cantons. In the Canton of Basel-Stadt, the cantonal tax office applies its own law that sets specific rates, exemptions, and declaration procedures.

This means that an inheritance received in Basel-Stadt incurs a different tax burden compared to the same inheritance received in another Swiss canton. For those who own assets or reside in the canton, understanding the local structure is essential for proper estate planning.

Operational details

Tax rates, exemptions, and degree of kinship

In the structure of inheritance taxation in Switzerland, the degree of kinship is the determining factor for calculating the tax. The surviving spouse and direct descendants (children, grandchildren) typically benefit from reduced rates or total exemptions in almost all cantons. This practice recognizes the importance of patrimonial continuity within the family unit and is in line with established principles of succession law.

Collateral relatives (brothers, nephews) and third-party beneficiaries pay significantly higher rates. The tax progression varies from canton to canton. Some cantons adopt a progressive system where the rate increases with the amount of the inheritance; others apply fixed rates for each category of kinship. In the Canton of Basel-Stadt, as in other cantons, the inheritance and gift tax rates are established by the cantonal law.

While it is known that the general structure provides benefits for close relatives, the specific rates vary and must be consulted with the cantonal tax administration for correct planning. This variability makes professional advice crucial for those with assets or heirs in multiple cantons.

Gifts and estate planning

Gifts are subject to taxation parallel to that of inheritances, although with their own procedures and timelines. In the Canton of Basel-Stadt, gifts must be declared to the tax office according to the terms provided by the local cantonal law.

Key points

Declaration Procedure and Deadlines

The declaration of an inheritance in the Canton of Basel-Stadt follows a structured administrative process. After the death, the heirs must submit a declaration to the cantonal tax office within the deadlines set by cantonal law. These deadlines vary from canton to canton and must be verified with the competent tax administration to avoid penalties or interest.

The inheritance declaration typically includes:

  • A complete list of inherited assets (real estate, bank accounts, financial instruments, etc.)
  • Market value assessment documentation of the assets
  • Identification of all heirs and their degrees of kinship
  • Calculation of the applicable tax according to the cantonal rates

For donations, the procedure is sometimes more straightforward but equally binding. The donor or the donee must declare the donation to the cantonal tax office according to the times and methods provided by the law of Basel-Stadt. Timeliness is crucial to avoid administrative complications.

Professional Consultation and Administrative Resources

Given that inheritance and donation regulations are complex and vary significantly from canton to canton, it is strongly advised to consult a notary or specialized tax advisor. In Switzerland, notaries play a central role in drafting wills, managing inheritances, and ensuring compliance with cantonal laws.

The Tax Administration of the Canton of Basel-Stadt provides administrative consultation offices, declaration forms, and procedural guides. The official portal of the canton contains updated information on rates, exemptions, declaration deadlines, and the contacts of the competent offices.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why is inheritance tax not the same in all Swiss cantons?
In Switzerland, inheritance taxation is a cantonal, not a federal, competence. Each canton, including Basel-City, defines its own rates, exemptions and procedures. This decentralised structure reflects the Swiss federal system and allows cantons to adapt taxation to the local context, but means that the same inherited amount can be taxed differently in different cantons.
Does the surviving spouse pay inheritance tax in the Canton of Basel-City?
In most Swiss cantons, the surviving spouse benefits from significant exemptions or substantial reductions in inheritance tax. However, the specific details vary by canton. In the Canton of Basel-City, it is necessary to consult the cantonal tax administration to learn about the current and precise exemptions applicable to the spouse.
How long do I have to declare a succession in Basel City?
The terms of declaration of a succession vary from canton to canton and are established by cantonal law. For the Canton of Basel-City, the specific deadlines must be verified at the cantonal tax office or in the official portal of the canton. Adhering to the terms is essential to avoid additional penalties or interest.
Are donations taxed in the same way as inheritances?
Donations follow similar principles to successions but with different procedures and timing. Some cantons apply more favourable taxation to donations made during life than those transmitted by inheritance. In the Canton of Basel-City, specific details on taxation and donation reporting procedure should be consulted with the cantonal tax administration.
Who is responsible for paying the inheritance tax, the heirs or the deceased?
In general, inheritance tax is the responsibility of the heirs or beneficiaries who receive the assets. However, the legal details of who is formally responsible for payment may vary from canton to canton. In Switzerland it is advisable to consult a notary or tax advisor to understand the specific legal obligations in the Canton of Basel-City.
