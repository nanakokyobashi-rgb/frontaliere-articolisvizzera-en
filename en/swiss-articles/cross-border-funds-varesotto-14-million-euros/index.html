14 million to border territories: funds for border workers in Varese

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 30 July 2026 · Updated on 25 September 2026

Over 14 million euros earmarked for Italian border municipalities. Refunds and new taxation for border workers in Varese: what changes.

Context

TL;DR

  • More than 14 million euros allocated to Italian border territories
  • Funds also allocated to municipalities in the Varesotto bordering Switzerland
  • Tax rebates linked to the withholding tax on cross-border workers
  • New Cross-Border Workers Agreement in force as of January 1st, 2024

Key facts

  • What: Allocation of funds to border territories for cross-border workers
  • When: Refers to the context following the entry into force of the New Agreement (January 1st, 2024)
  • Where: Italian border municipalities, the Varesotto area and Luino
  • Who: Italian local authorities benefiting from tax rebates
  • Amount: More than 14 million euros in total

The announcement of more than 14 million euros earmarked for Italian border territories, with resources also reaching municipalities in the Varesotto, once again shines a spotlight on the system of tax rebates linked to cross-border workers. The news, reported by Luino Notizie, falls within the framework of implementing the New Agreement between Italy and Switzerland on cross-border workers, signed on December 23, 2020 and entered into force on January 1st, 2024, following Italy's ratification through Law 83 of June 13, 2023.

The rebate mechanism under the new framework

The rebate system provides that a portion of the withholding tax withheld in Switzerland on cross-border workers' income is remitted to the Italian municipalities where they reside. Under the New Agreement, the tax-free allowance for new cross-border workers rises to 10.000 euros, while for old cross-border workers (those who already had that status before July 17, 2023), the exemption of 7.500 euros remains in place under the 2024-2033 transitional regime. Withholding tax continues to be withheld exclusively in Switzerland, while Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit in section CE of Form 730.

Withholding tax rates vary by Swiss canton and municipality. In Ticino, where a large proportion of cross-border workers residing in the Varesotto work, the cantonal tax administration manages collection under the supervision of the AFC/ESTV at the federal level. Rebates represent a significant item in the budgets of Italian border municipalities, funding local services and infrastructure.

Operational details

Practical implications for cross-border workers and municipalities

The inflow of more than 14 million euros into the border areas has concrete effects on two fronts. For the Italian municipalities in the Varesotto and upper Verbano areas, these resources bolster the ordinary budget: road maintenance, social services, schools and local public transport. For cross-border workers residing in this area, the flow of revenue-sharing payments is the territorial counterpart of the taxes they pay in Switzerland.

With the entry into force of the New Agreement, the distinction between old and new cross-border workers produces different effects on the Italian tax base. A cross-border worker hired after July 17, 2023 is subject to the 10.000-euro tax-free allowance: the portion of income exceeding it contributes to total income in Italy, taxed according to IRPEF brackets (23% up to 28.000 euros, 33% between 28.001 and 50.000 euros, 43% above). Those who enjoy old cross-border worker status retain the 7.500-euro exemption until 2033, with a tax credit for the withholding tax already paid in Switzerland.

Comparison with the previous regime

Before 2024, the Convention of March 9, 1976 provided for full exemption in Italy for historic cross-border workers, with revenue-sharing payments set at a fixed amount per resident. The new system ties the revenue-sharing payments to the actual revenue from withholding tax, making them vary from year to year. This explains why the amount of more than 14 million can fluctuate: it depends on the number of resident cross-border workers, their incomes and the cantonal tax rates applied. For the municipalities in the Varesotto area, revenue predictability therefore becomes a financial planning issue.

An often-overlooked aspect concerns social security contributions. In Switzerland, cross-border workers pay AVS/AI/IPG (5,3% paid by the employee), AD/AC (1,1% on a maximum amount of CHF 148.200), LAINF (0,7-1,5%) and LPP (7-18% by age bracket from age 25). In Italy, they do not pay INPS contributions on income from Swiss employment, but they accrue pension rights in the Swiss system. Healthcare coverage is provided through LAMal, with an opt-out right for cross-border workers (G permit), with adult deductibles between CHF 300 and 2.500.

Key points

What to do: deadlines and requirements

For cross-border workers residing in Varesotto, the Italian income tax return (Form 730 or Redditi PF) requires attention to section CE for the tax credit on Swiss withholding tax. The Swiss Unified Certification (equivalent to the CUD) must be requested from the employer or the cantonal tax administration by March. The standard deadline for the pre-filled 730 is September 30, with the option to submit it through a CAF or tax accountant.

Document checklist for the tax return

  • Swiss Unified Certification (employer or cantonal office)
  • Certificate of Italian registered residence
  • Valid G permit
  • Any LAMal certificates for premium deductions
  • Documentation of medical expenses and deductible charges

Frequently Asked Questions
Who are the beneficiaries of the over 14 million euros for the border territories?
The Italian border municipalities, including those of Varesotto and Alto Verbano, receive the tax rebates deriving from the withholding tax withheld in Switzerland on the income of border workers residing in their territory.
How does the taxation for new frontier workers change compared to the old ones?
The new frontier workers (hired after 17 July 2023) have an exemption of 10,000 euros in Italy; the old frontier workers maintain the exemption of 7,500 euros until 2033. Both benefit from the tax credit for source tax paid in Switzerland.
What documents are needed for the Italian tax return of a border worker?
Single Swiss certification, certificate of residence, valid G permit, any LAMal certificate for premium deduction, documents for deductible charges and health expenses. The ordinary deadline of 730 is September 30.
Are the refreshments fixed or variable every year?
With the New Agreement in force from 1 January 2024, the refunds are variable and linked to the actual revenue of the tax at source, which depends on the number of border workers, their incomes and the cantonal rates applied.
Where is the tax paid at source for a border worker resident in Italy?
Tax at source is withheld exclusively in Switzerland by the employer or the cantonal tax administration. Italy avoids double taxation by granting the tax credit in the EC framework of 730.
