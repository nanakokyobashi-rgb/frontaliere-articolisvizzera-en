Operational details

Housing represents the most significant item of expenditure for those residing in the Canton of Zurich in 2026. Rentals in the city centre and neighbouring areas reflect the scarcity of supply, necessitating an accurate comparison between the different cantonal areas. At the same time, health management through the LAMal has a heavy impact on disposable income. Compulsory health insurance provides adults with deductibles ranging from a minimum of CHF 300 to a maximum of CHF 2500. Choosing the deductible is a crucial step: a high deductible allows you to reduce your monthly premium, but requires an immediate liquidity reserve to cover your initial medical expenses. Type G border workers retain the right of option, being able to choose between the Swiss insurance system and that of the country of residence, a decision that must be weighed on the basis of the frequency of use of health services.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the mandatory social contributions in Zurich in 2026?
In 2026, workers in the Canton of Zurich are subject to several mandatory withholdings. The contributions for AVS/AI/IPG are fixed at 5.3% of the gross salary for the employee share. Unemployment insurance (AD/AC) is added to 1.1% for incomes up to CHF 148,200. Other items include the LAINF (0.7-1.5%) and the LPP occupational pension, which varies between 7% and 18% depending on the age of the worker from 25 years.
How does taxation work for border workers working in Zurich?
Under the New Frontier Agreement of 23 December 2020, in force from 1 January 2024, the source tax is withheld exclusively in Switzerland. The worker must then declare income in Italy, where the tax credit is applied (EC framework of 730) to avoid double taxation. New frontier workers enjoy a deductible of €10,000, while for old frontier workers (active before 17/7/2023) the exemption is €7,500 under the transitional regime 2024-2033.
What is LAMal and how much does it cost in Zurich?
LAMal is the mandatory health insurance in Switzerland. This is not a tax, but an insurance premium paid to private health insurance funds. Adults can choose deductibles between CHF 300 and CHF 2500. The prizes vary depending on the chosen cashier and the deductible. Border crossers have the right of option, which allows them to choose whether to insure in Switzerland or maintain coverage in the country of residence according to international standards.
