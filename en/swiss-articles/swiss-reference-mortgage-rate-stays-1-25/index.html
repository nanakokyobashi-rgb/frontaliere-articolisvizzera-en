Reference mortgage rate remains at 1.25%: what changes for rents (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 September 2026

FOAB confirms the parameter at 1.25% for the third quarter: historic low, average mortgage rate at 1.31%, SNB stuck at 0.0%. What happens now for tenants and landlords.

Context

In brief

  • Reference mortgage rate confirmed at 1.25%
  • The indicator is at an all-time low for a year now
  • SNB maintains key interest rate at 0.0%, inflation at 0.4%
  • Average mortgage interest rate at 1.31%, unchanged

Key Facts

  • What: Reference mortgage rate confirmed at 1.25%
  • When: Quarterly decision of the FOAB, published today
  • Where: whole Switzerland (single national parameter)
  • Who: Federal Office for Housing (FOH)
  • Amount: 1.25% (policy rate), 1.31% (average rate), 0.0% (SNB policy rate), 0.4% (July inflation)
  • Thresholds: Automatic triggers if average falls below 1.13% or exceeds 1.37%

The reference mortgage rate does not change: the indicator used to determine rents for rental apartments throughout Switzerland remains at 1.25%. This was announced by the Federal Office for Housing (FOH) this morning, confirming the forecasts of all economists interviewed by the AWP agency, who had predicted the status quo.

A level that has stood for twelve months

The parameter has been at this level, which has also been its lowest level for a year now. Today's decision is part of a very firm general interest rate picture, with the Swiss National Bank (SNB) maintaining its key interest rate at 0.0%. However, experts do not rule out a possible rise, in the wake of inflation which, although still under control (inflation in July was at 0.4%), shows signs of progress.

Operational details

What changes for tenants

If the rent is still based on a rate of 1.5% or higher, the right to a rent reduction remains in principle. To put this in context: if the reference rate rises by 0.25 percentage points, landlords can increase the rent by 3.00%; in the opposite case, tenants have the option of requesting a rent reduction of 2.91%. With the parameter stuck at 1.25%, those who are already aligned with this percentage see no changes, while those who pay a rent calculated on higher previous values retain the option of applying to the conciliation authority for a discount.

A history of downward movements

Since September 2008, the reference mortgage rate has been unified throughout Switzerland and has replaced the previously decisive rate for variable-rate mortgages in the individual cantons. The rate started at a peak of 3.5% and had fallen to a low of 1.25% for the first time in March 2020. In 2023, there were then two hikes, the first ever forward movements of the indicator, which brought the parameter to 1.75%, a level maintained between December 2023 and December 2024, when it had fallen to 1.5%. The last downward jump dates back to twelve months ago.

Outlook to 2027-2028

According to experts, the current rate of 1.25% could remain relatively long until 2027-2028, provided that major geopolitical or economic events do not give a strong boost to inflation, resulting in an increase in the SNB's key interest rate and, in turn, an increase in mortgage rates. For Swiss landlords and tenants, a phase of prolonged stability is therefore beginning, which allows them to plan family budgets with greater certainty, but which could come to an abrupt end in the event of external shocks.

Key points

How the request for rent reduction works

If you wish to assert your right to a rent reduction, the procedure involves sending a formal request to the landlord, indicating the current reference rate (1.25%) and the current rent calculated at a rate of 1.5% or higher. If the landlord refuses or does not reside within the deadline, the tenant can contact the cantonal conciliation authority within 30 days of the dispute. It is advisable to keep all documentation: lease agreement, previous notices and references to federal tenancy law.

When to expect the next update

The indicator is published quarterly by the FOAB. The next revision is expected in the months following 30 September, the reference date for calculating the next average rate. If the average interest rate were to fall below 1.13%, the parameter would rise to 1.00%; if it were to exceed 1.37%, it would rise to 1.50%.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the reference mortgage rate in Switzerland?
The Federal Office for Housing (FOH) has confirmed the reference mortgage rate at 1.25%, the same value as it has been for twelve months now and which represents the lowest level since national unification in September 2008. The average interest rate on mortgage loans, as of 30 June, remained at 1.31% as in the previous quarter.
When can a change in the reference rate be triggered?
The parameter is set in quarters of a percentage point and only changes if the average interest rate on mortgage loans falls below 1.13% (in which case the reference would rise to 1.00%) or exceeds 1.37% (it would rise to 1.50%). With the average still at 1.31%, the conditions for a new rounding have therefore not been met.
What effect does the rate have on Swiss tenants' rent?
If the rent is still calculated at a rate of 1.5% or higher, the right to a rent reduction remains in principle. An increase of 0.25 points in the reference rate allows landlords a rent increase of 3.00%, while an equivalent reduction entitles landlords to a discount of 2.91%. Those who are already aligned with 1.25% see no changes.
How can I ask the landlord for a rent reduction?
The procedure involves sending a formal request to the landlord indicating the updated reference rate (1.25%) and the current rent calculated on a rate equal to or greater than 1.5%. If the landlord refuses or does not respond within the deadline, the tenant can contact the cantonal conciliation authority within 30 days of the dispute, keeping the lease agreement and previous communications.
When is the next revision of the policy rate expected?
The indicator is published quarterly by the FOAB. The next revision is expected in the months following 30 September, the reference date for calculating the next average rate. Experts believe that the current 1.25% could remain unchanged until 2027-2028, barring geopolitical or economic shocks that push inflation and the SNB's policy rate, currently at 0.0%.
