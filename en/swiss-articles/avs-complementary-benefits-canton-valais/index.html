AVS and complementary services in the Canton of Valais: practical guide (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 6 September 2026

Find out how the AVS and complementary services work in the Canton of Valais, with a focus on the cantonal compensation fund and the calculation of the annuity.

Context

In brief

  • The AVS is the first pillar of Swiss social security.
  • The Canton of Valais has a cantonal compensation fund.
  • Supplementary benefits cover the minimum living standard.

Key Facts

  • What: AVS and supplementary benefits.
  • When: Always.
  • Where: Canton of Valais.
  • Who: Cantonal compensation fund.
  • Amount: Not specified.

The first pillar of Swiss social security is the AVS (Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance), which guarantees a basic pension to all residents. In the Canton of Valais, the management of the AVS is entrusted to the cantonal compensation fund. This fund is responsible for calculating AVS pensions and managing contribution gaps. Supplementary benefits, on the other hand, are intended to cover the minimum living standard for those who do not have sufficient financial resources.

Calculation of the AVS Pension

The calculation of the AVS pension is based on the contributions paid during working life. The cantonal compensation fund of Valais is in charge of this calculation, ensuring that each contributor receives a pension proportional to the contributions paid. It is important to note that contribution gaps can negatively affect the pension amount.

Supplementary Benefits

Supplementary benefits are an additional support for those who do not have sufficient financial resources to cover the minimum living standard. These benefits are managed by the cantonal compensation fund and are intended to ensure a dignified standard of living for all residents of the Canton of Valais.

Operational details

Practical Implications

Supplementary benefits are a crucial element in ensuring that all residents of the Valais Canton can live with dignity. These benefits are especially important for those with contribution gaps in the AVS or those who have never worked. The cantonal clearing house plays a key role in ensuring that these services are provided correctly and in a timely manner.

Hypothetical Scenarios

Consider the case of a frontier worker who worked for many years in Switzerland but has contribution gaps. This person may be entitled to a reduced AVS annuity. However, thanks to the supplementary benefits, he could still receive sufficient financial support to cover the minimum living allowance. The Valais Cantonal Compensation Fund is responsible for assessing the individual situation and providing the necessary supplementary benefits.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Prior to the introduction of supplementary benefits, many people with contribution gaps in the AVS were in financial difficulty. With the introduction of these features, the situation has improved significantly. Today, thanks to the cantonal compensation fund, all residents of the Valais Canton can count on adequate financial support to ensure a decent standard of living.

Key points

Action: What to Do

If you are a resident of the Canton of Valais and need information about supplementary benefits, you can contact the cantonal compensation fund. This fund will provide you with all the necessary information and guide you through the application process. It is important to submit the application promptly to ensure that the benefits are provided in a timely manner.

Step-by-Step Procedure

1. Contact the cantonal compensation fund of Valais. You can find the contacts on their official website. 2. Provide all necessary information about your financial situation. This includes documents such as income statements, employment certificates, and bank documents. 3. Wait for the evaluation of your application. The process can take several weeks, so it is advisable to start as early as possible. 4. Receive the supplementary benefits, if you are entitled to them. The benefits can vary depending on your financial situation and the current regulations.

Concrete Examples

Suppose you are a pensioner living in Sion, with an annual income of CHF 25,000 and assets of CHF 100,000. According to current regulations, you might be entitled to a supplementary benefit of CHF 500 per month. However, it is important to note that these amounts can vary depending on your specific situation.
