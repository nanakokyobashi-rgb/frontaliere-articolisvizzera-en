Family doctors criticize Tardoc tariff (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 September 2026

Tardoc does not re-evaluate family medicine and increases outpatient costs. The MFE association asks the Federal Council to avoid a linear devaluation.

Context

In brief

  • Tardoc has not revalued family medicine as expected by decision-makers
  • Outpatient costs have increased significantly, imbalance in primary care
  • mfe association rejects a linear devaluation of all specialties

Key facts

  • What: new outpatient medical tariff (Tardoc)
  • Who: Swiss Association of Family Doctors and Pediatricians (mfe)
  • Status: family medicine not revalued; outpatient costs increased
  • Source: Monika Reber, co-president mfe
  • Body: Federal Council (requirements for cost neutrality)

The Swiss Association of Family Doctors and Pediatricians (mfe) strongly criticizes the new Tardoc medical tariff. In today's communication, the organization denounces the failure to revalue family medicine, a widespread expectation among federal and cantonal political decision-makers. Instead of economically recognizing the value of primary care, the new system has resulted in a significant increase in costs in the Swiss outpatient sector, creating a structural imbalance.

The tariff imbalance risks worsening

The situation is even more critical in certain cantons, where family medicine has even suffered a devaluation. Only pediatricians and general practitioners see a slight improvement within the new tariff. This creates a growing imbalance at the expense of basic care, the sector that ensures primary care for patients in the Swiss healthcare system.

Operational details

The cost of family medicine in crisis

The tariff imbalance created by Tardoc represents a concrete challenge for the future of the Swiss healthcare system. When outpatient costs increase significantly but family medicine does not benefit economically, a structural disincentive is created that could discourage family doctors from remaining in this professional field. The shortage of family doctors is already a recognized problem in many Swiss cantons, and the new tariff schedule risks worsening this situation.

The overall increase in costs in the outpatient sector is not invisible to the rest of the system. Due to these increases, the Federal Council has set cost neutrality requirements in the healthcare sector. This means that any cost increases will have to be neutralized through subsequent tariff adjustments, creating a chain reaction that affects all medical specialties and further complicates the regulatory framework.

Implications for health insurance premiums and the cost of living

For those living in Switzerland, the most direct impact could be reflected in the premiums of the LAMal, the mandatory health insurance. If outpatient costs continue to rise without a corresponding tariff revision that compensates for the increase, health insurance funds will face increasingly narrow margins. This can translate into upward pressure on premiums, as insurers must ensure full coverage of outpatient medical services.

Key points

How to monitor tariff changes in your canton

The tariff adjustment process is ongoing, and the decisions of the Federal Council will have direct implications for residents. Those living in Switzerland can follow the evolution of the situation through official channels: the UFSP (Federal Office of Public Health) website publishes information on tariff revisions and federal consultations. Medical associations such as mfe continue to participate in negotiations and make their positions public through regular press releases.

It is useful to stay informed about tariff developments as they indirectly affect the healthcare services available in your canton and potentially the costs of access. If you notice changes in the costs of basic medical consultations in your area, it could be related to the current tariff adjustments being made by the federal administration.

Practical tools and resources for further exploration

For those who wish to delve deeper into the cost of their health coverage, there are online platforms that allow you to compare the premiums of different health insurance providers. The official LAMal resource offers an overview of premiums by canton and region, as well as information on any subsidies for premium payments. The mfe association remains the reference point for family doctors who wish to understand the implications of the new tariff system on their professional practice.

Frequently Asked Questions
What exactly does it mean that Tardoc has not re-evaluated family medicine?
It means that the new medical tariff, which defines the remuneration for each outpatient service, has not economically improved the recognition of family doctors compared to the previous version. In fact, in some cantons it has caused a reduction. The Mfe association had asked that the new system better recognize the value of primary care, but Tardoc did not realize this expectation. How can the new tariff plan affect my LAMal insurance premiums?
If the outpatient costs increase overall, the health funds must compensate for these increases. The increase in benefit costs may be reflected in compulsory health insurance premiums. As GPs have not obtained an economic revaluation through Tardoc, but overhead costs have risen, financial pressure is created and potentially passed on to policyholders. Cantons and health insurance funds are considering whether to increase premiums to maintain coverage of outpatient services.
What medical specialties benefit from Tardoc?
According to the communication of the MFE association, only paediatricians and general practitioners recorded a slight improvement in the new tariff. The other specialties and, in particular, pure family medicine have not received a significant revaluation. This creates a tariff imbalance that favours some branches at the expense of basic care, compromising the overall balance of the Swiss outpatient health system.
How can I check if my canton has been affected by a tariff depreciation?
The MFE association indicates that 'some cantons' have suffered a devaluation of family medicine, but does not specify which ones. For detailed information on the tariff situation in your canton, you can contact the family doctor directly, contact the cantonal health office or consult the official website of the MFE association. These entities will be able to provide specific data on how Tardoc has impacted outpatient performance in your region.
What decisions does the Federal Council have to make on the new tariff adjustments?
The Federal Council has set spending neutrality requirements in the health sector. Because Tardoc has resulted in a net increase in outpatient costs, subsequent fee adjustments will appear unavoidable to meet these requirements. The association MFE calls for avoiding a linear devaluation that affects all specialties; the federal government will have to find a balance that neutralizes costs without further compromising basic care.
