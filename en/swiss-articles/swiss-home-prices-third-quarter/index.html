The national figure

According to the transaction index published by Raiffeisen and reported by ATS, in the third quarter, single-family homes in Switzerland recorded an increase of 2,0% compared with the previous three months. The change concerns prices actually paid, not asking prices; during the same period, apartments rose by 1,7%.

The year-on-year comparison widens the gap between the two types: single-family homes recorded +3,6%, while apartments reached +5,9%. The source therefore measures two different timeframes: the quarterly movement and the year-on-year change. These are not asking prices, but actual prices.

Regional differences

The picture varies depending on the area. Ticino, Graubünden and Valais are considered together as Southern Switzerland. Here, apartments show annual growth of 7,4%, the highest figure in the country, while houses posted +3,3%. For single-family homes, the strongest annual increase was observed in Zurich, at +4,8%.

The type of location also affects how the index is interpreted. In tourist municipalities, the strongest increase for condominium properties is 7,9%. In urban centers, by contrast, the strongest increase concerns houses, at 5,2%. The two results describe different segments of the market and should not be conflated.

Fredy Hasenmaile, chief economist at the cooperative institution, points to the connection with financing. In the statement, he explains that the prospect of a possible end to the Swiss National Bank’s zero-interest-rate policy has caused fixed mortgage rates to rise in recent months. Hasenmaile adds that, although moderate, the rise in interest rates is not enough to alter price dynamics, since supply remains scarce.

Over the past five years, the purchase cost of a house in Switzerland has increased by 4,9% per year; for apartments, growth was 4,8%. For those following costo della vita in Svizzera, the index thus offers a distinct measure: the purchase price of owner-occupied homes.

Operational details

The quarterly figure and the annual figure answer two different questions. The former shows the movement observed over three months; the latter measures the position on an annual basis. Adding the five-year period introduces a third perspective, useful for avoiding confusion between a recent acceleration and the average trend indicated by Raiffeisen.

Five reading steps

1. Define the type. State immediately whether the data concerns a single-family home or an apartment. The two categories recorded different changes over the quarter and year on year, so they should not be merged into a single figure.

2. Choose the timeframe. Write whether you are looking at the three months, the year or the last five years. The same statistic changes meaning when it moves from the quarterly change to the annual growth indicated for the longer period.

3. Check the nature of the price. The index measures actual transaction prices, not asking prices. This distinction must be included in the profile before comparing one change with another.

4. Mark the area. If the reference is Southern Switzerland, note that it includes Ticino, Graubünden and Valais. For a comparison of houses, also include the Zurich figure when needed to verify the more pronounced progression.
