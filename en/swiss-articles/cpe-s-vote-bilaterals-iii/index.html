Bilateral III: Commission says yes to agreements (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 September 2026

The CPE-S gave a favourable opinion to Bilateral Agreements III with 9 votes in favour. The debate will reach the Council of States at the end of the month.

Context

In brief

  • The CPE-S recommends a favorable vote on the Third Bilateral Agreements
  • Decision expected from the Council of States by the end of the month
  • Debate on the adoption of rights and safeguard clauses

Key facts

  • What: Favorable recommendation for the Third Bilateral Agreements
  • When: End of the month during the autumn parliamentary session
  • Where: Council of States, Bern
  • Who: Foreign Policy Committee (CPE-S)
  • Vote outcome: 9 in favor, 3 against, one abstention

The Foreign Policy Committee of the Council of States (CPE-S) has officially stated its position on the Third Bilateral Agreements between Switzerland and the European Union. After a lengthy analysis process lasting several months, during which the technical and political details of the package of agreements were examined, the majority of the committee expressed a positive recommendation, inviting the upper house to vote in favor of integrating and stabilizing relations between our country and Brussels. The vote within the committee saw a clear consensus: 9 members voted in favor, 3 voted against, and there was one abstention.

Operational details

Key topics of the parliamentary debate

The debate that will take place at the end of the month among the members of the Council of States will focus on fundamental pillars that define the relationship between Switzerland and the European Union. Among the points of greatest attention is the dynamic reception of law, a mechanism that raises constitutional and political issues. At the same time, wage protection remains a topic of extreme relevance, especially with regard to maintaining worker protection standards in Swiss territory. Another central element, strongly requested by various political parties, is the inclusion of a specific safeguard clause. This measure aims to intervene in cases of excessive immigration, allowing for more controlled management of flows in situations of particular pressure on the labor market or national infrastructure. The discussion will not be limited exclusively to the content of the treaties but will also touch on the legal form that these must assume.

Key points

Approval procedure and next steps

The parliamentary process following the end-of-month session will be marked by well-defined stages. After the discussion and vote in the Council of States, the dossier will continue its institutional journey, which will require coordination with the National Council to reach a shared position. Meanwhile, institutions continue to work on the analysis of the final texts to ensure that each clause is in line with the Swiss legal framework. For citizens and workers, constant monitoring of official updates provided by Parliament remains the best tool to understand the developments of the dossier. It is advisable to regularly consult the institutional portals to verify the dates of the next discussions and any reports published by the parliamentary committees. The transparency of the Swiss legislative process allows anyone interested in national dynamics to follow public deliberations and understand how political choices will influence, in the long term, working conditions and social security services.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Foreign Policy Commission's position on Bilateral Agreements III?
The Council of States' Committee on Foreign Policy (CPE-S) has made a favourable recommendation regarding Bilateral Agreements III between Switzerland and the European Union. The internal vote in the committee recorded a clear consensus, with 9 members in favour, 3 against and one abstention, thus inviting the Upper House to proceed with the integration and stabilisation of relations between the country and Brussels.
When will the dossier be discussed in Parliament?
The parliamentary debate on Bilateral Agreements III is scheduled during the next autumn session of the Council of States, which will be held at the end of the current month. This step is considered crucial for defining the final negotiating mandate of the Federal Council and for addressing the complex issues related to national sovereignty and the management of migration flows.
What are the central themes of the discussion on the agreements?
The debate will focus on fundamental pillars such as the dynamic transposition of the law, wage protection to maintain worker protection standards in Switzerland and the possible inclusion of a safeguard clause to manage excessive immigration. In addition, a discussion is underway on the legal form of the referendum, considering whether to resort to the optional referendum or the mandatory one, which would directly involve the majority of the Cantons.
