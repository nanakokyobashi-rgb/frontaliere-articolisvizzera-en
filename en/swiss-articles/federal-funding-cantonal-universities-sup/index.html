A decision that confirms two profiles

On 25 September 2026, in Bern, the Federal Council approved the report drawn up in fulfillment of postulate 23.3841, "Fair and competitive financing in the higher education sector". The document examines the evolution of differentiated funding between cantonal universities and universities of applied sciences, with attention to access to competitive funds, possible disparities in direct and indirect federal funding and any recommendations.

The starting point is the law on the promotion and coordination of the Swiss university sector, indicated by the acronym LPSU. Today the Confederation finances 20 percent of the overall amount of the reference costs of the cantonal universities and 30 percent of that of the SUPs. From 1 January 2027, the percentages are indicated as 20 and 30 percent maximum respectively. The postulate asked to also examine access to the SNSF, Innosuisse and international programmes.

Different profiles, equal value

The evaluation carried out by BSS Volkswirtschaftliche Beratung confirms the differentiation. The cantonal universities are more focused on theoretical teaching and fundamental research. The SUPs, on the other hand, offer practice-oriented training and mostly carry out applied research activities. The federal system, according to the report, implements the principle of distinct profiles but of equal value.

Together with cantonal funding, the basic subsidies provided by the LPSU constitute a central element of the financial stability of universities. The distribution follows criteria linked to performance in teaching and research, with weightings appropriate to the missions of the two types of school. Competitive research also maintains the distinction: the cantonal universities benefit above all from the SNSF instruments, while Innosuisse has a comparatively more important role for the SUPs.

The report links differentiation to mission complementarity and strengthening research and innovation. According to the evaluation, this contributes to Switzerland's high-level international performance in the fields of education and research.

On 26 February 2026, the Higher Education Council (CSU) of the Swiss Conference of Higher Education (SCSU), the joint political body of the Confederation and the cantons in the coordination of higher education institutions, decided to establish monitoring. A set of key indicators will make it possible to regularly follow the evolution of global federal financing and their respective profiles, a decision which the Federal Council takes note of.
