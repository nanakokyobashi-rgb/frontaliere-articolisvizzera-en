The voice of dissent

For weeks, tech giants have been multiplying warnings about the risks of uncontrolled artificial intelligence development. But not everyone shares this view. Marcel Salathé, a Swiss scientist and professor at the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Lausanne, co-directs the AI center and has described some scenarios as exaggerated. In his view, these messages conceal economic interests and the desire to obtain tailor-made rules.

On the news, Salathé explained that part of the public genuinely fears that new technologies could take over. His question, however, concerns how quickly these fears made it onto the front pages of the Wall Street Journal and the New York Times. The professor said he did not understand why.

The alarm and its effects

Salathé also took issue with the language used by some researchers in the preceding weeks. He associates the expression “we will all die within ten years” with the way cults speak and considers it dangerous. The concern is that a prediction presented as certainty could drive someone to extreme acts against other people or themselves, including suicide, collective suicides and attacks against those developing these technologies.

The debate thus has two levels. On the one hand, there are warnings about possible extreme scenarios. On the other, there is suspicion that alarmism may function as a market strategy. The source points to companies nearing a stock market listing, with valuations in the trillions. According to Salathé, these companies fear competition from smaller companies, start-ups and China, identified as the leading power in artificial intelligence. In his view, fear would become a tool for obtaining protection.

In the picture presented by the report, Stefano Quintarelli and Marco Zaffalon instead identify social manipulation and concentration of power as real risks. Salathé does not deny every danger: he acknowledges malicious uses, such as military applications and cyberattacks, and notes that the same technology can be used for defense.

Three questions for reading the warnings

The first question is who is speaking. An alarm raised by tech giants and an assessment by Salathé do not represent the same perspective. The second is which risk is being described: a cyberattack or military use are concrete examples identified by the professor; the statement about everyone dying within ten years is instead an extreme prediction reported in the piece. The third is what countermeasure is proposed. Salathé argues that the same artificial intelligence can be used for defense.

Key points

An operational method for not amplifying the alarm

The source does not provide a deadline or an administrative procedure. The action available to the reader is to verify the content of the statements before turning them into personal conclusions. Five steps are enough, all drawn from the contrast presented in the report.

1. Isolate the sentence. Mark whether the message discusses uncontrolled development, a possible dominance of AI, or the formula about everyone dying within ten years. Do not treat these expressions as equivalent: the source presents them within a controversy over the tone of the alarms.

2. Attribute the voice. Write separately what the tech giants claim, what Marcel Salathé disputes, and what the report says about experts Stefano Quintarelli and Marco Zaffalon. Avoid the generic label of experts if it changes the substance of the claim.

3. Classify the risk. Put military uses and cyberattacks, identified by Salathé as possible malicious uses, in a first box. Place social manipulation and concentration of power in a second. The third box concerns extreme scenarios and the social consequences of alarmism.

4. Look for the countermeasure. Salathé does not describe AI only as a threat: he argues that the same technology can be used for defense. Comparing risk and defense prevents stopping at the most dramatic headline.

5. Separate facts from motivations. The future stock-market listing and competition from startups, smaller companies, and China are part of the explanation proposed by the professor. Present it as his interpretation, not as conclusive proof.

What not to do

There are no amounts to calculate, documents to prepare, or deadlines to meet in the text. Therefore, there is no need to invent an administrative response or attribute an immediate change to the news for those who live or work in Switzerland. The procedure serves to read the topic better and distinguish threat, market, power, and defense.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who is Marcel Salathé and what is his role?
Marcel Salathé is a Swiss scientist, a professor at the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Lausanne (EPFL) and co-director of the university’s center for artificial intelligence. He took part in the Swiss news bulletin to comment on the warnings about AI, emphasizing his academic and research position in the field of artificial intelligence.
Why does Salathé consider apocalyptic scenarios about AI exaggerated?
According to Salathé, scenarios predicting the destruction of humanity within ten years are exaggerated and would serve to mask the economic interests of big tech companies and the desire to obtain tailor-made rules. He interprets them as market alarmism aimed at protecting companies from competition from start-ups, smaller companies and China.
What concrete risks does Salathé recognize, and how does he suggest defending oneself?
Salathé acknowledges real risks such as military uses of artificial intelligence and cyberattacks, but says the same technology can be employed to defend against them. It calls for separating technical risks from market motivations and evaluating claims before drawing personal conclusions.
