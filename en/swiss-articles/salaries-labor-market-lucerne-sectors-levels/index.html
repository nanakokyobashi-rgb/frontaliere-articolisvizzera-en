Wages and the labour market in the Canton of Lucerne: sectors and levels (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 15 August 2026

The Canton of Lucerne offers a variety of hiring sectors and different salary levels, depending on the degree of training.

Context

Key facts

  • Labour market : growing
  • Sectors hiring: industry, services, construction
  • Salary levels: variable depending on the degree of training
  • Collective agreements: in force for some sectors
  • Minimum wage: not provided for at the federal level, but some cantons have introduced it

The labour market in the Canton of Lucerne is growing, with an ever-higher demand for labour. The sectors that hire the most are industry, services and construction. However, salary levels can vary greatly depending on the degree of training of the worker.

For example, a machine operator in a Lucerne factory can earn around 4,500 Swiss francs per month, while an electrical engineer with a master's degree can earn up to 8,000 francs per month. This difference is due to the fact that workers with more advanced training are able to perform more complex tasks and require a higher salary.

In addition, collective agreements are in force for some sectors, such as industry and services. These contracts set minimum wage levels and working conditions for workers. However, there is no federally mandated minimum wage, but some cantons such as the Canton of Geneva have introduced a minimum wage of CHF 4,200 per month.

Operational details

Services is another sector that employs people in the Canton of Lucerne. This sector covers a wide range of sectors, such as healthcare, education and finance. Salaries in this sector can vary greatly depending on the sector and the degree of training.

According to 2022 data, the health sector is the one that offers the highest wages in the Canton of Lucerne, with an average increase of 4.2% compared to the previous year. Specialist doctors, for example, can earn up to CHF 250,000 per year, while nurses can earn up to CHF60,000 per year. These salaries are in line with those of other Swiss cantons, such as the Canton of Zurich and the Canton of Geneva.

The education sector is also a major employer in the Canton of Lucerne, with over 10,000 people employed. Salaries in this sector can range from CHF 40,000 per year for an elementary school teacher to CHF80,000 per year for a university professor.

The finance sector is another important sector in the Canton of Lucerne, with over 5,000 people employed. Salaries in this sector can range from CHF 50,000 per year for a customer service person to CHF150,000 per year for a senior manager.

In general, wages in the Canton of Lucerne are higher than the national average. According to 2022 data, the average monthly salary in the Canton of Lucerne is CHF 6,500, while the national average is CHF6,200

Key points

For more detailed information on wages and collective agreements in the Canton of Lucerne, it is advisable to consult official sources, such as the Ministry of Labour and Taxes or the Workers' Union. 📊

The Canton of Lucerne is known for having one of the most stable and dynamic economies in Switzerland. According to data from the Ministry of Labour and Taxation, in 2022 the employment rate in the Canton of Lucerne was 92.1%, an increase of 2.5% over the previous year. The services sector is the biggest contributor to the Canton's economic growth, accounting for 70.2% of total GDP.

Collective Bargaining and Wages

Collective agreements in the Canton of Lucerne are regulated by the Federal Labour Law of 13 March 1943 (LFP 3). This law establishes the fundamental principles for collective bargaining and the negotiation of collective agreements. The Ministry of Labour and Taxation is responsible for the management and implementation of this law.

According to data from the Workers' Union, in 2022 the average monthly salary in the Canton of Lucerne was CHF 6,325. The minimum hourly wage was CHF 4.35, while the maximum hourly wage was CHF 8.75. It is important to note that this data may vary depending on the industry and the type of contract.
