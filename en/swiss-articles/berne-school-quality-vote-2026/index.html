Quality school in Bern: the grade is set for 2026 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 14 September 2026

The initiative to insert a constitutional mandate on the school will be submitted to the voters on September 27, 2026.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Popular initiative to include a mandate on school quality in the Bernese Constitution
  • Voting scheduled for 27 September 2026
  • Promoted by the Cantonal Teachers' Association, opposed by the government and bourgeois parties

Key facts

  • Initiative: Bern quality school
  • Voting date: 27 September 2026
  • Promoters: Cantonal Teachers' Association
  • Opponents: Cantonal Government, Majority Grand Council, UDC, PLR, Alleanza del Centro, UDF, Cantonal Economic Organizations
  • Main supporter: Bildung Bern (Director Stefan Widmer)

The context of the initiative

In the Canton of Bern, as in the rest of Switzerland, the school faces a shortage of qualified staff, large classes and an increase in administrative tasks that take time away from teaching. To respond to these difficulties, the Cantonal Teachers' Association has launched a popular initiative calling for the inclusion in the cantonal constitution of an explicit mandate in favour of school quality. According to Stefan Widmer, director of Bildung Bern, the aim of the initiative is to ensure sufficiently qualified staff and relieve them of bureaucratic tasks, so that they can focus more on teaching. The idea is to invest where the effect is greatest to promote the academic success of pupils and students.

Operational details

Analysis of possible consequences

If accepted, the initiative would introduce a constitutional obligation for the Canton of Bern to guarantee adequate resources for the quality of schools. This could translate into greater financial commitment from the canton, even though the source does not specify amounts or percentages. In practice, the mandate could lead to a revision of spending priorities, with a possible increase in funds allocated to teaching staff and a reduction in administrative duties.

On the contrary, if the initiative were rejected, the status quo would be maintained. The cantonal government and opposing political forces argue that current laws are sufficient and that improvements should come through operational measures, such as hiring new teachers or simplifying bureaucratic procedures. In this scenario, schools would continue to rely on existing funding mechanisms, without a constitutional constraint requiring the allocation of specific resources.

Another aspect to consider is the impact on personnel management. The stated objective of the promoters is to have more qualified teachers who are less burdened by bureaucratic tasks. If the mandate were accepted, the canton might be pushed to invest in continuing education and support services that free up time for teaching. This could have positive repercussions on teaching staff motivation and, potentially, on student outcomes.

Finally, the vote on September 27 represents a moment of direct participation by Bernese citizens in educational policy decisions. The result may influence not only the internal debate within the canton but also the comparison at the Swiss level on how to address common challenges of the school system, such as teacher shortages and the increase in administrative workload.

Key points

What to do ahead of the vote

If you live or work in the Canton of Bern and want to participate in the decision-making process, here are some concrete steps to take:

1. Find out the exact text of the popular initiative. The official document is published on the Bern State Chancellery website and contains the proposed constitutional mandate.

2. Follow the communications of the parties involved. On the portal of the cantonal government you can find the communiqués of the Council of State, while the promoting and opposing committees regularly publish updates on their sites and social networks.

3. Check the date of the vote: September 27, 2026. Register with the electoral register if you have not already done so and check your polling place indicated in the letter of call to the polls.

4. If you want to understand how any public spending decisions may affect your personal situation, you can use calculation tools available online. For example, to get an estimate of your net income after taxes, you can use the calculator offered by the site.

5. After collecting the information, go to the polling station on the day of the vote and express your yes or no preference on the initiative.

These steps allow you to exercise your right to vote in an informed manner and to contribute to the debate on the quality of the school in the Canton of Bern.

Frequently Asked Questions
When will we vote on the school quality initiative in Bern?
The popular vote is scheduled for September 27, 2026. Voters in the canton of Bern will be called to the polls to decide whether or not to include in the cantonal constitution an explicit mandate in favour of school quality, promoted by the cantonal teachers' association.
What is the main objective of the 'Bern Quality School' initiative?
The aim, according to Bildung director Bern Stefan Widmer, is to ensure a sufficiently qualified teaching staff and relieve them of bureaucratic duties. The idea is to invest where the effect is greatest to foster school success, addressing staffing shortages and large classes.
Who opposes the initiative and for what reasons?
The cantonal government, the majority of the Grand Council, UDC, PLR, Alleanza del Centro, UDF and the economic organisations support the 'no' vote. Nadja Günthör (UDC) says that quality is already guaranteed by current laws and that practical measures and direct support to schools are needed, not constitutional changes.
