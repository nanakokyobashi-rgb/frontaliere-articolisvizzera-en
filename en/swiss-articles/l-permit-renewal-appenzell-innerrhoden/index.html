Permit L: duration and extension in Switzerland (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 September 2026

The short-term L permit allows you to work for up to 1 year in Switzerland. Learn how extension works, employer constraints, and the move to B leave.

Context

In brief

  • Permit L valid for up to 1 year for temporary work
  • Tied to the employer, not automatically transferable
  • Extension requires new SEM assessment

Key facts

  • What: Short-term permit for non-EU/EFTA foreigners
  • Duration: Maximum 1 year from issuance
  • Where: Managed at the federal level by SEM throughout Switzerland
  • Who: State Secretariat for Migration (SEM)
  • Renewal: Subject to continuous employment and market needs assessment

In Switzerland, the short-term permit L is the main federal tool for the temporary employment of foreign workers from countries outside the European Union and the European Free Trade Association (EFTA). Managed by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM), it allows working in the country for a maximum period of one year.

The permit L is aimed at workers who obtain a job offer in Switzerland and for whom the employer demonstrates that the position cannot be filled by Swiss citizens, residents with a permit B, or EU/EFTA citizens. It is not restricted to a specific canton: although the issuance is managed by the SEM, the cantonal authorities (in the case of Appenzell Innerrhoden, the cantonal immigration office) provide administrative support.

Main features of the permit L

The permit L has three key elements. First: the maximum duration is one year from the date of issuance. Second: it is tied to the employer indicated in the original application — a change of employment requires a new application to the SEM. Third: the extension is not guaranteed and depends on a new assessment of Swiss employment needs and the availability of suitable candidates in the domestic market.

Operational details

Conditions for renewal

The extension of the L permit is not automatic and requires a precise procedure. The employer must submit a new application for employment to the SEM before the expiry of the current leave. The renewal is subject to three factors: continuity of employment with the same employer, demonstration that no suitable candidates are available among Swiss nationals, EU/EFTA or resident B permits, and verification of the economic situation in the relevant sector.

During the period of validity, the holder of the L permit remains subject to control by the SEM and the competent cantonal authority. Any change in employment, residential or administrative situation must be communicated promptly. An involuntary interruption of employment (dismissal, end of contract) results in the loss of the validity of the permit.

Transition from permit L to permit B

An L permit holder can apply for a B residence permit, which offers significant advantages over the L. Permit B is renewable and not tied to a single employer, ensuring greater stability and occupational freedom. The transition takes place at the joint request of the employer and evaluation of the SEM. There is no automatic entitlement; the granting depends on the continuity of the need for recruitment and the approval of the competent cantonal authority.

Key points

Practical procedure: how to request and renew the L permit

The initial request procedure follows four phases. Phase 1 — Request: the employer (not the worker directly) submits the request to the SEM with complete documentation related to the job offer, candidate qualifications, reason for the need to hire, and proof that the position has not been filled by suitable Swiss or EEA candidates.

Phase 2 — SEM Verification: the State Secretariat for Migration evaluates the request and coordinates with the Appenzell Innerrhoden cantonal immigration office. During this phase, the SEM ensures that the proposed salary is in line with market conditions and that the search procedure has been conducted correctly.

Phase 3 — Issuance: once approved, the L permit is issued (in paper or digital form depending on cantonal procedures) and communicates to the holder the exact duration (up to 12 months) and terms of validity.

Phase 4 — Administrative Registration: the holder must register with the Population Register of the Municipality of residence and request the foreign identity card (paper or digital L permit) at the competent cantonal office.

Critical deadlines and violations

The renewal request must be submitted BEFORE the expiration of the current permit — it is not possible to renew retroactively. After expiration, if the renewal has not been approved, the worker is no longer authorized to work in Switzerland and must leave the country. Continuing to work without a valid permit exposes the employer to administrative sanctions and the worker to repatriation procedures.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is the L permit valid throughout the Swiss territory or only in the canton where it was issued?
The L permit is valid throughout Switzerland, but is binding on the employer indicated in the application. It is not limited geographically to a specific canton; however, the holder must reside in the canton where the permit was issued (except for variations authorised by the SEM).
Can I renew my L permit if I change employers?
No. The L permit is linked to the specific employer indicated in the initial application. If you change jobs, the permit expires and the new employer must submit a new application for employment to the SEM. There is no automatic transferability between employers.
How long before the deadline do I need to request the extension?
The extension request must be submitted before the expiry date of the current permit. It is not possible to renew retroactively. It is recommended to start the procedure at least 2-3 months before the deadline to ensure sufficient time for the SEM to evaluate.
What are the advantages of switching from L permit to B permit?
Permit B (residence) is renewable and not tied to a single employer, offering greater freedom of employment. Allows changes of employment without having to apply for a new permit. It is the road to more lasting job stability in Switzerland.
If my L permit expires and the extension is not approved, do I have to leave Switzerland immediately?
Yes. If the extension is refused by the SEM, you will cease to be authorized to work in Switzerland upon the expiration of your current permit. You must leave the country within the deadline indicated in the official communications. Continuing to work without a valid permit exposes you to sanctions and repatriation procedures.
