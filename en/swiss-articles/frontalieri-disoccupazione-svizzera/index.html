Context

Switzerland does not pay unemployment to frontier workers. One billion francs under discussion The Federal Council has decided that Switzerland will not pay unemployment to border workers. This decision was taken recently and could have significant consequences for Switzerland and Italy. Switzerland employs around 412,000 border workers and Swiss unemployment insurance spending could range from a few hundred million francs to a billion per year. Switzerland is one of the countries with the highest number of border crossers, with a significant presence in sectors such as agriculture, industry and services. According to 2022 data, the cantons with the highest number of border crossers are Graubünden (93'171), Ticino (71'471), Valais (34'211) and Bern (24'141). The city of Lausanne, in particular, has a significant number of border workers employed in the tourism and catering sector. The decision of the Federal Council is based on the federal law of 14 December 2021, which states that unemployment of border workers is not a vested right, but is subject to revision. Switzerland has already previously reduced spending on unemployment insurance for frontier workers, from CHF 1.4 billion in 2015 to CHF 900 million in 2022. Key points

One billion francs under discussion

Switzerland is known for its stable economy and strict work policy. However, a little-known aspect of Swiss legislation is the lack of an unemployment system for frontier workers. Border workers are workers who arrive in Switzerland to work temporarily and then return to their country of origin. According to Frontier Ticino's salary calculator, Swiss border workers are not entitled to unemployment benefits.

This means that if a border worker loses their job, they cannot access unemployment benefits as is the case for Swiss workers. This situation could have important consequences for frontier workers, especially those who live in Switzerland for the long term and may not have access to alternative resources.

According to a source from the Federal Ministry of Economy, the total amount of one billion francs could be in question if border workers were entitled to unemployment benefits. This amount has been estimated considering the number of border workers working in Switzerland each year and the average time they spend in Switzerland.
