Context

On 23 December 2020, Switzerland and Italy signed the New Frontier Agreement, which entered into force on 1 January 2024. This agreement provides for the exchange of salary data between the two countries to improve the fiscal and social security management of border workers. For example, employers in the Canton of Ticino will have to communicate the wage data of border workers to the Italian tax authorities, which in turn will provide information on the income received in Italy by border workers working in Switzerland. Operational details

Practical implications The new legislation will have significant implications for border workers working in Switzerland and residing in Italy. The exchange of salary data will allow a more efficient management of taxes and social security contributions. For example, a border worker who works in Geneva and resides in Como will see the tax reporting procedure simplified thanks to the automatic sharing of salary data between the two countries. This will reduce the risk of errors or omissions that could result in penalties or refunds not due. ### Comparison with the previous situation Before the entry into force of the New Frontier Agreement, the exchange of information between Switzerland and Italy was limited. Now, with the new legislation coming into force on 1 January 2023, wage data will be regularly shared between the two countries, reducing the chances of errors or omissions in border workers' tax returns. Swiss cantons such as Ticino, which has a significant presence of border crossers, will be directly involved in the management of this data exchange. > "The new regulation represents an important step towards greater transparency and cooperation between Switzerland and Italy." Border workers will be able to verify the data shared through the official channels provided by the tax authorities of the respective countries.
