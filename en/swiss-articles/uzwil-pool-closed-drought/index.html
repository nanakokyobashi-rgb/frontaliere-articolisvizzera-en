Extreme drought: Uzwil municipal swimming pool closes (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 August 2026

Potable water shortages in eastern Switzerland impose restrictions: Uzwil closes the pool to save up to 150,000 litres per day.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Uzwil municipal pool closes early due to drought.
  • Estimated savings between 50 and 150 cubic meters of water per day.
  • The local network is under pressure to quadruple consumption.

Key facts

  • What: Early closure of the bathing facility
  • When: Sunday, August 16, 2026
  • Where: Municipality of Uzwil, Canton of St. Gallen
  • Who: Uzwil Municipality
  • Amount: Savings of up to 150,000 litres of drinking water per day

The extreme drought that is affecting eastern Switzerland has prompted the authorities of the Municipality of Uzwil, in the Canton of St. Gallen, to decree the early closure of the outdoor pool. The facility, which was originally supposed to remain operational until the end of August, will cease all activity from Sunday, August 16. This drastic decision was motivated by the urgent need to preserve local water reserves, currently put to the test by the adverse climatic conditions affecting the entire region.

According to the Municipality, the intervention will save between 50 and 150 cubic meters of drinking water daily. In volumetric terms, this equates to a saving of about 150,000 litres per day. Local authorities stressed that this amount of water is sufficient to meet the average daily needs of around 1,000 people. Operational details

Impact on regional water resources

The situation in Uzwil is an emblematic case of the management difficulties that Swiss municipalities must face in contexts of prolonged drought. Approximately two-thirds of the water consumed within the municipal territory is used inside homes, a figure that highlights how the behavior of individual residents is the determining factor for the stability of the water system. To mitigate the crisis, the authorities have issued explicit calls to the citizenry, requesting them to refrain from washing private vehicles, filling private swimming pools, and watering domestic lawns. In normal situations, these activities do not cause concern, but they become unsustainable when the local water network is under severe stress and cannot meet basic demand.

A comparison with the situation prior to the crisis shows a resource management approach based on constant availability which, due to the heatwave, has failed. The need to rely on the regional consortium indicates that the problem is no longer limited to a single municipality, but involves the entire regional distribution infrastructure. Future scenarios, should climatic conditions not improve, foresee the introduction of further restrictions, which could be applied more stringently to ensure the priority of drinking water and fire-fighting supplies. For residents, this means that the management of domestic utilities is no longer just a matter of costs, but of the actual availability of the resource. Procedures and recommendations for residents

In a situation of water stress, residents in Switzerland are called upon to cooperate actively to avoid the imposition of stricter restrictive measures. The Uzwil authorities, in line with practices in other cantons in times of drought, call for responsible use of the resource. For those living in Switzerland, it is advisable to monitor the official communications of your Municipality of residence through institutional sites, as ordinances can vary significantly depending on the water network and local availability. The standard procedure in the event of a water crisis usually involves refraining from non-essential activities, such as washing cars or irrigating gardens, to prevent water pressure from dropping below guard levels.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why does Uzwil pool close early?
The closure was decided by the City Hall due to the extreme drought that put pressure on the local water network. Water consumption in some neighborhoods has quadrupled during the heatwave, making it necessary to save between 50 and 150 cubic meters of drinking water per day to ensure the necessary supplies to the population and the firefighting network.
What are the recommendations for the citizens of Uzwil?
The authorities have asked the population to avoid activities that involve a high consumption of drinking water, specifically washing cars, filling private swimming pools and irrigating lawns. These measures are essential to avoid further restrictions if the climate situation does not improve.
What happens if the climate situation does not improve?
The Municipality of Uzwil warned that if the drought persists and conditions do not improve, further restrictions could be introduced. The priority remains to ensure the supply of drinking water and water for firefighting purposes, which are currently under stress.
