On October 15, 2026, the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (AFC) announced the exchange of information relating to financial accounts with 113 States. The operation falls under the global standard for the automatic exchange of information.

The group includes the 110 States already subject to the reporting obligation, joined this year by Georgia, Moldova and Ukraine. With 86 States, the exchange took place in both directions: Switzerland received and transmitted data. The other 27 sent information to our country without receiving any, as 14 are not yet considered to meet the international requirements for data confidentiality and security, while 13 voluntarily chose not to receive information. This year, too, no data was exchanged with Russia.

A network involving thousands of institutions

Approximately 9000 reporting financial institutions are registered with the AFC. The group includes banks, trusts, insurance companies and other types of institutions. These entities collected and transmitted data to the AFC. The AFC sent partner States information on approximately 4 million financial accounts and received similar information on approximately 4 million. The AFC points out, however, that it cannot provide information on the amount of financial assets.

The information exchanged includes the identification of the account holder, account data and financial information. The elements indicated include name, address, country of residence, tax identification number, reporting financial institution, account balance and capital income.

Since January 1, 2017, Switzerland has had the legal bases necessary to apply the standard. The data transmitted enables the cantonal tax authorities to verify whether taxpayers have correctly declared financial accounts held abroad.

The communiqué thus presents the scale of the exchange through States, institutions and accounts, while keeping separate the data on the volume of financial assets, which the AFC does not quantify.

Operational details

Practical significance for Swiss taxation

For anyone who files a tax return in Switzerland and holds accounts abroad, the operational point is not the overall number of accounts, but the comparison between foreign information and what was declared. The source indicates that cantonal tax authorities can verify the accuracy of the tax return for financial accounts held abroad. The verification therefore concerns the correspondence between the taxpayer and the information communicated through the AFC.

The practical check: three steps

The communication does not indicate a new deadline for the taxpayer, nor does it list any forms or documents to submit. The action that can be inferred from the source is therefore an orderly review of one’s tax return, limited to financial accounts held abroad and information that may be subject to exchange.

1. Define the information to check

The first step is to compare the categories indicated by the AFC: identification of the account holder, name, address, country of residence and tax identification number. These should be supplemented with the account details, the reporting financial institution, the balance and capital income. There is no need to turn the number of exchanged accounts into an estimate of the assets: the source specifies that the AFC cannot provide information on the extent of financial assets.

2. Compare with the tax return

For each financial account held abroad, the check to perform is the one indicated in the communication: verify whether the taxpayer declared it correctly. The review should be understood as a comparison between the information available to the taxpayer and the data that may arrive through the automatic exchange. When the comparison reveals a difference, the institutional reference indicated by the source is the cantonal tax authorities.
