Consultation opened by the Federal Department (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 September 2026

The Federal Council has proposed a series of legislative adjustments for the 2030+ agricultural policy.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Parliament calls for a message on PA30+ by the end of 2027.
  • Consultation open from 2 September to 8 December 2026.
  • Proposals to adapt agricultural laws and new regulations.
  • Parliamentary initiative 22,477 submitted for consultation.

Key facts

  • What: Consultation on agricultural policy 2030+.
  • When: From 2 September to 8 December 2026.
  • Where: Switzerland.
  • Who: Federal Council and the National Council's Economic and Tax Commission.
  • Amount: Not specified.

The Federal Council has proposed a series of legislative adjustments for the 2030+ agricultural policy. Among the planned amendments, there are updates to the Law on Agriculture of 29 April 1998, the Federal Law on Agricultural Rent of 4 October 1985, the Law on Climate Protection, Innovation and Energy Security Objectives of 30 September 2022, and the Law on Foodstuffs of 20 June 2014. In addition, a new federal law was proposed concerning the platform for the exchange of data in the agri-food chain. The consultation, open from 2 September to 8 December 2026, also includes the proposals of the Commission of the economy and taxes of the National Council (CET-N) relating to the parliamentary initiative 22.477 (Pasquier-Eichenberger) Michaud Gigon "For an effective observation of prices in the agri-food chains".

Operational details

Implications for Swiss agriculture

The consultation on the 2030+ agricultural policy represents a crucial step for Swiss agriculture. The proposed legislative adaptations aim to modernise and make agricultural practices more efficient, in line with climate protection and energy security objectives. The new law on the platform for data exchange in the agri-food chain could improve the transparency and traceability of agricultural products, benefiting both producers and consumers.

Comparison with the current situation

Currently, Swiss agriculture is regulated by laws dating back to 1998 and 1985. The proposed adaptations reflect the evolving needs of the sector and new environmental and technological challenges. The public consultation provides an opportunity to gather feedback from all stakeholders, ensuring that the new regulations are balanced and sustainable.

Future scenarios

If the proposals are approved, Swiss agriculture could see an increase in innovation and efficiency. The new law on the platform for the exchange of data could facilitate collaboration between the various actors in the agri-food chain, improving the quality of products and reducing waste. However, it will be crucial to monitor the impact of the new regulations on small producers and ensure that the measures taken are accessible and sustainable for all.

Key points

Procedure to participate in the consultation

To participate in the consultation, you can submit your contributions by December 8, 2026. Contributions can be sent via the Federal Council website or by regular mail. It is important that the contributions are well argued and based on concrete data, in order to positively influence the decision-making process. For example, contributions could include detailed analyses of the impact of the new regulations on specific agricultural sectors, such as cattle farming in the Canton of Ticino or apple cultivation in the Canton of Valais. It is advisable to use recent statistical data, such as 2025 agricultural yields or 2024 production costs, to support your arguments.

Useful tools

For further information on the proposed legislative adjustments and new regulations, please consult the website of the Federal Council and the Swiss Parliament. In addition, the CHF/EUR salary calculator and comparator can be useful to assess the impact of the new regulations on the income and daily lives of Swiss citizens. For example, the salary calculator can show how a farmer in the Canton of Zurich would see his net income change with the introduction of new farm taxes from 2027. The CHF/EUR comparator can help to understand the impact of currency fluctuations on fertilizer import costs.
