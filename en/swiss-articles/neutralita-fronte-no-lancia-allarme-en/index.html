Context

Neutrality: the No camp sounds the alarm

The cross-party committee formed by PLR, Centro, Liberal Greens, PS and Greens issued a clear warning: reject the neutrality initiative on 27 September. The initiative in question aims to include a definition of neutrality in the Constitution, limiting foreign policy and weakening Swiss defence.

The cross-party committee highlighted that the initiative could have disastrous effects on Switzerland's image abroad and undermine its defence capability. Neutrality is a core value for Switzerland, but the definition proposed by the initiative could limit the government's freedom of action too much.

Switzerland, known for its historical neutrality, has always sought to maintain an equidistant position in international affairs. Key points

The committee calls on the people and cantons to reject the initiative. The Federal Council, Parliament and the Interparty Committee opposed the initiative. It is important to consider the practical implications of this initiative and how it may affect Switzerland's security and foreign policy. Switzerland is a country known for its neutrality, a fundamental concept of its foreign policy. This principle was codified in the Swiss Constitution in 1848 and subsequently amended in 1874, with Article 54 stating that “Switzerland maintains its neutrality and does not side with any external power.” However, in these times of increasing geopolitical instability, Switzerland's neutrality is put to the test. The initiative in question, which provides for the amendment of Article 54 of the Constitution, could have significant consequences on the country's security and foreign policy. The inter-party committee, which collected over 100,000 signatures in support of the initiative, argues that amending Article 54 would be necessary to allow Switzerland to participate in international peacekeeping missions and to cooperate with international organisations. However, the Federal Council and Parliament argue that the initiative could compromise Switzerland's neutrality and put its security at risk.
