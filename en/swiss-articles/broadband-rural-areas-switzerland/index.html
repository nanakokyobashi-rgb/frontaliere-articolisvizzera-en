Broadband in rural areas: CHF 730 million (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 9 September 2026

The Federal Council approves a CHF 730 million programme for fast internet connections in rural areas. Involved cantons and municipalities from 2030 to 2037 with fibre optics and radio communication.

Context

In brief

  • The Federal Council is investing 730 million CHF for broadband in rural areas
  • LSBL program of 8 years (2030-2037) with equal cost-sharing by cantons
  • At least 1 gigabit/second download: the national minimum standard

Key facts

  • What: Federal broadband support program (LSBL) for rural residential and commercial buildings
  • When: Messages transmitted to Parliament; operational program 2030-2037
  • Where: Swiss rural areas (municipalities on a voluntary basis, with cantonal participation required)
  • Who: Federal Council, DATEC (Albert Rösti, head of department)
  • Amount: 730 million CHF total (365 million Confederation, 365 million Cantons)
  • Speed: at least 1 gigabit per second in download

The Federal Council has decided to bridge the infrastructure gap in Swiss rural areas through a federal broadband support program. An investment of 730 million francs will be dedicated to expanding networks, with a stated objective: to guarantee fast fixed internet connections to all residential and commercial buildings, even where private providers do not find economic convenience. Albert Rösti, head of the Federal Department of Communications (DATEC), announced the details.

The program, provisionally named Law on Broadband Support (LSBL), provides equally distributed financing: the Confederation covers 365 million francs through a commitment credit for the years 2030-2037, while the Cantons pay the same amount. The constraint is clear: no municipality can receive federal funds without the commitment to co-financing from the relevant canton.

Operational details

How the mechanism works: the role of the Cantons

The system of access to LSBL funds has a clear but binding structure. The first step is the cantonal decision: each Canton decides whether to join the program, participation remains voluntary. However, those who choose not to participate leave their municipalities without access to federal funding. It is a powerful incentive: to receive even a single federal franc, the Canton has no alternative but to co-finance to the established extent (50% of the total amount of the project).

This structure creates a clear procedural hierarchy: the Canton decides to join, undertakes to pay 50% of the total funding, the Municipality applies for access to funds, the Confederation disburses its 50% only if the Canton has entered the program. The practical consequence is that the Municipalities of non-adhering Cantons remain completely excluded. A binding cantonal decision therefore affects all the underlying administrative levels.

Design priority: fiber vs. radiocommunication

The choice of infrastructure is not neutral. Fiber optics remains the gold standard: superior speed, stability, prospects for future upgrades. But it's also expensive. The government has recognised this reality and inserted a flexibility clause: where the expansion of fibre entails very high costs, alternatives can be used via terrestrial radiocommunication.

Key points

How to Access Funding: Step by Step

If your municipality is located in a rural area and wishes to benefit from the LSBL program, the process is straightforward but requires coordination between administrative levels.

First step: verification of cantonal status. Contact the competent cantonal administration (Department or Office of Energy, Infrastructure or Telecommunications, depending on the canton) and ask if the Canton has joined the LSBL program. If the answer is negative, your municipality does not have access to federal funds. If positive, proceed to the next step.

Second step: territory evaluation. The program finances residential and commercial buildings in areas where private providers do not find economic viability. If your area is already covered by private operators with adequate speed (≥1 gigabit/s), the project may not be eligible. If fast connections are lacking, continue.

Third step: application submission. The municipality formally submits an application to the competent cantonal entity. The request must indicate: territory to be covered, target buildings, proposed infrastructure (fiber optic vs. radio communication), cost estimate and implementation timeline.

Fourth step: federal evaluation. The Confederation evaluates whether the project meets technical standards (minimum speed 1 gigabit/s, reasonable costs, territory effectively lacking bandwidth). If approved, the municipality receives the federal share (50%) and the Canton receives its share (50%).

Fifth step: implementation. The municipality or designated operator implements the infrastructure within the approved timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who can access LSBL funding?
Municipalities located in rural areas where private providers do not find economic convenience and where fast connections are currently lacking (below 1 gigabit/second). It is necessary for the Canton to voluntarily join the program and commit to co-financing 50% of the costs. Residential and commercial buildings with insufficient connections are a priority.
How much should Cantons and Municipalities invest?
The Confederation finances 50% of the costs (365 million total, 2030-2037). The Cantons pay the other 50%. Municipalities are not financially obligated at the federal level, but are completely dependent on cantonal decisions to join the program. Without cantonal membership, no funding is available.
What technology will be used: always fiber optic?
The priority is fiber optics. Where expansion costs are very high, the programme will also fund terrestrial radio connections. The minimum standard remains 1 gigabit/second in download for both technologies. The choice between the two will depend on the technical-economic evaluation of each project.
How long will the program be active?
The programme runs from 2030 to 2037 (8 years). The Federal Council may extend it only once for a maximum of 3 additional years (until 2040) if unused funds remain. After 2040, the program ends permanently. Municipalities must apply within these deadlines.
How does the Confederation finance the 365 million francs?
The funds come from taxes deriving from future mobile telephone concessions, not from the ordinary budget. This ensures a dedicated source with no direct impact on the rest of federal finance and ties funding to the telecommunications sector.
