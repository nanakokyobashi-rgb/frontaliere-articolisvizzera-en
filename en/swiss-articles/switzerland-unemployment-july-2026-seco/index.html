Unemployment in Switzerland at 3%: SECO DATA (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 August 2026

In July, unemployment in Switzerland rose to 3%, with 139,300 URC members. Ticino recorded 2.8%, while Graubünden remained stable at 1.1%.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Swiss unemployment rate at 3% in July, up 0.1%.
  • In Ticino the rate rises to 2.8% (+0.2%), while in Graubünden it remains unchanged at 1.1%.
  • 139,300 URC members, 1,500 more than in June.

Key facts

  • What: Economic increase in unemployment in Switzerland

  • Who: SECO (Secretariat of State for the Economy), URC and ATS

In July, unemployment returned to show a slight growth in the Swiss territory. According to information released by the ATS agency and officially confirmed by data published by the State Secretariat for the Economy (SECO), the share of jobless nationwide stood at 3%, registering a progression of 0.1% compared to 2.9% measured in June. This change brings the indicator back to the same value recorded in May and April. On an annual basis, the overall growth is 0.3 percentage points.

The national figures collected by SECO and ATS

The data provided by SECO indicate that at the end of the seventh month of the year, people without a job registered with the Regional Employment Offices (URC) rose to 139,300 (figure expressed in absolute value

Operational details

Territorial analysis and impact on the labor market

The evolution of the Swiss labor market in July presents marked differences depending on the cantonal context. The surveys compiled by SECO clearly show how regional employment dynamics respond unevenly to seasonal factors and the general economic situation.

In Canton Ticino, the unemployment rate recorded an increase of 0.2% during July, bringing the local indicator to 2.8%. On an annual basis, growth for the Ticino region stands at 0.3%. In absolute terms, in the region between Airolo and Chiasso there are currently 4,600 registered unemployed individuals, an increase of 200 units compared to June and 400 units compared to the same period of the previous year. This places Ticino in twelfth place in the ranking of Swiss cantons most affected by the phenomenon.

In Canton Graubünden, the picture remains significantly more stable. The unemployment rate stood at 1.1%, remaining completely unchanged in both monthly and annual comparisons. In absolute figures, 1,200 unemployed people are registered in Graubünden, a figure that places the canton in fifth place among cantons with the lowest rates in the entire Confederation.

At the two extremes of the national ranking we find Canton Geneva, which confirms itself as the region with the greatest employment difficulties with a rate of 5.1%, and Canton Appenzell Innerrhoden, which takes first place as the most virtuous territory with a value of just 0.6%.

Key points

Operating procedures and requirements in case of unemployment

The increase in unemployment data highlights the importance of thoroughly understanding the administrative steps required by Swiss authorities to protect one's income and contribution status in the event of job loss.

Procedure and requirements in case of unemployment

When an employment relationship ends on Swiss territory, timeliness in completing bureaucratic requirements is crucial to guarantee access to the benefits provided by the unemployment insurance law (ALV/LADI).

1. Immediate registration with the URC: The worker must register with the competent Regional Employment Offices (URC) as soon as possible, ideally during the notice period and no later than the first day of actual unemployment. 2. Required documentation: You must present a valid identity document, the AVS/AHV insurance certificate, the letter of termination or contract cancellation, the employment contract, and the last 12 payslips to determine the insured earnings. 3. Proof of job searches: It is mandatory to demonstrate active and documentable job searches starting during the notice period. The absence of concrete evidence may result in suspension days of the right to benefits. 4. Choice of unemployment fund: During registration, the worker must indicate the unemployment fund (public or trade union) responsible for verifying formal eligibility and making monthly benefit payments.

📊 Operational note: Unemployment insurance benefits typically cover 70% or 80% of insured earnings (depending on the presence of dependent children or the amount of the previous salary). It is advisable to regularly check the accuracy of the calculations on your Swiss payslip.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the unemployment rate in Switzerland in July and how has it varied?
In July, the unemployment rate in Switzerland stood at 3%, an increase of 0.1% compared to 2.9% in June. On an annual basis, there is an increase of 0.3 percentage points, with 139,300 unemployed registered in the URC.
What are the specific unemployment figures in Ticino and Graubünden?
In Ticino, the unemployment rate rose to 2.8% (+0.2% on a monthly basis and +0.3% on an annual basis), equal to 4,600 people without work. In Graubünden, the rate remained stable at 1.1% both month-on-month and year-on-year, with 1,200 unemployed.
What is the difference between the unemployment data provided by SECO and those of the ILO?
SECO only takes over persons registered with the URC and does not include those who have exhausted their rights to compensation or live on assistance. The ILO (International Labour Office) measures unemployment through sample surveys, indicating a rate of 5.2% for the first quarter.
