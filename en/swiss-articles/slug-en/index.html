Key points

Cooling down Swiss cities? “The work has just begun”

Swiss cities are testing concrete measures to reduce the temperature. The city of Zurich, for example, is testing sponge neighborhoods, with rainwater collections and permeable soils. This initiative aims to reduce rainwater absorption and improve wastewater efficiency.

According to a report by the University of Zurich, sponge districts can reduce the absorption of rainwater by up to 70%. This can help prevent flooding and reduce pressure on water infrastructure. The city of Zurich has already invested 10 million Swiss francs (CHF) in the construction of an experimental sponge district.

Not only Zurich, but other Swiss cities are also following suit. For example, the city of Bern has launched a project to create a 10-hectare sponge district. The project involves the installation of a rainwater collection system and a permeable soil. The city of Geneva, on the other hand, announced the creation of a 5-hectare sponge district, with an investment of CHF 5 million.

Switzerland is already a leader in sponge neighbourhood technology. The city of Lugano has developed a system of sponge neighbourhoods that can absorb up to 90% of rainwater. The city of Lausanne, on the other hand, has created a sponge district that can reduce the absorption of rainwater by up to 85%.
