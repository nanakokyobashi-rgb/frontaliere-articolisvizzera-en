The Federal Council has sent Parliament a request for a commitment appropriation of CHF 15.8 million to implement a new alarm system called "cell broadcast". This system allows you to send short text messages directly to mobile phones in an affected area in the event of an alarm or urgent alert.

Unlike sirens, which reach an audience limited to urban areas, cell broadcasting offers widespread coverage of the national territory through the infrastructure network of telecommunications providers. The new channel complements and reinforces the civil protection devices already in operation: traditional sirens, radio releases subject to the broadcasting obligation, as well as the Alertswiss website and app, which have been active since 2018.

Operational details

# How cell broadcasting works nationwide

Cell broadcasting represents a significant evolution in the federal civil protection strategy. Unlike alarm sirens, which require geographical proximity and direct visibility to function effectively, the cell broadcast sends short, automatic text messages directly to the mobile phones of those in the affected area at that specific time.

This approach does not require the receiver to have a special subscription, to have installed an app, to have activated a notification service or to be listening to a radio. The system operates at the national telephone network infrastructure level: when a federal or cantonal authority authorizes the sending of an alarm, the text message automatically reaches all phones connected to the telephone cells in the affected area, regardless of the network provider chosen.

Multi-channel integration: redundancy and resilience

The strategic importance of cell broadcasting lies not in the replacement of existing systems, but in their integration. The sirens remain operational for immediate visual and auditory alarm in urban and peri-urban areas. Radio announcements subject to the broadcasting obligation continue to provide detailed and contextual information on an ongoing alarm. Key points

The request for credit from the Federal Parliament is the first concrete step towards the operation of cell broadcasting in Switzerland. The schedule indicated in the federal documentation provides for full implementation between 2027 and 2035, with a progressive setting up of the infrastructure at national telephone operators. The exact timing will depend on the completion of the parliamentary credit approval procedure and the technical time required for the construction of the infrastructure at the suppliers.

What to do and what to expect

Residents and workers in Switzerland will not have to take any explicit action to benefit from cell broadcasting. Unlike other alarm services that require manual registration or activation, the broadcast cell will work automatically by activating the mobile phones of those in the affected area at the time of the alert. You will not need to download additional apps, subscribe to specific subscriptions, or check security settings: the message will arrive on all devices connected to the affected phone cells, regardless of the mobile network provider and the tariff plan chosen.

Frequently Asked Questions
Will cell broadcast replace sirens and the Alertswiss app?
No. Cell broadcasting integrates and strengthens existing systems without replacing them. The sirens remain operational for immediate visual and auditory alarm in urban areas. The Alertswiss app, active since 2018, continues to provide a proactive search channel. This multi-channel approach ensures redundancy: if one system does not work, the others remain active to ensure that the population receives the alarm.
How much will cell broadcast cost and who funds it?
The Federal Council has requested a commitment credit of CHF 15.8 million to be included in the 2026 federal budget. The total costs for the preparation and management from 2027 to 2035 amount to 67.7 million, of which 51.9 million do not require additional commitment credit. The development is federally funded and does not constitute an additional tax for Swiss residents.
Do I need to subscribe or activate something to receive cell broadcast alarms?
No. Cell broadcasting is an automatic system at the network infrastructure level. It does not require registration, specific subscription or manual activation. It will automatically work on all mobile phones in the affected area, regardless of the network provider chosen (Swisscom, Sunrise, salt or others).
When will I be able to receive SMS alerts on my mobile phone?
Implementation is expected between 2027 and 2035. Once the Federal Parliament approves the commitment credit, the telephone operators will start the development of the infrastructure compatible with the cell broadcast standard. The exact timing will depend on technical progress with national suppliers.
Has Parliament already approved this commitment appropriation?
No. The Federal Council forwarded the request for CHF 15.8 million to the Federal Parliament for approval. During the consultation on the multi-channel civil protection strategy, the Cantons and Parliament expressed support for a rapid implementation of cell broadcast technology.
