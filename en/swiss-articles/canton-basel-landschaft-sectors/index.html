Labour market Basel Countryside: wages and sectors (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 20 August 2026

Practical guide to wages, hours, rights and social security in the Swiss labour market. Discover AVS contributions, LPP, cantonal taxation and how to evaluate an offer.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Maximum working hours 45 hours/week (industry, offices, sales) or 50 hours in other sectors
  • Absent federal minimum wage; some cantons provide for this through laws or collective agreements
  • AVS/AI contributions: 5.3% employee, 10.6% total (with employer)
  • Federal entitlements: 4 weeks vacation, 14 weeks maternity, 2 weeks paternity

Key facts

  • What: Structure of the Swiss labour market, wages, entitlements and social security
  • When: Rules in force in 2026 for the entire Swiss territory
  • Where: Canton Basel-Country (northwestern Switzerland), subject to federal regulations and cantonal rules
  • Who: Employees (Swiss citizens and foreigners with work permits)
  • Rights: Minimum holiday 4 weeks, maternity 14 weeks at 80%, paternity 2 weeks

The Swiss labour market is based on uniform federal protection and cantonal integrations that allow the cantons to define some specific standards. In the canton of Basel-Country, as in the other cantons, the federal regulatory framework (working hours, holiday rights, unemployment protection) coexists with cantonal rules on taxation, supplementary pension and minimum wage where provided.

Working hours and basic rights

Switzerland sets a maximum time of 45 hours per week for industry, offices and sales, while in other sectors (agriculture, construction) the maximum is 50 hours. These federal limits apply uniformly throughout the territory.

Operational details

Taxation and social contributions on salary

Each Swiss salary is withheld for three main categories of contributions and taxes:

1. Compulsory social contributions (federal): AVS (old-age pension) and AI (disability) together represent 5.3% borne by the employee; the employer pays an additional 5.3% (total 10.6%). The same item includes the IPG (Compensation for loss of earnings, which covers maternity and paternity). Unemployment insurance (AD/AC): 1.1% up to an annual ceiling.

2. Accident insurance (LAINF/LAA): from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector, often paid in full by the employer.

3. LPP — Occupational pension (second pillar): at least 7% of the coordinated salary from 25 to 34 years, up to 18% from 55 years. The LPP is mandatory in Switzerland starting from CHF 21'330 of annual salary (indexed data every year). An important exception: freelancers and freelancers do not have a federal LPP obligation, but can join voluntarily.

4. Direct federal, cantonal and municipal tax: An employee in Switzerland pays three levels of income tax. The federal tax uses uniform progressive rates throughout Switzerland. Each canton sets its own rates and tables (Basel Country has its own tables). The municipality applies a multiplier to the cantonal tax base, creating variability at the local level as well. The combination of these three levels determines the overall effective tax rate.

Key points

Practical Procedure: How to Evaluate a Job Offer and Verify Rights

When you receive a job offer in Switzerland, check these elements:

1. Working Hours: Verify the declared working hours in the contract (usually 45 hours/week for industry, offices, sales, or 50 hours for other sectors). If your sector has a specific CCL, the working hours may be lower (e.g., 42-43 hours). Always ask for a copy of the applicable CCL for your role.

2. Gross Salary: Clearly ask if the proposed amount is gross or already net. In Switzerland, the salary indicated in a job offer is always gross. The net amount you will receive will be reduced for AVS/AI contributions (5.3%), unemployment (1.1%), LPP (7-18% depending on age and coordinated salary), federal, cantonal, and communal taxes, and LAMal (separate payment).

3. Sickness Fund: Ask if the company has agreements with specific funds or if there is a free choice. Remember that you must conclude the LAMal within 3 months of hiring; the 2026 premiums for Basilea Campagna must be requested directly from the funds (the company does not pay them, unless there are additional company agreements).

4. Old-Age Provision (LPP): If the salary exceeds CHF 21,330 per year, LPP is mandatory. The company has a collective LPP fund; you will receive a fund regulation and a forecast of the accumulated capital at the age of retirement. Read the essential points: contribution rates by age range, guaranteed interest rates, management fees.

5. Termination: The contract must specify the probation period (if any) and the terms of termination thereafter. Federal law: 1 month in the first year, 2 months from the second to the ninth year, 3 months from the tenth year onwards. Some CCLs allow 1 month always.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a federal minimum wage in Switzerland?
No, Switzerland does not have a mandatory federal minimum wage. However, some cantons — such as Basel Campagna in certain sectors — provide for own minimum wages through cantonal laws or collective bargaining agreements (CCLs). LCAs are agreements between trade unions and employers' associations that set minimum wages for entire sectors. Check with the trade union or employers' association in your sector if there is a CCL.
How much is withheld from my gross salary for AVS and other contributions?
AVS/AI: 5.3% to be paid by the employee (the employer pays an additional 5.3%). Unemployment: 1.1% up to an annual ceiling. LAINF (accidents): 0.7–1.5% depending on the sector, often borne by the employer. LPP (occupational pension): 7% from 25–34 years old, up to 18% from 55 years old, calculated on a part of the salary called 'coordinated'. You also pay federal, cantonal and municipal income tax. The total net depends on cantonal taxation.
How does health insurance (LAMal) work in Switzerland?
The LAMal is mandatory for every resident in Switzerland and must be stipulated within 3 months of arrival. It is private (not public), at per capita premiums (you pay directly to the cashier, it is not deducted from your salary). Premiums vary by canton, age, gender and deductible model (CHF 300–2500). Many cantons offer subsidies to low-income premiums. Choose a cashier from those authorized in your canton.
How do you calculate the net from a gross salary?
From the gross salary subtract: AVS/AI 5.3%, unemployment 1.1%, LPP (7–18% according to age), federal tax, cantonal tax, municipal tax. LAMal is not a salary deduction, but a separate payment at the cash desk. Use the federal [calcolatore stipendio](nav:calculator) to enter your annual gross, canton, age and family situation: get an accurate estimate of the net and all contributions.
What minimum rights do I have as an employee in Switzerland?
Guaranteed federal rights: maximum 45 hours/sept (industry/offices/sales) or 50 hours other sectors; minimum 4 weeks of annual leave (5 under 20); 14 weeks maternity at 80%, 2 weeks paternity. Cancellation by the employer: 1 month in the first year, 2 months from the 2nd-9th year, 3 months from the 10th year. Some CCLs offer greater protections. Read your contract and check your industry CCL.
