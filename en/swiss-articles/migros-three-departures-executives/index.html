Migros on Tuesday announced three simultaneous changes at the top of three of its divisions. Katrin Tschannen leaves the leadership of Migros Online at the end of March 2027, concluding six years at the helm of the group's online shop. The decision was communicated as a voluntary choice, with the CEO intending to take on a new professional challenge. Migros is currently looking for a succession solution.

Thomas Gubler, who has been driving Delica for three years, will retire at the end of the year. The changing of the guard will take place at the beginning of 2027 with the arrival of Moritz Werner. Gubler has a long history in the Migros group, with fortyyears of overall experience in the company. Operational details

Generational transition underway for the retail giant

The departure of Gubler, after four decades at Migros, represents a moment of generational transition for the group. The decision to carry on the succession as early as early 2027, a few months after the announcement, suggests a planned continuity rather than a sudden crisis. Moritz Werner has been identified as the right profile to support Delica in the next cycle of its strategic evolution.

Tschannen's decision to leave Migros Online after six years, however, comes as the e-commerce segment continues to represent a competitive priority for the Swiss retail sector. A change of leadership in this sector is not automatically negative: it could signal both a completed consolidation phase and a search for new strategic dynamics. However, the fact that the position remains vacant until the end of March 2027 creates an operational vacuum that will need to be managed during the next critical months for online trading.

Replacement patterns and organizational implications

The succession of exits from senior positions raises questions about the organizational health of the group and its ability to retain high-profile talent. Whether they are agreed choices to achieve new professional challenges or signs of management in transition, multiple departures indicate an internal reset moment for Migros. Key points

The labour market at the top of Swiss companies

For the Swiss labour market, Migros' top-level moves have moderate but concrete implications. When executives of large companies leave their positions, the group initiates searches for successors. This means that the already competitive Swiss C-suite talent market will see further movement in the coming months. Those who work in retail, food processing, or Swiss financial services could find opportunities deriving from these readjustments, both internally at Migros and at competitors interested in attracting profiles expelled from the spare part.

Strategic perspectives and organizational continuity

The rapid designation of Moritz Werner as Gubler's successor, and the research already underway for the Tschannen and Schawalder positions, suggest that the group wants to avoid prolonged decision-making gaps. This is standard practice in well-structured companies, but the simultaneous timing of the three announcements and their coordinated communication demonstrate conscious and planned transition management.

For Swiss consumers and shareholders, the lesson is that Migros continues to function as an evolved body, capable of generating management turnarounds without immediate crises. Frequently Asked Questions
When do they leave Migros executives announced on Tuesday?
Katrin Tschannen leaves Migros Online at the end of March 2027, after six years. Thomas Gubler will retire at the end of 2026, after three years as CEO of Delica and a total of 40 years at Migros. Markus Schawalder, who has been responsible for private clients at Migros Bank for five years, will leave to face a new professional challenge, with a successor in the research phase.
Who will replace Thomas Gubler at the helm of Delica?
Moritz Werner will replace him at the beginning of 2027. Delica produces the Migros group's own brands such as Chocolat Frey, Farmer, Blévita and Delizio coffee. The transition has been planned to ensure managerial and strategic continuity of the division during the next cycles of commercial evolution.
What is the reason for the three contemporary departures?
According to spokesperson Tristan Cerf, there is no link between the three announcements, despite their simultaneous communication. The decisions would be the result of individual choices: Tschannen and Schawalder seek new professional challenges, while Gubler reaches retirement age after a long career in the group.
What other departures has Migros had in the previous months?
Denner's boss Torsten Friedrich left in April, followed shortly after by Christian Staub, Denner's sales manager. In the middle of the year he also left Martin Wechsler, IT manager of the group. These transitions reflect a wider organizational turnover cycle in the group in recent months.
What are the implications for the Swiss labour market?
Migros' leadership moves prompt searches for executive talent in Swiss retail, e-commerce and financial services. These exchanges create professional mobility opportunities for qualified candidates in the sectors connected to the group, both within Migros and at competitors interested in attracting experience profiles from these transitions.
