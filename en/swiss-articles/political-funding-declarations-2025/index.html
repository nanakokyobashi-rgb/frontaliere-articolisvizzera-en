2025 parties: 24.6 million, -1 million from 2024 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 September 2026

In 2025, Swiss parties declare 24.6 million francs of revenue, a decrease of about one million compared to 2024. The SP leads with 7.9 million francs.

Context

In brief

  • 24.6 million: total funding declared by the 9 parties in 2025
  • Decrease of ~1 million compared to 2024 (they were 25.6 million)
  • PS leads with 7.9 million, FDP second with 4.8 million

Key Facts

  • What: Annual Declaration of Funding of Swiss Federal Political Parties
  • Who: Swiss Federal Audit Office (SFAO)
  • When: 2025 (data published by the SFAO)
  • Where: Switzerland (9 of 10 parties in the Federal Assembly)
  • Total amount: CHF 24.6 million
  • Change: -1 million compared to 2024 (3.9% decrease)

The Swiss Federal Audit Office (SFAO) has published data on the revenue reported by political parties for 2025. Nine of the ten parties represented in the Federal Assembly recorded total revenues of CHF 24.6 million, around one million less than in the previous year, when total revenues amounted to CHF 25.6 million. The declarations are made on the basis of the rules on the transparency of the financing of Swiss politics.

The Socialist Party (SP) remains the first in terms of fundraising capacity, with revenues of CHF 7.9 million. It is followed by the Radical Liberal Party (FDP) with CHF 4.8 million, the Swiss People's Party (SVP) with CHF 3.8 million and the Centre Party with CHF 2.7 million. These four parties have representatives in the Federal Council, Switzerland's executive chamber.

Among the four government parties, the SP and the FDP recorded a decrease in revenue compared to 2024, by CHF 0.3 million and CHF 1.8 million, respectively. The People's Party and the Centre, on the other hand, recorded an increase in revenue, by CHF 1 million and CHF 0.1 million, respectively.

Operational details

The overall decline of around one million francs compared to 2024 highlights a downward trend in fundraising in the Swiss political landscape. This figure, despite its apparent numerical modesty (a decline of 3.9% year-on-year), reflects deeper dynamics in Swiss politics and the ability of parties to attract resources from private donors and supporters.

Internal redistribution

Looking at the internal movements between the main government parties, a mixed picture emerges. While the SP and FDP suffered repercussions in 2025 (-0.3 and -1.8 million respectively), the SVP compensated with a growth of 1 million and the Centre maintained stability with a minimal increase of 0.1 million. The largest relative loss belongs to the FDP, which recorded a 27% drop from its 2024 revenue, suggesting a possible rearrangement of the donor base between centre-right forces.

At the same time, the overall decline did not evenly affect all parties. The Greens maintained a substantial collection (1.8 million), while the liberal Greens remain at 1.2 million. This segmentation of the party landscape shows that the volatility of funding is uneven, with some parties maintaining a stable donor base and others suffering from greater variability.

Transparency and democratic accountability

The annual declarations published by the SFAO are a pillar of Swiss democratic transparency. They allow citizens to understand how the parties finance themselves and, indirectly, what the economic influence of the various stakeholders is in the Swiss political landscape. The fact that one of the ten parties (Lega dei Ticinese) has not yet provided data for 2025 underlines the importance of systematic monitoring of this information and compliance with federal transparency regulations.

Key points

Where to find the data and how to access it

Data on party-reported funding is publicly available thanks to transparency regulations on political financing. The Swiss Federal Audit Office (SFAO) publishes this information annually, allowing anyone to verify the revenues declared by each party. Accessing this data does not require complex procedures: it is sufficient to consult the SFAO's annual reports, available through the official website of the Swiss federal administration.

For citizens interested in understanding the Swiss political landscape and the fundraising capacity of the various parties, this data represents an essential tool. Knowing the funding sources and declared amounts helps form an informed opinion on any economic influences within the national political system.

Future scenarios and monitoring

The decline recorded in 2025 could be temporary or signal a structural shift in donor generosity toward the Swiss political system. In the coming years, it will be interesting to observe whether the downward trend continues or stabilizes. The annual publication of data by the SFAO ensures that every citizen can follow this trend, contributing to an informed public debate on the country's economic-political dynamics.

Swiss national politics, financed by private revenues, directly influences the economic well-being of residents. From decisions on the cost of living to taxes and the labour market, everything traces back to the political priorities set by the parties and their funders.

To stay up to date on these developments and understand how federal political decisions affect your personal tax wallet, consult the official administrative transparency platforms.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the transparency criteria for party funding in Switzerland?
In Switzerland, the transparency of policy funding is governed by federal rules that oblige parties represented in the Federal Assembly to declare their income annually. The Federal Financial Control (FFC) collects and publishes this data, allowing citizens to know the financial resources that feed the national political system. The goal is to ensure accountability and democratic transparency.
Why did the PLR fall by 1.8 million in 2025?
The CDF source does not specify the reasons behind the decline in the PLR from 2024 to 2025 (-1.8 million). Reasons could include changes in the donor base, one-time donations in 2024, or changes in fundraising strategies. For specific details on the causes, official statements of the party itself should be consulted.
Has the Ticino League declared its funding for 2025?
No. According to data published by the CDF, the Ticinese League, the only one of the ten parties represented in the Federal Assembly, has not yet disclosed its funding for 2025. The failure to declare remains a gap in the transparency landscape, even if it is still monitored by the federal authorities.
How do I access Swiss party funding data?
The data is publicly available through the Federal Financial Control (FFC), which publishes annual reports on the revenues declared by federal political parties. You can consult these reports on the official website of the Swiss Federal Administration. No special credentials are required; access is free and open to all interested citizens.
