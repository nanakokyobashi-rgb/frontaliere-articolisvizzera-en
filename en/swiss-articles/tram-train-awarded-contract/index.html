Context

The Bosciorina Consortium has won the contract for the construction of the rear walls between Bioggio and Manno. The contract was awarded for an amount of CHF 8.6 million.

The construction of the rear walls is an important project for road safety in the Canton of Ticino. According to data from the Ticino Department of Transport, 1,456 road accidents were recorded in the canton in 2020, an increase of 10% over the previous year.

The project involves the construction of 3.2-kilometre-long outer walls, covering a total area of 40,000 square metres. The Bosciorina Consortium submitted a total bid of CHF 8.6 million, which was considered the most advantageous compared to the other proposals submitted.

The construction of the back walls is an ambitious project that requires careful management and detailed planning. According to data from the Ticino Department of Transport, the average construction cost of one square metre of backside wall is CHF 150. Therefore, the total cost of the project amounts to CHF 6 million.

The Bosciorina Consortium stated that it is ready to start work within the next few weeks and to plan the completion of the project within the next 12 months. The project will be carried out in collaboration with the Ticino Department of Transport and with the support of experts in the sector.

The intervention involves the demolition of two existing retaining walls and the construction of new reinforced concrete works. The preparatory work is expected to start in October and will serve to widen via Cantonale to make room for future tracks. This project is part of a larger public transport development plan in Switzerland, which involves the implementation of a tram-train system connecting the cities of Zurich and Bern.

The implementation of this project was possible thanks to the collaboration between the cantonal and municipal authorities. The Canton of Bern has allocated 4.2 million francs for the realization of the works, while the city of Bern has contributed 2.2 million francs. The remaining CHF 2.2 million was financed by the Swiss State.

Track construction work is scheduled to begin in September 2024 and is expected to be completed by June 2026. The line will be served by 12 electric trains, which will be able to carry up to 800 passengers per hour.
