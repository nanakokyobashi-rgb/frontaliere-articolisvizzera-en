Inheritance tax in Valais: rates and procedures (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 6 September 2026

Succession in Valais is subject to cantonal taxes calculated by degree of kinship. Spouse and descendants have specific exemptions. Here's how it works and what procedures to follow.

Context

In brief

  • Inheritance in Valais is subject to cantonal tax
  • Rates vary depending on the degree of kinship
  • Spouse and descendants have specific exemptions
  • Mandatory declaration to be submitted to the tax administration

Key facts

  • What: Cantonal tax on inheritances and donations
  • Where: Canton of Valais
  • Who: Heirs, legatees, Valais tax administration
  • Degree of kinship: Determines the applicable rate
  • Legal basis: Valais cantonal law on inheritances

In Valais, as in all Swiss cantons, inheritance is subject to cantonal taxes. Unlike the federal direct tax, managed nationally by the FTA/ESTV, the inheritance tax remains the competence of the cantonal administrations. This means that the rates, exemptions, and declaration deadlines vary significantly from canton to canton. Valais applies its own tax structure that depends on the degree of kinship between the deceased and the heirs, and has legislated independently from other cantons such as Zurich, Bern, or Ticino.

What are the degrees of kinship considered

The taxation of inheritance in Valais follows a well-established standard Swiss classification: the spouse (i.e., the marital partner of the deceased), direct descendants (children and grandchildren in a straight line), ascendants (parents and grandparents), collaterals in various lines (brothers, sisters, cousins), and finally strangers (people with no kinship to the deceased). Each category represents a different degree of kinship and pays a different tax. Spouses and direct descendants usually enjoy more favorable rates, reflecting the intent to favor the transfer of wealth among the closest relatives. Strangers, on the other hand, face significantly higher impositions, sometimes well above the rate applied to spouses. This progressive structure reflects the cantonal policy of protecting the family nucleus and encouraging the intergenerational transfer of wealth within the family.

Exemptions for spouse and descendants

A particularly important feature of Valais taxation concerns the specific exemptions reserved for the spouse and direct descendants. These beneficiaries receive significant deductions on the taxable value of the inheritance, drastically reducing the overall amount subject to tax. In some cases, the exemptions are so generous that the surviving spouse might not pay any inheritance tax. However, the precise details of these exemptions (specific amounts, conditions, exceptions, wealth thresholds) require direct consultation and the completion of the inheritance declaration with the Valais Tax Administration, as they vary according to the current cantonal legislation and may change over time with tax reforms.

Operational details

How is taxation structured

In Valais, inheritance tax is calculated by applying percentage rates to the net value of the inherited estate. The calculation takes into account multiple factors: the degree of kinship of the heir, the portion of the estate received by each heir, the deductions, and the exemptions applicable to each heir based on cantonal regulations. Unlike some other cantons that apply a progressive system (increasing rates beyond certain wealth thresholds), others use fixed rates for each category of kinship. Valais follows its own methodology, which requires careful calculation and often involves the use of a professional to avoid errors.

Those who receive a lifetime gift are also subject to cantonal gift taxes in Valais, although with slightly different declaration terms and procedures compared to inheritance upon death. Gifts can be accumulated over time for certain types of kinship, and this accumulation directly affects the rate applied in the future. For example, a parent who has already given significant sums to their heirs has limits within which further gifts are not additionally taxed, but exceeding that threshold changes the tax regime. This interaction between lifetime gifts and inheritance is a critical aspect of estate planning in Valais.

Practical Scenarios Without Invented Numbers

To understand the practical impact: a spouse who inherits 100% of the deceased's estate will face a significantly lower tax burden compared to a grandchild who receives the same share; a stranger (a person with no kinship to the deceased) will pay an even more substantial tax. These differences are not marginal but represent real structural differences in tax burdens. These differentials reflect the canton's deliberate policy to protect the family nucleus. Those who receive small bequests from strangers may find themselves having to pay amounts that significantly reduce the net value received as an inheritance. For this reason, many wills in Valais are drafted with attention to tax burdens, sometimes with the support of specialized consultants.

Consulting the tax calculator can help obtain a preliminary estimate of the tax burden, although Valais always requires verification with the local tax authority for definitive figures. A first DIY calculation can serve as a rough check, but it never replaces the official declaration.

Lifetime Gifts vs. Inheritance

Gifts made during one's lifetime in Valais are subject to specific regulations, often different from those of inheritance upon death. Some cantons tax gifts with significantly lower rates compared to inheritance, while others apply accumulation deadlines (periods within which gifts are accumulated). Valais has its own criteria, and anticipating a lifetime gift can sometimes reduce the overall tax burden compared to leaving the assets as an inheritance at the time of death. This tax planning strategy is legal and frequently practiced but requires specialized tax advice to avoid unwanted effects or regulatory misunderstandings.

Key points

Inheritance Declaration Procedure

In Valais, the inheritance declaration must be submitted within the deadlines strictly set by the cantonal tax administration. Normally, the heirs or designated legal representatives (such as the legal guardian, the executor named in the will, or the estate administrator) have the legal obligation to report the death to the administration and provide a complete and detailed inventory of the inherited assets. The required documentation generally includes the holographic or notarized will, death certificates issued by the deceased's municipality of residence, an ordered and complete list of inherited assets and liabilities, bank statements, titles, real estate properties, and valid identification documents of all involved heirs.

Where to Go

The Tax Administration of the Canton of Valais (officially Direction des contributions in French, or equivalent in German Kantonales Steueramt Wallis, depending on the linguistic district of residence) is the competent authority and the official interlocutor. You can contact them to request the official inheritance declaration forms, receive clarifications on specific rates applicable to your case, and verify the possible existence of particular benefits. Many cantons, including Valais, provide online guides, information sheets, and public counters with regular hours. Some heirs turn to local notaries, tax experts, specialized lawyers, or certified tax consultants to manage the entire procedure and minimize significant risks of administrative errors that could result in penalties.

Deadlines and Penalties

The exact declaration deadlines vary based on the time of death, specific circumstances, and the heir's status. Missing the deadline or providing incomplete declarations can directly lead to administrative penalties applied by the tax authority, sometimes accompanied by late interest calculated on the unpaid tax debt. For this critical reason, it is essential to verify with the cantonal authority what the exact deadlines applicable to your specific case are and what documentation is required in detail. Deadlines are usually communicated through official notices directly to the registered heirs, but it is prudent and advisable to proactively inquire at the tax office to avoid regulatory surprises.

Next Steps

If you are an heir to an estate in Valais, the first practical step is to carefully collect all relevant documentation (will, death certificates, bank statements, complete list of assets and debts) and promptly contact the Valais Tax Administration through the official channels indicated on the cantonal website. You can request a copy of the official model declaration and receive precise indications on specific rates and amounts potentially exempt for your relationship. Subsequently, fill out the declaration with extreme care (or with professional support if the estate is complex) and submit it within the peremptory deadlines. Use the tax calculator as a preliminary verification tool, but always rely on the cantonal authority for the final and binding figure.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main categories of kinship taxed differently in Valais?
Valais applies different rates for spouses, descendants, ascendants, collaterals and strangers. Spouses and direct descendants usually benefit from lower impositions; strangers face higher rates. Each category is entitled to specific deductions according to the cantonal law of Valais. For precise details on the current rates, please consult the Valais Tax Administration.
Does a spouse always pay inheritance tax on the partner in Valais?
The spouse benefits from specific exemptions in Valais that significantly reduce or eliminate the tax burden on the direct succession of the partner. However, the exact conditions (exempt amounts, Community scheme vs segregation) vary according to cantonal legislation. It is important to check with your local tax authority in the specific case of your situation.
How long does it take to declare a succession in Valais?
The declaration of succession must be submitted within the deadlines set by the Valais Tax Administration, which communicates to the heirs through official notifications. Terms vary depending on the time of death and circumstances. It is prudent to contact the cantonal tax authority for confirmation of the terms applicable to your specific case.
How do I calculate the tax on a donation in Valais?
Donations in Valais are taxed according to specific rates that depend on the degree of kinship between donor and donor. Valais applies accrual deadlines (add previous donations to calculate the rate). Unlike succession, the terms of declaration are often different. Consult the Valais Tax Administration for the current calculation methods.
Should I contact a notary or a tax advisor for the succession in Valais?
It is not always mandatory, but it is advisable if the succession is complex or the estate is significant. A notary can authenticate the will and guide the proceedings; a tax advisor can minimise the tax burden. For simple successions, you can independently manage the declaration with the forms of the Valletta tax administration.
