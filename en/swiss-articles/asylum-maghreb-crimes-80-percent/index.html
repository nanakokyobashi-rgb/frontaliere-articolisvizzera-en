Asylum seekers from the Maghreb, 80% are involved in crimes (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 22 August 2026

80.5% of applicants from the Maghreb were charged with crimes. The SEM accelerates procedures and makes agreements with Morocco for faster repatriations.

Context

In a nutshell

  • 80.5% of applicants from the Maghreb accused of crimes; 12.3% for other countries
  • Confederation introduced 24-hour accelerated procedure in 2024
  • In 2025, 96% of people in the asylum system were not charged
  • About 99% of applications from the Maghreb are rejected

Key facts

  • What: 80.5% of applicants from the Maghreb accused of one or more crimes during the asylum procedure
  • When: 2024 (SEM analysis); accelerated procedure introduced 2024
  • Where: Swiss Confederation, Federal Asylum Centres
  • Who: State Secretariat for Migration (SEM)
  • Amount/Percentages: 80.5% (Maghreb), 12.3% (other countries), 96% (not charged 2025), 99% (rejected applications)

In 2024, the State Secretariat for Migration conducted an internal analysis on the data of asylum seekers, obtained by Blick according to the Transparency Act. The numbers are staggering: 80.5% of applicants from Algeria, Morocco and Tunisia were charged with one or more crimes during the asylum procedure or after receiving a negative decision. A hugely higher percentage than applicants from other countries, where only 12.3% were involved in crime.

Operational details

How fast-track changes the process

The 24-hour expedited procedure represents a significant change from the historical processing times of appeals. Instead of proceeding with the normal administrative procedures that could last weeks or months, the Confederation now aims to identify, prosecute and decide in a single day. This acceleration, however, encounters concrete resistance: applicants who refuse to provide biometric data make rapid identification impossible and force the administration to manage incomplete document archives.

The impact at the national level is considerable. Each canton administers its own asylum centres and complies with federal SEM guidelines. The enhanced cooperation requested by Bern means that cantonal asylum offices will have to better coordinate return procedures, speeding up administrative expulsion times. Some cantons may need to increase the resources dedicated to this task, while others may benefit from a reduction in management burdens.

Key points

Monitor federal policy on migration

The data published by SEM is public and accessible through the Federal Transparency Act. Anyone interested in following the evolution of Swiss asylum policy can consult the official press releases of SEM on the website www.sem.admin.ch. The State Secretariat for Migration regularly publishes statistics and reports on the state of the asylum system, including information on accelerated procedures and repatriations.

In addition, the strengthened collaboration between cantons and Confederation means that news on procedural changes and administrative accelerations are communicated through official cantonal channels. Anyone operating in the field of social assistance, integration, or public security at the cantonal level may need to monitor these federal decisions to adapt their operations and internal administrative procedures.

Track changes in repatriation procedures

From 2024, the 24-hour accelerated procedure has come into effect for applicants from the Maghreb. The response times of federal authorities have been significantly reduced compared to the past. If a request is rejected during the accelerated procedure, repatriation can take place in very short times. This means that the time window for appeals or challenges is extremely limited.

For those working in legal consulting, social mediation, or assistance for applicants, the period 2024-2026 represents an important procedural adaptation moment. Deadlines are now measured in hours, not days. The risk of administrative errors increases proportionally to the speed of the process.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the percentage of applicants from the Maghreb accused of crimes?
According to 2024 SEM data, 80.5% of applicants from Algeria, Morocco and Tunisia were charged with one or more crimes during the asylum procedure or after a negative decision. By comparison, only 12.3% of applicants from other countries were involved in crime. SEM spokesman Daniel Bach said that "the vast majority behave correctly", and in 2025 96% of people in the asylum system were not charged with any crime.
What procedure did the Confederation introduce in 2024?
In 2024, the Confederation introduced a 24-hour accelerated procedure for applicants from Algeria, Morocco and Tunisia. The goal is to identify them quickly and deal without delay with questions that have no real chance of success. However, some applicants attempt to obstruct it by refusing to hand over their fingerprints or show up for appointments.
What is the rate of rejection of applications from the Maghreb?
About 99% of asylum applications from the Maghreb are rejected. This means that the vast majority of applicants are destined to leave Switzerland, which explains why the Confederation has decided to speed up return procedures and reduce the time spent in the Swiss administrative system.
What agreement has recently been concluded with Morocco?
The Confederation recently concluded an agreement with Morocco to speed up the identification of persons to be repatriated. The agreement is part of the federal strategy to speed up expulsions and ensure faster removal of those who do not have the right to stay in Switzerland.
What does the Confederation intend to do to further accelerate the expulsions?
Bern intends to strengthen collaboration with the Cantons, assess a tightening of administrative detention, and continue cooperation with the Maghreb countries. The aim is to prevent people without real prospects of protection from burdening the Swiss asylum system and to ensure a faster removal of those who commit crimes.
