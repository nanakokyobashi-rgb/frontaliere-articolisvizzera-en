Operational details

Mandatory Contributions and Insurance Obligations

A worker's salary in the Canton of Solothurn consists of a gross part (the contractual salary) and a series of mandatory deductions. These reflect federal, cantonal contributions and insurance obligations established by Swiss laws.

First Pillar: AVS/AI/IPG

The Old Age and Survivors Insurance (AVS), Disability Insurance (AI), and Maternity/Paternity Loss of Earnings Indemnity (IPG) constitute the first pillar of Swiss pension provision. The dependent worker pays 5.3% of the gross salary; the employer pays 10.6% in total (with its own share of 5.3%). These contributions finance public pensions: from 64 years for women (65 for men), the worker receives a monthly AVS pension based on contributions paid and years of work.

Unemployment

Unemployment contributions (AD — Unemployment Insurance; AC — Unemployment Insurance for the Worker's Fault) total 1.1% up to a yearly maximum, divided between the dependent and the employer. These payments ensure a daily allowance if the worker loses their job involuntarily.

Accidents at Work Insurance

The Work Accidents Insurance (LAINF) is paid by the employer to an insurance company (SUVA or recognized private insurers) and varies from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the activity sector. The employee does not contribute directly; the employer includes the cost in the operational budget.

Frequently Asked Questions
Does the Canton of Solothurn have its own cantonal minimum wage?
There is no single federal minimum wage in Switzerland. Some Swiss cantons have their own minimum wages for specific sectors (trade, construction, health, cleaning) often set through collective labour agreements (CLAs) signed between employers' associations and trade unions. The situation varies significantly by canton. To check whether the canton of Solothurn has a cantonal or sectoral minimum wage, it is advisable to contact the cantonal Labour Office directly or consult the cantonal ordinance
What are the monthly social security contributions deducted from the paycheck?
Mandatory contributions deducted from gross salary include: AVS/AI/IPG (5.3% for employee), AD/AC Unemployment (1.1% up to annual ceiling), and LPP on coordinated salary (percentage variable with age: 7–18%). Direct federal, cantonal and municipal tax is deducted at cantonal rates. LAMal (health insurance) is a private policy paid directly to the sick fund, not through a pay slip. The employer also pays its share of AVS/AI (5.3%), unemployment (1.1%) and LAINF (0.7-1.5% according to sector).
How long do I have to take out health insurance (LAMal) after the first day of work?
Every resident of Switzerland must take out health insurance within 3 months of arrival or the start of the employment relationship. If you do not insure yourself within this period, the sick pay fund covers the costs retroactively by applying an administrative penalty. LAMal is not a salary contribution, but a private policy with variable premiums by canton, age group and annual deductible (CHF 300-2,500). The Canton of Solothurn offers subsidies for low-income residents.
What are the days of holidays and leave guaranteed by Swiss federal law?
Federal law guarantees a minimum of 4 weeks of annual leave (20 working days), or 5 weeks if the worker is under 20 years of age. In addition to holidays, the law provides for: maternity (14 weeks at 80% of salary via loss of earnings allowance — IPG) and paternity (2 weeks from 2021). Many collective bargaining agreements offer additional holidays and more generous forms of leave. Tax deductions are made at three levels: federal (IFD), cantonal and municipal (variable rates per municipality in the Canton of Solothurn). The amount depends on gross income, marital status, number of dependent children, real estate, and donations.
