The congress and the decisions

Thun (BE) hosted the PS’s two-day congress, which concluded with official support for three popular initiatives. On the second day, the Socialists put forward proposals concerning transport, pollination and the responsibility of digital platforms.

The previous day, the congress had reached its climax with the launch of the Initiative for the protection of women against violence. The final day, by contrast, focused on the three initiatives supported by the party. The first concerns the mobility voucher: the aim is to promote Swiss public transport and rail links with neighboring countries.

The second is the bee initiative. It calls on the Confederation and cantons to ensure the pollination of cultivated and wild plants by bees, making the necessary resources available. The third concerns the internet and seeks to require platforms, including search engines and artificial-intelligence developers, to assess the risks associated with the services they offer and adopt measures to contain them.

Federal Councillor Elisabeth Baume-Schneider also addressed the delegates. She spoke about financing the 13th AVS/AHV pension and warned that, without an additional contribution, the AVS would already record a negative result this year, triggering a downward spiral. To finance the expenditure, the increase in VAT would mean an additional burden of around 15 francs a month for an average household.

Baume-Schneider, from Jura, praised the PS’s strength for the community and its willingness to change power relations. She also described the heatwaves and persistent drought as worrying, identifying them as symptoms of climate change.

The congress also approved the policy document “Decent work for everyone!”, calling for shorter working hours, higher wages and greater participation in decision-making. The delegates, however, rejected the bill aimed at abolishing cantonal and municipal minimum wages. The referendum against the bill was launched by the PS together with the trade unions and other organizations. Co-president Cédric Wermuth described it as:

Cédric Wermuth described the project as a threat to workers’ purchasing power and an affront to democracy and federalism.

Finally, the party supported the referendum aimed at opposing the free-trade agreement with the Mercosur countries. Mattea Meyer, co-president, lamented the lack of effective measures against deforestation and forced labor.
