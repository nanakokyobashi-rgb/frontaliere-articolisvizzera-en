Block of rebates, tensions with Ticino (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 July 2026

The blockade of refreshments by the Canton of Ticino in response to the Italian health tax has generated controversy and tensions.

Context

In brief - The Canton of Ticino has blocked cross-border workers' rebates in response to the Italian health tax. - Jonny Crosio, Deputy Federal Secretary of the Pact for the North, criticizes the decision as arbitrary and paradoxical. - The Ticino measure is seen as a reprisal that damages the territories and cross-border workers. - What: Blocking of cross-border workers' rebates. - When: In response to the Italian health tax. - Where: Canton of Ticino. - Who: Ticino Council of State. - Amount: Not specified. The blocking of rebates, as expected, is generating reactions and controversy. Among these there are those who take it out as much on Lombardi as on the Canton of Ticino. "Today, the Lombardy Region and the Canton of Ticino share one more thing: an embarrassing political improvisation. Unfortunately, I have to note with regret that our friends from Ticino have managed to do even worse than the Lombardy Region." This is how Jonny Crosio, Deputy Federal Secretary of the Pact for the North party and former Senator of the Republic, intervenes, commenting on the decision of the Ticino Council of State to proceed with a "precautionary" freeze on cross-border workers' rebates in response to the hypothesis of a health tax envisaged by Italy. ### Implications of the Decision The decision of the Ticino Council of State to block cross-border workers' rebates is seen as an arbitrary and paradoxical act. Jonny Crosio points out that, while the Lombardy Region has not yet applied the health tax, the Canton of Ticino has carried out a concrete act that violates agreements already signed. This has led Bellinzona to find itself on the wrong side, violating international agreements. ### Political Contradiction Crosio emphasizes the glaring political contradiction behind the Canton's maneuver. Ticino's response is paradoxical: to counter an Italian measure that, if it ever comes into force, would reduce the competitive advantage of old cross-border commuters, the Ticino government blocks rebates. This means that they are protesting against a tax that could even favor Ticino resident workers, who have been denouncing wage pressure for years. A real logical and political short circuit. ### Cross-border relations The bitter observation remains that cross-border relations cannot be managed with spitefulness and reprisals. Our border municipalities, which rely on rebates to provide services to citizens, and the cross-border workers themselves, cannot be used as hostages or as bargaining chips in a race to see who can shoot the biggest. It is imperative that we immediately return to the tracks of international law and common sense: the politics of announcements and spitefulness damages only and exclusively the territories.

Operational details

Analysis of the Implications The blocking of rebates by the Canton of Ticino has significant implications for cross-border workers and border municipalities. Rebates are a vital source of funding for public services, and blocking them could lead to cuts in essential services. In addition, cross-border workers are in a situation of uncertainty, with the possibility of seeing their salaries reduced due to the Italian health tax and the blocking of rebates. ### Comparison with the Previous Situation Before the rebate freeze, cross-border workers could count on a stable financing system for public services. With the lockdown, this stability is put at risk, and border municipalities must find new sources of funding to maintain essential services. This could lead to an increase in local taxes or cuts in public services. ### Possible Scenarios If the Italian health tax comes into force, cross-border workers could see a reduction in their competitive advantage. However, the blocking of rebates by the Canton of Ticino could have an even greater impact, leading to a reduction in their salaries and greater economic uncertainty. It is important that the Ticino and Italian authorities find a solution that protects the interests of cross-border workers and border municipalities.

Key points

Actions to be taken Cross-border workers and border municipalities must be informed of the implications of the rebate freeze and possible solutions. It is important that the Ticino and Italian authorities work together to find a solution that protects the interests of all those involved. Cross-border workers must be informed of their options and rights, and border municipalities must be supported in finding new sources of funding for public services. ### Step-by-Step Procedure 1. Get informed: Cross-border workers must inform themselves about the implications of the freeze on rebates and the Italian health tax. 2. Consult a lawyer: Cross-border workers should consult with a lawyer to understand their rights and options. 3. Contact the authorities: Cross-border workers must contact the Ticino and Italian authorities to express their concerns and ask for a solution. 4. Seek Support: Border municipalities need to seek support to find new sources of funding for public services. ### Useful Tools For further information and support, cross-border workers can use the calcolatore stipendio and comparatore CHF/EUR available on the Frontaliere Ticino website.
