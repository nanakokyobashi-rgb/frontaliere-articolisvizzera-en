How inheritance and gift tax works in the Canton of Schwyz: rates for degree of kinship, exemptions and deadlines for declaration.

Context

At a glance

  • Inheritance and gift taxes are CANTONAL in Switzerland, not federal
  • In the Canton of Schwyz, tax rates vary according to the beneficiary's degree of kinship
  • Spouses and direct descendants benefit from exemptions or reduced rates
  • The inheritance declaration must comply with specific deadlines set by cantonal law

Key facts

  • What: Cantonal inheritance and gift taxes, graduated by kinship class
  • Where: Canton of Schwyz, in Central Switzerland
  • Who: Heirs, donors, Schwyz cantonal tax administration
  • When: Upon transfer of assets via inheritance or gift, with declaration within cantonal deadlines
  • Legal basis: Schwyz cantonal laws and ordinances (not federal)
  • Exemptions: Surviving spouse and direct descendants benefit from a privileged regime

In Switzerland, inheritance and gift taxes are not regulated at the federal level but remain the EXCLUSIVE competence of the cantons. In the Canton of Schwyz, as in the other twenty-six Swiss cantons, the tax structure for successions follows its own rules defined by specific cantonal laws.

The rate system in the Canton of Schwyz varies significantly based on the degree of kinship between the deceased (or donor) and the beneficiary. Spouses and direct descendants — children, grandchildren — receive preferential tax treatment, often with total exemptions or significantly reduced rates, while more distant relatives and unrelated persons are subject to higher progressive rates.

Operational details

Practical implications for those inheriting in the Canton of Schwyz

For a person inheriting assets from a relative in the Canton of Schwyz, the importance of the local tax scheme is immediate. The final amount of the available inheritance depends significantly on the degree of kinship with the deceased and the tax rates applied according to current cantonal regulations.

Kinship classes and graduated rates

The Swiss inheritance tax system universally uses the criterion of 'degree of kinship' as the central element to calculate the applicable rate. In the Canton of Schwyz, as in other cantons, kinship classes are organized according to standard schemes:

Class I — Spouse and direct descendants: includes the surviving spouse, children, and grandchildren. It benefits from the lowest rates or total exemptions, reflecting the intent to protect the family unit.

Class II — Ascendants and collaterals: includes parents, brothers, sisters, and their descendants. Subject to intermediate rates, significantly higher than Class I.

Class III — Other relatives and non-relatives: includes distant relatives and unrelated persons. Subject to progressively higher rates.

Each class has an associated specific rate: Class I benefits from substantial reductions or total exemptions, Class II from intermediate rates, and Class III from much higher rates. Furthermore, each class generally has a tax-free allowance (an untaxed amount), below which no tax is paid.

Exemptions for spouse and descendants

In the Canton of Schwyz, as in most Swiss cantons, the surviving spouse almost always benefits from a complete or near-total exemption from inheritance tax. This legislative choice reflects the intent to protect the family unit and ensure the surviving spouse the peaceful enjoyment of the common assets.

Key points

Operational procedure: how to declare an inheritance in the Canton of Schwyz

In the Canton of Schwyz, as in all Swiss cantons, the succession procedure follows well-defined and administratively binding steps.

Step 1 — Declaring the inheritance to the competent cantonal offices

When a succession opens (upon the death of the owner), the heirs have a legal obligation to declare the inherited assets to the cantonal tax administration of Schwyz within a specific deadline. The exact terms vary according to the applicable cantonal law and are communicated by the municipality of residence of the deceased. The declaration must include a complete and detailed list of assets (real estate, bank accounts, securities, insurance policies, other valuables), accompanied by ownership certificates and estimated valuations, as well as documentation of the degree of kinship between the deceased and the beneficiaries.

Step 2 — Tax calculation according to cantonal rates

Once the declaration is received, the cantonal tax administration proceeds to calculate the applicable tax, systematically considering: the beneficiary's kinship class, the total value of the inheritance, the exemptions and allowances provided by cantonal law, the progressively applied rates according to the statutory brackets, as well as any tax credits or deductions.

Frequently Asked Questions
In Canton Svitto does the spouse pay inheritance tax on the inheritance received?
In Canton Svitto, as in most Swiss cantons, the surviving spouse is generally totally exempt from inheritance tax on all property inherited from the deceased spouse. This exemption reflects the protection of the family unit and the surviving spouse's right to community property. However, in order to confirm the exact status and any specific exceptions to the cantonal law in force, it is advisable to check directly with the cantonal tax administration.
What rate applies to children in case of inheritance in the Canton of Schwyz?
Children are in Class I (direct descendants) and benefit from the lowest rates or exemptions in the Canton of Schwyz. Generally, up to a pre-established deductible (specific amount of the cantonal law), they do not pay any tax; beyond this amount, a much lower rate applies compared to the most distant kinship classes (class II and III). The exact amount of the deductible and the applicable rate depends on the cantonal law in force and can be verified at the financial administration of Svitto.
How does inheritance tax differ from gift tax?
Both successions and donations are transfers of assets, but the succession takes place at the death of the owner, while the donation intervenes during the life of the donor. In Canton Svitto, as in other Swiss cantons, the rates, deductibles and review deadlines may differ significantly between the two cases. Donations may also be subject to cantonal review if other donations occur within certain periods, allowing the canton to recalculate the overall donor tax.
What documents are needed to declare an inheritance in the Canton of Schwyz?
To declare an inheritance in the Canton of Schwyz you need: the will (if available) or documentation of legal succession, death and civil status certificates of all heirs and the deceased, documents of ownership of property (real estate deeds, bank statements, insurance certificates), and official documentation of the degree of kinship.
