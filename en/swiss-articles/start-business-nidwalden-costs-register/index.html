Anyone wishing to start a business in the Canton of Nidwalden must navigate a multi-step process that begins with choosing a legal form and continues through registration in the commercial register, opening the necessary bank accounts, and fulfilling tax and insurance obligations. In Switzerland, there is no one-size-fits-all approach: the choice of legal form determines the minimum capital requirement, personal liability, accounting requirements, and recurring costs.

Legal Forms Available in Switzerland

The legal forms permitted by the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO) range from a sole proprietorship—which does not require a minimum paid-in capital but exposes the owner to unlimited personal liability—to a public limited company (SA), including the limited liability company (Sagl). The Sagl requires a minimum capital of CHF 20,000, fully paid in, while the SA requires a minimum capital of CHF 100,000, of which at least CHF 50,000 must be paid in at the time of incorporation. There are also general partnerships, limited partnerships, and cooperatives, each with its own rules regarding capital and liability.

Registration with the Commercial Register

Registration with the Commercial Register is mandatory for corporations (Sagl, SA, general partnerships, limited partnerships, and cooperatives) and optional but recommended for sole proprietorships that exceed certain revenue thresholds. The procedure is handled by the cantonal commercial register office, which in Nidwalden is administered by the canton. The application for registration requires the articles of incorporation authenticated by a notary, the bylaws, the declaration of incorporation, and details of the capital contributions. Registration fees are set at the cantonal level and vary depending on the capital and the complexity of the application.

Start-up Costs

Initial costs include notary fees for the authentication of the articles of incorporation, cantonal fees for registration with the commercial register, and fees for any tax or legal consulting services. Notary fees depend on the capital and the complexity of the articles of association, while registration fees are calculated based on the paid-in capital or, for sole proprietorships, on a fixed cantonal rate. The minimum balance to be deposited into the company’s bank account prior to registration must be documented with a bank confirmation.

Employer Insurance Obligations

A new entrepreneur who hires staff becomes an employer and must enroll in the federal social security programs. AVS/AI/IPG contributions amount to 10.6% of the salary, split 5.3% each between the employee and the employer. AD/AC contributions amount to 1.1% of the salary up to the annual cap. LAINF/LAA premiums range from 0.7% to 1.5% of salary, depending on the sector. For occupational pension plans (LPP), the employee’s minimum contributions on the coordinated portion are 7% (ages 25–34), 10% (ages 35–44), 15% (ages 45–54), and 18% (ages 55 and older up to the reference age). All these amounts follow the federal structure in effect in each canton, including Nidwalden.

Three-Tier Tax System

In Switzerland, taxation operates on three levels: direct federal tax (IFD), regulated in Bern, cantonal tax, and municipal tax. The IFD is calculated in accordance with federal law and is administered by the Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV). Cantonal and municipal taxes are governed by the tax law of the Canton of Nidwalden, which sets the cantonal tax rate; the municipalities then apply their own multiplier. This means that two businesses with identical revenue in different municipalities within Nidwalden may face different tax burdens. VAT is administered at the federal level by the FTA/ESTV itself; businesses with annual revenue exceeding the threshold of CHF 2.5 million must register for VAT and collect the tax on their services.

Operational details

The decision to start a business in the Canton of Nidwalden is not just a matter of initial capital: the practical implications include taxation, social security contributions, mandatory insurance coverage, and retirement planning. Once the legal form has been chosen and registration in the commercial register is complete, the entrepreneur enters a system of recurring obligations that will accompany them throughout the life of the company.

Tax Implications for the Entrepreneur

The first aspect to consider is the distinction between direct federal tax, cantonal tax, and the municipal multiplier. In Nidwalden, each municipality sets its own multiplier, expressed as a percentage of the base cantonal tax. As a result, two companies with identical revenue but located in municipalities with different multipliers pay different amounts of cantonal-municipal tax. Direct federal tax (IFD), on the other hand, follows federal rates, which are uniform throughout Switzerland. Location planning can therefore become a factor in tax optimization, subject to the principle of taxation based on economic capacity.

A second factor concerns the Value-Added Tax (VAT). Businesses whose annual revenue does not exceed the threshold of CHF 2.5 million are exempt from the requirement to register, but may opt for voluntary registration to recover prior-period tax. Above this threshold, registration is mandatory. VAT returns are generally filed quarterly, using rates based on the sector and nature of the service.

Recurring Personnel Costs

If the business involves hiring employees, the employer must manage payroll, which includes: AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3% paid by the employee and 5.3% paid by the employer; AD/AC at 1.1% up to the federal annual cap; LPP based on age and coordinated salary; LAINF/LAA with sector-specific premium (0.7–1.5%). The LPP coordinated salary ranges from the entry threshold up to the statutory ceiling, and the minimum contributions indicated above apply only to this range. The minimum LPP contribution rates based on age are mandatory: the employer must pay at least half of the total contributions, as required by the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO) and the LPP.

Mandatory Health Insurance

Self-employed individuals registered in the commercial register and family members working in the business, as well as employees, must take out mandatory health insurance under the LAMal within three months of arriving in Switzerland or starting their business. The LAMal provides for per-capita premiums that are not tied to salary: each resident chooses a deductible of CHF 300, 500, 1,000, 1,500, 2,000, or 2,500. Higher deductibles reduce the monthly premium but expose the insured person to out-of-pocket costs up to the annual maximum. Premiums vary by canton and region; in Nidwalden, the rates of the relevant insurance region apply. The law also provides for premium reductions (cantonal subsidies) for low-income earners, which are paid by the canton of residence.

Comparison of Legal Structures: A Scenario

Let’s consider a business starting with limited capital: a limited liability company (Sagl) requires a capital contribution of CHF 20,000, which must be deposited into a restricted account prior to registration; an SA requires CHF 100,000 in nominal capital, with at least CHF 50,000 paid in. A sole proprietorship has no minimum capital requirement but exposes the owner to unlimited liability: in the event of business debts, personal assets may be seized. For those starting a business with limited risks and low initial investments, a sole proprietorship is the quickest route; for those who intend to bring in partners and limit liability, the Sagl offers the best balance between capital requirements and asset protection. This choice has a direct impact on the tax burden: the profits of an Sagl are taxed at the corporate level (federal income tax + cantonal + municipal), while those of a sole proprietorship are included in the owner’s personal income.

Relations with Federal and Cantonal Authorities

The entrepreneur must interact with several authorities: the FTA/ESTV for federal income tax and VAT, the cantonal authorities of Nidwalden for cantonal and municipal taxes, the cantonal commercial registry office for amendments to the articles of incorporation, and the cantonal offices for AVS/AI/IPG and AD contributions. For occupational pension coverage under the LPP, the employer must join a recognized pension fund. For LAINF/LAA, if the business falls within the mandatory sectors, insurance is administered by SUVA; otherwise, a licensed private insurer is used. Given this multitude of parties to deal with, it is advisable to seek initial consultation covering corporate law, taxation, and labor law.

Key points

After making the decision, an entrepreneur who wants to start a business in Nidwalden must follow a specific procedure. The process can be broken down into distinct steps, each with its own required documents and deadlines.

Step-by-Step Procedure

1. Choosing the legal structure. This decision must be made before taking any other steps. Compare minimum capital requirements, personal liability, accounting requirements, and recurring costs. A sole proprietorship is suitable for low-risk, low-capital businesses; a limited liability company (Sagl) is for those who want to limit liability with an affordable capital requirement; and a corporation (SA) is for projects requiring significant capital and a complex corporate structure.

2. Drafting the articles of association or the memorandum of association. For corporations, a public deed authenticated by a Swiss notary is required. The notary verifies the founders’ identities, collects the incorporation declarations, and prepares the articles of association specifying the capital, purpose, registered office, and organizational structure.

3. Deposit of the minimum capital into a restricted account. For an Sagl, CHF 20,000 must be deposited; for an SA, at least CHF 50,000 (out of a minimum nominal capital of CHF 100,000). The bank issues a deposit confirmation to be attached to the application for registration.

4. Application for registration with the cantonal commercial register. The application must be submitted to the Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Nidwalden, along with the articles of association, the deed of incorporation, the bank confirmation, and the founders’ identification documents. Cantonal registration fees are calculated based on the capital; a flat rate applies to sole proprietorships.

5. Registration for a VAT number with the Federal Tax Administration (FTA). If the projected annual revenue exceeds CHF 2.5 million, or in the case of voluntary opt-in, the company must register for the VAT system with the Federal Tax Administration and begin collecting tax on the services provided.

6. Enrollment in social security and insurance programs. The new employer must register with the cantonal AHV compensation fund, the unemployment insurance (AD) fund, SUVA or the private LAINF insurer for the sector, and a recognized BVG pension fund.

7. Notification to cantonal and municipal tax authorities. The business must be reported to the cantonal tax authority of Nidwalden and to the municipality where the business is located for the taxation of corporate profits (Sagl/SA) or individual income (sole proprietorship).

Deadlines to Be Met

Health insurance under the LAMal must be obtained within three months of the start of business operations or arrival in Switzerland: in the absence of coverage, the authorities will automatically assign you to a plan with a higher premium. The VAT return is filed according to the selected reporting period (typically quarterly) with fixed deadlines set by the AFC/ESTV. AVS/AI/IPG and AD contributions must be paid monthly or quarterly, depending on the agreements with the cantonal fund. LAINF/LAA premiums are generally annual. LPP contributions follow the schedule of the chosen pension fund.

When to Involve a Consultant

The process involves corporate law, tax law, labor law, and social security. A notary is required for the incorporation of a Sagl or SA. For selecting the legal form, optimizing the corporate structure, and establishing relationships with social security agencies, it is helpful to engage an authorized trustee (Treuhaender) who is registered with the cantonal registry. A tax advisor is recommended to set up the accounting system and manage dealings with the AFC/ESTV and the cantonal administration.

Frequently Asked Questions
What legal forms can you choose to open a business in Switzerland?
The Code of Obligations provides for the sole proprietorship (no minimum capital, unlimited liability), the general partnership, the limited partnership, the cooperative, the limited liability company (Sagl) with minimum capital of CHF 20,000 fully released, and the public limited company (SA) with nominal capital of CHF 100,000 and at least CHF 50,000 to be released upon incorporation.
When is registration in the trade register in Nidvaldo mandatory?
Registration is mandatory for Sagl, SA, a collective, limited and cooperative company. For the sole proprietorship it is optional but recommended above certain turnover thresholds. The application is submitted to the cantonal office of the commercial register with an authenticated deed of incorporation, articles of association, declaration of incorporation and bank confirmation of the paid-up capital.
What social contributions must a new employer pay in Switzerland?
The employer pays AVS/AI/IPG at 10.6% of the salary (5.3% paid by the employee and 5.3% paid by the employer), AD/AC at 1.1% up to the federal ceiling, LAINF/LAA between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector, and LPP with minimum rates of 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54) and 18% (55+) on the coordinated share.
How does three-tier taxation work in Nidvaldo?
The direct federal tax follows the rates of the AFC/ESTV and is the same throughout Switzerland. The cantonal tax is governed by the tax law of Nidvaldo; each municipality then applies its own multiplier. For this reason, two identical activities in different municipalities pay different amounts. Federal VAT rises above CHF 2.5 million in annual turnover, with a quarterly declaration as a rule.
Within how many months of the start of the activity should LAMal be stipulated?
Compulsory health insurance according to LAMal must be taken out within three months of starting business or arriving in Switzerland. In the absence of coverage, the insured is automatically assigned to a cashier with a higher premium. The Canton of residence provides premium reductions for low incomes and deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500.
