Anyone wishing to start a business in the Canton of Nidwalden must navigate a multi-step process that begins with choosing a legal form and continues through registration in the commercial register, opening the necessary bank accounts, and fulfilling tax and insurance obligations. In Switzerland, there is no one-size-fits-all approach: the choice of legal form determines the minimum capital requirement, personal liability, accounting requirements, and recurring costs.

Operational details

The decision to start a business in the Canton of Nidwalden is not just a matter of initial capital: the practical implications include taxation, social security contributions, mandatory insurance coverage, and retirement planning. Once the legal form has been chosen and registration in the commercial register is complete, the entrepreneur enters a system of recurring obligations that will accompany them throughout the life of the company.

Tax Implications for the Entrepreneur

The first aspect to consider is the distinction between direct federal tax, cantonal tax, and the municipal multiplier. In Nidwalden, each municipality sets its own multiplier, expressed as a percentage of the base cantonal tax. As a result, two companies with identical revenue but located in municipalities with different multipliers pay different amounts of cantonal-municipal tax. Direct federal tax (IFD), on the other hand, follows federal rates, which are uniform throughout Switzerland. Location planning can therefore become a factor in tax optimization, subject to the principle of taxation based on economic capacity.

A second factor concerns the Value-Added Tax (VAT). Businesses whose annual revenue does not exceed the threshold of CHF 2.5 million are exempt from the requirement to register, but may opt for voluntary registration to recover prior-period tax. Above this threshold, registration is mandatory. VAT returns are generally filed quarterly, using rates based on the sector and nature of the service.

Key points

After making the decision, an entrepreneur who wants to start a business in Nidwalden must follow a specific procedure. The process can be broken down into distinct steps, each with its own required documents and deadlines.

Step-by-Step Procedure

1. Choosing the legal structure. This decision must be made before taking any other steps. Compare minimum capital requirements, personal liability, accounting requirements, and recurring costs. A sole proprietorship is suitable for low-risk, low-capital businesses; a limited liability company (Sagl) is for those who want to limit liability with an affordable capital requirement; and a corporation (SA) is for projects requiring significant capital and a complex corporate structure.

2. Drafting the articles of association or the memorandum of association. For corporations, a public deed authenticated by a Swiss notary is required. The notary verifies the founders’ identities, collects the incorporation declarations, and prepares the articles of association specifying the capital, purpose, registered office, and organizational structure.

3. Deposit of the minimum capital into a restricted account. For an Sagl, CHF 20,000 must be deposited; for an SA, at least CHF 50,000 (out of a minimum nominal capital of CHF 100,000). The bank issues a deposit confirmation to be attached to the application for registration.

4. Application for registration with the cantonal commercial register. The application must be submitted to the Commercial Register Office of the Canton of Nidwalden, along with the articles of association, the deed of incorporation, the bank confirmation, and the founders’ identification documents. Cantonal registration fees are calculated based on the capital; a flat rate applies to sole proprietorships.

Frequently Asked Questions
What legal forms can you choose to open a business in Switzerland?
The Code of Obligations provides for the sole proprietorship (no minimum capital, unlimited liability), the general partnership, the limited partnership, the cooperative, the limited liability company (Sagl) with minimum capital of CHF 20,000 fully released, and the public limited company (SA) with nominal capital of CHF 100,000 and at least CHF 50,000 to be released upon incorporation.
When is registration in the trade register in Nidvaldo mandatory?
Registration is mandatory for Sagl, SA, a collective, limited and cooperative company. For the sole proprietorship it is optional but recommended above certain turnover thresholds. The application is submitted to the cantonal office of the commercial register with an authenticated deed of incorporation, articles of association, declaration of incorporation and bank confirmation of the paid-up capital.
What social contributions must a new employer pay in Switzerland?
The employer pays AVS/AI/IPG at 10.6% of the salary (5.3% paid by the employee and 5.3% paid by the employer), AD/AC at 1.1% up to the federal ceiling, LAINF/LAA between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector, and LPP with minimum rates of 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54) and 18% (55+) on the coordinated share.
How does three-tier taxation work in Nidvaldo?
The direct federal tax follows the rates of the AFC/ESTV and is the same throughout Switzerland. The cantonal tax is governed by the tax law of Nidvaldo; each municipality then applies its own multiplier. For this reason, two identical activities in different municipalities pay different amounts. Federal VAT rises above CHF 2.5 million in annual turnover, with a quarterly declaration as a rule.
Within how many months of the start of the activity should LAMal be stipulated?
Compulsory health insurance according to LAMal must be taken out within three months of starting business or arriving in Switzerland. In the absence of coverage, the insured is automatically assigned to a cashier with a higher premium. The Canton of residence provides premium reductions for low incomes and deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500.
