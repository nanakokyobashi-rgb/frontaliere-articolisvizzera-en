A Comparis survey of 1,040 people reveals that climate change has little impact on purchasing choices. High incomes are less sensitive.

The Swiss national landscape registers a clear trend regarding climate sensitivity in financial choices. According to a representative survey conducted by Comparis, which involved a sample of 1,040 people across the country in July, 80% of adults state that the climate issue has little or no impact on their consumption and purchasing habits. This data reflects a marked distance between the public debate, focused on heatwaves and European climate emergencies, and the daily economic decisions of individual citizens.

Behavioral detachment analysis

Going into the details of the data collected, 34% of respondents openly state that the climate debate does not exert any influence on their purchasing decisions. Operational details

Analysis of Comparis' demoscopic data reveals interesting divergences based on the socio-economic status of citizens residing in Switzerland. The income factor plays a decisive role: among people with a gross household income of more than 8,000 francs, 84% do not allow themselves to be influenced by the climate debate in their purchasing decisions. On the contrary, this share drops to 75% among those with lower incomes, up to 4,000 francs. According to Michael Kuhn, an expert at Comparis, the difficulty in changing their behaviour is greater for high-income groups, which can afford a higher volume of consumption and travel than households with lower incomes.

Socio-economic dynamics in decisions

This discrepancy suggests that, in Switzerland, economic capacity acts as a buffer that allows us to maintain energy-intensive lifestyles without suffering excessive pressure from the ecological debate. While families with children tend to be, in some cases, more attentive to issues concerning the future, the aggregate data shows widespread resistance to changing consumption. It is a reflection of how the cost of living, which heavily affects household budgets, is often managed regardless of climate considerations. Key points

For those living in Switzerland, managing the family budget remains a challenge that requires careful monitoring of income and expenditures. Although climate change does not appear as a priority in the Comparis survey, financial planning remains an essential tool to cope with the rising cost of living. The correct management of wages, considering mandatory social deductions and taxes, allows citizens to have a clear view of their real economic availability. When planning major investments or purchases, it is useful to evaluate each item of expenditure to optimize the household budget, regardless of external pressures.

What percentage of Swiss say that the climate has little impact on their consumption?

Are there differences between high and low incomes regarding climate sensitivity?
Yes, the data indicate that higher incomes are less sensitive: 84% of those who earn more than 8,000 gross francs do not change their habits, against 75% of incomes up to 4,000 francs.
