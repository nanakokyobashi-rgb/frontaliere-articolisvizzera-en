Key Facts

  • What: Performance and data of the pharmaceutical industry

The pharmaceutical industry confirms its role as a central driver for the Swiss economy, despite evolving international pressures. According to 2023 data from the Federal Statistical Office (FSO), the sector comprises around 350 companies operating in the country, the vast majority of which operate internationally. Alongside global giants under Swiss control such as Novartis, Roche, and Sandoz, there are numerous subsidiaries of foreign competitors like Pfizer, Takeda, and AstraZeneca.

Market Structure and Employment

Although global giants attract media attention, the sector's structure is diverse: nearly 6 out of 10 companies are classified as micro-enterprises, with fewer than 10 employees. However, the ability to generate employment is driven by large multinationals. The sector's workforce has grown by over 30,000 since the mid-1990s boom, reaching 56,000 people in 2023. The impact on the labor market extends beyond direct boundaries: according to a 2024 study by BAK Economics, the pharmaceutical value chain generates around 250,000 indirect jobs. This volume of jobs requires high skills, as 67% of the workforce holds a higher education diploma, a percentage well above the Swiss average of 45%. For those looking for work or evaluating their Swiss payslip, this sector represents one of the most qualifying and remuneratively solid professional outlets in the country.

Operational details

The competitiveness of the sector is based on unparalleled innovative capacity but must contend with a rapidly changing geopolitical landscape. Data published in 2025 by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office (SFSO) highlights that the chemical-pharmaceutical industry is the absolute leader in research and development (R&D) investments in Switzerland. In 2023, investments reached nearly 5.5 billion francs, accounting for almost one-third of the total private sector R&D expenditure, which amounts to approximately 18 billion francs.

Leadership in Research and Development

In percentage terms, Switzerland stands out distinctly within the OECD landscape. While the average pharmaceutical R&D investment among developed countries that are members of the organization is 8%, Switzerland records significantly higher values, solidifying its position as a global hub for innovation. This research intensity is crucial for maintaining its competitive advantage, especially considering that chemical-pharmaceutical products, particularly serums and vaccines, constitute the second-largest category of goods exported from Switzerland, surpassed only by gold.

Key points

To understand the country's economic future, it is essential to analyze not only current data but also the dynamics that will influence the sector in the coming years. The Swiss pharmaceutical industry stands at a crossroads: on one hand, a highly qualified workforce and massive investments in innovation; on the other hand, increasingly aggressive international competition.

Implications for the labor market

The demand for highly qualified personnel, with 67% of employees holding higher education degrees, clearly indicates the direction of the market. For workers, this means that access to this sector requires continuous training and advanced specializations. The stability of the 250,000 indirect jobs depends on the ability of large multinationals to maintain their leadership, which is in turn linked to research outcomes.

Future prospects and strategies

The challenges posed by the United States and emerging markets such as China should not be underestimated. Switzerland's ability to defend its global market share (currently second in the world) will depend on its capacity to sustain R&D investment rates above the OECD average. This high-tech and high-wage environment remains a pillar of the national economy, but it requires political and strategic vigilance from federal authorities to protect this "flagship."

How many people work in the Swiss pharmaceutical industry?

What is the value of Switzerland's pharmaceutical exports?
In 2025, the industry generated exports of over CHF 100 billion, accounting for almost 22% of total Swiss exports.
How much does Switzerland invest in pharmaceutical research and development?
In 2023, R&D investments in the chemical-pharmaceutical sector reached almost CHF 5.5 billion, almost a third of total private spending in Switzerland.
