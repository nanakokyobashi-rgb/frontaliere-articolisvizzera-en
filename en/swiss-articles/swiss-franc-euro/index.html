Operational details

Expectations of an increase in interest rates in the Eurozone are one of the main causes of the weakening of the Swiss franc. Eurozone central banks have raised interest rates to counter inflation and support the economy. This led to an increase in the value of the euro and a weakening of the Swiss franc.

The last increase in interest rates by the European Central Bank (ECB) was 0.75 percentage points, bringing the interest rate to 3.75% per annum. This had a significant impact on the Swiss franc, which lost about 2.5% of its value against the euro. The current exchange rate is about 0.94 euros for 1 Swiss franc.

The economic situation in Switzerland is complex and influenced by several factors. The demand for foreign exchange has increased due to the scarcity of funds available for investment. In addition, the Swiss National Bank (SNB) increased the interest rate to 0.5% per annum to counter inflation and support the economy. However, this increase was not enough to counteract the impact of rising interest rates in the Eurozone.

Switzerland is an important financial centre and the stability of its financial system is essential for the European economy. The SNB assured that Switzerland will remain a safe place for investment and that it will continue to support the economy. However, the current situation is worrying and requires a rapid and effective response.

Key points

For frontier workers, the exchange rate of the franc against the euro can have a significant impact on their lifestyle. If the franc weakens, they may have to pay more for purchases and holidays in Switzerland. On the other hand, a stronger euro could push up house and property prices in Switzerland.

According to statistics from the Canton of Geneva, in 2022, 70% of Swiss border workers said they had experienced an increase in living costs due to the exchange rate of the franc. This is a trend that has also occurred in the other border cantons such as the Canton of Lausanne and the Canton of Neuchâtel.

The exchange rate of the franc can also have an impact on Swiss exports. According to statistics from the Swiss Confederation, in 2022, exports of Swiss products to the EU increased by 10% compared to the previous year. However, the exchange rate of the franc can make them less competitive due to the higher cost of production.

For frontier workers who depend on lower profitability, the exchange rate of the franc can be a serious problem. According to statistics from the Canton of Geneva, in 2022, 40% of Swiss border workers claimed to have an income of less than 40,000 Swiss francs per year. This can make them more sensitive to changes in the franc exchange rate.
