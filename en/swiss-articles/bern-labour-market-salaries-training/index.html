The labor market in the Canton of Bern operates according to Swiss federal regulations, with cantonal and municipal specifications that affect wages, social security and taxation. Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage, but some cantons, potentially including Bern, have established one for certain sectors. Job seekers in Bern need to understand how salary levels relate to professional training, what mandatory contributions are paid and how the local tax system reduces the monthly net.

The Swiss training system is divided into three paths: professional training (Federal Certificate of Skills, AFC), general training (cantonal or Swiss high school diploma) and tertiary training (universities, university of applied sciences). Each level generally corresponds to a different salary range, although the actual salary also depends on the sector, the experience accumulated and the collective labor agreements in force.

Salary structure and social security deductions

In Bern, as throughout Switzerland, the gross salary initially proposed reflects the educational qualification and professional qualification. However, several federally mandated deductions immediately apply to your paycheck. Old Age and Survivors Insurance (AVS), Disability Insurance (AI) and Loss of Earnings Compensation (IPG) together absorb 5.3% of the employee's salary. Added to this is Unemployment Insurance (AD) and Insolvency Compensation (AC) with 1.1% up to an annual maximum set at federal level. Accident insurance (LAINF), managed by SUVA or private insurers, varies between 0.7% and 1.5% of the salary depending on the professional sector. Mandatory occupational pension provision (LPP, Pension Fund) varies from 7% to 18% of the coordinated salary depending on age: 7% between 25 and 34 years old, 10% between 35 and 44, 15% between 45 and 54, and 18% from 55 years old up to the reference age for retirement. Health insurance (KVG) is mandatory for every resident within 3 months of arriving in Switzerland and is managed by the private sector at per capita premiums. Deductibles for adults vary from 300 to 2500 CHF per year, and premiums differ by canton and region. The Canton of Bern may offer cantonal subsidies to reduce the premiums of low-income residents. For more information, see the health insurance page.

Working hours, holidays and parental leave

The maximum working time is set at 45 hours per week for industry, offices and retail trade, or 50 hours for other sectors. The minimum holidays are 4 weeks per year (5 for those under 20). Federal maternity leave lasts 14 weeks at 80% of pay, covered by Loss of Earnings Allowance (IPG). Paternity is 2 weeks. The notice for the employer is 1 month during the first year, 2 months from the second to the ninth year of employment, and 3 months from the tenth year onwards. Collective labor agreements in Bern may provide conditions that exceed these federal legal minimums.

Operational details

The final tax burden on an employee's salary in Bern depends on three levels of taxation: direct federal tax (IFD), cantonal tax and municipal tax. The IFD is collected by the Federal Administration; cantonal and municipal taxes are managed by the Bernese local authorities at their own rates. The municipal multiplier applies a coefficient to the cantonal rate, so the final tax burden varies depending on the place of residence within the Canton of Bern. These amounts do not appear as 'social security contributions' but as 'withholding tax' (PPC), deducted directly from your salary each month by your employer.

Collective labor agreements (CCL) are agreements negotiated between trade unions and employers' organizations that establish, for a specific sector or geographical area, the guaranteed minimum wage, working hours, holidays, insurance premiums and other working conditions. In the Canton of Bern, several sectors (manufacturing, commerce, healthcare, construction, public services) have CCLs in force and registered with SECO. An employee covered by CCL cannot receive less than the minimum wage established by the contract, even if Swiss federal law does not impose a national minimum. To check whether your professional sector has a CCL applicable in Bern, consult the cantonal employment office or contact the local trade union headquarters. SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs) provides a complete national database of current collective agreements, searchable by canton and sector.

Cantonal minimum wage and wage determination

The Canton of Bern, like some other Swiss cantons, may have established a cantonal or specific minimum wage for certain sectors. Switzerland as a whole does NOT have a federal minimum wage, but the situation varies significantly from canton to canton. To find out the exact amount and the sectors affected in the Canton of Bern, it is essential to contact the cantonal labor and economics office directly. SECO and the Bernese employers' associations also provide up-to-date information. Wage determination is not arbitrary: the Swiss labor market operates on a competitive basis. Companies looking for talent must offer competitive salaries to attract qualified candidates. Industries with labor shortages (e.g., nursing, structural engineering, information technology) tend to offer significantly higher wages than industries with lower demand. Accumulated experience, specialized skills, working language and geographical location within the canton further influence the proposed salary.

Professional pension and third pillar

In addition to the federally managed mandatory AVS and AD contributions, occupational pension provision (LPP) is mandatory for employees whose annual salary exceeds a set threshold (the 'coordinated salary' starts above approximately CHF 22'050, but the exact amount varies from year to year due to indexation). Those who earn lower amounts may not be covered by the LPP, but are still covered by the federal AVS. The pension fund is chosen by the employer and the contribution is paid by both the employee and the employer. For those who want to save beyond the mandatory occupational pension, the third pillar 3a offers a federal tax deduction up to an annual maximum that changes each year (different maximums for employees vs. self-employed). If you want to increase your retirement savings, additional payments into third pillar 3a are deductible from federal and cantonal taxes in the Canton of Bern. Please consult the Bern cantonal tax office for specific limits and payment procedures.

Key points

Procedure for Job Seekers in Bern

If you are looking for work in the Canton of Bern, the first step is to identify the professional sector and the level of qualification you are looking for. The SECO publishes a national portal with job postings; in addition, national and local Bernese private portals offer opportunities. Once you receive a job offer, negotiate the salary taking into account your level of training, accumulated experience, and, crucially, the applicable CCL for your sector (if any). Ask the employer what salary ranges are for your position and compare them with what is provided for in the collective contract. If there is no applicable CCL, the salary is free but must still respect any written commitments.

Before signing a employment contract, carefully read all the conditions: working hours, holiday days, public holidays, shift supplements, and if the contract falls under a registered CCL. Verify that the proposed salary respects the minimum of the collective contract for your role and your level of experience. If the contract does not mention a CCL, the salary is negotiable but the minimum federal conditions (45-50 hours, 4 weeks of holiday, maternity/paternity) remain always valid.

Calculation of Net Salary

The gross salary you see in the job offer is NOT what you will receive: various mandatory deductions reduce it monthly. In addition to the mandatory federal contributions (AVS/AI/IPG 5.3%, AD/AC 1.1%, LAINF/LAA 0.7-1.5%), the professional pension (LPP) further reduces the amount paid. Furthermore, the federal direct tax (IFD) and the cantonal and municipal taxes of the Canton of Bern are applied through the withholding tax system (PPC). Deadlines, Documents, and Mandatory Registrations

When you accept a job offer, the employer will provide you with a contract and guide you through the administrative registration. You must declare your definitive address in the Canton of Bern, as the municipalities calculate the payroll tax (PPC) from there. Make sure you have a mandatory health insurance (LAMal) within 3 months of starting work. If you don't have one yet, contact a health insurance company directly or contact the Bernese cantonal social welfare office, which can indicate the options with reduced premiums thanks to cantonal subsidies. The LAMal premium is your personal responsibility: the employer does not pay it nor deduct it from your payroll.
