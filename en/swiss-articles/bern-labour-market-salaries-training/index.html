The labour market in the Canton of Bern operates according to Swiss federal regulations, with cantonal and municipal specifications affecting wages, social security and taxation. Switzerland does not have a federal minimum wage, but some cantons, potentially including Bern, have established one for certain sectors. Job seekers in Bern need to understand how wage levels relate to vocational training, what mandatory contributions are paid and how the local tax system reduces the monthly net.

Operational details

The final tax burden on a Bern employee's salary depends on three levels of taxation: the federal direct tax (IFD), the cantonal tax, and the municipal tax. The IFD is collected by the Federal Administration; the cantonal and municipal taxes are managed by the local authorities in Bern according to their own rates. The municipal tax multiplier applies a coefficient to the cantonal tax rate, so the final tax burden varies depending on the place of residence within the Canton of Bern. These amounts do not appear as 'pension contributions' but as 'source tax' (PPC), withheld directly from the salary every month by the employer.

Key points

Procedure for Job Seekers in Bern

If you are looking for work in the Canton of Bern, the first step is to identify the professional sector and the level of qualification you are looking for. The SECO publishes a national portal with job postings; in addition, national and local Bernese private portals offer opportunities. Once you receive a job offer, negotiate the salary taking into account your level of training, accumulated experience, and, crucially, the applicable CCL for your sector (if any). Ask the employer what salary ranges are for your position and compare them with what is provided for in the collective contract. If there is no applicable CCL, the salary is free but must still respect any written commitments.

Before signing a employment contract, carefully read all the conditions: working hours, holiday days, public holidays, shift supplements, and if the contract falls under a registered CCL. Verify that the proposed salary respects the minimum of the collective contract for your role and your level of experience. If the contract does not mention a CCL, the salary is negotiable but the minimum federal conditions (45-50 hours, 4 weeks of holiday, maternity/paternity) remain always valid.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a minimum wage in the Canton of Bern?
Switzerland does not have a mandatory federal minimum wage. However, some cantons, potentially also Bern, have established one for certain sectors or professions. Check directly with the cantonal labour office Bern what the exact amount is and the sectors concerned. Collective labour agreements also set minimum wages by sector, even where there is no formal cantonal minimum.
What contributions are withheld from your paycheck each month?
From the gross salary are deducted: AVS/AI/IPG (5.3%), AD/AC (1.1%), LAINF/LAA (0.7–1.5% according to sector), LPP occupational pension (7–18% according to age), federal/cantonal/municipal taxes of the Canton of Bern. Health insurance (LAMal) is a separate private premium, not a wage deduction.
What is the maximum working time in Canton Bern?
The maximum federal hours are 45 hours per week for industry, offices and commerce; 50 hours for other sectors. Minimum holiday: 4 weeks per year (5 for those under 20). Maternity: 14 weeks at 80%. Paternity: 2 weeks. Advance notice: 1/2/3 months according to seniority. Collective bargaining agreements may provide for better terms.
How do I calculate my monthly net salary?
Use the salary calculator: enter the annual gross, canton (Bern), municipality, age and marital status. The tool automatically deducts AVS, AD, LPP, LAMal, IFD and cantonal/municipal taxes, providing the monthly net and a detailed breakdown of all withholdings.
What are Collective Labour Agreements (CLAs)?
Agreements negotiated between trade unions and employers setting, by sector or area, the guaranteed minimum wage, hours, holidays and other conditions. In the Canton of Bern, several sectors have CCLs in force. Check with the cantonal labour office or the SECO if your sector has an applicable CCL: a covered employee CANNOT receive less than the minimum established by the contract.
