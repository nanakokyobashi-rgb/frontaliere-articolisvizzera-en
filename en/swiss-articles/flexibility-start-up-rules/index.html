On October 28, 2026, in Bern, the Federal Council adopted new special rules for start-ups and some of their employees. The measure aims to ensure greater flexibility in organizing working hours. The press release specifies that the rules were negotiated with the social partners and that they will enter into force on December 1, 2026.

The revision process

The measure takes concrete form in the revision of Ordinance 2 concerning the Labour Act (OLL 2). During the examination of the preliminary draft of parliamentary initiative 16.442, the National Council’s Economic Affairs and Taxation Committee (EATC-N) had asked the Federal Council to implement the latter by means of an ordinance. The revision of OLL 2 achieves this objective.

The press release describes a targeted derogation, not an indiscriminate amendment. The text refers to derogations from the general rules on working hours and rest periods, subject to compliance with various conditions. The workers concerned must be employed by a start-up, have specific expertise, enjoy broad autonomy in their work and benefit from a plan for participation in the company’s capital. The source adds an additional requirement: the work must concern specific projects of limited duration.

The text stems from discussions held with the social partners in round tables and received a positive reception in the public consultation. The amendment will enter into force on December 1, 2026 and will be incorporated into OLL 2, the ordinance that provides special provisions for certain categories of companies and workers.

Operational details

What changes in terms of the rules

The clearest relationship contained in the press release is between the general rule and the exception provided for a defined group. On the one hand, the starting point indicated by the text is the general rules relating to working and rest periods; on the other, the amendment to OLL 2 introduces targeted exemptions for workers who meet the stated requirements. The source does not quantify the hours concerned and does not describe a new standard duration: instead, it documents the principle of flexibility and the conditions for applying it.
