The mortgage reference rate remains at an all-time low of 1.25 percent. This decision by the Federal Office for Housing (BWO) means that rents in Switzerland will remain unchanged for the foreseeable future. The reference rate fell to 1.25 percent in September 2025, after previously being 1.5 percent. This means that the average interest rate determining the reference rate remains unchanged at 1.31 percent. The rate is rounded commercially to quarters of a percentage point, so the reference rate only falls or rises when a corresponding threshold is exceeded. Currently, this threshold is set at 1.13 percent for a reduction and 1.37 percent for an increase.

Policies and dates

The BWO's decision is in line with the federal legislation governing rents in Switzerland. According to Article 270 of the Swiss Code of Obligations, rents can only be increased if the mortgage reference rate exceeds a certain threshold. Currently, this threshold is set at 1.37 percent for an increase. This means that as long as the reference rate remains below this threshold, rents cannot be increased.

Standards and Regulations

In Switzerland, the Federal Law on Tenancy and Building Rights (LLD) of 2000 regulates rent increases. According to this law, rents can only be increased once a year, and the increase must be proportional to the increase in the mortgage reference rate. For example, if the reference rate increases by 0.25 percentage points, the maximum permissible increase is 3 percent.
