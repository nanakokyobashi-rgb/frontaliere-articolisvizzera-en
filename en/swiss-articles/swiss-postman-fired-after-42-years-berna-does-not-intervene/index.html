Operational details

Implications of the decision The Post Office's decision to fire a postman from Vaud after 42 years of uninterrupted service has sparked a heated debate in Switzerland. At the heart of the discussion is the question of the Federal Council's powers over redundancies within the Swiss Post, a public company with broad managerial autonomy. Federal Councillor Albert Rösti, head of the Federal Department of Environment, Transport, Energy and Communications (DETEC), reiterated that the Federal Council does not have the power to intervene directly in personnel law matters, a position that underlines the boundary between political and operational governance. This position suggests that the Post Office, although owned by the Confederation, enjoys a high degree of independence in its business decisions, including dismissals. However, this autonomy also implies greater responsibility in ensuring that the decisions taken are perceived as fair and transparent. This specific case could set an important precedent for the more than 39,000 employees who work for the Post Office throughout Switzerland. Frequently Asked Questions
What are the appeal possibilities for an employee dismissed from Swiss Post?
The possibilities of appeal for an employee dismissed from Swiss Post seem to be limited. The Federal Council has stated that it has no competence to conduct an examination in matters of personnel law, so the Post Office has full autonomy in its personnel decisions.
What to do if you are fired from Swiss Post after many years of service?
If you are dismissed from Swiss Post after many years of service, it is important to check the Post's code of conduct and contact the employee union or association, if applicable. You can also consult a lawyer specializing in employment law to evaluate the options available.
