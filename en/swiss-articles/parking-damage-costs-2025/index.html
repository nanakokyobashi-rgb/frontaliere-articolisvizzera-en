Stagnation on parking damage: price increases of up to 50%

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 August 2026

Comparis study: rear door repair from CHF 92 to 134 in two years. Car technology and workshop rates behind the prices.

Context

In brief

  • Parking damage: rear door +50% (CHF 92→134) between 2023-2025
  • Side panel +41%, fender +32%, painting only +3,5%
  • Causes: increasingly technological cars, workshops raising rates (+3% in 2024)
  • Comparis analyzes 7,3 million cases in Switzerland

Key facts

  • What: Increase in repair costs for parking damage in Switzerland
  • When: 2023-2025
  • Where: Switzerland (Comparis national analysis)
  • Who: Comparis (7,3 million claims analyzed)
  • Rear door: CHF 92 (2023) → CHF 134 (2025)
  • Workshop hourly rate: CHF 172,30 (2024, +3% compared with 2023)
  • Painting: +3,5% (among the lowest)
  • Combined work: from CHF 330 to CHF 380 (+15%)

A Comparis analysis based on approximately 7,3 million cases reveals that repair costs for parking damage increased considerably between 2023 and 2025, well beyond the general inflation rate. In Switzerland, the situation is particularly pronounced for dismantling and structural repair work, while painting recorded much more moderate increases.

The most striking figure concerns the rear door: the average cost rose from 92 francs in 2023 to 134 francs in 2025, an increase of almost 50%. Front doors experienced similar increases. The side panel increased by an average of 41%, while a front fender rose by 32%.

What happened to repair and painting costs

Comparis data nevertheless show a substantial difference between the cost of structural work and that of painting. In 2023, painting a rear door cost an average of 238 francs. In 2025, the cost rose to 246 francs, an increase of 3,5% — a much more modest figure compared with structural price increases. However, considering the repair and painting work on the rear door as a whole, the total cost increased from approximately 330 to 380 francs, corresponding to an overall increase of approximately 15%.

This means that the main increase is concentrated in the dismantling, repair and replacement of damaged parts, not in the final painting.

The reasons behind the price increases: technology and rates

According to Harry Büsser, Comparis expert in car insurance, one of the determining factors is the technological evolution of vehicles. 'Today, an apparently simple parking damage incident may also involve electronics, sensors or assistance systems,' explains Büsser. The average car on Swiss roads is newer than three years ago, which means it is more often equipped with advanced sensors, complex electronic components and driver assistance systems. This significantly increases the time and expertise required to complete a repair.

However, Büsser emphasizes that technology alone does not explain the percentage increases reported. A second crucial factor is the increase in billing rates. According to the industry report by the fiduciary and consulting firm FIUSGA, which specializes in the Swiss automotive sector, in 2024 the average hourly rate charged by workshops increased by approximately 3%, reaching 172,30 francs per hour. This increase, although modest in percentage terms, quickly accumulates in work that requires many hours of labor.

Operational details

The economic context of the Swiss automotive sector

The economic dynamics of the Swiss automotive sector have changed in recent years. According to FIUSGA data, while the profitability of the workshop and spare parts sector has increased, margins in the trade of new and used vehicles have decreased significantly. This means that garages are becoming increasingly dependent on maintenance and repair as a source of profit, a factor that directly contributes to the increase in the prices of these services.

Büsser notes that, from an economic point of view, the workshop and spare parts sector is becoming increasingly important for garages. However, he specifies that, based on the available data, it is not possible to determine with certainty whether the increase in costs was also caused by higher rates or margins, and to what extent. What is certain is that the general trend is upward.

Who is most affected by increases in parking damage costs

The economic consequences of these increases are not the same for all Swiss motorists. Those who own a car with standard paintwork will not face dramatic increases for the paintwork alone (which increased by only 3,5%), but structural repair work has increased significantly and represents the most important item on the bill. For those who have a car with special, metallic, pearlescent or effect paintwork, repair costs will be even higher, since these specialized services are not covered by the national averages reported by Comparis.

The fact that increases in combined repair and paintwork costs amount to 15% means that, for a rear door, an average motorist now spends about 50 francs more than two years ago. For those with multiple damaged areas—which is common in crowded parking areas such as Swiss urban centers—the annual economic impact could easily be 150-300 francs more.

Implications for choosing parking damage insurance coverage

The significant increases in repair costs raise a crucial question for Swiss motorists: is it worth taking out specific insurance for parking damage? Büsser emphasizes that the answer is not clear-cut and depends entirely on the individual situation. Limited coverage often covers amounts between 1.000 and 2.000 francs per claim, with a maximum of two damages per year. Some policies offer coverage without a fixed amount limit, but with proportionally higher premiums.

The decision to take out insurance must consider both one's ability to cover a loss of that magnitude and the specific type of car owned. For those who regularly park in high-risk areas (congested urban centers, public car parks), the risk is objectively higher.

Key points

How to assess whether parking damage insurance is really worthwhile

The decision to take out coverage for parking damage should be based on a pragmatic analysis of your budget and the type of car you own. Here is how to proceed in practice.

Step 1: Calculate your personal financial tolerance If parking damage costing 1,000 or 2,000 francs would represent a significant blow to your monthly budget, insurance could be useful to reduce the financial risk. If, on the other hand, you can afford to pay this amount without any problems, the coverage may not be necessary from an economic perspective.

Step 2: Assess your car’s type of paintwork If your car has special, metallic, pearlescent or otherwise distinctive paintwork, repair costs will be higher than the national average reported by Comparis. In these cases, comprehensive coverage with no amount limit could be more cost-effective than coverage limited to 2,000 francs.

Step 3: Verify that the insured amount is adequate Make sure that the coverage you choose is sufficient for potential damage to your car. Use a car insurance comparison tool to assess different options and premiums in your Swiss region.

Step 4: Carefully check the policy limits Read the policy terms: maximum number of covered claims per year (often 2), maximum amount per claim (frequently 1,000-2,000 francs), deductible amount if applicable. Realistically calculate how many claims it might be reasonable to have based on your usual parking situation.

When parking damage insurance is actually worthwhile

According to Büsser’s analysis, those for whom damage would have a significant impact on their personal budget should be the main ones to take out insurance. For a motorist who regularly parks in crowded areas such as Swiss city centers, high-density commercial areas or exposed public car parks, having coverage concretely reduces financial stress. In addition, if your car is new, high-end or has special paintwork, repair costs quickly exceed 2,000 francs, making insurance more economically advantageous.

By contrast, those who mainly park in private garages, low-traffic areas or spacious, monitored spaces, where the risk of claims is objectively lower, might consider self-insuring by setting aside an amount equivalent to the insurance premium each month.
