Slightly falling electricity prices in 2027 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 September 2026

In 2027, Swiss domestic economies will pay 26.5 ct./kWh average tariff: a 4% drop and savings of CHF 53 per year according to ElCom.

Context

In brief

  • In 2027, average energy tariff at 26.5 ct/kWh, a 4% decline compared to 2026
  • Annual bill: 1,194 francs (-53 francs), thanks to falling procurement costs
  • Energy tariff down 6.28%, but grid rises by approximately 2%
  • ElCom: 580 operators reported new tariffs by August 31, 2026

Key facts

  • What: Falling electricity prices in the Swiss universal service
  • When: Effective January 1, 2027 (announcement September 8, 2026)
  • Where: Switzerland (households in universal service)
  • Who: Federal Electricity Commission (ElCom) + 580 network operators
  • Average tariff: 26.5 ct./kWh (decline of 1.2 ct./kWh from 2026)
  • Annual amount: Bill CHF 1,194 (-CHF 53 compared to 2026)
  • Energy tariff: Down 6.28% to 11.35 ct./kWh
  • Network tariff: Up from 10.73 to 10.94 ct./kWh

The Federal Electricity Commission (ElCom) announced on September 8, 2026, new tariffs for 2027. In the coming year, Swiss households will continue to benefit from falling electricity prices: the median tariff will drop to 26.5 cents per kilowatt-hour (ct./kWh), with an average decline of 4% compared to 2026. This means that a standard household with an annual consumption of 4,500 kWh will pay a bill of approximately 1,194 francs, saving 53 francs per year.

The decline is driven primarily by a reduction in energy procurement costs. The specific energy tariff (the component most directly linked to raw material prices) will fall by 6.28%, dropping from 12.11 to 11.35 ct./kWh. However, this advantage is partially offset by an increase in the network tariff, which will rise from 10.73 to 10.94 ct./kWh, and by the increase in the metering tariff, which will rise by 4 francs annually to 78.48 francs.

Operational details

The energy bill remains a significant element of the Swiss cost of living. An average family with a consumption of 4,500 kWh per year spent approximately 1,247 francs in 2026. In 2027, this amount will drop to approximately 1,194 francs, a modest cut in absolute terms but one that fits positively within a context of inflationary pressures still present in various areas of household spending.

The overall 4% decline masks contrasting internal dynamics. While the price of energy falls thanks to the easing of global supply costs, network infrastructure (management, maintenance, investments in technology) continues to weigh on bills. This phenomenon reflects a structural Swiss trend: the country is heavily dependent on the transport of energy through high and medium voltage lines managed by Swissgrid and regional operators. These infrastructure costs do not decrease proportionally to wholesale prices and, indeed, tend to grow when investments in renewables and grid digitalization (smart metering) are needed.

Regional variability and the role of network operators

A crucial aspect highlighted by ElCom is the high variability of tariffs among the 580 Swiss network operators. The median tariff of 26.5 ct./kWh is precisely a median value, which means that half of the operators apply lower tariffs and half apply higher ones. In some cases, this dispersion is considerable: the metering tariff, for example, ranges between 0 and 180 francs per year for a household, a range that reflects very different strategic choices regarding measurement technology and service management.

Key points

How to Verify Your 2027 Rate

The bill at the end of 2026 or early 2027 will bring the official rate update. However, you can anticipate this by reading the communication from your local distribution company. Every supplier must communicate the rates for the new year to the customer, clearly indicating: energy rate (in ct./kWh), network rate (in ct./kWh), metering rate (in CHF/year), fixed and variable supplements (winter reserve, levies, Swissgrid rates, etc.). If you receive the communication and the calculation is unclear to you, contact the customer service of the grid operator. Swiss regulations require full transparency in bills, and many operators also provide online calculators to estimate annual costs based on your consumption history.

ElCom has noted that improvements are needed in communications about tariff changes: often the justifications provided by operators are confusing and difficult to understand. If you notice this shortcoming, inform the operator of the need for clarification. The Federal Commission continues to monitor operators' compliance with communication and transparency requirements.

Concrete Steps to Prepare for 2027

1. Read the official communication. The grid operator must communicate the new rates in writing at least one month before they take effect (January 2027). Keep this document for your tax and accounting records.

2. Compare with the previous year. Extract your November or December 2026 bill, note the current prices and compare them with the new ones. ElCom also publishes regional comparison data and data by consumption bracket.

Frequently Asked Questions
In 2027 will everyone pay 26.5 ct./kWh average rate?
No, it's the national median. The 580 Swiss network operators apply different tariffs, from 20 to 35 ct./kWh and above, based on purchasing strategies, local infrastructure costs and share of own production. Check the official communication of your manager to know the specific rates of your area.
Why does the grid rate go up if that energy goes down?
The energy tariff reflects the price of the raw material (which falls). The network rate covers management costs, maintenance, depreciation of transport and local distribution facilities, which do not fall proportionally to wholesale prices. In 2027, the network tariff increases by about 2% due to necessary infrastructure investments.
What is NAD+ and why is it important?
WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital) is the rate of return on capital invested in distribution networks. In 2027 it fell from 3.43% to 3.28%, generating an aggregate saving of about 34 million francs distributed on Swiss networks, equal to 0.06 ct./kWh average reduction. It changes based on global interest rates and ElCom decisions.
Will the war in Iran affect my 2027 bill?
No, because suppliers have already entered into structured purchase contracts for 2027 before the escalation in March 2026. The effects of the current conflict will appear in 2028-2029 renewals, when old contracts expire and renew at potentially higher prices.
How much are the metering fees and do they vary a lot?
In 2027 the median is 78.48 francs/year (+4 francs compared to 2026). However, they vary from 0 to 180 francs per year between the different managers, based on local management costs, smart metering technology choices and the manager's strategies. Please check your tariff communication for the specific value.
