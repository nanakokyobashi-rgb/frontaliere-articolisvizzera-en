In the Swiss tax system, inheritance and gift taxes remain the exclusive responsibility of the cantons, not the federal government. This means that each canton has the right to establish its own rules, rates and procedures, generating significant differences from region to region. In the canton of Basel-Landschaft, as in the other 25 Swiss cantons, anyone who inherits assets or receives a donation is subject to taxation according to specific cantonal rules.

The basis of the system is the degree of kinship of the beneficiary. Not all family members pay the same tax: the deceased's spouse, direct descendants (children, grandchildren) and other relatives are treated at different rates. In most cantons, the surviving spouse benefits from full or significantly reduced exemptions, recognizing their special status. Direct descendants also often benefit from favorable treatments, with lower rates than more distant relatives.

The procedure begins with the opening of the succession, at which time the beneficiaries and administrators must declare the inherited assets to the cantonal authorities of Basel-Landschaft. This declaration must comply with precise deadlines, generally set within a few months of death. Those who do not respect the deadlines risk fines and late payment interest, making it essential to know the local deadlines precisely.

The Swiss cantonal tax system

Unlike other countries, Switzerland does not impose a federal inheritance and gift tax. This choice reflects the principle of cantonal sovereignty: each canton manages the withdrawal independently, generating considerable geographical variability. An inherited asset may pay completely different taxes depending on whether it is located in the canton of Zurich, Geneva, Ticino or Basel-Land. This fragmentation is a structural feature of Swiss federalism and directly influences estate planning choices.

Basel-Landschaft, located in the north-western region of Switzerland, applies tax rules aligned with general intercantonal principles, but with local peculiarities in the determination of rates and the articulation of exemptions. Anyone who inherits in the canton must therefore orient themselves in the specific legislation of Basel-Landschaft, not in a standardized federal law.

Operational details

Rates and degree of kinship

In the canton of Basel-Landschaft, as in the whole of Switzerland, inheritance tax is structured into classes of beneficiaries, each with different rights and burdens, according to general intercantonal principles. Typical categories include:

The spouse: In most cantons, the surviving spouse is fully exempt from inheritance tax, recognizing that the remaining spouse should not have his or her assets reduced to continue his or her life. The exemption is usually complete, regardless of the amount of the inheritance.

Direct descendants: children, grandchildren and subsequent generations benefit from progressive rates that vary depending on the consanguinity relationship. Direct children pay less than grandchildren; grandchildren less than great-grandchildren. The intent is to encourage the transmission of assets within the immediate family unit. Many cantons also provide exemption thresholds for descendants, i.e. amounts below which no tax is triggered.

Collateral relatives: Brothers, sisters, cousins ​​and more distant relatives face higher rates under cantonal tax rules. The more distant the relationship, the more the tax increases, reflecting the idea that the inheritance of an unrelated person is an economic event more distant from the beneficiary.

Non-relatives: Beneficiaries without blood ties (friends, institutions, not spouses) face the often significant maximum tax rates.

These progressive principles are common at the intercantonal level, although each canton, including Basel-Landschaft, may apply specific percentages according to its own rules.

Tax exemptions and thresholds

In addition to rate differences, many cantons apply exemption thresholds: amounts below which no tax is due, regardless of the degree of kinship. For example, an inheritance of a few thousand francs may not be taxed if it is below the threshold set by the canton. These thresholds are especially important for lineal descendants and spouses, where often the exemption is full or the threshold is more generous.

In the canton of Basel-Landschaft, specific thresholds and rates are established by cantonal tax law and can be consulted at the local tax administration. Their configuration varies periodically with legislative changes, making it essential to update yourself on the rules in force at the time of opening a succession.

Donations and tax regime

Donations received during the donor's lifetime are often subject to separate gift taxes, which are also administered at the cantonal level. In the canton of Basel-Landschaft, gifts may benefit from time exemptions (for example, gifts made more than years before death may not count for inheritance tax purposes) or specific thresholds. The regime is usually less strict than that of succession in many cantons, encouraging early estate planning.

Key points

How to Declare an Inheritance in Basel-Stadt Canton

When a person dies and leaves assets in Basel-Stadt Canton, the heirs or legal representatives (executor, administrator) must declare the assets to the cantonal tax office within the established deadlines. This process is mandatory and not optional.

Step 1: Gather Documents The executor or principal heir must collect all relevant documentation: death certificate, will (if present), inventory of assets, bank statements, securities, and previous tax returns of the deceased. The tax office of Basel-Stadt Canton will communicate exactly which documents are required at the time of opening the inheritance.

Step 2: Submit the Declaration on Time

The deadlines for declaring inheritance are set by the canton of Basel-Stadt, typically within 90 days of death or knowledge of death. Failure to respect the deadline entails penalties and late interest, making timeliness critical. The declaration can be submitted in paper form or, in many cantons, electronically through the tax administration portal.

Step 3: Valuation and Imposition

The tax office of Basel-Stadt Canton values the inherited assets based on the criteria established by the cantonal tax law. The valuation can be made at market value, cadastral value, or according to other methods provided by the regulations. Once the taxable amount is determined, the office calculates the tax due based on the applicable rates for the beneficiary.

Step 4: Liquidation and Payment

The tax office issues a tax notice (imposition notice) specifying the amount due. The heirs generally have a specified deadline to pay (usually 30-60 days from receipt). It is possible to request payment extensions or contest the imposition before the cantonal appeal authorities if the calculation is deemed incorrect.

Critical Deadlines and Timelines

Each phase of the procedure is governed by rigid deadlines. Failure to meet deadlines for declaration, payment, or appeal entails increasing penalties. It is essential to contact the tax office of Basel-Stadt Canton promptly at the time of inheritance and clarify the specific deadline schedule applicable.
