Operational details

Because Uri is an interesting case on the national scene

The Swiss system of family allowances is characterized by a mixed structure: a common federal base (LAFam) and strong cantonal variability in amounts and coffers. This architecture means that two families residing in neighbouring cantons can receive different amounts on equal terms. Uri, a small Alpine canton, falls within this logic and applies its own cantonal legislation, with family compensation funds recognised by the canton.

Difference between federal minimum amounts and cantonal amounts

LAFam sets national minimum amounts for the child allowance and the training allowance. These amounts represent the floor under which no canton can go down, but most cantons — including Uri — pay higher amounts. The source does not specify either the updated federal minimum amounts or those practiced in the Canton of Uri, so the article merely describes the mechanism, referring the reader to official sources for exact values.

Who is entitled and from when

The right to family allowances arises from the month in which the child is born or received into the family, and normally ends at the age of 16 for the child allowance, and at the age of 25 for the training allowance (if the child is still in training). Key points

Concrete procedure for requesting family allowances in the Canton of Uri

The application procedure for family allowances in Switzerland follows similar logic in all cantons, with cantonal adaptations regarding forms and the competent compensation fund. Uri is no exception: the applicant must contact the recognized family compensation fund and submit the application with the required documents.

Step 1: identify the competent compensation fund

The first concrete step is to understand which fund to contact. For an employee, the fund is normally the employer's fund. A self-employed worker must register with the professional or cantonal family compensation fund designated by the Canton of Uri. A person without gainful activity who is entitled to allowances must contact the cantonal fund. The source does not indicate the name and address of the Uri cantonal fund, but the information can be obtained from the Uri cantonal administration.

Step 2: gather the necessary documents

The application typically requires: identity document, certificate of family status or family register extract, children's birth certificates, education certificate for children over 16, and — for self-employed workers — documentation of income from gainful activity. For foreign citizens, a residence or permanent residence permit issued by the SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) is also required.

Step 3: submit the application

The application is submitted to the competent family compensation fund. In many cases, the employer automatically starts paying the allowances, but it is good practice to verify that registration has been completed. Funds may request additional documents in case of special situations (custody, spouse's children, separation, work activity in several cantons).

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the legal basis for family allowances in Canton Uri?
Family allowances in Switzerland are governed by the Federal Act on Family Allowances (LAFam), which sets at national level the categories of beneficiaries, the general conditions of law and the minimum amounts. The Canton of Uri then regulates its own cantonal law on family allowances, which sets the amounts actually paid and designates the family compensation funds recognized in the territory.
Who is entitled to family allowances in Switzerland?
The personal scope of application of the LAFam includes employees subject to AVS contributions, self-employed persons with a minimum income from gainful activity and persons without gainful activity with a modest income. The right exists when the beneficiary has own, spouse or foster children who meet the personal requirements provided for by law.
How long are child and education allowances payable?
The right to family allowances arises from the month in which the child is born or welcomed into the family. The child allowance normally ends at the age of 16, while the training allowance may be paid up to the age of 25, provided that the child is still in training.
Which clearing house should I contact in Canton Uri?
For employees, the cash is normally that of the employer. Self-employed persons must register with the professional or cantonal family compensation fund designated by the Canton Uri. People without gainful activity turn to the cantonal cashier. The source does not indicate the name and address of the Uranian cantonal fund.
Are family allowances taxable?
No. Family allowances are tax-exempt contributions and do not contribute to forming taxable income. They are covered by social security and the federal reference body is the UFAS (Federal Office of Social Insurance), not the AFC or ESTV, which deal with federal taxation and VAT.
