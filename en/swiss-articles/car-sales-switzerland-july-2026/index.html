Cars in Switzerland: decline in July, BYD electric boom (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 August 2026

Registrations fell by 3.2% in July, but electrification continues. BYD overtakes Tesla in the Swiss electric car market.

Context

In brief

  • New car sales down 3.2% in July 2026.
  • Total of 19,000 new registrations in Switzerland.
  • Alternative propulsion systems account for 76% of the market share.
  • BYD overtakes Tesla in electric car sales.

Key Facts

  • What: Trend of the Swiss automotive market
  • When: July 2026
  • Where: Switzerland
  • Who: Auto-Switzerland
  • Amount: 3.2% decrease compared to the previous July

The Swiss car market slowed down in July 2026, following a brief recovery in June. According to data provided by the importers' association Auto-Switzerland, 19,000 fresh cars were put on the road, a decrease of 3.2% compared to the same period of the previous year. Despite this decline in overall volumes, the transition to greener engines continues to consolidate, profoundly influencing the choices of Swiss consumers.

The growth of the electricity sector

The detailed analysis for July reveals that alternative powertrains, which include electric, hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicles, achieved an overall market share of 76%, up sharply from 68% in July 2025. At the heart of this dynamic is the regular hybrid segment, which holds a 37% share, up from 35% a year earlier. The purely electric sector also shows considerable vigor, reaching 26% of the market, with 4500 cars registered and a growth of 20% on an annual basis. At the same time, plug-in hybrid stands at 14%, compared to 12% previously. On the contrary, traditional engines are losing ground: petrol cars are down to 18% from 24% last year, while diesel vehicles are down to 6% from the original 8%. This shift towards environmentally friendly technologies is the direct result of an aggressive commercial strategy by manufacturers, who offer a wide range in different price ranges, while ensuring improved ranges and reduced operating costs. The director of Auto-Switzerland, Thomas Rücker, emphasizes that this electrification is a gradual process, actively supported by importers despite the economic challenges, which remain, according to the executive, "immense".

Operational details

Analyzing the cumulative data for the first seven months of 2026, the picture of the Swiss market appears more complex. Overall, 135,500 cars were registered, an increase of 2.1% compared to the same period in 2025. This reflects the resilience of the sector over the long term, despite monthly fluctuations. Looking at the specific volumes by type of propulsion, it can be seen that hybrids reached 49,600 units (+2%), while electric cars stood at 32,900 units (+20%). On the contrary, classic engines suffered: petrol vehicles fell to 28,600 units (-13%) and diesels to 7600 (-23%). Plug-in hybrids closed the period with 16,900 registrations (+21%). Of particular note is the increase in the share of 4x4 vehicles, from 50% to 53%, confirming a Swiss consumer preference for all-wheel drive.

The challenge between BYD and Tesla

Perhaps the most striking figure of this period is the rise of the Chinese manufacturer BYD. In the brand comparison, BYD sold 3300 vehicles between January and July, marking a growth of 1197% compared to 300 units in the same period of 2025. Tesla, while maintaining a growth of 2% over the seven months, experienced a decidedly difficult month of July with only 100 cars sold (-78%), against BYD's 600. This competition reflects a profound shift in Swiss market preferences, where new international players are rapidly eroding market share from established leaders. Volkswagen remains the leading brand with 15,300 cars sold, followed by Skoda (12,900), BMW (11,200) and Mercedes (10,700). Regulatory pressure remains a sticking point: Auto-Switzerland continues its battle against overregulation, calling on politicians to improve the regulatory framework to strengthen the engine of the economy. Importers remain focused on optimizing the offer, trying to meet the needs of customers who are increasingly attentive to operating costs and environmental performance, in a macroeconomic context that requires constant adaptation and technological innovation to maintain competitiveness on national markets.

Key points

For Swiss consumers who are considering buying a new car in 2026, the market now offers diversified opportunities, especially in terms of incentives offered by manufacturers. Those looking to switch to electric or hybrid need to consider not only the list price, but more importantly the long-term running costs. The choice between a pure electric model and a plug-in hybrid depends strictly on the daily usage profile, in particular the availability of home or business charging points. The growing share of 4x4 vehicles, which now accounts for 53% of the total, indicates that many buyers continue to prioritize safety on the road, a crucial aspect in a country with the geographical characteristics of Switzerland. As offers continue to evolve, it is advisable to closely monitor the price lists and promotions that dealers activate to dispose of petrol and diesel models, which show higher rates of depreciation due to changing regulatory and market preferences.

What is the current market share of electric vehicles in Switzerland?

How does BYD position itself compared to Tesla in the Swiss market?
During the first seven months of 2026, BYD recorded significant growth of 1197%, selling 3,300 vehicles. Tesla maintained a 2% growth in the overall period, but suffered a sharp slowdown in July 2026 with only 100 cars registered, against BYD's 600, marking a monthly decline of 78%.
What type of propulsion dominates the Swiss market?
Alternative propulsions hold a total of 76% of the market. In particular, the normal hybrid is the most popular engine with 37% of sales, followed by the electric at 26% and the plug-in hybrid at 14%. Petrol engines account for 18% of the total, while diesel stops at 6%.
