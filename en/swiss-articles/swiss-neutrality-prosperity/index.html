Lorenzo Quadri, national councillor of the Lega dei Ticinesi, recently discussed the importance of Swiss neutrality. According to Quadri, neutrality is not a matter of right or left, but a fundamental pillar of the Swiss model. Neutrality has contributed to making Switzerland prosperous, safe, and open to the world. On September 27, Swiss citizens will be called upon to decide whether to continue with permanent and armed neutrality, which has allowed the country to diversify economic relations and make itself less blackmailable.

Opponents of the neutrality initiative are often those who would like to see Switzerland integrated into the European Union. Neutrality, in fact, represents an obstacle to submission to the EU. Quadri emphasizes that neutrality is one of the pillars of the Swiss model, along with direct democracy, federalism, independence, and the militia principle. Thanks to neutrality, Switzerland has become a privileged platform for international dialogue and mediation.

Neutrality and the economy

Neutrality allows Switzerland to diversify economic relations, making the country less dependent on the EU. This is particularly important in a context of globalization, where diversification is a priority. Neutrality, however, must be consistent and not applied only when it is convenient. It is not an easy path: it requires a straight back and the ability to resist.

Operational details

Practical implications of neutrality

Swiss neutrality has significant practical implications for the country's economy and politics. Diversifying economic relations allows Switzerland to be less dependent on the EU, reducing the risk of economic blackmail. This is particularly important in a context of globalization, where diversification is a priority.

Neutrality allows Switzerland to play an international mediation role, making the country a privileged platform for dialogue. This role is fundamental to maintaining peace and stability in a context of international tensions.

Neutrality and European integration

Opponents of the neutrality initiative see neutrality as an obstacle to integration into the European Union. However, neutrality is one of the pillars of the Swiss model, along with direct democracy, federalism, independence, and the militia principle. Neutrality allows Switzerland to maintain its autonomy and to determine its security policy independently.

Neutrality and security policy

On September 27, Swiss citizens will decide whether to continue determining their security policy autonomously or to delegate it to Brussels and NATO. Neutrality, sovereignty, and independence go hand in hand. Giving up neutrality means calling into question the other two as well.

Therefore, on September 27, a Yes to the neutrality initiative is needed.
