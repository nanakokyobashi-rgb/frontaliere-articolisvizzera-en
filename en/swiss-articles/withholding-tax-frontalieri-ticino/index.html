Withholding tax not refunded: changes for cross-border workers (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 3 June 2026

Find out why some cross-border workers no longer receive a withholding tax refund and what to do in these cases.

Context

In brief

  • Some cross-border workers are no longer receiving the withholding tax refund
  • The situation is causing discomfort and uncertainty among workers
  • It is important to understand the reasons and possible solutions

Key facts

  • What: Withholding tax not refunded to cross-border workers
  • When: Current situation
  • Where: Canton Ticino
  • Who: Cross-border workers employed in Switzerland and residing in Italy
  • Amount: Not specified

The issue of the unrefunded withholding tax is causing significant problems among cross-border workers in Ticino. Many of them suddenly find themselves without the refund they had always received, creating a sense of injustice and uncertainty. This change has particularly affected those who rely on this refund to balance their household budget.

The current situation

Withholding tax is a tax deduction applied directly to the salaries of cross-border workers. Normally, part of this tax is refunded through a reimbursement mechanism. However, recently, some cross-border workers have reported no longer receiving this refund, without a clear explanation.

Reasons for the change

The reasons for this change have not yet been clearly communicated by the competent authorities. However, it is hypothesized that it may be related to new tax regulations or changes in administrative processes. It is important for affected cross-border workers to seek information from the relevant offices to understand the specific reasons for their case.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

The situation of unreturned withholding tax raises several practical questions for cross-border workers. It's important to understand the implications of this change and how to address it.

Tax Implications

Not returning the withholding tax can have significant tax implications. Cross-border workers may find themselves having to pay additional taxes in Italy, as the refund of withholding tax is often used to offset Italian taxes. Without this refund, the overall tax burden could increase.

Impact on Family Budget

As mentioned, the loss of the withholding tax refund can have a significant impact on the family budget. It's important to review your budget and consider any cuts to non-essential expenses. Additionally, it may be useful to explore other sources of income or savings to compensate for the loss.

Possible Scenarios

There are several scenarios that could explain the unreturned withholding tax. One possible scenario is that Swiss authorities have changed the rules for refunds, making it more difficult to obtain the refund. Another scenario is that there have been administrative errors or delays in the refund process.

Comparison with Previous Situation

Before this change, cross-border workers could count on a regular refund of withholding tax. This refund was often automatic and did not require particular actions from the workers. Now, however, it seems that the process has become more complex and less transparent.

Key points

Action: What to do concretely

If you find yourself in the situation of no longer receiving the withholding tax refund, it is important to act promptly and in an organized manner. Here is a step-by-step guide on what to do.

Step 1: Contact your employer

The first step is to contact your employer to obtain specific information about your case. Ask if there have been any changes in the refund procedures and if there are any specific actions you need to take.

Step 2: Gather documentation

Collect all documentation related to your salary and taxes paid. This includes pay slips, tax returns, and any other relevant tax documentation.

Step 3: Contact the tax office

Contact the competent tax office to obtain specific information about your case. Ask if there have been any changes in tax regulations or administrative processes that could explain the failure to refund the withholding tax.

Step 4: Consult an expert

Turn to a tax consultant specializing in cross-border issues to receive personalized assistance. An expert can help you understand the tax implications of your case and find practical solutions.

Step 5: Evaluate alternative options

Explore other sources of income or savings to compensate for the loss of the refund. This might include looking for a second job, reducing non-essential expenses, or seeking other forms of financial support.
