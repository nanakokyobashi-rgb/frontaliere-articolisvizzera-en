Operational details

The crash on the A2 in Mezzovico caused widespread chaos, with car colonies up to 13 kilometers and motorists trapped for hours. The great League advisors ask to understand if the current procedures are still adequate for the management of such situations.

According to statistics, there are about 150,000 road accidents every year in Switzerland, an increase of 10% compared to 2020. This means that around 410 accidents occur every day, with a total of 150 cars damaged or destroyed every 24 hours. 📊

Switzerland is a country with a highly developed road network, with over 71,000 kilometres of roads, of which about 1,700 km of motorways. However, despite these figures, the accident on the A2 in Mezzovico highlighted a number of problems in the management of road emergencies.

According to sources, the accident was caused by a car suddenly stopping on the roadway, causing an immediate reaction from the drivers of the other vehicles. The root cause of the accident was attributed to a driving problem, with the driver losing control of their car.

Key points

The Council of State must answer the questions of the League Grand Councillors and provide clarification on the management of the incident on the A2. This is important to ensure safety and efficiency in traffic management. 📊

In Switzerland, the State Council is responsible for the management of road infrastructure, including motorways. However, responsibility for the handling of the accident and the subsequent reopening of the motorway rests with the local authorities of the canton where the accident is located. For example, in the case of the A2 accident, responsibility would lie with the authorities of the canton of Lucerne.

According to Swiss regulations, reopening a highway after an accident can take several hours, depending on the severity of the accident and the complexity of management. For example, Article 15 of the Regulation on the Use of Motorways (RS 0.723.1) states that the reopening of a motorway after an accident may take at least 2 hours, if the accident is serious and requires complex management.

In fact, handling the incident on the A2 was particularly complex due to the presence of heavy vehicles and the need to evacuate passengers. However, local authorities have worked hard to reopen the highway as soon as possible, ensuring the safety of travellers.
