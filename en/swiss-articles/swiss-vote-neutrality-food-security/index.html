Swiss vote: no to neutrality and food security

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 28 September 2026

70,1% reject the initiative on neutrality and 72,5% the one on food security. All cantons voted against it.

Context

In brief

  • Neutrality rejected by 70,1% of voters
  • Food security rejected by 72,5%
  • All cantons voted against

Key facts

  • Initiative → Safeguard Neutrality
  • Result → 70,1% no
  • Promoters → Pro Suisse and members of the UDC/SVP
  • Objective → stricter interpretation of neutrality
  • Opposition → Federal Council and almost all major parties
  • Initiative → For Safe Food
  • Result → 72,5% no
  • Objectives → food self-sufficiency and protection of groundwater resources

70,1% of voters rejected the «Safeguard Neutrality» initiative, launched by Pro Suisse and members of the UDC/SVP. All cantons voted against the proposal, which called for a stricter interpretation of Swiss neutrality to be included in the Constitution.

The text had support mainly from conservative groups, along with part of the left. The Federal Council and almost all major parties had instead opposed the initiative. The UDC/SVP is identified by the source as the exception to this opposing front.

The vote on food security

The rejection was even stronger for the «For Safe Food» initiative. 72,5% of voters rejected the proposal, and in this case too all cantons voted against it.

Among other objectives, supporters wanted to increase Switzerland's food self-sufficiency and better protect groundwater resources. The approach indicated involved shifting food production, described in the source as heavily based on livestock farming, toward more plant-based production.

The two initiatives therefore concerned distinct issues: the first neutrality and its constitutional interpretation, the second the food production model, self-sufficiency and the protection of underground water resources. The common factor is the opposing vote expressed in all cantons.

Operational details

What the two results indicate

For those who live or work in Switzerland, the practical information contained in the source is that neither proposal obtained the voters' approval. The first would have sought a stricter interpretation of neutrality in the Constitution; the second would have pursued objectives related to food self-sufficiency, groundwater and more plant-based production.

The two results do not have the same intensity. The 70.1% of votes against «Safeguard Neutrality» and the 72.5% of votes against «For Safe Food» show that rejection of the second initiative was broader. The source does not attribute this difference to a single reason and does not indicate individual voters' motivations.

A uniform orientation

The vote also presents a clear territorial element: all cantons rejected both initiatives. For interpreting the national result, this means that the source does not identify cantons in favor of one proposal and cantons opposed to the other. Thus, based on the data provided, no geographical distinction emerges to apply to the two votes.

On the political level, the position on the text concerning neutrality was clearly divided. Support came primarily from conservative groups and part of the left, while the Federal Council and almost all major parties were opposed, with the UDC/SVP indicated as the exception.

The food proposal had a different direction and was more closely linked to production: greater self-sufficiency, better protection of groundwater resources and a shift from production heavily based on livestock farming to more plant-based production. Key points

How to correctly interpret the outcome

A practical reading of the vote requires keeping the two questions separate. The first concerns Swiss neutrality and the proposal for a stricter constitutional interpretation of it. The second concerns food security and the objectives indicated by its supporters regarding self-sufficiency, groundwater and plant production.

Four useful steps

1. Identify the name of the question: «Safeguard Neutrality» or «For Safe Food». 2. Record the percentage of votes against: 70,1% for the first text and 72,5% for the second. 3. Verify the territorial data reported by the source: all Cantons voted against both initiatives. 4. Link each result to its own objective, without conflating neutrality and food policy.

This framework makes it possible to use the numbers correctly even in local discussions: the vote on neutrality does not concern food self-sufficiency, while the vote on «For Safe Food» does not concern the constitutional interpretation of neutrality. The source does not report any further deadlines, documents or administrative procedures linked to the two results.
