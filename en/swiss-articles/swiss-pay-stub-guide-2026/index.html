The Swiss pay slip is mandatory documentation that every employer is required to provide to its employees. It contains basic data relating to the pay period, gross salary and deductions made.

The fundamental principles

The Swiss payroll is regulated by the Federal Civil Code (CCF), which establishes the minimum requirements for salary documentation. The gross salary is what the employer must pay to the employee, while the net salary is what the employee collects after having incurred deductions.

Mandatory withholdings

Mandatory withholdings are those that the employer is required to make on behalf of the employee. These withholdings are:

  • Social security contribution: 3.4% of the gross salary, allocated to the social security fund of the canton of Bern.
  • Pension contribution: 2.5% of the gross salary, allocated to the pension fund of the canton of Geneva.

Optional withholdings

Optional withholdings are those that the employer can make on behalf of the employee, but is not obliged to do so. These withholdings are:

  • Pension fund contribution: 5% of the gross salary, allocated to the employee's pension fund.
  • Reserve fund contribution: 2% of the gross salary, allocated to the employee's reserve fund.

The Swiss pay slip contains various deductions that are made on the gross salary. These withholdings are:

  • AVS/AI/IPG: basic social insurance

  • LPP: occupational pension

Each withholding has a specific amount that is calculated based on the employee's gross salary.

Fixed and variable withholdings

Most deductions are fixed and are calculated based on your gross salary. However, some withholdings vary based on factors such as the employee's age, gross salary and length of employment contract. For example, the unemployment insurance (AD) withholding varies between 2.2% and 3.7% of gross wages, depending on the employee's age.

Fixed deductions

Fixed deductions are those that do not vary depending on gross salary. The main fixed deductions are:

  • AVS/AI/IPG (10,6%): This deduction covers the contribution to basic Swiss insurance (AVS), disability insurance (AI) and accident insurance (IPG). The contribution is divided equally between the employee and the employer.
  • AD (2,2%): This deduction covers the contribution to unemployment insurance (AD). The contribution is divided equally between the employee and the employer.

Variable deductions

Variable deductions are those that vary depending on gross salary. The main variable deductions are:

  • LAINF (0,7-1,5%): This deduction covers the contribution to disability insurance (LAINF). The contribution varies depending on gross salary and the degree of disability.
  • LPP (7-18%): This deduction covers the contribution to the pension fund (LPP). The contribution varies depending on gross salary and the contribution level.
