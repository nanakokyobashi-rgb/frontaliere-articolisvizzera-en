Drought in Switzerland: 'water thieves' sprout, 46% worried (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 17 August 2026

Dry fountains and rationing: the Swiss water crisis between theft and alarms for the future. 60% foresee difficulties within 10 years.

Context

In a nutshell

  • 46% of Swiss concerned about water supply
  • Fountains closed in Veltheim (AG) after water theft with cisterns
  • 87% fear an increase in forest fires due to drought
  • 142 litres real daily consumption per capita (perceived: 79 litres)

Key facts

  • What: Water crisis with water theft and social alarm
  • When: August 2026, survey conducted late July-early August
  • Where: National Switzerland (emblematic case of Veltheim, Aargau)
  • Who: General population, water experts, municipality of Veltheim
  • Amount: 46% worried, 60% foresees future difficulties

AARAU - Drought transforms water into"blue gold", unleashing unprecedented phenomena such as "water thieves". In Veltheim (AG), a municipality of 1,600 inhabitants, public fountains were closed after illegal withdrawals of large amounts of water, even with submerged tanks and pumps. This episode reflects a national concern: 46% of Swiss say they are alarmed about their water supply, according to a Marketagent survey of 1,000 people between July and August 2026.

43% already consider the current situation problematic. Looking to the future is even gloomier: 60% foresee difficulties in the availability of water within the next ten years. Almost nine out of ten respondents fear catastrophic repercussions: 87% fear the increase in forest fires, 85% damage to agriculture (already affected by crop losses), and 57% health risks

Operational details

National consequences: from ecology to economy

The water crisis is not just a local emergency. 87% of Swiss people link drought to an increased risk of forest fires, an ecological threat with high costs for the community. 85% foresee damage to agriculture, a sector already in crisis due to crop losses, with repercussions on food prices and national food security.

Perception-reality gap in consumption

Switzerland's per capita water consumption is among the highest in Europe (142 litres/day), but the population estimates using only 79 litres. This gap reveals a lack of awareness of the real needs. Bridging this gap through information campaigns could be crucial for a national water management strategy, urged by experts.

Sectors at risk

In addition to agriculture, sectors such as tourism (swimming pools, golf courses) and industry (plant cooling) could be restricted. Municipalities could introduce mandatory rations, as already happened in Veltheim, with impacts on the cost of living. Access to water could become a factor in choosing health insurance, if rates reflect water management costs.

Key points

What can residents of Switzerland do?

Reduce daily consumption

Small collective gestures can make the difference: repairing leaks, using appliances at full load, preferring short showers (5 minutes instead of 10 saves 50 litres). Consumption monitoring via smart meters can help you meet any future quotas.

Support water policies

Citizens can click on the cantons to:

  • Adopt progressive rates for water (those who consume more pay more)
  • Invest in anti-waste infrastructure (efficient water networks, collection basins)
  • Promote low water consumption agriculture

Prepare for Restrictions

In the case of rationing, priority should be given to essential uses (hygiene, cooking). Private cisterns may become necessary in some areas. Check cantonal regulations on water accumulation.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why did the 'water thieves' appear in Switzerland?
Prolonged drought has reduced water basin levels, prompting some citizens to illegally draw water from public fountains, as happened in Veltheim (AG), where even cisterns were used.
How much water does a Swiss person actually consume per day?
The average real consumption is 142 litres per capita per day, although respondents estimate using only 79 litres, showing a significant underestimate.
What are the most feared consequences of water scarcity?
87% fear the increase in forest fires, 85% damage to agriculture, and 57% health risks related to the shortage of drinking water.
How can I reduce my water consumption?
Repair leaks, use appliances at full load, reduce shower life, and monitor consumption with smart meters. Find out about cantonal incentives for low-flow devices.
