Public holidays in the Canton of Zurich: guide to the calendar and rules

How to distinguish cantonal holidays from federal ones.

  • The canton must be distinguished from the federal holiday calendar
  • Closures also depend on the municipality and institution
  • Business hours follow rules that need to be checked

In the canton of Zurich, the public holiday calendar is used to identify observances recognized by the canton and compare them with federal public holidays. For those who live or work in the canton, the distinction is the first step in correctly reading a date and understanding which institutional level to consult.

The control becomes concrete when the date concerns an office, a service or a commercial activity. The reference source indicates that public holidays and business hours may depend on the canton, municipality or competent institution. The presence of an anniversary in the cantonal calendar, therefore, must be accompanied by verification of the actual closure of the institution concerned.

The annual calendar as a starting point

The practical reference is the official source of the canton, where you can check the annual calendar of days recognized by the Canton of Zurich. The consultation does not require transforming the calendar into a list valid for every situation: it serves to establish the cantonal framework from which to start.

The second step is the comparison with federal holidays. The corner of the guide expressly distinguishes the two plans: that of the days recognized by the canton and the federal one. Without this comparison, a person can use a cantonal observance as if it were a federal rule, or apply a federal reference to a service organized at the cantonal level.

Office closures require further reading. If the appointment concerns a cantonal or municipal administration, the official reference linked to the competent authority must be followed. If it concerns another institution or business, the verification must include the rule applied by that entity. The source does not replace the control of the individual opening with a general rule.

Federal bodies also have different responsibilities and should not be confused. AFC/ESTV concerns direct federal tax and VAT; UFAS/BSV concerns social security, between AVS/AHV, AI and LPP/BVG; FSO/BFS is about statistics. For the calendar of public holidays in the Canton of Zurich, the reference remains the official cantonal source, with any control at the competent municipality or institution.

The calendar is therefore an organizational tool for the year, not an automatically valid list for each office or service. For spending and organizational decisions, it can be combined with costo della vita in Svizzera, keeping the opening verification separate.

Operational details

Reading the calendar without confusing the levels

The practical value of the guide emerges when the same date is used for different purposes. Knowing that a day is recognized by the Canton of Zurich answers the question at the cantonal level; knowing whether an office is closed instead requires checking the authority or institution that manages it. For commercial activities, the applicable business hours come into play.

The source places public holidays and business hours among the areas that may depend on the canton, municipality, or competent institution. The calendar should therefore be read as a starting point for choosing the right source, not as automatic confirmation of every opening.

Hypothetical scenario: anyone who needs to go to an office can ask themselves, in sequence, three questions. Is the day recognized by the Canton of Zurich? Does the office belong to the canton or the municipality? Has the institution announced a closure? The first question uses the official calendar, the second identifies the administrative level, and the third checks the actual opening. None of the three should be replaced with a generic calendar.

Different hypothetical scenario: anyone who needs to access a commercial activity must check not only the day recognized by the canton, but also the applicable business hours. The source indeed points out that this area may follow cantonal, municipal, or competent institution rules. The check should be carried out before organizing access to the service.

For those organizing work, appointments, and expenses, separating the three levels reduces the risk of attributing to all of Switzerland a rule specific to the Canton of Zurich. Comparing it with the federal plan helps keep the references clear, while the local check helps establish the situation of the institution concerned.

Key points

How to check the annual calendar

The check can become a brief routine, to be repeated for each relevant year. The order of the steps separates cantonal recognition, federal comparison and the actual closure of the service.

Five-step procedure

1. Define the subject. Establishing whether you need to know the days recognized by the canton, verify the closure of an office or check business hours prevents you from using a source unsuitable for the question.

2. Open the canton’s official source. Consult the annual calendar of the Canton of Zurich and use it as a reference for the days recognized at cantonal level.

3. Make the federal comparison. Keep the days recognized by the canton separate from federal public holidays; the comparison ensures that one list is not turned into the other.

4. Check the specific authority. For a cantonal or municipal service, verify the reference provided by the competent administration. For another institution, check the organization’s communication; for a commercial activity, verify the applicable hours.

5. Record the answer in the operational calendar. Note the closure, any applicable hours and the source consulted, so that you can repeat the check when planning another year.

When an additional check is needed

If the official cantonal source does not clarify whether the specific office is open, it is not advisable to derive the answer from a general rule. The next step is to verify with the competent municipality or institution, because the source indicates that public holidays and business hours may depend on these levels. The same procedure applies when the service is managed by an entity other than the cantonal administration.

Distinguishing the areas of responsibility also prevents you from looking for the calendar in the wrong offices. The annual calendar remains a cantonal check; the actual closure requires the reference of the authority managing the service. For those who live or work in Switzerland, this sequence makes it possible to organize appointments and activities without replacing an official source with a general indication.

Frequently Asked Questions
How to distinguish cantonal from federal holidays?
In the Canton of Zurich, the calendar of public holidays serves to identify the anniversaries recognized by the canton. It's critical to compare this list to federal holidays, as these are two separate plans. The official source of the canton is the necessary starting point for establishing the cantonal framework, avoiding erroneously applying a federal rule to a service managed at the cantonal level or vice versa.
Who manages the closures of offices and institutions in the Canton of Zurich?
The closures depend on the competent authority or institution. If the appointment concerns a cantonal or municipal administration, the official reference of the body must be consulted. For other institutions or businesses, the verification must include the specific rule applied by thatentity. The cantonal calendar does not guarantee the automatic opening of each service, so it is always necessary to check with the municipality or the reference body.
What is the correct procedure to check the openings?
The procedure involves five steps: define the object (office, business hours, holidays), consult the official source of the canton for the annual calendar, perform the comparison with federal holidays, check the authority or institution responsible for the specific closure, and finally record the information in the operational calendar. This sequence allows you to organize appointments and activities precisely, associating each question with the correct authority.
