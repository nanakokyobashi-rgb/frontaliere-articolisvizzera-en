Permit B Bern: requirements and renewal

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 21 August 2026

Residence permit B Canton Bern: requirements, documentation, duration and renewal procedure. Complete and step-by-step guidance from SEM authority.

Context

In brief

  • B permit: renewable residence permit for foreigners in Switzerland with an intermediate duration
  • Variable duration: renewal depends on continuity of employment and residence
  • Renewal not automatic: submit an application 3-6 months before expiry
  • Canton of Bern Migration Office: competent authority for permit management

Key facts

  • What: B residence permit (renewable) for foreigners
  • Where: Canton of Bern, managed by the cantonal migration authority
  • Who: SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) coordinates, cantons administer
  • Nature: Intermediate and renewable residence, different from L (short-term) and C (permanent residence)
  • Requirements: Stable employment and suitable accommodation in the canton
  • Renewal: Not automatic, assessed on a case-by-case basis

The B residence permit represents one of the three main pillars of the Swiss migration system for foreigners. Unlike the L permit (short duration of up to 1 year, non-renewable) and the C permit (permanent residence normally after 10 years of residence, or 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens), the B permit allows residence of an intermediate duration that is fully renewable based on the holder's employment and residential situation.

In the Canton of Bern, as in all Swiss cantons, the administrative management of residence permits falls under the cantonal migration office, in coordination with the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) at the federal level. This means that any application for the granting, renewal or modification of B permit status must be submitted to the competent cantonal authority, which assesses the request according to common federal criteria and specific cantonal considerations.

Characteristics of the B residence permit

The B permit differs from category L due to its renewable nature. While the L permit has a maximum validity of 1 year and is granted for temporary or project-based employment, the B permit allows longer periods of residence (usually 1-5 years), renewable upon expiry subject to ongoing verification of the employment and residence conditions. Renewal is not automatic: the cantonal authorities assess each application, determining whether the foreign national continues to meet the requirements that justified its initial issuance.

In the Canton of Bern, the cantonal migration office verifies during each renewal cycle the continuity of the employment situation (stable employment with a registered employer), the availability of suitable accommodation that complies with cantonal housing standards, and the absence of factors that could compromise lawful residence (criminal convictions, insolvency, etc.). This system allows for flexible migration management, enabling the canton to adapt the number of authorized foreigners to the availability of jobs and suitable accommodation.

Operational details

Requirements and documentation for the B permit

The application for a B residence permit — whether initial or renewal — is based on requirements clearly defined at national level, although their practical application may vary slightly between cantons. The foreign national must first demonstrate that they have stable employment, usually proven by an employment contract or documentation from the employer. Secondly, they must prove the availability of suitable accommodation in the Canton of Bern, compliant with cantonal regulations on housing quality.

The Bernese cantonal migration office typically requires the following documentation for a renewal application:

  • Valid employment contract confirmed by the employer
  • Proof of accommodation (properly registered lease agreement, owner's certificate, or equivalent)
  • Valid passport and photocopy
  • Criminal record certificates from the federal authorities and the authorities of the country of origin (if applicable)
  • Income tax returns for the last 2 years or a bank certificate attesting to the ability to support oneself
  • Completed and signed cantonal application form

If, during the permit period, the holder has changed employer or accommodation, they must report it promptly and attach the new documentation. Significant changes (loss of employment, change of accommodation to another municipality) may affect the outcome of the renewal.

Renewal procedure and timelines

Renewal of the B permit is not automatic and requires action by the holder. Approximately 3-6 months before the permit expires, the foreign national must contact the Bern cantonal migration office and submit a renewal application following the form and instructions provided by the office itself. The exact timing varies: some offices accept applications 6 months in advance, while others only accept them 3 months in advance. Checking directly with the office avoids administrative delays.

During the assessment period for the renewal application — usually 2-4 weeks for complete files, up to 6-8 weeks if the documentation is incomplete — the permit holder remains authorized to reside and work in the canton even if the permit has formally already expired (transitional protection). This rule prevents situations of administrative illegality during the review process. However, if the application is not submitted BEFORE expiry, this protection may not apply: for this reason, it is critical to comply with the deadlines.

If the employment conditions cease to exist (prolonged unemployment without employment prospects), the cantonal office may deny the renewal or make it conditional. Likewise, loss of accommodation or moving outside the canton entails a review of the permit conditions.

Key points

Step-by-step renewal procedure

Step 1: Early verification (4-6 months before expiration) Contact the cantonal migration office of the Canton of Bern (contact information is generally available on the canton’s website, migration section). Verify the documentation required for your specific case, as individual situations (change of employer, change of accommodation, etc.) may involve slightly different requirements.

Step 2: Collection and preparation of documentation Prepare an updated employment contract (recent, signed), lease agreement (or certificate of ownership if you are the owner), original passport and copy, criminal record certificates from the competent authorities, and tax returns for the last 2 years. If you changed employers during your current permit, make sure the new contract is registered with the cantonal authorities and that the previous employer has formally notified the termination of the employment relationship.

Step 3: Submission of the renewal application Submit the application to the Bern cantonal migration office following the indicated procedures (paper-based, online, or mixed). Attach all required documentation as certified copies. I recommend obtaining confirmation of receipt of the application, even if this is not mandatory.

Step 4: Administrative review The office will examine the application and documents. If the documentation is incomplete, it will contact the applicant to provide additional information (generally 2-3 weeks to complete). If everything complies, the review will proceed toward completion.

Step 5: Notification of the outcome The office will usually communicate the outcome (approval, refusal, or approval with conditions) by official letter. If approved, you will receive a new permit document with a new expiration date (validity usually 1-5 years, at the office’s discretion based on the stability of the employment situation).

Critical deadlines

The renewal application must be submitted before the formal expiration of permit B. If you forget to renew and the permit expires, your administrative situation becomes irregular: your right of residence and right to work cease immediately. Therefore, set a calendar reminder at least 5-6 months before the expiration date indicated on the permit.

If the permit expires during the review of the renewal application, you generally remain protected by the "transitional rule" — authorized to continue residing and working until the review is completed — but this protection is not always guaranteed. I recommend checking directly with the cantonal office to confirm whether it applies in your specific case.

Competent office and resources

The cantonal migration office of the Canton of Bern is the sole competent authority for all matters relating to residence permits (issuance, renewal, modification, cancellation, appeals). You can contact it directly via the telephone number or email published on the website of the Republic and Canton of Bern, administration section.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between permit B and permit L?
The L permit has a maximum duration of 1 year and is not renewable, granted for temporary or project work. The B permit is a renewable residence with a longer duration (usually 1-5 years), conditional on maintaining employment and accommodation. The B permit is therefore more stable over time, but requires continuity of work.
What happens if I lose my job during the B leave?
You must inform the cantonal migration office promptly. Loss of employment does not result in automatic revocation, but the office will assess whether the conditions that justified the leave still exist. If you remain unemployed for a long time, the next renewal may be refused or granted with stricter constraints.
How long before the expiry do I have to request the renewal of permit B?
Generally, you need to apply for renewal 3-6 months before the deadline. The exact timing depends on the procedures of the cantonal migration office of the Canton of Bern. I recommend that you contact the office directly to find out the specific terms and avoid the risk of expiration.
What documentation do I need to renew the B permit in Canton Bern?
You need: valid employment contract, proof of accommodation (lease), passport, criminal certificates, and proof of income (tax return or bank certificate). The cantonal migration office Bern may require further documentation if you have changed employer, address, or if there are other significant changes in your situation.
If my B permit expires during the renewal investigation, do I remain authorized to work?
Generally yes, thanks to the transitional rule: you remain authorized to reside and work until the office has concluded the examination of the renewal application. However, it is not guaranteed in all cases. I recommend contacting the cantonal migration office Bern to confirm the situation in your specific case.
