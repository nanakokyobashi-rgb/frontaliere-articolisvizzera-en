Inheritance taxes in the Canton of Bern (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 14 August 2026

Know the kinship rates and exemptions for spouses and descendants

Context

The inheritance and gift tax in the Canton of Bern is governed by cantonal law. There are different rates depending on the degree of kinship. There are exemptions for spouse and descendants.

Bernese law provides for a complex approach, with rates ranging from 0% to 4% depending on the degree of kinship. For example, for spouses, the inheritance tax is 0% for amounts up to CHF 200,000, while for amounts over CHF200,000 it is 2%. For children, the inheritance tax is 0% for amounts up to CHF 100,000, while for amounts over CHF100,000 it is 1%.

For other relatives, the inheritance tax is higher. For example, for parents, the inheritance tax is 2% for amounts up to CHF 50,000, while for amounts over CHF50,000 it is 3%. For brothers and sisters, the inheritance tax is 3% for amounts up to CHF 20,000, while for amounts over CHF20,000 it is 4%.

There are also exemptions for donations. For example, donations between spouses are exempt from tax, while donations between parents and children are only exempt for amounts up to CHF 100,000.

Bernese law also provides for a specific approach for companies. For example, if a person dies and leaves a company, the inheritance tax is 2% for amounts up to CHF 500,000, while for amounts over CHF500,000 it is 3%.

Cantonal law regulates inheritance and gift tax in the Canton of Bern, following principles of equity and proportionality. Rates vary depending on the degree of kinship, with lower rates for close relatives and higher rates for distant relatives. There are exemptions for spouse and descendants, as provided for by federal law and cantonal regulations.

Rates and exemptions

The rates for inheritance and gift tax in the Canton of Bern are set by the cantonal law of 2018. Taxes range from a minimum of 2% for close relatives to a maximum of 40% for distant relatives. Spouses and descendants are exempt from taxes, as required by the federal law of 2008.
