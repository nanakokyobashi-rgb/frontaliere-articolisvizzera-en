The path of real estate purchase in the Canton of St. Gallen

Buying property in the Canton of St. Gallen is an important decision that involves a cascade of financial and administrative factors. It's not just a matter of finding the right property: behind it is a complex structure of assessments, financing and tax levies that follow precise rules.

The first step is to assess your financial capacity. Those who want to buy do not start from scratch: they need their own funds (the initial capital paid at the time of signing) and a mortgage to cover the rest. Swiss banks assess the sustainability of this debt based on the buyer's income, other existing liabilities and the stability of its status

Operational details

Three-level taxation and the transfer tax

The Swiss tax system operates on three levels simultaneously: federal, cantonal, and municipal. Anyone buying a house in the Canton of St. Gallen enters directly into this multi-layered system, as the acquisition of real estate triggers tax obligations at both the cantonal and municipal levels. Each level has different competences: federal taxation mainly concerns direct federal tax and VAT, while cantonal and municipal administration handles real estate property taxes, the transfer tax, and municipal fees.

The most relevant element for buyers is the transfer tax (also known as the 'acquisition tax' in some cantons). This is a cantonal and/or municipal levy that applies to the transfer of ownership. Unlike other property taxes, the transfer tax is paid only once at the time of purchase and varies significantly from canton to canton: some cantons have higher rates, others lower; some municipalities within the same canton may apply additional tiers.

Notary procedures and registration

Before becoming the official owner, the buyer must use the services of a notary, who drafts the deed of sale and manages the payments. The notary verifies that the seller is indeed the owner, that there are no hidden mortgages, and that all costs (including the transfer tax owed to the canton and the municipality) are paid correctly. Notary fees vary based on the value of the property and according to the rates set by the canton.

Once the notarial deed has been drawn up and all funds have been paid, the notary submits the file to the cantonal land registry. This is where the transfer of ownership becomes official: the buyer appears as the new owner in the registry, the mortgage is registered as security for the debt to the bank, and the sale is completed. This process is an integral part of your pension planning: a real estate investment reduces the capital to be paid into other retirement instruments.

Managing total costs

Those buying a house must consider not only the purchase price. In addition to the mortgage and own funds, the calculation includes notary fees (typically a percentage of the property's value), the transfer tax, and potentially a registration fee. The total of these additional costs can reach up to 5-10% of the purchase price, depending on the tax structure of the municipality in the Canton of St. Gallen.

Furthermore, costs do not end with the purchase: every owner will have to pay real estate property taxes (cantonal and municipal property tax), maintenance expenses, and any contributions for condominium improvements. It is therefore important to consider a property purchase not as an occasional expense, but as a decision that affects the family budget for decades.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the minimum own funds required to buy a house in the Canton of St. Gallen?
Swiss banks generally require between 20% and 30% of the value of the property as own funds. Some institutions accept 10-15% with more restrictive conditions (higher rates, additional insurance contributions). There is no uniform federal obligation: each bank decides according to its risk profile and its internal directives. Contact credit institutions directly to find out their exact requirements and specific offers.
What are the hidden expenses to consider when buying a home?
In addition to the purchase price and mortgage installments, consider: notary fees (typically 0.5-1.5% of the price), transfer tax (cantonal/municipal, varies widely), registration in the land register, property insurance, municipal property taxes, and ordinary and extraordinary maintenance. The total of these additional costs in the first year can reach 5-10% of the purchase price.
How do you calculate the sustainability of a mortgage?
Banks check that the monthly installments of the mortgage, plus any other debts, do not exceed a certain percentage of gross income (typically 30-35%). They also apply a stress test: what would happen if interest rates rose by 1-2 percentage points? If the situation remains sustainable, the bank will grant the loan. Use an online calculator to assess your ability to pay in advance.
What does the notary do when buying a house in the Canton of St. Gallen?
The notary draws up the deed of sale, verifies the seller's property titles at the land register, coordinates payments between buyer and seller, deposits the dossier at the cantonal land register, and ensures that the transfer tax is paid to the competent authorities (canton and municipality). It is the legal guarantor that the transaction is legal, complete and correctly recorded.
Does the transfer tax really vary from canton to canton?
Yes, significantly. Each canton (and sometimes each municipality) sets its own pass-through tax rates. In the Canton of St. Gallen, as in the other Swiss cantons, the rate depends on the value of the property and the local tax structure. Contact the specific municipality where you intend to buy to find out the exact amount applicable to your case.
