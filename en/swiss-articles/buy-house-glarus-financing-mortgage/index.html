Buying a house in Switzerland is not just a housing choice, but a decision that involves three distinct levels of taxation. When you buy a property in the canton of Glarus, like in any other canton, you must consider the federal direct tax (IFD) on potential capital gains, the transfer tax managed by the cantonal administration, and municipal taxes applied with local multipliers. The competence of the Federal Administration of Customs (AFC/ESTV) concerns only the IFD, while the transfer tax is entirely a matter of cantonal law: the Glarus fixes its own law and rates, different from Zurich, Bern, or any other canton.

Notarial and procedural fees for drafting the purchase agreement follow federal common regulations throughout Switzerland. Banks operating in Glarus structure mortgage loans according to their own criteria and personal risk assessments, but are indirectly influenced by the base rates of the Swiss National Bank (BNS/SNB), which guides the general market conditions.

Operational details

Cost Comparison: Who Pays What

When you buy a house in the canton of Glarus, the costs are not limited to the property price and the mortgage. You must add: the federal IFD (if you sell within the time frame set by the cantonal law), the cantonal transfer tax (the real discriminator between cantons, as each canton has different tax rates), the municipal taxes (the municipality applies a multiplier based on the cantonal taxable base), and the notary fees (fixed based on the property value, not varying by canton).

The mortgage interest rate depends on the bank's risk assessment and the general market environment. A bank operating in Glarus applies similar interest rates to a bank in Zurich, as the capital markets are integrated at the national level. The Swiss National Bank (SNB) does not directly set the interest rates for retail mortgages, but indirectly influences the cost of money through the discount rate and its monetary policy decisions. When the SNB raises the discount rate, banks tend to increase the interest rates on mortgages. This mechanism works the same way in Glarus as in Geneva, Bern, or Basel.

Practical Scenario: A Typical Sale

Let's assume a buyer purchasing a property in Glarus. The accessory costs are not universally fixed: they depend on the cantonal transfer tax (which varies significantly), the municipal taxes (which vary by specific municipality), and the amount of the mortgage granted by the bank. If the buyer takes a mortgage for 70% of the price, the bank considers the mortgage risk, disposable income, and overall credit situation. The interest rate reflects market conditions, not a cantonal decision.

Key points

How to Proceed: Step-by-Step for Buying a House

Ecco la procedura standard quando decidi di acquistare un immobile nel canton Glarona.

1. Preliminary Checks: Before signing the contract, have the cadastral and mortgage registry checked by the competent office of the canton Glarona. This ensures that the property is free from mortgages and that the ownership is clear.

2. Sales Agreement: Negotiate the price and terms with the seller. At this stage, you can involve a notary to draft the preliminary contract and assess the additional costs.

3. Loan Request: Present yourself to a bank with the financial documents (last 3 years of income tax returns, bank statements, employment contracts or letters). The bank evaluates your creditworthiness and the amount that can be financed.

4. Property Valuation: The bank orders an appraisal to verify that the property value matches the agreed price and represents an acceptable mortgage risk.

5. Notarial Deed: The notary drafts the sales contract and coordinates all parties (bank, seller, cantonal administration).

6. Additional Costs Verification: Before signing, the notary informs you of the exact amount of the transfer tax calculated according to the cantonal regulations of Glarona. Contact the cantonal administration for technical questions on the calculation basis.

7. Signing and Registration: Once the notary has signed the deed, it is registered at the cadastral and mortgage registry of the canton Glarona. From that moment on, you are the legal owner.

8. Loan Activation: The bank transfers the funds to the notary, who then passes them to the seller according to the agreed-upon terms.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the three tax levels when buying a home in Switzerland?
Direct Federal Tax (DFT) affects real estate earnings if the property is sold within specific cantonal timelines. The transfer tax is a cantonal responsibility and varies considerably between cantons. Municipal taxes are applied with multipliers on the cantonal tax base. Each level has its own rules and rates.
Who handles the pass-through tax in the canton of Glarus?
The Glarus cantonal administration is responsible for the transfer tax. It is the exclusive competence of the canton: each Swiss canton has different laws and rates. The buyer pays this tax to the canton during the cadastral registration procedure. To find out the exact amount, please contact the Glarus cantonal tax administration.
How does the Swiss National Bank affect my mortgage?
The SNB does not set mortgage rates directly, but influences the cost of money through directing rates. When the SNB raises rates, banks tend to raise mortgage rates. This mechanism acts at the national level: in the Glarus the conditions are similar to other cantons, because the capital market is integrated.
What is the role of the notary in the purchase and sale of real estate?
The notary draws up the deed of sale, verifies the legitimacy of the transaction and checks that there are no previous mortgages on the mortgage register. It coordinates all parties (buyer, seller, bank) and ensures that the contract follows federal and cantonal rules. After signing, the notary records the deed at the land registry.
Where can I find official data on property prices in the canton of Glarus?
The Federal Statistical Office (UST/BFS) publishes data on property prices at the national level and by canton. These data are public and useful to assess whether the price of a property is competitive with the local market. The cantonal administration of the Glarus can also provide specific regional statistics.
