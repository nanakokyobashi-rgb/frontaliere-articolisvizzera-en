In September, the average cost per kilo of Whey products reached nearly 55 francs, according to Galaxus. The comparison given by the online retailer is with the 46-franc average for 2025. These are supplements derived from milk whey, among the most widely used.

Why prices are moving

The price movement is linked to growing demand. Galaxus cites both those who want to increase muscle mass and those who wish to control their weight. The explanation is contained in a statement attributed to Martina Tatavitto, sector manager at the retailer.

According to Tatavitto, dairies and producers are now close to their limit, and supply cannot keep pace. Meanwhile, more expensive milk, energy and packaging directly weigh on production prices.

The figure per kilo is therefore the reference point for interpreting the report and tracking spending related to this category in costo della vita in Svizzera. Galaxus does not present the figure as a single price for every product, but as an average for Whey.

For those seeking an alternative to whey, Martina Tatavitto points to casein and plant-based protein powders. According to the advice reported, these options cost up to 40% less than whey proteins. However, the difference does not eliminate the need to try them, because it is necessary to understand what one's body tolerates well.

In closing, Galaxus specialists remind us that even the most expensive and sophisticated protein powders cannot work miracles if appropriate training and a balanced diet are lacking. Price alone cannot replace these two conditions.

The first scenario is that of someone who wants to keep spending down. The source indicates two specific alternatives to whey: casein and plant-based protein powders. The caveat to bear in mind is the wording up to: 40% represents the maximum reduction indicated, not a guaranteed saving for every purchase. The comparison therefore serves to guide the search, not to set a final price.

The second criterion is tolerability. Galaxus's advice is to try it to understand what one's body tolerates well. The lower cost does not, by itself, say which option is suitable for an individual. This check comes before presenting an alternative as a valid choice for everyone.

The third criterion concerns the objective. Rising demand is associated both with increasing muscle mass and with weight control. Finally, there is the supply side. How to turn data into a choice

Galaxus's report can become a small comparison procedure for anyone who has to choose between whey protein and the alternatives mentioned. The goal is not to establish which product is best overall, but to order the steps using the available data.

Five practical steps

1. Start with the unit of measurement. Use the cost per kilogram as the basis for comparison. The September figure is almost 55 francs for Whey, while the 2025 average was 46 francs. This keeps the reference tied to the measurement communicated by the retailer.

2. Compare the alternatives side by side. Include casein and plant-based protein powders in the same assessment. Galaxus reports that they can cost up to 40% less than whey protein. The percentage indicates a limit, not a fixed price for every product.

3. Try them and observe tolerability. The advice reported by Martina Tatavitto is to check what your body tolerates well. This is a concrete step: before basing the choice solely on savings, you need to consider your personal response to the product.

4. Connect the purchase to the routine indicated by specialists. A supplement is not presented as sufficient on its own. Adequate training and a balanced diet remain the conditions highlighted by Galaxus, even when expensive or sophisticated proteins are chosen.

5. Use the comparison to decide how much to spend. If the priority is to limit outlay, the two alternatives indicated by the source offer a starting point. If the priority is to understand tolerability, personal testing becomes the central step. In both cases, the cost per kilogram helps you read prices more effectively.
