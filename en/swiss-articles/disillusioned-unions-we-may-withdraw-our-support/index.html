The decision of the Economic Commission to the Council of States not to change the measures on wage protection irritates the president of Travail Suisse, Adrian Wüthrich. He argues that it is “incomprehensible” that the Commission has decided to maintain the current wage protection measures, which are far stricter than those required by the EU. Wüthrich represents ten acronyms with 130,000 members and argues that wage protection does not improve, but can be maintained and modernized.

Operational details

The Economic Commission's decision to the Council of States not to change wage protection measures has created difficulties for trade unions. The issue of bail is particularly controversial. Today, in certain sectors, companies must in any case pay a deposit before being able to carry out an assignment in Switzerland. This serves to ensure that fines are actually paid if a company violates wage rules. Under pressure from the EU, the bail obligation should be abolished in the package of agreements. Only companies that have already breached wage rules once should still be required to post a deposit. However, the Economic Commission of the Council of States wants to maintain the current regulation, which is much stricter.

Wüthrich admits that the problem is that we do not maintain a good relationship with the EU. It is unclear whether the Economic Commission will be able to get its proposals through the plenary of the Council of States. The Foreign Policy Committee asked on Friday to reject his proposals. The Chamber of Cantons will therefore probably have to deal with contradictory proposals from two committees in the autumn session.
