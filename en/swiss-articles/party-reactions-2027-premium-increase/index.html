2027 Premium Increase: Reactions from the PLR, PS, and Unions

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 29 September 2026

Estimate at 3,7% for 2027. PLR calls for cost control, PS and OCST defend families. Average Ticino premium at 519,90 francs.

On September 29, 2026, an estimate of 3.7% was given for the increase in health insurance premiums in Ticino in 2027. This figure is lower than in recent years. and lower than the national average, but it doesn't eliminate the bitterness of yet another price increase. At 519.90 francs, the canton remains the one with the highest average premium overall.

The announcement provoked reactions from political parties and unions, with differing approaches. The FDP considers the trend of increases unsustainable and shifts the focus from the discussion on how to offset the increase to the causes of rising healthcare costs. For readers who want to delve deeper into the topic, the dossier cassa malati offers a consistent entry point to the announcement.

"The principle for the FDP is simple: if we want to curb premiums, we must curb the growth of healthcare costs."

The parties' demands

In the Grand Council, the party called for a cantonal strategy based on cost monitoring, measurable objectives, and a more effective use of supply planning. It also highlighted the strong growth of physiotherapy centers, private Spitex services, and independent nurses, calling for transparency and rigor. The FDP also links this work to tax breaks and subsidies, hospital planning, and increased efficiency in the healthcare system. At the federal level, the motion "Quality over Quantity," resubmitted on September 29, 2026, calls for prioritizing the outcome of care over the number of tests, treatments, and surgeries. The party also points to EFAS, increased competition, and interventions on drug prices.

The SP views the price increase as a direct blow to Ticino households, along with rent, energy, and fuel. It calls for increased subsidies and a reduction in healthcare costs. It cites the 10% Initiative, approved by the Ticino electorate on September 28, 2025: the first stage will come into force on January 1, 2027. Regarding hospital control, the party points to delays in the new planning, points out that the Grand Council is still awaiting the messages on the most recent annual reports from the EOC, and notes that no report on the performance mandates of private institutions has yet been submitted to Parliament. Finally, the OCST insists on a substantially fixed income and on the sacrifices that can accumulate, from dinner to cultural activities, to helping children and grandchildren.

Operational details

Two Levels to Separate

A practical interpretation of the 2027 estimate requires two comparisons. In terms of pace, the increase is more moderate than in recent years and below the national average. At the starting level, however, the average premium in Ticino remains the highest overall. The first information puts the change into perspective; the second explains why the increase continues to weigh on budgets. The useful comparison is therefore not just between percentages, but between the announced change and the average value already indicated for the canton.

The SP and the OCST describe the same pressure from the perspective of households, families, and retirees: wages and incomes are not growing at the same rate, while rent, energy, fuel, and the cost of living are increasingly important in budgets. The OCST translates this pressure into daily choices: a dinner, a vacation, a cultural activity, or help for children or grandchildren. This sequence is presented not as a single sacrifice, but as a sum of sacrifices that diminish the quality of life year after year. The positions gathered can be read on two levels, without overlapping:

This distinction helps understand why the debate isn't limited to percentage figures. Subsidies are presented as a tool to relieve families; monitoring, objectives, planning, and transparency, on the other hand, concern spending growth and supply control. The PLR argues that shifting the burden from insured persons to taxpayers will not make it easier, while the SP calls for action on both fronts.

The hospital dossier is where these demands converge. Planning, EOC reports, performance mandates for private institutions, and financial flows are identified as areas requiring greater transparency, oversight, and collaboration between the government and parliament. The debate involves the Grand Council and the Council of State; the federal level appears with the motion "Quality over Quantity," "EFAS," competition, and drug prices.

Key points

A checklist for following the next steps

The discussion offers some concrete references for those who want to follow the evolution of premiums without confusing support measures with cost control. The following sequence remains anchored to the dates, bodies and dossiers cited by Ticino’s political forces.

1. Mark the date. January 1, 2027 is the date indicated for the entry into force of the first stage of the 10% Initiative, approved on September 28, 2025 by the people of Ticino. It is the first temporal reference to keep separate from the interventions still under discussion. 2. Divide the measures by objective. Under relief measures, include the Initiative and the strengthening of subsidies requested by the PS. Under costs, note monitoring, measurable objectives and supply planning, requested by the PLR. This distinction makes a decision understandable without attributing effects to it that differ from those declared. 3. Follow the cantonal work. The point to verify is the progress of the strategy to contain healthcare spending requested in the Grand Council: cost monitoring, measurable objectives and more effective use of planning. These are the three elements indicated by the PLR for moving from announcement to structured control. 4. Check the hospital dossier. The PS’s requests include greater timeliness in hospital planning, greater transparency in overseeing public and private institutions, data on financial flows and collaboration between the Government and Parliament. The dossier also continues to include the EOC’s annual reports and the service mandates of private institutions. 5. Measure the pressure to give things up. The OCST cites dinner, vacation, cultural activities and help for children or grandchildren. Using these items as budget categories makes it possible to observe the reduction in quality of life described by the union, while keeping the focus on the categories cited.

To follow developments, it is advisable to distinguish each communication between subsidies, spending containment, hospital planning and federal proposals. The motion Quality instead of quantity, EFAS, competition and medicine prices in fact belong to a different level from the requests addressed to the Grand Council and the State Council.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the estimate of an increase in sick pay premiums for 2027 in Ticino?
The estimate indicated for Ticino is an increase of 3.7% for the year 2027. This figure is considered lower than in recent years and lower than the national average, but the canton maintains the highest average premium ever, equal to 519.90 francs.
What does the PLR ask to curb premium growth?
PLR requires a cantonal strategy based on cost monitoring, measurable targets and a more incisive use of supply planning. The party calls for transparency on physiotherapy centers, private Spitex and independent nurses, and at the federal level supports the 'Quality instead of quantity' motion and interventions on drug prices.
When does the first stage of the 10% Initiative go into effect?
The first stage of the 10% Initiative, approved by the Ticinese people on 28 September 2025, will come into force on 1 January 2027. The PS and the OCST cite it as a tool to strengthen subsidies and lighten the burden on family budgets, together with the request to contain health costs.
