The inheritance and gift tax in Switzerland is not part of the direct federal tax: it is a tax of exclusive cantonal and municipal competence. Each Canton independently regulates rates, deductibles, exemptions and declaratory obligations in its own law, and the Municipalities add a multiplier that can vary significantly from one municipality to another. The Uri Canton is one of those that tax both transfers mortis causa and donations between the living, applying separate rates according to the degree of kinship between settlor and beneficiary.

Operational details

Degrees of kinship and logic of rates

The logic followed by the Uranian cantonal legislator mirrors that of many other Swiss cantons: the tax burden grows as the family bond weakens. Specifically, the categories of beneficiaries are divided into groups (spouse and registered partner, direct descendants, parents and brothers/sisters, other relatives, people without kinship ties). Each band has its own percentage rate, which can be expressed as progressive steps depending on the amount transferred. For the Uri Canton, the source does not report the specific percentages of each tier: it merely confirms the existence of a system by degree of kinship, modulated by the cantonal law.

Municipal multiplier and impact on final load

Unlike the DFI, where the federal multiplier is unique, the Uranian cantonal tax is multiplied by the municipal coefficient. This means that with the same amount transferred and degree of kinship, two heirs residing in different municipalities of the Canton of Uri can pay different amounts. It is an element to keep in mind when comparing apparently similar regimes between Cantons: the municipal component can have a non-negligible impact on the final balance.

Key points

Practical procedure for the filing

In general, the procedure for the Canton of Uri requires that the heirs or beneficiaries of a donation submit the tax return within the deadlines set by cantonal law. The return must be filed with the competent tax administration, accompanied by identification documents of the deceased or donor, the beneficiaries, and the assets involved (real estate, bank accounts, corporate shareholdings, valuables). The office then settles the tax by applying the rates provided for the degree of relationship and the municipal multiplier of the beneficiary.

Typically required documents

Although the source does not list them in detail, the documents normally required by the tax administration in this type of procedure include: death certificate or deed of donation, identification of heirs/recipients with degree of relationship, inventory of the deceased's or donor's assets, any real estate valuations, bank statements, and attestations of corporate shareholdings. Complete documentation reduces settlement times and the risk of disputes.

Deadlines and time limits

The cantonal law of Uri sets specific deadlines for filing the return: failure to comply results in the application of default interest or fines. To avoid penalties, it is advisable to inquire with the cantonal tax office of Uri regarding the applicable deadlines in the specific case (inheritance or donation). In some Cantons, the terms run from the date of death, in others from the opening of the succession or from the notice of acceptance of the inheritance.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who sets the inheritance and gift tax rates in Canton Uri?
The rates are defined by the Uranian cantonal law and applied by the Uri cantonal tax administration. Direct federal tax (DFI) does not apply to inheritances and donations: the matter is the exclusive competence of the cantonal and municipal authorities. The source does not report the specific percentages of each kinship group.
How does the Municipality affect the final amount due?
The cantonal tax is multiplied by the municipal coefficient of the beneficiary. With the same amount transferred and degree of kinship, two heirs residing in different municipalities of Canton Uri can therefore pay different amounts. The municipal component may have a non-negotiable impact on the final balance.
What is the difference between inheritance and donations in Canton Uri?
The Uranian cantonal law distinguishes transfers mortis causa (successions) from transfers between the living (donations), with rates articulated by degree of kinship. Donations made in the years prior to death can be aggregated to the hereditary mass to avoid circumvention, and it is advisable to plan generational passages taking into account this mechanism.
Within what timeframe should the return be filed?
The deadlines are set by cantonal law and failure to comply will result in the application of late payment interest or fines. In some cantons the terms start from the date of death, in others from the opening of the succession or from the news of the acceptance of the inheritance. For the specific case, it is advisable to inquire at the cantonal tax office of Uri.
What documents are needed for the declaration?
The source indicates in general terms that the death certificate or deed of donation is needed, the identification of heirs or grantees with a degree of kinship, the inventory of assets (real estate, bank accounts, corporate holdings, valuables) and any real estate valuations. Comprehensive documentation reduces settlement times and the risk of disputes.
