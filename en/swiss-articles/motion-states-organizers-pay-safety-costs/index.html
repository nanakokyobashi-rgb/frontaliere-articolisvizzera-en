Motion States: organizers pay safety costs

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 24 September 2026

The States approve the motion 24-18 with 3 abstentions: the Federal Council will have to prepare a legal basis to make the organizers pay the costs of intervention and security of the demonstrations, after the riots of October 11 in Bern.

The vote in Bern

In Bern, the Council of States approved with 24 votes against 18 and 3 abstentions a motion that opens up the possibility of charging the organizers for the costs caused by serious disturbances and violent acts during the demonstrations. The Nazionale had already expressed itself favorably; the passage to the States therefore completes the parliamentary support for the request, which now passes to the Federal Council for the legislative work indicated in the text.

The motion tasks the Federal Council with developing a legal basis. The objective is to allow public communities to collect all or part of the costs of the intervention and the measures necessary to guarantee its safety from the organizers of protests and demonstrations. The source does not yet describe a collection procedure, nor does it indicate amounts or a date of application: the announced step is the preparation of the legal basis.

Because the proposal reached Parliament

The text was presented by national councilor Sidney Kamerzin (Centre/VS). The motivation recalls several recent demonstrations, in particular that of October 11th in Bern, which resulted in violence against the population at the hands of violent extremists. According to the motion, prevention and protection have made important measures by the police necessary.

Daniel Jositsch (Independent/ZH) supported the request, stating: «It is not up to the community to bear the costs associated with these safety measures». In his speech he also mentioned a situation of impunity which, he said, he had personally observed in the streets of Zurich.

The vote did not end the institutional debate. Carlo Sommaruga (PS/GE) contested the motion for the possible conflict with the principle of subsidiarity and for the limits it could impose on the autonomy of the cantons. Mathias Zopfi (Greens/GL) added that the text is formulated in too broad terms and could concern all demonstrations, including those on May 1st. Beat Jans, justice minister, reiterated the same objection. The states' vote therefore concerns the mandate of the Federal Council, not a figure already established for individual events.

Frequently Asked Questions
What was the vote of the States on the motion?
The States approved the motion by 24 votes to 18, with 3 abstentions. The vote sent the Federal Council to prepare a legal basis that allows public authorities to collect from the organizers all or part of the intervention costs and measures necessary to ensure safety during the demonstrations.
Who filed the motion and what event is it referring to?
The motion was filed by National Councillor Sidney Kamerzin (Centre/VS). It refers to the demonstration of 11 October last in Bern, which resulted in violence against the population at the hands of violent extremists, which required important measures by law enforcement.
What still needs to be defined by the legal basis that the Federal Council will have to elaborate?
The legal basis will have to establish which events fall under the “serious disturbances” or violent acts, which items include the costs of intervention and security, and how the national rule will be coordinated with the already existing cantonal rules on the right to demonstrate and on the maintenance of public order. Without these details it will not be possible to calculate a concrete charge.
