Living in Como and working in Ticino as a cross-border commuter (cross-border guide)

Complete guide for those who live in Como and work in Ticino: journey times, cost of living, recommended neighbourhoods and comparison between train and car.

Context

In brief - Commuting Como-Chiasso: times and costs - Cost of living in comparison - Recommended neighborhoods in Como - Train vs car: which is convenient ## Key facts - What: Comparison between living in Como and working in Ticino - Travel times: 20-30 minutes by car, 15-20 minutes by train - Cost of living: Lower in Como than in Ticino - Recommended neighborhoods: Old Town, Borghi, Rebbio - Transport: Cheaper train, more flexible car Living in Como and working in Ticino is an increasingly common choice among cross-border commuters. The geographical proximity between the two cities makes this commuting particularly attractive. Travel times vary between 20 and 30 minutes by car, depending on traffic, while the train takes between 15 and 20 minutes, often making it the fastest choice. ### Travel times Travel times between Como and Chiasso can vary greatly depending on the means of transport used. By car, the average time is around 20-30 minutes, but it can increase during rush hour due to traffic. The train, on the other hand, offers a faster and more reliable service, with journey times ranging between 15 and 20 minutes. ### Cost of living The cost of living in Como is generally lower than in Ticino. This is especially evident in real estate, where rents and house prices are significantly lower. Everyday costs, such as groceries and services, also tend to be cheaper in Como. ### Recommended neighborhoods In Como, the most recommended neighborhoods for cross-border commuters are the historic center, Borghi and Rebbio. These neighborhoods offer a good quality of life, with well-developed services and infrastructure, and are well connected to Ticino.

Operational details

Practical analysis ### Train vs car The choice between train and car depends on personal needs. The train is generally cheaper and faster, but the car offers more flexibility, especially for those with varying working hours. It is also important to consider additional costs, such as parking and fuel, which can significantly affect your monthly budget. ### Cost of living in comparison Living in Como and working in Ticino can offer significant economic savings. For example, the cost of rents in Como is on average 30-40% lower than in Ticino cities such as Lugano or Chiasso. The costs for services and daily shopping are also lower, allowing significant savings. ### Recommended neighborhoods The neighborhoods of Como offer several opportunities for cross-border commuters. The historic center is ideal for those looking for an active social life and a good offer of services. Borghi is perfect for families, thanks to the presence of schools and parks. Rebbio, on the other hand, is a good choice for those looking for tranquility and a more residential environment. ### Tax implications It is important to consider the tax implications of living in Como and working in Ticino. Withholding tax on earned income is only withheld in Switzerland for cross-border commuters. Italy avoids double taxation with the tax credit (EC framework of 730). The New Cross-Border Commuter Agreement, signed on 23/12/2020 and entered into force on 1 January 2024, provides for a deductible of €10,000 for new cross-border commuters.
