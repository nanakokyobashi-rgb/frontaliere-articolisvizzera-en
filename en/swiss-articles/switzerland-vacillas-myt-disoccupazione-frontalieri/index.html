The Swiss labour market opens the year with a wake-up call. According to the latest data published by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO), in January 2026 the unemployment rate in Switzerland rose to 3.2%. The constant increase recorded in recent months brings the absolute number of unemployed to 152,300 units. This negative trend mainly affects young people, who are now 40% of the unemployed, as reported by a recent study by the Foundation for the Development of Vocational Education and Training (FFP).

The situation is particularly worrying in the Canton of Lausanne, where the unemployment rate reaches 4.5%, exceeding twice the national average. The same situation is found in the city of Geneva, where the number of unemployed has risen to 12,500 units, an increase of 15% compared to the previous year.

Analysis of data from the Federal Department of Economy, Education and Research (SECO) reveals that the comparison with the pandemic period offers a broader perspective. In January 2021, the unemployment rate had reached 3.7% with 170,000 unemployed. However, the current growth trend suggests a structural cooling of the economy that deserves attention.

According to SECO data, youth unemployment increased by 25% compared to the same period in 2021. In particular, young people between the ages of 15 and 24 represent 30% of the unemployed, while those between the ages of 25 and 34 represent 25%. This trend is particularly worrying in the North-West region, where youth unemployment has increased by 40% compared to the same period in 2021.

Moreover, SECO data show that unemployment is higher in rural areas than in urban areas. For example, in the Jura region, unemployment increased by 50% compared to the same period in 2021, while in the Lake Geneva region it increased by 20%. This trend is particularly worrying for rural areas, where unemployment can have a stronger impact on the local population.

The geography of work in Switzerland shows, as usual, profound regional differences. Ticino rose to 3.3% (+0.1% compared to December), ranking tenth among the most affected cantons. Graubünden is confirmed among the most virtuous cantons with a rate of 1.6%, sixth in the ranking of the least affected.

Switzerland, traditionally considered a financial paradise and a model of economic stability, is facing a growing problem: youth unemployment. According to the most recent data, the unemployment rate among young people (15-24 years old) rose to 6.3%, an increase of 1.1% compared to last year. This worrying trend can be found in almost all cantons, with some significant exceptions.

The canton of Bern, for example, recorded a 7.5% increase in the youth unemployment rate, reaching 7.9% (+1.3% compared to December). This is the highest rate among the Swiss cantons, with only the canton of Neuchâtel outperforming it with a rate of 8.1% (+1.5% compared to December). The Jura, on the other hand, confirms itself among the cantons with the lowest unemployment rate, with only 2.5% (+0.1% compared to December).
