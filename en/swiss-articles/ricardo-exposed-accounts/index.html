On Wednesday, October 7, the Swiss online marketplace Ricardo detected suspicious activity on its servers. It immediately initiated technical measures to secure the systems, according to the statement issued by its parent company, SMG Swiss Marketplace Group.

The subsequent investigation established that personal data from approximately 890'000 user accounts had been exposed. The information disclosed includes names, postal addresses and telephone numbers. The same statement specifies that email addresses and passwords were not compromised.

The sequence of the incident

The first step was the detection of suspicious activity; the second, technical intervention on the systems; the third, the subsequent investigation into access and personal data. Ricardo reports that the security vulnerability that enabled access has been fully resolved. The statement therefore describes a vulnerability linked to access and declared fully resolved after the suspicious activity.

For those who use the platform, the distinction between exposed data and uncompromised credentials is the central point of the communication. Names, postal addresses and telephone numbers are the information identified as having been made public; email addresses and passwords, on the other hand, remain excluded from the list of compromised data released by the company.

Ricardo is directly informing the affected users about the incident, its implications and the precautions they can take. In accordance with legal requirements, Ricardo informed the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner, the IFPDT. It also announced a criminal complaint to the police and a report to the Federal Office for Cyber Security, the OFCS.

The detection took place on the platform's servers, while the communication to users directly concerns the consequences for personal data. The timeline provided by the company brings these two aspects together without confusing access with credentials.

The parent company's communication therefore brings together the outcome of the technical investigation, the exposure of personal data and the initiatives launched by Ricardo. For the reader, the communicated picture is limited to these categories: affected accounts, personal data exposed, uncompromised credentials and initiatives launched by the company.

Operational details

For a Ricardo user, the practical issue is not merely that a breach occurred, but understanding which part of the profile falls within the reported exposure. The source clearly separates the data made public from access information: names, postal addresses and telephone numbers on the one hand; e-mail addresses and passwords on the other. This distinction makes it possible to assess the incident without attributing to it any data other than those indicated.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which personal data were exposed in the Ricardo incident?
The communication from SMG Swiss Marketplace Group states that the names, postal addresses and telephone numbers associated with approximately 890'000 user accounts were exposed. However, the source does not indicate that email addresses or passwords were compromised. Ricardo is directly informing the affected users about the implications of the incident and the precautions they can take.
Were the passwords and email addresses compromised?
No. The parent company clarified that email addresses and passwords were not compromised. The personal data identified as having been made public are names, postal addresses and telephone numbers. The distinction refers to the incident reported by Ricardo and does not add any other categories of data beyond those reported in the statement.
Which authorities were informed of the incident?
In compliance with legal requirements, Ricardo notified the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner, IFPDT. The platform also announced that it would file a criminal complaint with the police and report the incident to the Federal Office for Cybersecurity, UFCS. The source does not describe the outcome of these initiatives.
When did Ricardo detect the suspicious activity?
Ricardo detected suspicious activity on its servers on Wednesday, October 7. It immediately initiated technical measures to secure the systems. Subsequently, the investigation established that personal data had been exposed, and the company states that the security vulnerability that allowed access has been completely resolved.
