Key points

From research to choice

The most orderly procedure starts with the category and reaches the personal budget without mixing different data. The goal is not to compile a generic list, but to build a comparison that can be reread before making the choice.

Five operational steps

1. Identify the item. At the top of the sheet, write whether you are assessing AG, half fare, a cantonal subscription or a community subscription. The category remains the reference throughout the comparison.

2. Separate the criteria. Create three distinct columns for cost, discount and benefits. Do not enter financial elements and benefits in the same cell, because it would become difficult to understand what you are comparing.

3. Use the same grid. Fill in the columns with the same setup for every option. If the documentation presents information with different wording, transcribe it without turning it into amounts or additional conditions.

4. Check the assumptions. A name is not enough to demonstrate a benefit. Before assigning a preference, verify that the cost, discount and benefit are actually indicated for the subscription under consideration.

5. Connect the result to the budget. After organizing the entries, assess the expense together with the other personal expenditures. The final choice must reflect the relationship between costs and benefits, not merely the presence of the word discount.

This sequence allows you to stop at any time without losing the work done. If the initial comparison is not sufficient, you can review just one column instead of starting over. The same logic helps keep cantonal and community plans distinct from the other categories, without attributing characteristics to them that are not reported.

The comparison is ready when every option is described with the same level of detail. At that point, the reader can decide which subscription to explore further and which cost to include in their personal budget.
