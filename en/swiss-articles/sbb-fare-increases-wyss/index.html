SBB fare increases: Wyss declarations (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 August 2026

1.45 million passengers a day, crowded trains. SBB announces price increases for general subscriptions and half price. The president speaks.

Context

In a nutshell

  • SBB announces price increases for general subscriptions and half price
  • Rail traffic at historic levels: 1.45 million passengers per day
  • President Wyss: rates decided by the entire sector, not only SBB

Key facts

  • Who: SBB and Chairman of the Board André Wyss
  • What: Announcement of price increases for general subscriptions and half price
  • When: Recently announced
  • Where: Switzerland (national scope)
  • Daily traffic: 1.45 million travelers

André Wyss, chairman of the board of directors of the Swiss Federal Railways, publicly defended the decision to increase the fares for general and half-price subscriptions. The SBB announcement comes at a time of record rail traffic, with 1.45 million commuters a day.

In an interview with Tages-Anzeiger online, Wyss (59, in office since April) rejected criticism that raising prices while trains are crowded represents a contradiction. "The tariffs are not only set by the SBB," he said, "but by the entire industry."

The president, who boasts previous experiences as CEO of Implenia (construction giant) and background in chemical-pharmaceutical, stressed how the Railways have maintained stable prices for a long time despite inflation and increased operating costs. “We've kept prices stable for a long time, despite inflation and rising costs weighing on us as well,” says Wyss.

###

Operational details

The issue of overcrowding in SBB transport

Wyss did not deny the problem of congestion, but limited it to specific time slots. “I know that too, although it doesn't happen that often. This is a phenomenon linked to peak hours. During the day, trains are not overloaded, "points out the president.

This statement introduces an important distinction between public perception (trains always full) and operational reality (crowding concentrated at specific times). For SBB, this nuance is crucial to justify the fare model: those who choose different time slots may find less congestion, while those who travel during peak hours accept the compromise of a more crowded environment.

From a practical point of view, commuters and commuters who depend on trains during rush hours (typically 7–9 in the morning, 17–19 in the evening on weekdays) face a de facto situation: increases without improvement in comfort at critical times. This creates a tension between promises of supply expansion (which SBB cites as the reason for the hikes) and perceived experience (still crowded at peak times).

Key points

What to do if you are a Swiss commuter: check subscriptions and options

If you use a general SBB subscription or half price, you should check the updated costs before the next deadline. SBB will communicate the details of the price increases through the official channels (sbbcff.ch website, SBB Mobile app, branches), but you can already start to evaluate alternatives.

Reevaluate your mobility needs

A first step is to ask: Is the full-rate annual subscription still convenient compared to other options? If you work or study at an irregular pace, a weekly, daily, or youth card may be less convenient post-price than local variants.

If you are an employee, check with your employer if they offer transportation reimbursements: many Swiss companies cover part (or all) of the subscription as a company benefit. Negotiating this clause can nullify or drastically reduce the impact of the charge on your portfolio.

Similarly, if you are a student, elderly, disabled, or registered as unemployed WITH SECO, there are preferential tariff categories: inform yourself at SBB or the canton of residence.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which SBB subscriptions are affected by the announced price increases?
According to the SBB, the price increases concern general subscriptions and half the price. The specification of the exact costs and the effective date will be officially communicated by the Federal Railways through the sbbcff.ch website, the SBB Mobile app and branches. If you have one of these subscriptions, it is advisable to check the official channels directly to know the amount and methods of the transition.
Why does the SBB raise prices if trains are crowded during rush hours?
According to President Wyss, the tariff increase responds to inflationary pressures and increasing operating costs (infrastructure maintenance, energy, salaries), not to the phenomenon of crowding. Wyss points out that congestion is linked to peak hours and is not systematic throughout the day. The SBB argues that keeping prices stable despite inflation would require interventions via public taxation.
Who decides on Swiss public transport fares?
According to Wyss, tariffs are not only decided by SBB, but by the entire transport sector. Confederation and cantons participate in the financing and charging system. If passengers do not cover the cost of mobility through subscriptions, the Confederation and cantons must intervene through general taxation, which entails tax increases for taxpayers.
