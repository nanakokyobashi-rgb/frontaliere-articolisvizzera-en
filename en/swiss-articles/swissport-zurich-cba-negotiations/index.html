Negotiations for a new collective bargaining agreement for Swissport’s ground staff in Zurich have failed, according to the unions. The agreement covers approximately 2’000 people and will expire at the end of the year. The last renewal dates back to 2022; the unions maintain that, on that occasion, the covid pandemic had forced employees to accept numerous concessions.

A breakdown that does not end the talks

The dispute nevertheless has two distinct elements. On the one hand, the unions describe the negotiations for the new CBA as having failed. On the other, they say they remain open to discussions. The strike threat is tied to an explicit condition: the demands must not be heeded. The source identifies January 2027 as a possible time for the action, not a strike that has already been decided.

The demands concern better working conditions and salary increases of up to 7 percent. The text does not present 7 percent as an achieved result, but as the upper limit of the unions’ demand. For the employees involved, the documented progression is therefore from failed negotiations to a phase in which a strike is being threatened, while a willingness to discuss remains indicated.

Comparing the dates contained in the report makes it possible to read the dispute as a sequence, without turning the hypotheses into a definite schedule. 2022 is the last year indicated for the renewal; the end of the year is the date indicated for the contract’s expiry; January 2027, on the other hand, is the date mentioned as the possible start of a strike. The three indications therefore carry different weight: one concerns a past event, one the expiry of the CCL, and one an action that remains hypothetical.

What is the current status of negotiations for Swissport ground staff?

Union demands include improved working conditions and wage increases of up to 7 percent.
