The labour market in the Canton of St. Gallen reflects the economic structure of eastern Switzerland: a diverse productive fabric, where manufacturing, financial and insurance services, trade, health and training coexist. Those looking for work or who want to understand how their salary is positioned must consider three fundamental variables: the sector of activity, the degree of training and the experience gained.

Operational details

Salary levels by level of training

In Switzerland, the salary level is closely linked to the training path. The Confederation traditionally distinguishes four levels: compulsory education, secondary education II (apprenticeship or maturity), non-university tertiary education (SFP) and university education ( bachelor's, master's, doctorate). With the same sector, those who have a university degree have access to higher salaries, but the range can vary significantly between sectors.

In the Canton of St. Gallen, aggregate wage data for the entire labour market is published periodically by the UST/BFS, which tracks the structure of wages on a national scale. The Confederation does not set a statutory minimum wage at the federal level: the wage is determined by supply and demand, the CCLs and individual bargaining. However, several cantons — notably Geneva, Neuchâtel, Jura and Basel-City — have introduced a cantonal minimum wage by law. St. Gallen does not fall within these Cantons: therefore, the principle of contractual freedom applies, tempered by the sectoral CCLs where present.

For guidance, it is useful to consider the Swiss median salary as a reference point: the exact figure is updated annually by the UST/BFS and is indexed on a national scale. Key points

How to navigate the job search in St. Gallen

For those looking for a job in the Canton of St. Gallen, the first step is to consult the national job boards and the cantonal placement portals. SECO manages the public employment service and provides information on professions in demand. At the same time, the regional placement offices (RAV/URC) offer support in preparing your application file and in matching supply and demand.

Before signing a contract, it is essential to check some key elements: the salary offered compared to the reference market, the existence of a sector-specific collective labour agreement (CLA/CCL) with any general obligation, the duration of holidays and the notice clauses. Federal law provides for increasing notice periods based on seniority: one month during the first year of service, two months from the second to the ninth year, three months from the tenth year onwards.

Procedure for those moving to Switzerland

For a Swiss citizen or a permit holder who wishes to work in St. Gallen, the process is relatively straightforward: active search through specialised portals, sending the application file, interview and signing of the contract. For foreign nationals coming from third countries, the issuance of the residence permit (L or B) falls under the responsibility of the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM). EU/EFTA citizens benefit from facilitated access to the Swiss labour market thanks to the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a federal minimum wage in Switzerland?
No. The Confederation does not set a statutory minimum wage at the federal level: the wage is determined by supply and demand, collective labour agreements (CLAs) and individual bargaining. However, several cantons — Geneva, Neuchâtel, Jura and Basel-City — have introduced a cantonal minimum wage by law. St. Gallen is not one of them.
Which sectors offer the most jobs in St. Gallen?
Demand is concentrated in services, healthcare, mechanical engineering and technology. The cantonal economy benefits from the proximity to the poles of Zurich and the Lake Constance region. The secondary sector remains a pillar, in particular precision mechanics and metalworking, while the tertiary sector has progressively expanded.
How do CCLs work with general obligation?
The ordinary CCL shall apply to workers who are members of the Contracting Parties. On the other hand, the CCL, which is declared to be generally mandatory, is extended by the Federal Council to all employers and workers in the sector or region: in this case, salary and working conditions become binding for everyone, even for those who are not members of the signatory associations.
What are the mandatory social contributions from wages?
Compulsory contributions from wages include AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3% paid by the worker (10.6% total), AD/AC at 1.1% up to the indexed annual ceiling and LAINF/LAA between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the risk sector. The LPP/BVG coordinated salary depends on the age group: 7% between 25-34 years, 10% between 35-44, 15% between 45-54 and 18% from the 55th year until the AVS reference age.
What are the federal terms of termination?
Federal law provides for increasing termination terms based on seniority: one month during the first year of service, two months from the second to the ninth year, and three months from the tenth year onward.
