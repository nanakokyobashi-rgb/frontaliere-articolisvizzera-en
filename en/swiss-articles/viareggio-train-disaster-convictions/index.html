Final sentences for the Viareggio railway massacre (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 26 June 2026

The Italian Supreme Court confirms the penalties: five years for Mauro Moretti, former CEO of Ferrovie Italiane, in the case of the Viareggio tragedy of 2009.

Context

In short - Final convictions for the Viareggio massacre - 32 dead and over 100 injured in 2009 - Mauro Moretti sentenced to five years ## Key facts - What : Final criminal convictions for the train accident - When: Recently confirmed by the Court of Cassation - Where: Italy, at the Court of Cassation - Who: Former CEO of Fs and RFI Mauro Moretti - Amount: 5 years in prison for Moretti On 29 June 2009, in Viareggio, a derailment of a freight convoy loaded with LPG caused an explosion that resulted in 32 dead and over 100 injured. The Italian Court of Cassation recently upheld the penalties set out in the ter appeal, making the five-year prison sentence final for Mauro Moretti, 72, former CEO of Ferrovie dello Stato and RFI. The sentence also involved nine other positions, all for the crime of negligent railway disaster, while Moretti also responds for fire. The Supreme Court rejected the defendants' appeals, accepting the request of the General Prosecutor's Office. Moretti's defence team commented on the sentence as "profoundly unfair" and assessed a request for house arrest. The decision concludes one of the longest and most complex proceedings in Italian judicial history, marked by various degrees of judgment and referrals. The Viareggio accident, one of the most serious in Italian railway history, has led to debates on railway safety and liability

Operational details

The analysis of the Viareggio judgment also reveals how much criminal liability can influence railway safety policies at the international level. The conviction of Mauro Moretti, former CEO of Ferrovie Italiane, could prompt companies and public bodies to review their risk management systems. In Switzerland, railway authorities and companies have always been committed to strict regulations, as evidenced by the focus on safety procedures and infrastructure maintenance. However, the Italian episode highlights how the risk of criminal liability can be a further incentive to maintain high standards. For border workers, safety at work and the responsibilities of companies are aspects that directly affect their daily lives. The possibility of verifying and requesting greater security guarantees is an important step to protect your rights. In addition, this story opens a comparison between Italian and Swiss safety regulations, with possible requests for harmonization or exchange of best practices. Cross-border collaboration on railway safety could intensify, also thanks to pressure from international bodies. Key points

For border crossers and commuters, knowing the procedures in case of railway accidents is essential. The first action to take is to verify the insurance and legal rights through official instruments. In Switzerland, the liability and compensation system is well-structured, but it is important to know how to act in the event of an accident. If an incident similar to the one in Viareggio occurs, it is advisable to follow these steps: - Immediately report the incident to the railway safety authorities and the companies involved. - Collect all possible evidence: photographs, testimonies, documents. - Consult your insurance contract and check the coverage provided. - Contact a lawyer specialised in transport law or civil liability.
