Wage protection: compromise approved on Bilaterals III

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 30 September 2026

In the debate on Bilaterals III, the Council of States approves measure 14: protection against dismissal for employee and trade union representatives.

Context

In brief

  • The Council of States approves the compromise on Bilaterals III
  • Measure 14 passes with 26 votes to 19
  • Protection against dismissal for employee and trade union representatives

Key facts

  • What → national implementation of the new rules on wage protection
  • Where → Bern
  • Who decides → Council of States
  • Measure → protection against dismissal, measure 14
  • Outcome → 26 votes to 19

With 26 votes to 19, the Council of States approved the so-called measure 14 in Bern, included in the compromise on wage protection. The vote took place as part of the debate on Bilaterals III.

The decision concerns the national implementation of the new rules on wage protection. In the text, the compromise is presented as a green light for greater guarantees for workers and trade union representatives. The most specific point is protection against dismissal for employee and trade union representatives active in companies.

The point decided in Bern

Measure 14 is therefore described through two elements: the category concerned and the guarantee provided. The indicated recipients are employee representatives and trade union representatives active in companies; the protection concerns dismissal. The source does not link the measure to a salary figure, but to a guarantee for those who perform these roles.

The parliamentary process remains at the center of the news. On the one hand, there is the framework of Bilaterals III; on the other, there is the national implementation of the new rules on wage protection. The compromise was accepted by the Council of States and is summarized as a green light from Parliament for greater guarantees for workers and trade union representatives.

The result of 26 votes to 19 refers to the approval of measure 14. For those working in Switzerland, the figure should be read together with the subject of the measure: the source speaks of protection against dismissal and not of an amount to be automatically added to the salary. The distinction prevents a vote on protection from being turned into an announcement of a salary increase.

The news text does not attribute statements to individual parliamentarians and does not describe steps other than those indicated. The verifiable fact therefore remains the approval of the compromise within the framework of Bilaterals III, with the national implementation of the new rules and with measure 14 approved in Bern.
