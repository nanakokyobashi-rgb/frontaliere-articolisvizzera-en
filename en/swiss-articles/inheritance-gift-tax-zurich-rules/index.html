In the Canton of Zurich, inheritance and gift tax should be understood as a cantonal levy. The central point is to identify the degree of kinship relevant to the rate and verify whether the case falls within the exemptions indicated for spouses and descendants.

The source distinguishes this tax from two federal levies. Switzerland does not levy a federal inheritance and gift tax; direct federal tax and VAT are separate levies and not components of Zurich inheritance tax. Therefore, a cantonal percentage must not be presented as a sum of direct federal tax, VAT and a municipal share.

The correct scope of the calculation

The areas of responsibility are not interchangeable. AFC/ESTV is associated with direct federal tax and VAT, while cantonal administrations concern cantonal and municipal taxation. UFAS/BSV oversees social security, including AVS/AHV and LPP/BVG, and does not set tax rates. UST/BFS produces statistics and does not determine the rates.

In the Zurich case, this map helps avoid a misdirected search. The reference remains the cantonal rules governing the levy. The municipal tax multiplier for ordinary taxes must not be added to the inheritance or gift tax calculation. Nor does the general principle that each Canton has its own law and multiplier authorize automatically including the municipal component in this calculation.

A table or calculation that presents the federal, cantonal and municipal levels together therefore risks describing a tax different from the one addressed by the source. Separating the levels is the starting point.

Kinship is the filter indicated by the source. The analysis must distinguish the family relationship and the corresponding tax category, without substituting one degree for another. Spouses and descendants are mentioned among the exempt cases; for other relationships, the criterion of degree of kinship remains to be followed.

The verification also concerns the form of compliance. The source refers to the declaration and deadlines to be observed: the rate alone does not exhaust the procedure. Anyone dealing with an inheritance or gift in the Canton of Zurich must therefore keep three levels distinct: the type of transaction, the relationship and compliance with the cantonal requirements.

From reading to filing

To turn the rule into a practical check, it is advisable to proceed without skipping steps. The goal is not to create a national formula, but to keep the qualification of the transaction, the tax rate, and the filing obligation separate.

Five practical steps

1. Define the transaction. Establish whether it is an inheritance or a gift. Both cases fall within the scope of Zurich's cantonal tax and must be identified before looking for the tax rate.

2. Identify the relationship. Place the relationship between the persons involved in the relevant degree of kinship. This is the benchmark indicated by the source and prevents applying a category different from the actual one.

3. Check the exemption. First verify whether the case concerns a spouse or descendants, for whom the source refers to an exemption. This indication should not automatically be extended to other relationships.
