Cannabis in the pharmacy: less risk and more awareness (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 September 2026

The pilot project for the sale of cannabis in pharmacies in Bern, Biel and Lucerne is proving to favour less risky consumption.

Context

In brief

  • Cannabis sold in pharmacies in Bern, Bienne, and Lucerne
  • Less risky consumption thanks to specialized advice
  • Pilot project until October 2028

Key facts

  • What: Sale of cannabis in pharmacies
  • When: From 2024, extended until October 2028
  • Where: Bern, Bienne, and Lucerne
  • Who: People participating in the pilot project
  • Amount: Prices slightly higher than the black market

The pilot project for the sale of cannabis in pharmacies in Bern, Bienne, and Lucerne is demonstrating a reduction in risky consumption. This is according to an interim scientific evaluation presented today in Bern by the three participating cities. The project, named 'Script', involves 1177 people and nine specialized pharmacies.

Specialized Advice

In the participating pharmacies, specialized staff inform customers about consumption methods that reduce harm, such as vaping or oral intake instead of smoking. 71% of those surveyed have passed on this advice to people in their social circle, contributing to greater awareness of the risks associated with cannabis consumption.

Prices and Funding

The prices of cannabis sold in pharmacies are slightly higher than those on the black market, which also helps fund the advisory activities. While older people accept the prices, young people between the ages of 18 and 25 react more sensitively. The study's management sees this as an effective tool for protecting minors.

Positive Results

According to the authorities, there have been no incidents of sales to third parties or consumption in public places. The pilot project, which began in 2024, has recently been extended until October 2028. The available results support the idea of a federal law for regulated access to cannabis, which is currently under discussion.

Operational details

Practical Implications

The sale of cannabis in pharmacies represents a step forward towards safer and more conscious consumption. The specialized advice offered in pharmacies participating in the 'Script' pilot project is crucial for reducing the risks associated with cannabis consumption. The slightly higher prices compared to the black market contribute to financing this advisory activity, ensuring that information on less harmful consumption methods reaches a wider audience.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before the start of the pilot project, cannabis consumption was often associated with significant health risks, especially among young people. Regulated sales in pharmacies have allowed the introduction of safer consumption practices, such as vaping and oral administration, thus reducing potential harm. This approach has also contributed to raising public awareness of the risks of cannabis consumption, as demonstrated by the fact that 71% of those surveyed passed on the advice received to people in their circle.

Future Scenarios

With the extension of the pilot project until October 2028, it is likely that data will continue to be collected and the impact of regulated cannabis sales will be evaluated. If the results continue to be positive, a federal law may be proposed to regulate access to cannabis, thus ensuring safer consumption at the national level. This could have significant implications for public health and safety, especially among young people.
