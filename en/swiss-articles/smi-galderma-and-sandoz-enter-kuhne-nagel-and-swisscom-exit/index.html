Big news for the Swiss stock index SMI established by SIX: Galderma and Sandoz enter, while Kühne+Nagel and Swisscom come out. The health sector rises to 40%.

The revision of the Swiss stock index

Starting Monday, the Swiss Market Index (SMI), the most important Swiss stock index, is changing its face with the entry of skin care product manufacturer Galderma and generic drug maker Sandoz. To make room for the two new companies, logistics firm Kühne+Nagel and telecommunications giant Swisscom are leaving the main composition. This move represents a small revolution and is described as the biggest change recorded in recent years on the Swiss stock market. SIX, the company that manages the Swiss stock exchange, had announced this change at the beginning of July. The ranking that determines promotions and relegations in the Olympus of the top 20 market values is based on two specific factors relating to the last twelve months: the average trading volume and the free-float capitalization, meaning the share of a company's capital that is freely tradable on the market. For positions 19 to 22, identified as the buffer zone, additional rules apply to avoid continuous changes caused by small shifts, thus offering members already present in the SMI an advantage over potential challengers.

The impact on the pharmaceutical and healthcare sector

The most obvious consequence of this recent stock market revision is that the pharmaceutical and healthcare sector is acquiring an even greater weight within the main Swiss index. In addition to the new entries represented by Galderma and Sandoz, Novartis, Roche, Lonza and Alcon are also part of the SMI. The total number of representatives of this branch therefore rises from four to six units. Analyzing the data in terms of capitalization, the shift is nevertheless less pronounced compared to the number of companies: the overall share of healthcare companies on the total SMI index rises in fact to about 40%, registering an increase compared to the previous 36%. As explained at the time of the announcement by Remo Rosenau, an expert at Helvetische Bank, the index does not represent the entire Swiss economy but shows the trend of the largest and most liquid listed companies and sectors. Although the index loses slightly in terms of explanatory capacity as a measure of the entire breadth of the national economy, this characteristic was also present previously, confirming that the SMI nevertheless remains an excellent benchmark index for the Swiss stock market, where certain branches naturally maintain a strong overall weight.

Operational details

Comparison with other markets and stock visibility

The prominent presence of individual sectors within the benchmark stock market indicator is not an exclusively Swiss peculiarity. Other well-known international indices also reflect their respective national economies only in part. For example, the Dow Jones Industrial Average illustrates the American economy even less representatively than the SMI does for the Swiss one. The entry of a given company into a well-known and closely watched index like the SMI is not merely a matter of corporate prestige; it concretely increases the visibility and attention paid to that stock by institutional and international investors. Since a vast number of investment funds and ETFs, meaning exchange-traded funds, faithfully replicate the index performance, inclusion can generate additional demand for the affected share, as these financial products are obliged to adjust their investment portfolios. As a rule, this process also leads to an increase in liquidity and trading activity on the market, making the stock potentially more attractive to further categories of investors. However, this effect does not automatically prove lasting over time, since a significant portion of the price movement is often anticipated by market operators even before the company's actual entry into the official benchmark index.

Current composition and history of the index

Currently, excluding the changes just implemented, the SMI is composed in alphabetical order of ABB for electrical engineering, Alcon for ophthalmology, Amrize for industry, Geberit for sanitary products, Givaudan for flavors and fragrances, Holcim for building materials, Kühne+Nagel for logistics, Logitech for electronics, Lonza for pharmaceuticals, Nestlé for food, Novartis for pharmaceuticals, Partners Group for finance, Richemont for luxury goods, Roche for pharmaceuticals, Sika for specialty chemicals, Swiss Life for insurance, Swiss Re for reinsurance, Swisscom for telecommunications, UBS for banking services, and Zurich Insurance for insurance. If the ranking is instead drawn up on the basis of market capitalization, the giants Roche, Novartis, and Nestlé stand out. The Swiss Market Index was created almost four decades ago, exactly on June 30, 1988, starting from an initial level of 1,500 points. The lowest point in its history was touched on January 14, 1991, at 1,287.60 points, while over the years the value has multiplied by more than 9 times. Among the historical milestones are the 5,000 points reached in 1997, 10,000 in 2019, 12,000 in 2021, 13,000 in February 2025, and 14,000 points in February 2026, reaching an all-time high of 14,669.51 points on August 8 of last year. Today the index is hovering around 13,900 points. The best annual performance was in 1993 with a plus 40%, while the worst was recorded in 2008 with a minus 35%. Last year the index posted a plus 14.4%, while during 2026 the SMI has gained 4.9% so far. For economic history enthusiasts, in 1988 the first SMI included 22 companies, among which were Brown, Boveri & Cie, the Swiss People's Bank, Swiss Credit, Swiss Bank Corporation, Union Bank of Switzerland, Swissair, and Winterthur.

Key points

Operational analysis for investors in the Swiss market

The revision of the stock exchange price lists managed by SIX entails significant strategic evaluations for those operating directly on financial markets or holding savings instruments linked to Swiss equity indices. Those managing personal portfolios through indexed funds or ETFs must consider that the new sectoral weighting further shifts the center of gravity of the SMI towards the pharmaceutical and healthcare sector, which now accounts for approximately forty percent of the total capitalization. This means that risk exposure and the diversification of one's equity portfolio change structurally, marginally reducing the weight of traditional sectors such as logistics and telecommunications, previously represented by Kühne+Nagel and Swisscom. Institutional and private investors monitoring securities added to or removed from the index must carefully verify the liquidity and trading volumes of the individual securities involved, bearing in mind that price movements linked to the actual entry or exit from the main baskets often tend to be anticipated by the market in the weeks preceding the official implementation of the SIX resolution.
