Buying a home in the Canton of Bern requires an understanding of the Swiss financing system and the cantonal tax structure. The process is governed by federal and cantonal laws governing mortgage mortgages, property taxes, and notarial procedures. Those who want to buy a house in Bern must first of all know that the Swiss tax system is divided into three levels: federal, cantonal and municipal. Each tier applies its own rates on property and real estate transactions.

To buy a house, the buyer must have a share of equity (generally 20-30% of the price), while the rest is financed by a mortgage loan from a Swiss bank. Credit institutions assess repayment capacity

Operational details

Financial Planning

Before buying, it is essential to evaluate your ability to repay the mortgage. The Swiss bank carries out strict assessments of solvency, considering income, monthly expenses, other debts, and the relationship between the mortgage and the value of the property. The rule of thumb is that mortgage installments (including interest, amortization, taxes, insurance, maintenance expenses) must not exceed 33% of monthly gross income.

A buyer with monthly gross income of CHF 6'000 may not have total real estate expenses in excess of CHF 2'000 per month. This assessment is crucial to getting the bank's approval. In addition, the maintenance of a property entails annual recurring costs in addition to the mortgage installment: maintenance of the structure, construction insurance (mandatory in many Swiss cantons), condominium expenses if in a condominium, and above all annual real estate taxes (federal + cantonal + municipal). These expenses vary significantly depending on the canton and municipality of residence, making proper economic planning essential before purchase.

Key points

Step-by-Step Procedure

The home buying process in the Canton of Bern follows a standardized path. Here are the main steps:

1. Pre-approval of the mortgage: Contact a Swiss bank for a preliminary solvency assessment. The bank requires documents such as the last 2-3 pay slips, income tax declaration, bank statements, and a list of debts. This step takes 1-2 weeks.

2. Property search: Search for properties on the Bernese real estate market through specialized Swiss portals or local real estate agents.

3. Offer and preliminary agreement: Once you've found the property, send an offer to the seller. If accepted, the notary draws up the preliminary purchase agreement (Kaufvertrag) that both parties must sign. The preliminary agreement is a legally binding document.

4. Final mortgage approval: The bank communicates the final approval after verifying the property appraisal. Simultaneously, the notary checks the property title at the Land Registry Office.

5. Definitive deed and closing: The notary draws up the definitive deed that both parties sign in the presence of the notary. The notary registers the transfer at the Cantonal Land Registry Office, which issues the property certificate.

6. Fund transfer: The buyer pays the balance of the funds and the mortgage is disbursed by the bank to the notary's account for payment to the seller. The property is now officially registered in the Land Registry.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main requirements to get a mortgage loan in Switzerland?
The bank requires verified repayment capacity (usually no more than 33% of gross income), own funds equal to at least 20-30% of the value of the property, stable income documented by last 2-3 coupons and income statement, and no significant credit problems. The property must have a value ascertained by an independent expert.
What are the main expenses to consider when buying a home in the Canton of Bern?
In addition to the purchase price and mortgage loan, there are notary fees (about 1-2% of the price), transfer tax (variable rate according to Bernese cantonal law), registration fees at the Land Office (about 0.1-0.5%), and construction insurance (mandatory in Switzerland). After purchase, annual property taxes (federal, cantonal, municipal) and maintenance costs are added.
How long does the whole process of buying a house in Bern take?
From preliminary (Kaufvertrag) to closing, the process typically takes 2-4 months, depending on the complexity of the property, the speed of bank approval (usually 2-4 weeks) and notary work (title checks, drafting deeds, registration with the Land Office).
What is the Land Registry and why is it important when buying a home?
The Land Register (Grundbuch in German) is the official public register of real estate in the Canton of Bern, maintained by the Cantonal Land Office. Documents the owner of the property, real estate rights, mortgages, and other constraints on the property. Only registration in the land register confers official ownership rights to the buyer.
Do I have to pay the transfer tax if I buy a house in the Canton of Bern?
Yes. The transfer tax (Grunderwerbsteuer in German) is a cantonal tax applied at the time of transfer of ownership. The rate and methods of calculation vary according to Bernese cantonal law. The notary calculates the amount and communicates it to the buyer before signing the final deed. It is a mandatory expense included in the total purchase costs.
