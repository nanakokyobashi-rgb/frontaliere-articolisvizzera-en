Seco's position

On October 14, 2026, Helene Budliger Artieda defended the gifts given to Donald Trump by a delegation of Swiss entrepreneurs. The director of Seco clarified that the initiative came from the private sector and that the watch and the ingot were not intended personally for the then 79-year-old president of the United States, but for the American people.

In an interview with Bloomberg TV, given on the sidelines of an economic forum in New Delhi, the outgoing secretary of state added that the gifts were registered at the White House. If all goes well, he explained, they will be destined for a presidential library. Budliger Artieda has resigned effective at the end of March 2027.

The visit and the duties dossier

The delivery took place in November 2025, during a visit to the United States organized while the dispute over customs barriers was ongoing. The delegation went to Trump to negotiate and brought him a Rolex tabletop and an engraved gold bar, with a total value of around 100,000 francs.

The director described the participants as serious businessmen, convinced that they had acted within the framework of the legal provisions and after having carried out legal checks. Among them were Alfred Gantner, co-founder of Partners Group, Jean-Frédéric Dufour, number one at Rolex, and Johann Rupert, chairman of the board of directors of Richemont.

The matter remains open on a political-administrative level. The Management Commission of the National Council wants to examine the actions of the federal authorities in relation to American tariffs; the report is expected to be concluded by the end of the year. On the negotiation front, Seco says it is satisfied: since November 2025, when the reduction of US duties from 39% to 15% was announced, the United States has respected the agreement. Bern seeks security and calmer trade relations after the turbulent times following the tariff announcement on the so-called Liberation Day in April 2025. Talks for a legally binding agreement, however, continue.
