Operational details

Gender medicine in Switzerland: new challenges and opportunities

Gender medicine in Switzerland encounters political and financial resistance that hinders its development. MPs are working to align sperm donation laws, but progress is slow. Single women have greater difficulty accessing WFP abroad, where laws are more permissive. This creates a situation of gender inequality that must be addressed.

📊 Facts and Context In 2023, only 3% of women in Switzerland used WFP, compared to 12% of men. This gap is partly attributable to legal and financial restrictions. For example, the canton of Zurich introduced a law in 2021 that limits the use of PMA to married couples or in a civil union, while the canton of Geneva has adopted more permissive legislation, allowing the use of PMA even for single women.

💡 Proposed solutions To address these inequalities, the Swiss Parliament is considering the introduction of a federal law regulating the use of WFP. This law should include:

  • Equal access to WFP for all women, regardless of their marital status.
  • Public funding to cover the cost of the WFP, which currently amounts to CHF 15,000-20,000 per cycle.
  • Legal protection for women using WFP, ensuring their privacy and the security of their data.

What to do

Swiss parliamentarians are working to align laws on sperm donation in Switzerland. Currently, only the canton of Ticino allows sperm donation, while other cantons like Zurich and Geneva have more restrictive regulations. Single women can consider accessing PMA abroad, where laws are more permissive. For example, in Germany, sperm donation is allowed and regulated, with an average cost of 3,000-5,000 euros per cycle. In France, the process is simpler and less expensive, with costs that can go down to 2,000-3,000 euros. However, it is important to consult local regulations and the specific requirements of each country.

📊 Comparison between Switzerland and foreign countries:

  • Switzerland: Only Ticino allows sperm donation. Other cantons require the use of anonymous donor sperm or international sperm banks.
  • Germany: Sperm donation allowed, with medical and legal requirements. Average cost: 3,000-5,000 euros per cycle.
  • France: Simpler and less expensive process. Average cost: 2,000-3,000 euros per cycle.
  • Spain: Sperm donation allowed, with medical and legal requirements. Average cost: 2,500-4,000 euros per cycle.
