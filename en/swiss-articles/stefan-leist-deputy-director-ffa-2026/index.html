Key facts

  • What: Appointment as deputy director of the Federal Finance Administration

  • Importance: Key role for financial policy and inter‑cantonal equalisation

Federal Councilor Karin Keller‑Sutter, head of the Federal Department of Finance, appointed Stefan Leist as deputy director of the Federal Finance Administration (AFF) effective 1 October 2026. The decision was communicated to the Federal Council in the meeting of 9 September 2026.

Professional background and skills

Stefan Leist, 45, has many years of experience within the Federal Administration. Since 1 March 2026 he has already held the position of deputy director of the AFF and head of the Financial Policy, Financial Equalisation and Financial Statistics division. He earned a degree in political economy at the University of Bern and obtained his PhD in 2012, the year he joined the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO). Before moving to the AFF, he headed the Labour Market Analysis and Social Policy unit at SECO and was deputy head of the Economic Policy Directorate.

Operational details

Implications for Swiss financial policy

The appointment of Leist comes at a time when federal financial policy must confront structural challenges: the brake on indebtedness, the equalisation of resources among cantons and the evolution of the federal direct tax. The Financial Policy, Financial Equalisation and Financial Statistics division that he leads is the analytical engine behind the proposals that the Federal Council submits to Parliament for the budget and the final accounts.

Continuity and signals from within

The fact that Leist was already deputy director of the AFF since 1 March 2026 signals operational continuity: the promotion to deputy director formalises a role that he has de facto exercised for six months. His career at SECO, where he acquired expertise on the labour market and economic policy, brings to the AFF a transversal vision that links federal revenues, social spending and employment dynamics. This hybrid profile is rare at the top of the financial administration and could strengthen coordination between the Federal Department of Finance and the Federal Department of Economic Affairs, Education and Research.

Key points

What changes for those who live and work in Switzerland

The appointment does not directly change rates or deadlines for taxpayers. However, stability at the top of the AFF is a necessary condition for the budget, final account and equalization processes to respect constitutional deadlines. Delays or uncertainties in these processes have an impact on cantonal estimates and, ultimately, on the taxes that residents — including border workers — pay at the cantonal and municipal levels.

How to follow the work of the AFF

Financial policy documents, equalisation reports and financial statistics are published on the AFF and UST/BFS website. Those who want to monitor the impact of federal decisions on their canton can consult the cantonal estimates, usually adopted between November and December for the following year. To estimate the impact of direct federal tax and cantonal/municipal taxes on your income, it is useful to compare the updated municipal multipliers.

Frequently Asked Questions
When does Stefan Leist take office as AFF Deputy Director?
Stefan Leist assumes the role of deputy director of the Federal Finance Administration from 1 October 2026, as decided by Federal Councillor Karin Keller-Sutter and communicated to the Federal Council on 9 September 2026.
What are the core competencies of the division that Leit leads?
The Financial Policy, Financial Equalization and Financial Statistics division deals with the elaboration of federal financial policy, the equalization of resources between the Confederation and the Cantons, and the production of official financial statistics that feed into decision-making processes.
How does this appointment affect the taxes I pay?
The appointment does not directly change rates or deadlines. However, the continuity at the top of the AFF ensures that the federal budget and intercantonal equalization processes respect time, avoiding delays that could reverberate on the cantonal and municipal budgets and, consequently, on local taxes.
