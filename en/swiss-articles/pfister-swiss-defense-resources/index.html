At the LAC in Lugano, Martin Pfister raised an alarm about the ability of the Swiss army to protect the country. The federal councilor responsible for Defense stated that, currently, the available resources are not sufficient to adequately deal with the most likely threats. The speech took place during the annual conference of the Association of the Military Magazine of Italian Switzerland.

The place of intervention is Lugano, but the object of the message is national: the protection of the entire country and the resources necessary for the army. The Lugano headquarters thus hosted a position statement which also concerns federal funding.

The threats indicated in Lugano

Pfister's message concerns a broader definition of conflict. Armed clashes remain only part of the scenario: the defense manager also indicated espionage, sabotage, cyber attacks and disinformation campaigns. Added to this list are air raids, including those carried out with drones. The protection of the territory, in the interpretation presented in Lugano, therefore requires capabilities that cover different threats and not just traditional combat.

The problem, therefore, is not presented as linked to a single tool: the list includes physical, digital and information threats.

To address these challenges, Pfister argues for new investments. The indicated channel is the army financing plan, linked to a temporary increase in VAT. The reference to the federal councilor brings the dossier within the scope of the Federal Council and links it to a financing choice, without providing an individual calculation.

The federal councilor then recalled two elements external to military spending alone. On the one hand, collaboration with European countries; on the other, the revision of the rules on the export of war material. The declared objective is also to protect Swiss industry.

The picture delivered by the conference is therefore double: on the one hand the denunciation of insufficient resources, on the other a line of action based on investments, international cooperation and revised export rules. Operational details

The reference to VAT changes how families, workers and businesses should read the announcement. It is not a payroll item: VAT affects consumer prices. To understand what to monitor, the first distinction is between the federal and cantonal levels.

VAT and other tax items

VAT is distinct from direct federal tax and cantonal and municipal taxes. The plan mentioned by Pfister, therefore, should not be confused with the municipal tax multiplier or with a change to cantonal and municipal taxes. Cantonal administrations are the point of reference for the cantonal and municipal levels, while the federal level includes VAT and direct federal tax. This separation prevents any potential temporary increase in VAT from being assumed to have automatic effects on other taxes.

There is a second useful distinction for those who work in Switzerland. The announcement concerns army funding and VAT; it should not be read as a change to AVS/AHV, LPP/BVG or LAMal/KVG premiums. Those items belong to social security or health insurance, while VAT concerns consumption. The possible impact to observe at the household level is therefore on the cost of living and the final price of purchases, not on a new salary deduction indicated by the news.

Public spending and industry

The proposal connects two aspects: the need to invest to address the threats mentioned and a funding line linked to VAT. The approach described by Pfister links security and the public budget, without providing an individual amount to be applied to families.

For Swiss companies, the other aspect concerns the rules on the export of war materiel and relations with European countries. The message concerns not only investments: it also includes the protection of Swiss industry.
