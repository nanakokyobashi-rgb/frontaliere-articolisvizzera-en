Draghi at ETH: EU should follow Swiss example to grow

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 October 2026

Draghi in Zurich: debt/GDP at 130% in 2040, 1.3 trillion for AI by 2030. The EU must integrate like the US, citing the Confederation as a model.

Context

In brief

  • Draghi spoke at ETH Zurich.
  • He calls for supranational reforms and an EU roadmap.
  • The debt/GDP ratio will reach 130% by 2040.
  • AI requires 1,3 trillion by 2030.

Key facts

  • Speaker → Mario Draghi
  • Location → ETH Zurich
  • Event → anniversary of the SNB lecture series
  • Debt/GDP → 130% by 2040
  • Investments in AI → 1,3 trillion by 2030

In Zurich, at ETH Zurich, Mario Draghi invited the European Union to look to Switzerland's example. The former president of the European Central Bank and former Italian prime minister spoke at the anniversary of the Swiss National Bank's lecture series in honor of economist Karl Brunner. As guest of honor, he brought the scale needed to address growth back to center stage.

The reference is precise: according to Draghi, Switzerland is not a federation but a Confederation in which, in some sectors, real growth objectives can be achieved only on a large scale. The proposal to the EU is therefore supranational. For the former prime minister, Europe must overcome nationalisms and pursue greater integration, comparable to that which unites the United States of America, in order to regain a role in the global geoeconomy.

Draghi's proposal

«Supranational reforms have become the most important lever for growth. A detailed plan for implementing these reforms was outlined in the report on Europe's competitiveness, and much of it is contained in the EU roadmap. The first thing we must do is implement this roadmap,» said Draghi, guest of honor at the event.

The speech, more focused on monetary policy than on economic policy compared with other occasions, described a Europe facing a different phase from that of a few years ago. Its economy is the most open in the world, but it must contend with Donald Trump's tariff policy, Chinese aggressiveness, and two conflicts whose end is not in sight.

Debt, inflation and artificial intelligence

Growth, according to Draghi, is the lever on which Europe can still act, while interest rates respond to global synchronization. Fiscal consolidation therefore depends on economic expansion. Monetary policy remains decisive for controlling inflation, but it cannot solve everything: when debt dynamics worsen, markets put the central bank's commitment to the test and demand to know how it will respond to every scenario.

Draghi indicated a debt/GDP ratio of 130% by 2040 and unavoidable fiscal consolidation in the medium term. Spending cuts and tax increases alone would hardly be enough. On the technological front, rapid AI adoption could add up to 0,4 percentage points to total factor productivity growth over the next decade. To make AI competitive in the EU, 1,3 trillion in investment would be needed by 2030, along with 100 billion from the EU budget to reduce its risk.

Operational details

Reading for those who live in Switzerland

For a resident or worker in Switzerland, the news has a precise but limited practical effect: it does not announce a new tax, a wage change or a decision by the SNB. Its weight lies in the way it describes European growth and in the fact that the Swiss reference is used to discuss the ability to act on a large scale. The place of intervention should not be confused with the recipient of the proposal: Draghi spoke in Zurich, but addressed his roadmap to the European Union.

The Swiss reference remains a scale model

The decisive word is integration. In Draghi's reconstruction, some goals cannot be effectively achieved by isolated states. Switzerland was called into question as a Confederation, not as the recipient of a package of reforms announced on the stage of the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology. For those who follow national economic policy, the distinction prevents turning a discourse on EU growth into an immediate measure for households, businesses or workers in Switzerland.

The same applies to the SNB. The event was linked to the cycle of lectures in honour of Karl Brunner, but the source does not report a decision on rates or a new direction from the Swiss central bank. Draghi discussed the role of monetary policy in general: controlling inflation remains a priority, while markets need to understand how the central bank will react to frequent and complex shocks. The operational point, for the reader, is therefore not to attribute to the SNB a position that does not appear in the text.

Three floors not to overlap

The first is fiscal: consolidation depends on growth and, according to Draghi, spending cuts and tax increases alone may not produce the necessary results. The second is monetary: the interest rate is increasingly set outside Europe, while growth remains the lever on which Europe can act. The third is technological: the single market and AI are presented as potential sources of growth and productivity, not as already achieved results.

For everyday life, the link to the costo della vita in Svizzera is therefore indirect. The source does not quantify effects on Swiss prices, incomes or household budgets. However, it offers a grid for reading the upcoming discussions: ask yourself whether a proposal concerns European growth, interest rates, consolidation or investment, and always check which institutional level it is aimed at.

Key points

What to do with this news

The useful procedure is not administrative: the source does not reveal a deadline to be met, a form to be filled in or an immediate change for those who reside or work in Switzerland. The action consists of separating Draghi's statements from the effects already decided. This avoids reading a proposal that asks the EU to implement a roadmap as a concrete measure.

A four-step checklist

1. Identify the recipient. The speech was delivered in Zurich, but the request concerns the European Union. Before applying a conclusion to Switzerland, check whether the passage talks about the EU, European governments or the Confederation cited as an example.

2. Classify leverage. If the text is about inflation, rates or central bank communication, it is about monetary policy. If it is about debt, spending cuts and taxes, it is about consolidation. If it is about the single market, productivity and AI, it is about growth and investment. The three categories do not have the same effect or the same deadline.

3. Separate estimates and decisions. 130% of the debt/GDP ratio by 2040, 100 billion from the EU budget and 1.3 trillion by 2030 are figures used to support Draghi's proposal. They should not be transformed into an amount owed by a Swiss resident, nor into a decision already taken.

4. Report the data to your question. Those who are evaluating their income should not use a European productivity forecast as if it were an update of their paycheck. To check the salary items, you can consult the busta paga svizzera. On the other hand, those who want to measure the effect on their budget can start from a personal calculation, keeping the macroeconomic plan separate from the individual one.

The point to follow

The subsequent verification must focus on any implementation of the roadmap and on the choices of governments and the EU, because it is there that Draghi places the responsibility for growth. The source does not announce a Swiss timetable and does not indicate documents or offices to turn to. For this reason, the correct action, today, is to use numbers and time horizons as reference points, without attributing them an automatic personal effect.
