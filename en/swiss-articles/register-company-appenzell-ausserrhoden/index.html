Registering a business in external Appenzello (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 September 2026

Guide to the steps, legal forms, registration costs and insurance obligations for those who open a business in canton AR.

Context

In brief

  • Three legal forms: sole proprietorship, partnerships and corporations
  • Registration in the cantonal commercial register; cantonal and municipal taxes
  • Mandatory AVS/AI from CHF 2,300 income; contributions 5.3% and 10.6%

Key facts

  • What: Registration of a company in the Swiss commercial register
  • When: Within 14 days of the company's foundation (mandatory)
  • Where: Commercial register of the canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden
  • Who: Natural and legal persons exercising commercial activities
  • Legal forms: Sole proprietorship, general partnership, limited partnership, corporation, LLC
  • Federal competence: SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs)
  • Cantonal competence: Canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden for cantonal-municipal taxes

Anyone wishing to start a business in the canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden must first choose the legal form that best suits their situation. Switzerland recognizes three main categories: the sole proprietorship (one person managing the business), partnerships (two or more partners with joint and several liability), and corporations (corporation and limited liability company, with liability limited to the capital contributed).

Registration in the commercial register is mandatory for anyone exercising a commercial activity in a professional and lasting manner. The deadline is 14 days from the date of foundation. Without registration, the company cannot operate legally in any Swiss canton.

Legal forms and requirements

The sole proprietorship is the simplest form: a natural person exercises the activity on their own, with full personal liability for debts. It does not require a minimum capital. The entrepreneur has full discretion over operational choices but is personally liable with their assets for any obligation of the company.

Operational details

Registration costs and taxes

The costs of registering with the commercial register vary from canton to canton. In Switzerland, there is no single federal rate: each canton and each municipality sets its own registration fees (first-time registration fee) and subsequent business taxes. This multiplicity of tax regimes reflects the Swiss federal system, where direct tax competencies are largely delegated to the cantons and municipalities.

The federal direct tax (FDT) applies at the federal level with progressive rates. To this is added the cantonal tax, the amount of which depends on the income and capital of the company. The canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden, like every canton, has its own municipal multipliers that modify the cantonal rate for the municipalities of the territory.

A company generating income for the first time must take into account three levels of taxation: federal, cantonal, and municipal. The cantonal and municipal tax return must be filed together with the federal return within the set deadlines. For natural persons in the form of a sole proprietorship, the company's income is added to the personal income and taxed in the overall personal return.

VAT and accounting obligations

If the annual turnover reaches or exceeds CHF 100,000, VAT (Value Added Tax) registration becomes mandatory. The Federal Customs Administration (FCA) manages the Swiss VAT register. Up to this threshold, registration remains optional but is recommended if the company has significant deductible costs or intends to export services abroad.

Key points

Insurance and Pension Obligations

Every entrepreneur and every employee must be insured with the Old Age and Survivors' Insurance (AHV) and the Disability Insurance (IV). The obligation arises for a net income from gainful employment of at least CHF 2,300 annually. The contributions are 5.3% for the employee and 10.6% in total for the employer (based on the salary mass). For self-employed individuals (sole proprietorships), the payment is 10.6% based on the net income.

In addition to AHV/IV, contributions to unemployment insurance (ALV) and accident insurance (UVG/UVG) are mandatory. ALV amounts to 1.1% up to an annual maximum set by law, while UVG/UVG varies from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector of activity (agriculture, industry, offices, sales, healthcare, etc.). The canton of Appenzell Ausserrhoden, like all others, applies these federal rates.

For employees, the contribution to the pension fund (BVG, occupational pension fund — mandatory from CHF 21,330 of coordinated salary) depends on age: 7% for 25-34 years, 10% for 35-44, 15% for 45-54, and 18% for 55+ until the reference age. These amounts apply to the so-called 'coordinated salary mass', i.e., the gross salary minus the coordination deduction. With AHV and BVG you can calculate your benefits and understand how they evolve over time.

Those residing in Switzerland (including entrepreneurs and employees) must take out health insurance (KVG, Federal Law on Health Insurance) within 3 months of arrival in the country. It is a private insurance with per capita premiums, NOT a tax or a deductible salary contribution. Each person, regardless of income, chooses their own policy among the authorized insurers.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum capital required to open a business in Switzerland?
It depends on the legal form. A sole proprietorship does not require minimum capital. A public limited company (SA) requires CHF 100,000, while a limited liability company (SARL) requires CHF 20,000. Partnerships only require the contribution agreed between the members, with no standardized federal minimum.
How soon do I have to register my company with the trade register?
Registration is mandatory within 14 days of the company's foundation. Without registration, the exercise of the activity is illegal and carries criminal penalties. The trade register issues a certificate, which is essential to operate legally and open company bank accounts, obtain commercial supplies and represent the company before the authorities.
Since when do I have to pay AVS/AI contributions as an entrepreneur?
The obligation starts when the net income from gainful activity reaches CHF 2,300 per year. For self-employed and employers, contributions are 10.6% based on net income (self-employed) or salary (employers with employees). The payment takes place at the federal level, managed by the cantonal tax offices.
When does VAT registration become mandatory?
VAT registration is mandatory if the annual turnover reaches or exceeds CHF 100,000. Below this threshold, enrollment remains optional, but may be convenient if the business incurs significant deductible costs or exports services abroad. The management takes place at the Federal Customs Administration.
What health insurance is needed to open a business in Switzerland?
LAMal (health insurance) is mandatory for all residents, including entrepreneurs. It must be stipulated within 3 months of the arrival or registration of the company. It is a private insurance with per capita premiums, variable by canton, region and deductible (from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year for adults). The canton of Appenzello Esterno offers cantonal subsidies for those with low income, based on income and assets.
