Wages and work in Switzerland: training levels (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 19 August 2026

How Swiss wages work by level of training, compulsory social contributions, collective agreements and cantonal minimum wage.

Context

In a nutshell

  • AVS/AI/IPG contributions amount to 5.3% for the employee (10.6% with the employer)
  • The maximum hours are 45 hours per week in industry, offices and sales; 50 in other sectors
  • The LPP provides for pension contributions from 7% to 18% of the coordinated salary according to age
  • Some cantons have their own minimum wage; Switzerland has no federal minimum

Key facts

  • What: Compulsory wage structure and social security contributions in Switzerland
  • Who: Employees, employers, Federal Office of Social Security (UFAS/BSV)
  • Where: Entire Swiss territory (national + cantonal regulations)
  • When: Continuous application; amounts indexed annually
  • AVS contribution: 5.3% employee + 5.3% employer (total 10.6%)
  • LPP age 25-34: Minimum 7% of the coordinated salary

The Swiss labour market is based on a wage structure that is strictly regulated at federal and cantonal level. Those who work in Switzerland automatically pay mandatory contributions for old-age and survivors' insurance (AVS), disability insurance (AI) and unemployment insurance (IPG). These three contributions amount to 5.3% of the gross salary for the employee, while the employer pays an identical share, for a total of 10.6%.

Operational details

Training, qualifications and salary levels

In Switzerland, the link between level of education and salary level is direct. Those who have an apprenticeship certification (initial vocational training, AFC) have access to different salary levels than those who have completed high school, and still different from those who have graduated from a university or polytechnic school. However, the SOURCE CONTENT does not specify precise minimum wage figures by degree of training, because the Swiss system does not have a single national minimum wage.

Minimum Wage: Federal Absence and Cantonal Diversity

Unlike many European countries, Switzerland does not have a federal law setting a national minimum wage. This means that the minimum wage protection is managed by the individual cantons and, in some cases, by collective labour agreements (CCLs). Some cantons have introduced their own cantonal minimum wages; others rely entirely on collective agreements and the market. For those who work or are looking for a job in Switzerland, it is therefore essential to check the regulations of the canton of residence or employment.

Collective labour agreements play a central role in determining wages and conditions of employment. These agreements are negotiated between trade unions (on the one hand) and employers' associations (on the other) and set minimum wages, wage increases, working hours, public holidays and additional benefits for entire sectors

Key points

How to navigate the Swiss salary system: concrete actions

Anyone starting a job in Switzerland or already working and looking to negotiate a promotion needs to understand the practical workings of the salary package. A typical Swiss salary package shows:

1. Gross salary: the amount agreed upon in the employment contract 2. Deductions (AVS/AI/IPG 5.3%, LPP according to age and coordination, LAINF/LAA 0.7-1.5%, VAT if due, any salary tax) 3. Net salary: what remains after all deductions

To verify the accuracy of your salary package, it's a good idea to consult the applicable rates in your canton and industry. The SECO (State Secretariat for Economic Affairs) and the cantonal offices of social security provide free online calculators.

Step 1: Check the cantonal minimum salary

Before negotiating or accepting a job offer, check the cantonal administration website to see if there is a minimum salary in your canton. If not, consult the applicable collective labor agreement (available on union or employer association websites).

Step 2: Calculate the coordinated salary for LPP

The coordinated salary is not the gross salary, but a technical concept: gross salary minus a fixed deduction (the amount of which is adjusted annually). From this coordinated salary, the pension contributions are deducted according to your age group. Use the pension funds' or your employer's online calculators to get precise estimates.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum wage in Switzerland?
Switzerland does not have a national federal minimum wage. Some cantons have introduced their own cantonal minimum wages, while others rely on collective bargaining agreements (CCLs). The minimum wage protection is therefore managed at the cantonal level and through the CCL negotiated between trade unions and employers' associations.
How much should I pay of AVS/AI/IPG from my paycheck?
As an employee, you pay 5.3% of the gross salary for AVS/AI/IPG. The employer pays an identical share (5.3%), for a total of 10.6%. These contributions are mandatory and are withheld directly from your paycheck.
How is the LPP contribution calculated?
The LPP contribution is based on your age and coordinated salary (gross salary minus a fixed indexed deduction each year). The minimum rate is 7% from 25 to 34 years old, increases to 10% from 35 to 44, to 15% from 45 to 54, and to 18% from 55 to the reference age.
What is the maximum working time in Switzerland?
The maximum hours are 45 hours per week in industry, offices and retail; 50 hours in other sectors. These limits are established by federal laws and SECO ordinances and apply throughout the national territory.
How much holiday am I entitled to?
Guaranteed minimum vacation is 4 weeks per year for employees over the age of 20. Workers under the age of 20 are entitled to 5 weeks. These minimums are set by the Swiss Code of Obligations and apply at national level.
