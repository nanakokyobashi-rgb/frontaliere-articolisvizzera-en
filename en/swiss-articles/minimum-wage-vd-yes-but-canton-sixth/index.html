Vaud backs constitutional min wage, rejects specific law (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 15 June 2026

49.1% in favor of the constitutional initiative, 47.5% against the operational law at 23 francs/hour. Vaud becomes the sixth canton with a minimum wage.

Context

In brief

49.1% of voters approve the constitutional initiative for a minimum wage in Vaud 47.5% reject the specific legislative proposal of 23 CHF/hour 46.8% oppose the State Council's counter-proposal

Key facts

  • Cosa: Introduction of the minimum wage in the canton of Vaud

  • Dove: Canton Vaud, Switzerland

  • Importo: 23 CHF/hour (proposed value but not approved)

The canton of Vaud held a crucial vote on the minimum wage that attracted a turnout of 54.3% of electors. Although 49.1% voted in favor of the constitutional initiative to introduce a minimum wage, the concrete legislative proposal setting that wage at 23 CHF/hour was rejected by 47.5% of voters. A similar result was recorded for the State Council's counter-proposal, which was rejected with 46.8% of votes against. In Vaud's counting system, blank ballots, accounting for 5%, are included in the calculation, making the approval of the constitutional initiative valid even without surpassing the absolute 50% threshold.

Vaud's decision fits into a broader national debate on the minimum wage. Currently, only five Swiss cantons have a legal minimum wage: Neuchâtel, Jura, Geneva, Ticino, and Basel-City. Among these, Geneva applies the highest minimum wage in the world, set at 24 CHF/hour (approved in 2020).

Operational details

Implications for the Labour Market

The outcome of the vote creates a unique situation in the Canton of Vaud: while the principle of a minimum wage was accepted in the cantonal constitution, an operational mechanism to make it effective is still lacking. This legislative void could generate uncertainty among both employers and employees, particularly for those working under individual contracts not covered by collective agreements. The sectors most exposed could include hospitality, retail, and personal services, where wages tend to be lower than the cantonal average.

💡 For example, in the Canton of Neuchâtel, the minimum wage of CHF 20/hour introduced in 2017 helped to stabilize wages in at-risk sectors, but required months of adjustment for small businesses.

📊 According to data from the Federal Statistical Office (OFS), approximately 10% of the workforce in Switzerland earns less than CHF 22/hour, a figure often considered the benchmark for a decent living wage. However, the absence of specific legislation in Vaud could slow down the reduction of this percentage.

Key points

What to do now for workers and employers

For workers in the canton of Vaud, it is essential to closely follow regulatory developments related to the introduction of the minimum wage. Although the popular initiative has led to its inclusion in the cantonal Constitution, the measure will not be immediately applicable: its implementation will depend on the enactment of specific legislation. This step requires negotiations between unions, representing workers, and employers' associations. For example, in the canton of Geneva, the minimum wage was set at 23 francs per hour (around 4,000 francs monthly for full-time work), but it took months of discussions to finalize the application details.

Affected workers should check whether their current salary might be impacted by the new regulation. Employers, on the other hand, are advised to start calculating the economic impact of potential salary adjustments, especially in sectors with a low-income workforce, such as hospitality or retail.

Frequently Asked Questions
When will the minimum wage come into effect in Vaud?
It has not been specified yet. The canton must first draft a new law to implement the constitutional provision that was approved.
What is the proposed amount for the minimum wage?
The proposed amount in the rejected law was 23 Swiss francs per hour. The new figure might change in the next legislative proposal.
How does Vaud compare to other Swiss cantons?
Vaud becomes the sixth canton with a minimum wage, following Neuchâtel, Geneva, Jura, Ticino, and Basel-City. At the municipal level, Lucerne, Zurich, and Winterthur also have similar measures.
