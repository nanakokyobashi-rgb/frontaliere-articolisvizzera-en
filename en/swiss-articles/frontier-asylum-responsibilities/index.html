Operational details

Split expertise, concentrated costs

Asylum management in Switzerland follows a clear division of competences between the three administrative levels. The Confederation, through the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM), defines the national migration and asylum policy, welcomes applicants to the Federal Centres, and conducts examination procedures. The Canton of Ticino manages cantonal administrative aspects. Municipalities find themselves at the forefront when it comes to dealing with the concrete consequences: public order, security, local police services.

The problem identified concerns the imbalance between this theoretical division of competences and the actual distribution of costs. When a federal asylum facility is located in the territory of a municipality, the local administration and citizens have no say in deciding how many people will be received, for how long, or what support and control measures will be implemented. However, when security incidents occur, municipalities must activate their police forces, support working hours, ensure safeguards in the territory.

For this reason, the questioning in Balerna raises concrete and principled questions: what are the current mechanisms for reimbursement or compensation for the additional costs incurred by the Municipalities? Key points

Next steps: from data to solution

The questioning in Balerna represents a first concrete step towards the collection of objective data on the situation. The Municipality is called upon to respond with precise figures: how many police interventions? What costs? What nationalities are involved? How many incidents have involved people already known to the authorities?

These data are crucial because they allow us to move from the general debate to the specific negotiation between the administrative levels. If Balerna can accurately document that the Federal Asylum Centers on its territory have generated a certain number of police interventions per year with an estimated cost, that number becomes the foundation of a credible claim for reimbursement or compensation. Without concrete data, the claim remains generic.

The next step, as indicated in the interpellation, is the coordination between the border municipalities of Mendrisiotto. Presenting a common position to the Canton and the Confederation increases the political weight of the request. A single municipality could be ignored; a coalition of border municipalities has greater capacity for pressure and negotiation with Bern.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who decides on asylum policy in Switzerland?
Asylum policy and the management of Federal Asylum Centres are the responsibility of the Confederation and the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM). The municipalities do not decide who is accepted, nor the duration of the procedures. However, when incidents of violence, theft, or disturbances of public order occur in the territory, it is the local authorities who face the consequences and bear the operational and security costs.
Are there currently reimbursement mechanisms for municipalities?
The interpellation to Balerna poses exactly this question. There is currently no automatic reimbursement or compensation system for the additional costs incurred by Municipalities due to incidents related to asylum seekers. The signatories ask the Municipality to clarify whether such mechanisms exist and, if they do not, claim a direct federal participation by the SEM.
What was the incident that motivated the interpellation?
On August 22 in Balerna, in Via Passeggiata, a woman was assaulted and robbed. According to the interpellation, the alleged perpetrator is a young man of Algerian nationality whose residence is unknown. The episode reignited the debate on security in Mendrisiotto and the relationship between federal asylum policy and the responsibilities of border municipalities.
What data do the petitioners ask for?
The request requires precise data on all interventions by the municipal or regional police from 2023 to date attributable to asylum seekers or guests of the Federal Centres. They ask about the type of interventions, the nationalities involved (in compliance with data protection), the hours of work employed and the related costs. Particular attention is paid to people already known to the authorities and to cases with repeated interventions.
What is the ultimate goal of the interpellation?
The declared objective is to coordinate the border municipalities of Mendrisiotto to present a common position to the Canton and the Confederation, calling for greater presence in the territory, more security and a correct distribution of costs between the administrative levels. The promoters stress that being a border region means taking on particular responsibilities in national migration, but it cannot mean passively bearing all the consequences of federal decisions.
