Because the heat drives up prices and climate inflation

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 12 August 2026

From the Rhine drought to the shopping cart, climate change creates new inflation and forces central banks to act.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The scarcity of goods due to drought increases prices.
  • Food costs in supermarkets are expected to rise.
  • Central banks are studying new control tools.
  • The cost of food could go up between 1.5% and 1.8% per year.

Key facts

  • What: Climate inflation and rising prices
  • When: Starting next year
  • Where: Switzerland and Europe
  • Who: Central banks and ECB
  • Amount: 2% (inflation threshold), 3% (Eurozone inflation in 2027), 1.5% and 1.8% (food cost increase), 6% (goods on the road)

One of the fundamental laws of economics states that the scarcity of a good increases its price. This rule applies today to various products, from fuels to agricultural ones, due to the drought affecting Europe. Experts predict that as a result of the so-called climate inflation that in recent weeks in Switzerland has already had its impact on the price of fuels, the cost of shopping in supermarkets will increase starting next year. In the past, the war in the Middle East has put a lot of pressure on prices. That conflict has led to an increase in energy and fertilisers. Today the main concern is climate change. Water scarcity across Europe reduces harvests and drives up prices. Global warming could cause worse damage than wars.

Operational details

The critical impact on agriculture and crops

The lack of rainfall for prolonged periods and heat waves also have a significant impact on agriculture in both Switzerland and much of Europe. The effects, although not yet fully quantifiable, are already measurable on pastures, crops, farms, pastures and the availability of fodder, resulting in increased costs for the entire production sector. Summer crops, in particular maize, potatoes, sugar beets and sunflowers, are among the most affected by the prolonged summer drought. Recently it was learned that more than 1,100 farms have already reported drought damage to Hail Switzerland, the company that protects the sector in case of weather adversity. The situation is so serious that it has led the Confederation to grant extraordinary support to the Swiss agricultural sector to mitigate the economic losses suffered by local producers.

This contraction in agricultural supply fits into a broader economic framework in which the ECB and international financial institutions closely monitor signals coming from food commodity markets. The lack of land products and climate inflation in general are of great concern to central banks, starting with the European one. Until now, financial institutions have considered these increases as isolated events, without implementing targeted interventions

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the climate inflation mentioned by the experts?
The Italian translation of the English Climateflation refers to the increase in prices caused by the physical effects of climate change on the economy, as explained by Isabel Schnabel of the ECB.
What are the sectors most affected by drought in Europe and Switzerland?
The hardest hit sectors are river logistics, due to the exceptionally low level of the Rhine holding back freight transport, and agriculture, with estimated damage to summer crops such as corn, potatoes, sugar beets and sunflowers.
What predictions do central banks make about food inflation?
ECB Chief Economist Philip Lane predicts rising food inflation. Oxford Economics estimates a 3% rise in the Eurozone in 2027, while an ECB and Potsdam Institute study forecasts annual increases between 1.5% and 1.8% by 2035.
