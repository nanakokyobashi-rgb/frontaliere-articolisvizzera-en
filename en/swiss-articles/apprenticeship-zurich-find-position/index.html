Practical guide to apprenticeship in the canton of Zurich: job search, internship contract, apprentice's salary and professional maturity.

Context

  • Guaranteed rights: minimum 4 weeks holiday, maximum time 45 hours per week.

Choosing the route and looking for a place

Apprenticeship in the canton of Zurich represents the pillar of Swiss vocational training, offering young people a unique combination of practical and theoretical teaching. The Zurich system follows federal guidelines, ensuring that training courses are nationally recognised and comply with workers' rights established by Swiss federal law.

Operational details

Remuneration and social contributions

The remuneration of the apprentice is regulated at the federal level and adapted by the individual professional associations and cantons. In the canton of Zurich, the system of collective bargaining and cantonal provisions ensure fair and progressive wages.

A crucial aspect of apprentice pay concerns social security contributions. The apprentice is subject to the payment of AVS/AI/IPG contributions (the so-called “first pillar”), with a rate of 5.3% to be borne by the employee and 10.6% total (including the employer's contribution). These payments are essential to accumulate pension credits from the beginning of your career.

In addition to pension contributions, the apprentice in Zurich is enrolled in occupational accident insurance (LAINF/LAA), with variable rates depending on the sector (0.7-1.5%). Unemployment insurance (AD/AC) also covers the apprentice at a rate of 1.1% until the annual ceiling is reached.

From a tax point of view, the apprentice's salary is subject to direct federal tax, in addition to the cantonal and municipal taxes in force in the canton of Zurich. Each level of government applies its own rates, so it is important to check the specific Zurich provisions with the competent cantonal administration.

Procedure for finding and signing the internship contract

Finding an internship in Zurich follows a structured process. The first step involves consulting national and regional platforms dedicated to offering internship positions. At the federal level, SECO (Federal Department of Economy) provides information on the Swiss labor market and vocational training, while the Zurich cantonal platforms offer specific databases of available positions.

Once the interesting positions have been identified, the candidate must apply directly to the chosen company or through the cantonal intermediation offices. The application includes typically the school curriculum, a motivational letter, and references from former teachers or supervisors.

After the candidate's acceptance by the company, the internship contract is drawn up. The contract is a standardized federal document that must contain: duration of the training period, sector and profile of the internship, monthly remuneration and payment methods, working days and hours, vacation periods, rights and duties of the intern and the company, and the probation period (usually 1-3 months).

The contract is signed by both parties (intern/parents if minor, employer) and deposited with the competent cantonal authority in the Zurich canton within the prescribed terms.

During the internship, it is essential to register with the AVS/LPP pension system, verify the mandatory insurance coverage (sickness and accidents), and consult the cantonal fiscal parameters to understand the applicable deductions on the remuneration. Frequently Asked Questions
What is the minimum age to start an apprenticeship in Zurich?
Typically, apprentices start the path after completing compulsory school (around the age of 15-16). Swiss federal law does not set an absolute minimum age, but the completion of compulsory schooling is the prerequisite. The canton of Zurich may have additional specifications; check with the cantonal vocational training office.
What are the holiday rights during the internship?
Under Swiss federal law, apprentices are entitled to a minimum of 4 weeks of annual leave. If the apprentice is less than 20 years old, the entitlement increases to 5 weeks. These days must be agreed between the company and the trainee, preferably before the start of the path in the contract.
How is an apprentice's pay taxed in Zurich?
The salary is subject to direct federal tax (DFI), in addition to cantonal and municipal taxes applied by the canton of Zurich. The overall rate depends on the salary amount and local rates. You can use a calculator to estimate the net amount of compensation.
What is the impact of AVS/AI contributions on the apprentice's paycheck?
Apprentices pay AVS/AI/IPG (social security) contributions at the rate of 5.3% of gross salary. The employer pays further 10.6% in total. These contributions accumulate pension credits essential for the future AVH annuity and for access to the second pillar (LPP/BVG).
What happens at the end of the apprenticeship?
At the end of the training course (usually after 3-4 years), the apprentice takes the final exam to obtain the Federal Certificate of Capacity (AFC) or the Certificate of Practical Training (CFP). These diplomas are nationally recognised and facilitate access to the Swiss labour market as a skilled worker.
