In August consumer prices increased by 0.4% (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 3 September 2026

The consumer price index (CPI) increased by 0.4% in August 2026 compared to the previous month, reaching 101.5 points (December 2025 = 100).

Context

In a nutshell

  • The consumer price index (CPI) increased by 0.4% in August 2026.
  • Compared to August 2025, the increase was 0.8%.
  • The Federal Statistical Office (FSO) has published the results.

Key facts

  • What: Increase in the consumer price index
  • When: August 2026
  • Where: Switzerland
  • Who: Federal Statistical Office (FSO)
  • Amount: 0.4% compared to July 2026, 0.8% compared to August 2025

The consumer price index (CPI) increased by 0.4% in August 2026 compared to the previous month, reaching 101.5 points (December 2025 = 100). Compared to the same month last year, the increase was 0.8%. This is what emerges from the results of the Federal Statistical Office (FSO).

Operational details

The 0,4% growth compared to the previous month is due to various factors, including the increase in housing rents. Prices for gasoline, diesel, heating oil, and stationary hospital services have also increased. On the other hand, prices for international package tours, car rental and car sharing, as well as those in the hotel sector, have decreased. This increase in the CPI has practical implications for Swiss consumers. With the rise in rent and fuel prices, the cost of daily life increases, affecting families' purchasing power. Additionally, the increase in hospital service prices could influence healthcare expenses, which are already a significant burden for many residents. For cross-border workers who work in Switzerland but live in Italy, these increases can have a significant impact. For example, transportation costs could increase significantly if gasoline and diesel prices continue to rise. Furthermore, cross-border workers who rent homes in Switzerland may see an increase in their housing expenses. Compared to August 2025, the CPI has increased by 0,8%. This increase is more modest than the monthly increase but still indicates a long-term trend of rising prices. This could be a sign of growing inflation, which could influence the economic policy decisions of the Federal Council and the cantons. The increase in the CPI could prompt the Federal Council to review its economic policies. For example, it may be necessary to increase the cantonal minimum wages to compensate for the rise in the cost of living. Additionally, the cantons may need to review their tax policies to ease the burden on taxpayers. To better understand the impact of this CPI increase, it is useful to compare it with other economic statistics. For example, the increase in the CPI could be correlated with wage increases or overall economic growth. However, without specific data on these aspects, it is difficult to conduct a comprehensive analysis.
