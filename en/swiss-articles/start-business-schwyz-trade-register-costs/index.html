Operational details

Capital, responsibility and strategic choice

The choice of legal form is not just a bureaucratic matter: it affects property liability, the ability to raise capital and the tax burden. An individual activity does not require minimum capital but exposes the holder to unlimited liability. The Kollektivgesellschaft (joint venture) operates with at least two shareholders who are jointly and severally liable; the Kommanditgesellschaft instead provides for at least one general partner, whose liability is limited to the contribution.

Särl/Sàrl and AG/SA are joint-stock companies: liability is limited to the capital contributed. The main difference lies in the minimum capital — higher for AG/SA according to Swiss practice — and in the possibility of issuing shares. The source confirms that Särl/Sàrl and AG/SA belong to the category of limited liability companies, without however indicating the exact amounts of the minimum capital.

Insurance and social security obligations

Key points

Step-by-step procedure for starting a business

1. Choose the right legal form. Assess liability, required capital and administrative complexity. For low-risk activities with no employees, a sole proprietorship may be sufficient; for activities with asset risk, a GmbH/Sàrl or AG/SA offer protection.

2. Check the availability of the name. Before registration, you must ensure that the chosen name is not already registered. The search service is available at the commercial register office.

3. Draft the founding deed and articles of association. For GmbH/Sàrl and AG/SA a public deed is required; for partnerships, a written contract between the partners may suffice.

4. Pay in the minimum capital and open a bank account. The payment must be certified by the bank and the amount deposited in the business account.

5. Register with the commercial register. The application is submitted to the cantonal office, with all required documents. The source notes the existence of registration fees, but the specific amount is not indicated: to be requested directly from the office.

6. Register with the tax authorities. Distinguish between direct federal tax/VAT (FTA/ESTV) and cantonal/municipal taxes (tax administration of Canton Schwyz). The source clarifies the respective competences: the FTA/ESTV handles direct federal tax and VAT, cantonal administrations handle the rest.

7. Register with a compensation fund. For OASI/DI/IC contributions and, if you hire employees, also unemployment insurance and LAINF (occupational accident insurance).

8. Take out the mandatory insurances. LAMal (health insurance) within 3 months of starting the activity for the owner and family members; LAINF for employees.

Frequently Asked Questions
What legal forms can be chosen in the Canton of Schwyz?
In Switzerland the most common forms are individual activity, partnership (Kollektivgesellschaft), limited partnership (Kommanditgesellschaft), limited liability company (Särl/Sàrl) and public limited company (AG/SA). Särl/Sàrl and AG/SA are limited liability companies with limited liability to the capital contributed; Kollektivgesellschaft and Kommanditgesellschaft are partnerships. When is registration in the trade register mandatory?
Registration is mandatory for joint-stock companies and partnerships that are not simple individual activities. The procedure provides for bylaws, identification of corporate bodies and, for Särl/Sàrl, certification of the payment of capital. Once registered, the new entity acquires legal personality and obtains a UID number valid throughout the federal territory. The source reports the existence of registration fees, but the specific amount is not indicated.
How does the Swiss tax system work for new businesses?
The system is divided into three levels: the federal direct tax (IFD), managed by the AFC/ESTV; the cantonal and municipal taxes, managed by the cantonal administrations. Each canton has its own tax law and its own multiplier; municipalities apply an additional multiplier on the cantonal tax. For VAT, the obligation to register is triggered when certain turnover thresholds are exceeded, with the AFC/ESTV as the competent authority. The source does not specify the current threshold.
What are the insurance and social security obligations at start-up?
Those who start a business with employees must enroll in a compensation fund for AVS/AI/IPG (10.6% total, of which 5.3% is borne by the employee) and AD/AC (1.1% up to the indexed annual ceiling). Compulsory accident insurance (LAINF) provides contributions between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector, paid to SUVA or private insurer. For the LPP, contributions on the coordinated salary follow an age scale: 7% (25–34 years), 10% (35–44), 15% (45–54) and 18% from 55 years.
How long must compulsory health insurance be taken out?
Health insurance under LAMal is mandatory for all residents and must be taken out within 3 months of arrival or commencement of business. Premiums are per capita and vary by canton and region; the Canton of Schwyz provides for a system of premium reduction for the lowest income brackets. Standard deductibles for adults start at CHF 300, but CHF 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500 are also possible.
