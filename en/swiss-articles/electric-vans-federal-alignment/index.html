Electric vans compared to traditional vans: new from 1 October 2026 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 19 August 2026

The Federal Council will equate electric commercial vehicles up to 4.25 tonnes with traditional vans from 1 October 2026. Driving at 120 km/h on the motorway, national tachograph exemption: everything you need to know.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Federal Council equates electric vans with traditional vans from 1 October 2026
  • Driving at 120 km/h on the motorway, national tachograph exemption
  • Five amended ordinances; cantons adjust by 1 February 2028

Key facts

  • What: Regulatory equivalence of electric commercial vehicles ≤4.25 tons to traditional vans
  • When: Decision 19 August 2026; entry into force 1 October 2026
  • Where: Switzerland (federal road traffic ordinances)
  • Who: Federal Council, after consultation
  • Adjustment deadline: 1 February 2028 for the cantons
  • Highway speed: 120 km/h for equivalent vehicles

The Federal Council announced on 19 August 2026 a decision destined to revolutionise the wheeled delivery industry in Switzerland. Electric commercial vehicles up to 4.25 tons will receive the same regulatory treatment as traditional vans, breaking down a series of administrative constraints that until now hindered the competitiveness of green mobility in national freight transport.

The decision stems from the examination of the results of the consultation and involves the amendment of as many as five federal ordinances on road traffic. The stated objective is twofold: to pave the way for the spread of electric mobility in the sector and to accelerate the decarbonisation of the delivery sector, a strategic priority for the Confederation in the framework of the transition

Operational details

Economic impact on national logistics

Equalization represents a concrete economic catalyst for delivery companies evaluating investments in electric fleets. Until now, a 4-tonne electric van remained subject to heavy vehicle rules: mandatory tachograph, motorway speed restrictions, and driving time constraints. These administrative burdens eroded the margins of ecological and economic advantage compared to traditional combustion models, discouraging the transition even for companies sensitive to sustainability.

With the new legislation entering into force on October 1, 2026, the calculation of operating costs changes radically. A driver of an equalized electric van will be able to:

  • Save travel time on long motorway stretches, thanks to the permitted 120 km/h (vs. 100 km/h for traditional heavy vans)
  • Avoid daily tachograph completion and associated administrative compliance costs
  • Benefit from less frequent technical maintenance cycles, shortening vehicle downtime
  • Operate with greater schedule flexibility, thanks to exemptions from work and rest time constraints

These considerations directly affect the profitability of delivery services, especially for couriers and small logistics companies operating on repeated national routes (e.g., same-day deliveries from a distribution center to distant cities). Electric mobility thus becomes not only an environmental choice, but an economically rational one for the company budget.

Key points

Implementation Timeline and Practical Adjustments

The chronology is precisely defined by the Federal Council: October 1, 2026, is the effective date of the new equivalency. Drivers and companies intending to benefit must plan the purchase of electric vehicles up to 4.25 tonnes in view of that date. Until then, current rules remain in force (mandatory tachograph, speed limits, restrictions on driving times for vehicles over 3.5 tonnes).

From October 1, 2026 onwards, new electric vans registered under this threshold will be able to operate according to the new rules from the day they are entered in the vehicle registry. Vehicles already in circulation can be updated to the new operating procedures even after entry into force, provided they meet the technical equivalency requirements (weight up to 4.25 tonnes, electric propulsion, compliance with amended ordinances).

Adjustment Procedures for Cantons and Administrative Management

The cantons must officially communicate transition procedures to industry operators by February 2028, particularly regarding:

  • Technical reclassification procedures for already registered vehicles
  • Criteria for verifying weight and actual configuration
  • Any local differences in the calculation of cantonal vehicle taxes
  • Timelines for upgrading IT control systems

This federal-cantonal alignment ensures that implementation takes place in a coordinated manner, reducing regulatory confusion across the various cantons and allowing companies to plan investments with certainty.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main benefits for equalised electric van drivers?
From October 1, 2026, drivers of electric commercial vans up to 4.25 tons will be able to drive on the highway at 120 km/h (instead of 100 km/h), will not be subject to the tachograph obligation in national traffic, and will not have to comply with federal restrictions on driving and rest times. Technical checks will also become less frequent, aligning with the cycles provided for small thermal vans up to 3.5 tons.
When will the new federal regulations officially take effect?
The entry into force is set for 1 October 2026. The Federal Council announced the decision on 19 August 2026, after reviewing the results of the consultation. The cantons will have until 1 February 2028 to fully align their administrative and control systems.
What maximum weight of commercial vehicles is affected by the equipment?
The equipment covers electric commercial vehicles up to 4.25 tons in total weight. This limit makes it possible to preserve the categorical distinctions for heavier vehicles, which remain subject to the ordinary provisions on heavy commercial transport.
How does the motorway regime change for equivalent vans?
Equalised electric commercial vans will be able to travel on the motorway at 120 km/h, like small thermal vans up to 3.5 tonnes. Before the reform, vans >3.5 tons were limited to 100 km/h. This 20 km/h acceleration allows for greater operational efficiency on long domestic journeys.
What federal ordinances have been amended by the Federal Council?
The Federal Council has amended five federal road traffic ordinances. The source does not specify the exact numbers of the ordinances, but the scope is mainly road traffic, the technical requirements of the vehicles, the tachograph provisions and the limits relating to working, driving and resting times.
Will electric vans already in circulation be able to benefit from the new equipment?
Yes, vehicles already in circulation may be reclassified according to the new standards, provided they meet the technical requirements (weight up to 4.25 tons, all-electric power supply, compliance with the amended ordinances). The cantons will communicate the reclassification procedures by 1 February 2028.
