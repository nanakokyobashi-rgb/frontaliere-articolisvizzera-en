The 2026 guide to the Swiss healthcare system starts with a practical distinction: LAMal/KVG is mandatory for residents, but the premium is not a levy and does not follow the model of payroll contributions. Anyone arriving in Switzerland has three months to obtain it.

The premium is private and per capita. Its amount varies by canton and region; premium reductions, by contrast, are a cantonal subsidy. These are therefore two separate levels when analyzing the cost of living.

In the canton of Aargau, the practical assessment starts with the applicable premium area, not with a national average. The choice of insurer and deductible should be linked to the area, while any potential reduction requires consideration of cantonal jurisdiction.

For adults, the available deductibles are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500. The thresholds define the deductible options; the premium must nevertheless be considered together with the canton or region, without replacing these references with a national figure.

Average LAMal premium figures are indexed annually. In 2026, it is advisable to verify the updated figure from the official source, without entering an amount if the year is uncertain.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who is required to take out LAMal insurance in Switzerland?
LAMal insurance is mandatory for all people residing in Switzerland. The prescribed deadline for taking it out is within three months of arrival in the country. This insurance is private, with per-capita premiums, and should not be confused with a tax or a payroll contribution such as AVS/AHV, AI/IPG, AD/AC or LPP/BVG.
What deductibles are available for adults?
For adults, the deductibles available under the system are CHF 300, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500. The choice of deductible and health insurance fund should be linked to the area of residence, such as the canton of Aargau, bearing in mind that the premium varies by canton and region.
How does the reduction of LAMal premiums work?
