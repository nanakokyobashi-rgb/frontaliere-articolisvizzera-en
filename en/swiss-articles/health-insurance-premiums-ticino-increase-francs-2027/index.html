Health Insurance Premiums: When 3.7% Doesn't Tell the Whole Story

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 30 September 2026

In 2027, the average health insurance premium in Ticino will increase by 3,7%, reaching 519.90 francs per month, an increase of 18.60 francs, 26% higher than the Swiss average of 412 francs.

For 2027, the average health insurance premium in Ticino is projected to rise by 3.7%. This percentage is below 5% of the Swiss average and comes on the heels of the steep increases of recent years. The initial sign is positive: Ticino appears to be among the cantons least affected.

Glarus recorded a 3% increase, while Vaud saw a 3.5% rise. These are the only cantons with a lower percentage increase than Ticino’s. However, the picture changes when the percentage is converted into francs—that is, the actual amount that comes out of policyholders’ pockets each month.

The Ranking in Francs

The average premium in Ticino rises from 501.30 to 519.90 francs per month. The increase is 18.60 francs. When compared with the other cantons, Ticino no longer ranks third among those with the smallest increase: it now ranks 13th out of 26 cantons. The difference between the two rankings is not a contradiction, but the result of a percentage applied to an already high baseline.

The gap becomes even clearer when compared to the national average. In 2027, the average premium in Ticino will reach 519.90 francs per month, compared to the Swiss average of 412 francs. The gap is 107.90 francs per month, equal to 1,294.80 francs per year for each insured person. The premium in Ticino will therefore be about 26% higher than the national average.

Lorenzo Onderka, a supporter of “Avanti con Ticino&Lavoro,” compares these figures with those from the Federal Statistical Office (UST/BFS). The latest data show a median wage of 5,708 francs in Ticino—the lowest among Switzerland’s major regions—compared to the national average of 7,024 francs. The key point is not to deny the slowdown, but to measure it alongside actual spending: the percentage describes growth, while the amount in francs shows the impact on the monthly budget. Operational details

When a Percentage Isn’t Enough

Comparing percentages and francs changes how the announcement is interpreted. A percentage measures the rate of increase; the monthly amount shows how much of a burden it places on the budget. In the case of Ticino, the 3.7% increase is applied to an average premium that is already high. That is why the increase amounts to 18.60 francs per month, even though the percentage increase remains lower than the national average.

The Growing Gap

The practical issue is the difference compared to the Swiss average. The average premium for policyholders in Ticino is 519.90 francs per month, compared to 412 francs. The monthly gap of 107.90 francs amounts to 1,294.80 francs over the course of a year. For a couple, the source estimates the impact at nearly 2,600 francs annually. This is not an abstract figure: these are resources that could be diverted from other necessities, consumption, or savings.

Comparing this to income figures makes the situation clearer. Ticino’s median wage of 5,708 francs is cited as the lowest among Switzerland’s major regions, while the national average is 7,024 francs. The average health insurance premium in Ticino, on the other hand, is about 26% above the Swiss average. The combination described by the source is therefore twofold: a higher monthly cost and a lower median wage.

This does not negate the slowdown. In 2025, the increase in healthcare costs in Ticino was below the national average, and the measures taken may be starting to yield results. This finding remains to be acknowledged, but it does not replace the question regarding the starting point. Even more modest growth can still exert significant pressure when the base premium is already high.

Key points

From Percentages to the Budget: A Practical Guide

To analyze an advertisement without focusing solely on the percentage, you can follow a four-step process. There’s no need to choose between percentages and francs: both measures are necessary, as the author argues.

Four Steps to Analyzing the Advertisement

1. Identify the reference point. Separate the average increase in Ticino (3.7%) from the Swiss average (5%). Also keep the figures for Glarus (3%) and Vaud (3.5%) distinct: these are percentage comparisons, not monthly amounts.

2. Convert the rate to francs. Pair the 3.7% figure with the increase from 501.30 to 519.90 francs per month. The figure to focus on is therefore 18.60 francs per month. This adjustment prevents a ranking based solely on percentages from obscuring Ticino’s position, which, in francs, ranks 13th out of 26 cantons.

3. Extend the comparison to a full year. Use the difference from the Swiss average: 107.90 francs per month and 1,294.80 francs per year for each insured person. For a couple, the figure is nearly 2,600 francs. This is the measure that most closely reflects the impact on household finances.

4. Consider income alongside premiums. Examine the premium in relation to the median income: 5,708 francs in Ticino versus 7,024 francs for the national average. This comparison is not meant to replace the premium data but to illustrate its relative impact on household budgets.

The same table also allows you to view the 2025 data: the increase in healthcare costs in Ticino was below the national average. This slowdown should be noted without overlooking the starting point. In public communications, percentages and amounts should therefore appear in the same context.
