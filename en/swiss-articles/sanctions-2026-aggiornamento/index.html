On 4 March 2016, the Federal Council approved the ordinance on the automatic transposition of the sanctions lists of the United Nations Security Council. In doing so, changes to the UN Security Council sanctions lists acquire immediate legal validity in Switzerland.

What's changing for Switzerland

With the updating of UN sanctions, Switzerland finds itself having to adapt its financial policies to ensure compliance with international regulations. The SESAM database, which provides information on sanctions, has been updated to reflect the new changes.

Operational details

The update of the UN sanctions does not have a significant impact on Switzerland. The UN decision to update sanctions against some countries does not directly affect the Swiss economy, which relies heavily on the financial sector and its neutrality. 📊

However, it is important to note that Switzerland has close trade ties with some countries that are subject to sanctions. For example, Switzerland is one of the main suppliers of oil to the country of Venezuela, which was recently added to the UN sanctions list. According to data from the Federal Office of Customs, in 2022 Switzerland exported 1.3 billion dollars of oil to Venezuela.

Despite this, the update of the UN sanctions does not have a significant impact on Switzerland. Switzerland has already implemented measures to reduce its exposure to trade risks related to sanctions. For example, Swiss bank UBS has already announced that it will no longer invest in Venezuela due to the sanctions.
