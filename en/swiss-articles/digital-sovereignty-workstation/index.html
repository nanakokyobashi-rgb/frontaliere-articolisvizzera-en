Context

In a nutshell

Key facts

  • What: Program for a workstation based on digital sovereignty.
  • When: By the end of 2027.
  • Where: Swiss Federal Administration.
  • Who: Approximately 3,000 employees.
  • Amount: Approximately 9 million francs for the first phase.

The Federal Chancellery (CaF) has announced the launch of a program for a workstation based on digital sovereignty. During its session on 2 September 2026, the Federal Council was briefed on the results of a feasibility study conducted by the CaF, which demonstrated the feasibility of basing the Federal Administration's digital workstation on open source software. By the end of 2027, approximately 3,000 employees should have access to a workstation based on digital sovereignty with software that offers the basic features of Office. The funding is ensured by the funds allocated by the Parliament for the creation of a sovereign and open source Swiss platform dedicated to bureaucracy.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

The program for a workstation based on digital sovereignty represents a significant step towards the technological independence of the Swiss Federal Administration. The use of open-source software allows for a reduction in dependence on external suppliers and an increase in the security and transparency of digital operations. This change could also have positive implications for Swiss citizens, who could benefit from greater security and reliability in digital public services.

Implications for Public Employees

For the approximately 3000 employees who will have access to the new workstation, there will be new opportunities for training and skill development. The transition to a digital sovereignty-based solution will require a considerable investment in terms of adjustments, security checks, and platform development, as well as the development of further skills. This could involve a transition period during which employees will need to familiarize themselves with the new system.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before this program, the Federal Administration mainly used proprietary solutions such as Microsoft 365. The new workstation based on digital sovereignty will offer similar functionalities but with the advantage of being more secure and transparent. This change could also reduce long-term costs, as open-source software does not require expensive licenses.

Future Scenarios

By the end of 2027, the developed solution will be made available to approximately 3000 users. The results of this first phase will allow for an estimate of the costs of a possible large-scale introduction and the costs of ordinary operation. This could lead to a future decision to fully adopt the digital sovereignty-based solution for the entire Federal Administration.

Action: What to Do Concretely

For public employees who will be involved in the program, it is important to follow the guidelines provided by CaF for the transition to the new workstation. This may include participating in training sessions and familiarizing yourself with the new features of the open source software. It is also important to monitor CaF's official communications to stay updated on the program's progress and any deadlines.
